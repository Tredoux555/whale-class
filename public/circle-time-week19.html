China

Week of Jan 25–29 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

Chinareddragonwallpanda

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “China!” (hug yourself), “Red!” (wave the red cloth), “Dragon!” (wiggle both arms).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “I live in China.” · “The flag is red.” · “The wall is very long.” · “The panda eats bamboo.”

The one big idea: the country we all live in has a name. Last week it was our city; this week it is the whole country round it — China — and it is red, and huge, and ours. Every day one real thing out of the box that is China in the hand: the flag, the panda, the dragon ribbon, a block of the wall, a pair of chopsticks.

Say both, out loud, on Monday. If a child's family is not Chinese, that is easy and lovely and it is said plainly at the circle: “You live in China, and your family is from ___.” Both true, both named, nobody left out of the hug. Then straight on with the flag.

Next week is Chinese New Year — the countdown week, and then the long holiday. This week is the ground underneath it: the country first, the festival second. Say once on Friday that it is coming, and no more than that.

Day 1 · This Is China

Today's words: China · red

Grab: a small five-star red flag in the Magic Box; a big map of China that will lie on the floor in the middle of the circle; one little paper flag or red sticker to plant on Beijing; the 中 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is flat and light and something inside it slides. Hold it up. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tip it slowly from side to side — a soft slither, no rattle. Peek in for a long time and pull out something folded. Unfold it once. Twice. Then let it fall open all at once and hold it up above your head.

Teacher“Look at this. It's red… it has little gold stars… and it belongs to the whole country we live in.” (let it hang still for one long beat, then wave it)

Arms wide, then hug yourself: “China!”

4 min Teach · China & red

Everybody stands. Say it twice — the second time they say it with you:

This is my country, (both arms out wide)

big and wide. (sweep the arms right round the circle)

The flag is RED… (wave the flag over your head)

and I live inside! (hug yourself tight)

Now the map goes down on the floor in the middle of the circle and everybody kneels round it. Walk one finger over it. Find our city, and plant the little flag on it. “We are here. This dot is us.” Then sweep your whole hand over the map: “and ALL of this is China.” Let each child put one finger on the map.

Littles hug themselves and say “China!”, wave and say “Red!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “I live in China.” “The flag is red.”

Teacher fails: point at the far blue edge of the map, out in the sea, and say “So this is our kindergarten, just here.” Nod, satisfied. Wait to be corrected.

Chinese moment: lay the 中 card on the map and say 中国 once, all together, then again in a whisper. The card stays on the map all week — the row grows by one character a day.

3 min Song · I Live in China — chorus + Monday verse

China, China — this is my home! (arms wide, then hug yourself)

Red, red flag — watch it fly! (wave the flag)

A dragon and a panda and a great long wall, (wiggle arms · panda ears · stretch wide)

China, China — I love it all! (hug yourself, then both arms up)

Monday's verse: This is China, big and wide, (sweep both arms round)

Beijing city — that's where I live inside! (point down at the floor, then hug)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole thing on Friday. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. Sing it standing round the map, always.

3 min Game · Flag wave

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” You hold the flag. The rule is one sentence long:

Teacher“Flag UP — shout CHINA! Flag DOWN — not one sound.”

Then play with it: up fast, down fast, up and hold it there while they run out of breath, a tiny lift that is nearly nothing, then a huge one. The silence is the half they love.

Finish by giving the flag to one child to be the flag-holder while the rest of you shout and stop, shout and stop.

This is the whole week's volume control and you will use it every day from now on — the flag comes out again on Friday for the quick-fire round.

1 min Close

Standing round the map. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Round Is a Mooncake (Roseanne Thong) — shapes found in ordinary Chinese things, a rice bowl, a lantern, a moon. Point at the round things in your own room afterwards.

Day 2 · Panda and Bamboo

Today's words: panda (China · red come back every day)

Grab: a panda toy and a few real bamboo leaves or a short bamboo stalk in the Magic Box; the map with yesterday's flag still on it; the panda cards from print pack page 11; the 熊 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box rustles. Chant, shake it gently — a dry papery whisper, nothing hard.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift the lid a crack and sniff: green, grassy, like the garden after rain. Pull out the bamboo first and pass it round to be smelled and felt — every child.

Teacher“Somebody in here eats this. All day. Who eats leaves all day?” (wait, then lift the panda out slowly by its ears)

Hands to your eyes like two black patches: “Panda!”

4 min Teach · Panda

The panda sits in your lap facing the circle. Twice, and the second time they do it with you:

Panda, panda, black and white, (hands over the eyes, then pat your tummy)

Sitting up so still. (sit up very tall, hands in your lap)

Munch, munch, munch the bamboo, (both hands to your mouth, chewing hard)

Munch until you're full! (rub your tummy, slow and happy)

Two colours only: black and white. Everybody find black on their own clothes and hold it up; now find white. The panda has both, always, in the same places — ears, eyes, arms, legs.

Littles do the patches and say “Panda!” Bigs take the frame: “The panda eats bamboo.” “The panda is black and white.”

Teacher fails: offer the panda a banana. Then a biscuit. Then your shoe. Look genuinely puzzled each time it is refused. Wait for the outrage.

Chinese moment: the 熊 card joins 中 on the map — 熊猫. Pandas live in 四川; put a finger on the map roughly there, west of us, and leave it at that.

3 min Song · I Live in China — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — on “and a panda” everybody now does the black-patch hands — then today's new verse:

Tuesday's verse: Panda, panda, black and white, (patches, then tummy)

Munching bamboo day and night! (chew with both hands, then head on hands, asleep)

Sing this verse slowly and heavily, like something very large that has just eaten — then snap straight back into the bright chorus. The change of speed is what they remember it by.

3 min Game · Panda munch

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody sits in the circle. The bamboo goes round, hand to hand, while you clap a steady beat.

Teacher“Pass… pass… pass… STOP.” (the clapping stops dead)

Whoever is holding the bamboo is the panda: three enormous munches — the whole circle counts them, one, two, three — and then FREEZE, panda and everybody, absolutely still, for as long as you can hold them.

Then the clapping starts again. Go round until every child has been the panda at least once; slow the pass down near the ones who have not.

The freeze is the real work — it is the quietest game of the week, and it teaches them that stopping is part of playing.

1 min Close

Sitting up tall like pandas, hands over your eyes. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Panda Bear, Panda Bear, What Do You See? (Bill Martin Jr & Eric Carle) — the whole class can join in on “What do you see?” by the second page.

Day 3 · The Red Dragon

Today's words: dragon (China · red · panda come back)

Grab: a long red silk ribbon in the Magic Box — two or three metres, long enough for the whole class to hold at once; a simple dragon head (a paper plate with eyes, or a small box on a stick); a clear floor to wind round; the 龙 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is full — the lid will not sit down flat. Chant, then shake it: almost no noise at all, which is wrong for something this fat.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek in. Gasp. Shut the lid. Peek again. Now take hold of one end and pull, hand over hand, and keep pulling — and keep pulling — until the red ribbon is longer than the circle and lying in coils on the floor.

Teacher“It's red… and it's long… and in China, when this dances, it is a DRAGON.” (lift the middle of it and make one big wave run down its whole length)

Both arms wiggling: “Dragon!”

4 min Teach · Dragon

Everybody stands, the ribbon on the floor in front of them. Twice, second time all together:

A dragon in China is a friendly thing, (both arms wiggling from the shoulders)

He wiggles and he waggles, (wiggle low, then high)

He does not bite — (shake your head, hands up, no)

he DANCES! (one enormous wave with both arms)

This is the part worth saying slowly: the Chinese dragon is lucky and kind. He brings the rain. He is not a monster to be frightened of — and he is never danced by one person on their own. It takes a whole class of people under one long body to make a dragon move.

Littles wiggle both arms and say “Dragon!” Bigs take the frame: “The dragon is long.” “The dragon dances.”

Teacher fails: hold both ends of the ribbon yourself and wiggle it feebly on the spot. “There you are. A dragon.” Look pleased. Wait until somebody tells you it takes everybody.

Chinese moment: the 龙 card joins 中 and 熊 on the map. Say 舞龙 — dragon dance — once, doing the arm wave on the word 舞.

3 min Song · I Live in China — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — on “a dragon” the whole circle now sends one wave down the ribbon — then today's new verse:

Wednesday's verse: The dragon dances, long and loud, (wiggle both arms, high and wide)

Wiggle, wiggle through the crowd! (wiggle in and out between each other)

Sing this one moving — it is the only verse of the week that is not sung sitting or standing still. Chorus standing, verse walking, chorus standing again; the stop at the end is what gets them back on the mat.

3 min Game · Dragon train

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody takes hold of the ribbon with one hand, spaced out along it. You are the head, with the dragon head held up in front.

Teacher“The dragon never breaks. If it breaks, we stop, and we join it up again.”

Wind slowly round the room: round the shelf, under the window, in a big curl and out of it. Then faster. Then high — everybody's hand up — then low, ducking. Then FREEZE, dead still, dragon and all.

Finish by curling the dragon into a spiral on the floor and everybody sitting down exactly where they are.

Put your steadiest child at the tail, not the head — the tail is where a dragon breaks. Littles who will not hold the ribbon can hold your hand and be the head with you.

1 min Close

Sitting in the ribbon spiral, everybody holding a piece of red. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Great Race: The Story of the Chinese Zodiac (Dawn Casey) — the dragon is in it, and so are twelve animals they will meet again next week.

Day 4 · The Great Wall

Today's words: wall (China · red · panda · dragon come back)

Grab: a big photo of the Great Wall and one wooden block in the Magic Box; the whole block basket standing by; a long curved line taped or chalked on the floor with a hill in the middle of it (the shelf tray's printed base-line works); the 城 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is heavy and it clonks. Chant, then shake it once, hard: one solid knock.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes… one wooden block. Hold it up. Turn it over. Let the disappointment sit there for a second.

Teacher“One block. That's all. — Unless…” (lift out the photo and hold it up beside the block) “…you have about a million of them.”

Arms stretched as wide as they will go: “Wall!”

4 min Teach · Wall

The photo goes round the circle, every child holding it. Then, standing:

Up the hill and down the hill, (one hand climbs, then drops)

the wall goes on and on. (roll both hands forward, away from you)

Longer than the longest thing — (stretch both arms as wide as they go)

the GREAT WALL of China! (shout it, arms still wide)

Say the size in things they know: it is longer than our room, longer than our whole school, longer than our whole street, longer than our city — you could walk all day and all night and not come to the end of it. It climbs over the mountains instead of going round them. And people built it with their hands, stone by stone, a very long time ago.

Littles stretch wide and say “Wall!” Bigs owe you: “The wall is very long.” “I can build a wall.”

Teacher fails: lay two blocks end to end, dust off your hands and step back. “Finished. The Great Wall of China.” Fold your arms. Wait.

Chinese moment: the 城 card joins the row — 长城, the long wall. Say 长 stretching your arms wide on the word, then 城.

3 min Song · I Live in China — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — on “a great long wall” everybody stretches as wide as they can and holds it — then today's new verse:

Thursday's verse: Up the wall and up the hill, (hand climbing, step by step)

Long, long, longer — longer still! (arms opening wider on every “long”)

Start this verse tiny, hands together, and open a little further on every single “long” until the last one is as wide as they go. Then the chorus straight after, at full size.

3 min Game · Build the wall

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The curved line is on the floor with its hill in the middle. The block basket sits at one end.

Teacher“One block each. On the line. And we count it as it grows.”

One child at a time walks a block down and lays it on the line, and the whole class counts out loud — one, two, three… When the wall reaches the hill it has to climb: the blocks go up the slope, which is the moment the photo suddenly makes sense.

If it falls, nobody is in trouble and we rebuild that bit together. Count the whole wall once more at the end, all together, pointing.

Leave the wall standing until Friday if you possibly can. It goes on the shelf as Tray 3 and it is the thing they will show their parents.

1 min Close

Standing along your own wall, arms stretched wide. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Grandfather Tang's Story (Ann Tompert) — a tangram story; put the seven shapes out on the shelf afterwards and let them make one animal.

Day 5 · Jiaozi, Chopsticks and All of China

Today's words: all five — China · red · dragon · wall · panda

Grab: child chopsticks, two small bowls and a handful of pom-poms; a lump of dough and a small rolling pin (play dough is fine); one jade pendant or a smooth cool green stone; every prop from the week back in the Magic Box; the 饺 card; the awards from print pack page 17.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the whole week is in the box and it is the fullest it has been. Chant, then take the things out one at a time — and the class names each one before it touches the floor.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Flag… “Red!” Panda… “Panda!” Bamboo, ribbon, block, photo — round they go. Then, last, with the lid nearly shut, one thing left.

Teacher“This one you have to feel, not look at. Eyes closed. Two hands.” (the jade goes round the circle with every child's eyes shut — cool, smooth, heavy for its size)

Then everybody opens their eyes at once and sees what they were holding.

4 min Teach · Jiaozi + the whole week

Fold a dumpling in the air, all together, twice — and then really do it with the dough:

A little round wrapper, (one flat hand held out)

a spoon of the middle, (the other hand spoons something in)

fold it, and pinch it, (fold the flat hand over, pinch along the edge)

pinch, pinch, PINCH! (three fast pinches, the last one shouted)

Jiaozi are the food a whole family makes together, everybody at one table, everybody's dumplings in the same pot — the ugly ones too. Let each child fold one real one out of dough and put it on the tray with the others.

Then the quick-fire round: hold up each prop, the class names it in English — and read the 汉字 row back off the map together: 中 · 熊 · 龙 · 城 · 饺. Littles give one word per prop; Bigs give the whole sentence for each.

Teacher fails: try to pick up a pom-pom with the chopsticks yourself. Drop it. Try again. Drop it. Hold the chopsticks like a spoon and look hopeful.

Chinese moment: the 饺 card finishes the row — 饺子. As the bowls go round, every single hand-over gets 你好 and 谢谢, out loud.

3 min Song · I Live in China — the whole song, top to bottom

Chorus → Monday (China) → Tuesday (panda) → Wednesday (dragon) → Thursday (wall) → Friday (jiaozi) → Chorus.

Friday's verse: Chopsticks, chopsticks — one, two, three, (two fingers pinching, counting)

Jiaozi, jiaozi — yum, for me! (pinch the dumpling, then rub your tummy)

This run-through IS the week's review. Every prop is out on the floor in front of them and every verse points at one. Sing it standing round the map with the wall still built beside you.

3 min Game · Chopstick race + quick-fire

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two bowls in front of each pair, pom-poms in one of them.

Teacher“Move them across — one at a time. Not fast. Neatly.”

Littles use their fingers or a small spoon and nobody says a word about it. Bigs use the chopsticks. It is not really a race — the child who moves three carefully beats the child who tips the bowl.

Then the flag comes back for quick-fire: flag UP and you hold up a prop — everybody shouts its name; flag DOWN — silence, and the next prop comes up. Finish on the flag itself: “CHINA!”

Awards. Print page 17, one half per child, and give them out here by name, one at a time, with the whole class clapping. Then the “Colour MY lantern” sheets go home in their bags.

1 min Close

Everything from the week on the floor, everybody standing round it. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Dim Sum for Everyone! (Grace Lin) — a family sharing little dishes, which is exactly what the chopstick tray on the shelf is for.

Theme Song · “I Live in China”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The three chords

C
F
G7
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“Chi—na—China”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the arm actions carry the song.

Tempo: proud and marching, not fast — this is a song you can walk to, and on Wednesday you will. Big open rest before the last “I love it all!” so they can hear it coming. Three chords, no Am anywhere, and every verse is only C and G7. Chant it without the uke first; add the chords once they know the actions.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CChina, China — Fthis is my Chome! (arms wide, then hug yourself)

FRed, red Cflag — watch it G7fly! C(wave the flag)

CA dragon and a Fpanda and a great long Cwall, (wiggle arms · panda ears · stretch wide)

FChina, CChina — I G7love it Call! (hug yourself, then both arms up)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who hugs herself on “home”, waves on “red” and stretches wide on “wall” has understood the week, whether or not a single word comes out.

One verse a day — every verse is just C and G7

Monday · China

CThis is China, big and wide, (sweep both arms right round)

G7Beijing city — Cthat's where I live inside! (point down at the floor, then hug yourself)

Tuesday · Panda

CPanda, panda, black and white, (hands over the eyes, then pat your tummy)

G7Munching bamboo — Cday and night! (chew with both hands, then head on hands)

Wednesday · Dragon

CThe dragon dances, long and loud, (wiggle both arms, high and wide)

G7Wiggle, wiggle — Cthrough the crowd! (wiggle in and out between each other)

Thursday · Wall

CUp the wall and up the hill, (one hand climbing, step by step)

G7Long, long, longer — Clonger still! (arms opening wider on every “long”)

Friday · Jiaozi

CChopsticks, chopsticks — one, two, three, (two fingers pinching, counting)

G7Jiaozi, jiaozi — Cyum, for me! (pinch the dumpling, then rub your tummy)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — and Friday's finale, sung standing round the map with the block wall still up beside them, doubles as the review of the whole week.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those three shapes — there is no Am this week at all. C is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the red flag to wave on the beat and do the actions instead: the class will not notice, and the arms carry the meaning either way.

Theme Shelf · China

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Flag collage
from MONDAY's Magic Box		A red card ground and five yellow felt stars to lay on it — one big, four small, in their proper places. Control: faint star shadows printed on the mat, so a star in the wrong place looks wrong before anybody says so. Language: “red · star · big · small.” Bigs: “The flag is red.”
Tray 2 · Panda black-and-white matching
from TUESDAY's Magic Box		Panda body-part cards cut in half — the black half and the white half of each ear, eye, arm, leg. Match them back into whole pandas. Control: matching symbols on the backs. The bamboo from Tuesday sits on the tray beside it. Bigs: “The panda is black and white.”
Tray 3 · Great Wall building
from THURSDAY — leave the class wall standing		The block basket and a printed curved base-line that climbs a paper hill. Lay the blocks along the line, over the hill, to the end. Control: the plan itself — the wall is finished when no line is showing. Counting happens here all week without anybody calling it maths.
Tray 4 · Chopstick transfer
practical life, from FRIDAY		Five pom-poms and five small bowls with child chopsticks. One pom-pom per bowl. Control: five bowls, five pom-poms — none left over and none doubled up. Beside it, crayons and the “Colour MY lantern” sheets (page 16). Sheets go home Friday with the award (page 17).

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can — the cards get handled all week and they come straight back out next week for Chinese New Year. The 3-part cards (pages 10–11), the twelve sorting cards (page 14) with the two signs (pages 12–13) and the face-down action cards (page 15) all live on Tray 2's shelf edge. The row of 汉字 cards the class built up (中 · 熊 · 龙 · 城 · 饺) stays on the map all week — that is the display, no extra work needed.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Jan 25–29 · Theme: China · Sound of the week: j

What we did

This week we learned the name of the place we live: China. On Monday a red flag came out of the Magic Box, a big map went down on the floor, and every child put one finger on it and found our own city on it — we are here, and all of this is China. On Tuesday a panda and a handful of real bamboo leaves went round the circle to be smelled, and we found black and white on our own clothes. On Wednesday a red silk ribbon came out of the box and kept coming out until it was longer than the circle — and then the whole class went under it and became one dragon, winding round the room without ever breaking. On Thursday one wooden block and a photograph turned into the Great Wall: we laid it block by block along a curving line, counting out loud, and made it climb a hill. And on Friday we folded jiaozi, moved pom-poms with chopsticks, and held a piece of jade with our eyes shut.

Sentences to listen for at home

“I live in China.” · “The flag is red.” · “The wall is very long.” · “The panda eats bamboo.” · “The dragon dances!”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “China!” with a hug, or “Dragon!” with both arms wiggling, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “I Live in China”

“China, China — this is my home! Red, red flag — watch it fly! A dragon and a panda and a great long wall, China, China — I love it all!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — China, panda, dragon, wall, jiaozi. Ask your child to sing the chorus and watch their hands: they hug themselves on “home”, wave on “red”, wiggle for the dragon and stretch as wide as they can for the wall.

Dark Phonics · the j sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met j — a quick voiced sound made right at the front of the mouth, the tongue jumping off the roof: jug, jam, jet. Our silly line for it is “jump in the jelly jam!” It landed perfectly this week: jiaozi, jade, jump. Say it and stop — j is short and sharp, never a long hiss.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · Ask your child “Where do you live?” and let them answer in English: “I live in China.” That is the whole week's assessment, done in five seconds.
2 · Show them the flag on a building on the way home and let them count the stars.
3 · Give them the chopsticks at dinner and let them try, badly, with something big. Praise the trying, not the catching.
4 · Hunt for the j sound together: jump, jam, jug, jacket, juice, jiaozi.
5 · Sing the chorus in the car. Four lines, four actions, no hard words.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
