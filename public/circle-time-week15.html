Whale Class · Weekly Circle Time

Christmas

Week of Dec 14–18 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

treestarpresentbellSanta

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Tree!” (arms out like branches), “Ho ho ho!” (pat your belly, big laugh), “Present!” (shake a box beside your ear).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “Merry Christmas!” · “This is a present for you.” · “I can see a star on the tree.” · “Thank you!”

The one big idea: Christmas is a giving festival. Every child makes one present during the week — in free work time, not at circle — and on Friday gives it away to a friend. Draw the giving pairs on Thursday so that on Friday every child has somebody to give to and somebody giving to them. Everything else this week is decoration; that is the lesson.

Last week before the winter holiday — we finish on Friday 18 December and come back on Monday 4 January. 冬至, the winter solstice, falls on the 22nd, in the holiday: dumplings at home.

Day 1 · The Christmas Tree

Today's words: tree

Grab: a real pine sprig in the Magic Box (a small branch off a real tree — it has to smell); the bare classroom tree standing where everyone can reach it; the picture cards from print pack page 11; the 树 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box weighs almost nothing. Hold it up. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — silence. Now lift the lid a crack and sniff, loudly, with your eyes shut. Sniff again. Let your face go amazed.

Teacher“Something in here smells like a whole FOREST.” (lift out the pine sprig and hold it under your own nose for one long beat)

The sprig goes right round the circle and every child sniffs it. Arms out like branches: “Tree!”

4 min Teach · Tree

Everybody stands up. We are the tree, twice — small and slow, then big:

My tree is green. (arms out wide, fingers spread like branches)

My tree is TALL. (stretch right up on tiptoe)

The lights go on… (open and close both hands, twinkling)

and a star on top of all! (both hands to a point above your head)

Littles do the branch arms and say “Tree!” Bigs take the frame: “This is my Christmas tree.” “The tree is green and tall.”

Then the bare tree itself. Walk round it once, touching nothing, and name what is not there yet: no lights, no star, nothing hanging. It is the emptiest tree in the world. Tomorrow the star, today everything else.

Teacher fails: try to hang a picture card on your own head, standing very still with your arms out. “Look. I am the tree.” Hold the pose. Wait.

Chinese moment: lay the 树 card at the foot of the tree and say 圣诞树 once. Leave the card there all week; the row grows by one card a day.

3 min Song · Ring the Bells, It's Christmas! — chorus + Monday verse

Ring, ring, ring the bells! (shake both hands like bells)

Christmas Day is here! (arms wide)

A star on top, a present down below, (reach up high, then right down to the floor)

Merry Christmas, everyone — HO HO HO! (pat your belly and laugh)

Monday's verse: A little green tree, a little green tree, (branch arms, small then big)

Lights and stars — come and see! (twinkling hands, then beckon everybody in)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole thing on Friday. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. Sing it standing at the tree, always.

3 min Game · Decorate the tree

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The picture cards are face down in a basket at the foot of the bare tree.

Teacher“Take one card… tell us what it is… now hang it on the tree.”

One child at a time: pick, name, hang. Littles name it with one word — “Bell!” “Star!” Bigs give the sentence as they hang it: “I can see a star on the tree.”

Go all the way round; everybody hangs one thing. By the end the tree is covered and it was covered by them.

Leave it up all week. Everything that comes out of the Magic Box from today on has somewhere to live, and on Friday the presents go at its feet.

1 min Close

Standing round the tree, branch arms out. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Tree… star… PRESENT! MERRY CHRISTMAS!” (arms out · point up high · hands held out · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Mr Willowby's Christmas Tree (Robert Barry) — a tree gets cut shorter and shorter and every animal in the wood ends up with one. It is a giving book, which is this week's whole point.

Day 2 · A Star on Top

Today's words: star (tree comes back every day)

Grab: the gold star for the top of the tree, in the Magic Box; about twenty small gold paper stars, hidden round the room before the children come in; a torch; the 星 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today there is exactly one thing in the box and it does not rattle. Chant, then tip the box slowly from side to side — something slides.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek in for a long time. Lift it out with both hands, very slowly, and turn it so the light catches the gold and flashes round the circle.

Teacher“It's gold… it's pointy… and it goes right at the very top.” (hold it above your own head)

Hands to a point above their heads: “Star!”

4 min Teach · Star

The star sits in your two open hands while you say it — twice, and the second time they say it with you:

One gold star, (hold it up in two hands)

Right on the TOP. (reach as high as you can)

It shines all night… (open and close both hands, twinkling)

and it does not stop! (keep twinkling, faster and faster)

Then the real thing: one child is lifted up (or stands on a safe chair, held) to put the star on the tree while the whole class counts down — three, two, one — and cheers.

Littles point up high and say “Star!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “I can see a star on the tree.” “The star is on the top.”

Teacher fails: put the star carefully on the floor beside the tree, step back, fold your arms and admire it. “Beautiful.” Wait. Let them march you back.

Chinese moment: the 星 card joins 树 at the foot of the tree — 星星. Sing one line of 《一闪一闪亮晶晶》 with the torch on the ceiling. One line only, then stop.

3 min Song · Ring the Bells, It's Christmas! — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — on “a star on top” everybody now points at the real star — then today's new verse:

Tuesday's verse: One gold star right on the top, (reach up high, hold it)

Twinkle, twinkle — it will not stop! (twinkling hands, faster and faster)

Sing this verse getting quieter and the twinkling getting faster, until the last word is a whisper and only the hands are moving. Then straight back into the big chorus. They love the drop.

3 min Game · Star hunt

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Twenty gold paper stars are hidden round the room, some in plain sight at knee height for the littlest ones.

Teacher“Walking feet… find the stars… GO!” (everything found comes straight back to the mat)

When the hunt is over, lay every star out in one long line on the carpet and count them all together, out loud, pointing at each one. Littles count with their fingers up; Bigs count the line themselves and tell you: “I found three stars.”

Hide exactly as many as the class can count to together, and count them into your hand before you hide them — the counting at the end is the real work, and it only works if the number comes out right.

1 min Close

Standing at the tree, everybody's star held up. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Tree… star… PRESENT! MERRY CHRISTMAS!” (arms out · point up high · hands held out · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Lights off, torch on the ceiling, and sing Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star lying on your backs. It costs nothing and they will ask for it every day this week.

Day 3 · A Present for You

Today's words: present (tree · star come back)

Grab: a wrapped present and a little bell in the Magic Box; the pass-the-present parcel, wrapped in one layer per child, with a bag of star stickers to share in the middle; music you can start and stop; wrapping paper, tape and ribbon on the shelf tray; the 礼 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box gives one bright ting when it moves. Chant, then shake it once — ting — and stop dead.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out the bell and ring it once, high above your head. Everybody goes quiet on the ring — that is the rule for the rest of the week.

Teacher“The bell means something is coming…” (reach back in and lift out the wrapped present in both hands)

Shake it beside your ear. “Present!”

4 min Teach · Present

The present stays in your hands the whole way through. Say it twice; the second time they do the actions with you:

A present, a present, tied with a bow, (hold it up and trace the ribbon with one finger)

What's inside? I do not know! (shake it beside your ear, then shrug enormously)

It's not for me… (hands off it, both behind your back)

it's for YOU, you know! (hold it out to one child with two hands)

Here is the whole week in one sentence, said slowly: a present is a thing you give away. Then send it round: two hands, eyes up, “This is a present for you.” — and the child who takes it says “Thank you!” before passing it on.

Littles shake it beside their ear and say “Present!” Bigs owe you both halves: “This is a present for you.” and “Thank you!”

Teacher fails: start tearing the paper off greedily with your teeth. “Mine. All mine.” Wait for the roar — “It's for YOU!” — then look very ashamed and hand it on.

Chinese moment: the 礼 card joins 树 · 星 — 礼物. And the two words that go with it every time something changes hands: 谢谢 and 不客气.

3 min Song · Ring the Bells, It's Christmas! — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — today one child holds the real bell and rings it on “ring, ring, ring” while everybody else shakes their hands — then:

Wednesday's verse: A present, a present, tied with a bow, (trace the bow in the air)

It's not for me — it's for YOU, you know! (hands to your own chest, then both hands out to a friend)

On “it's for YOU” everybody points at somebody else, not at the teacher. Twenty children pointing at each other and laughing is exactly the feeling you want in the room on Friday.

3 min Game · Pass the present

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody in a circle, knees touching. Say the rule out loud before you start: there is one layer for every child, so everybody unwraps exactly once, and what is inside is for all of us.

Teacher“Music on — pass it. Music STOPS — one layer off!” (the child who takes the layer off then hands the parcel straight on)

Round it goes. The waiting is the lesson and it is a hard one, so narrate it kindly: “It's not your turn yet… and you are waiting so well.” The last layer comes off to a bag of star stickers — one each, handed out by the child who opened it.

Never let the middle be a prize for one child. The whole game teaches waiting, and a single winner at the end quietly teaches the opposite.

1 min Close

Everybody's hands held out flat, as if offering something. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Tree… star… PRESENT! MERRY CHRISTMAS!” (arms out · point up high · hands held out · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Jolly Christmas Postman (Janet & Allan Ahlberg) — every page has a real envelope to open. Let a different child open each one and hand it round.

Day 4 · Ho Ho Ho, Santa!

Today's words: Santa (tree · star · present come back)

Grab: a Santa hat and a big red stocking in the Magic Box; a mirror the children can see themselves in; the 圣 card; paper and a pot to draw the giving pairs for tomorrow.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is fat and completely silent — press the lid down and it springs back up, soft.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it: nothing at all. Squash it: it bounces. Peek in and laugh out loud, then pull out something red, very slowly, and put it on your own head.

Teacher“HO… HO… HO!” (both hands on your belly, laughing from right down low)

Everybody pats their belly. “Ho ho ho!” Then the enormous red stocking comes out and gets held up against a child, who disappears inside it.

4 min Teach · Santa

Hat on. Say it twice, the second time with everybody joining in:

Red, red hat, (pat the hat on your head)

A big white beard, (stroke a long beard down to your tummy)

He comes at night… (tiptoe on the spot, finger on your lips)

HO HO HO — he's here! (both hands on your belly, big laugh)

Then the mirror. Every child who wants to wears the hat for five seconds, looks at themselves and does one “Ho ho ho!” Littles need nothing else. Bigs give the sentence: “Santa has a red hat.” “Santa gives presents to everybody.”

Say the true thing once, plainly: Santa is a story. Some families tell it, some families don't, and both are completely normal — exactly like families being different. Then get straight back to the laughing.

Teacher fails: put the Santa hat carefully on your foot and stand there beaming. “How do I look?” Hold it. Wait.

Chinese moment: the 圣 card joins the row — 圣诞老人 — and the whole class says 圣诞快乐！ once, at full volume.

Nobody has to wear it. If a child is frightened of the hat or the beard, it sits on your knee, on the far side of the circle, and nothing is said about it at all.

3 min Song · Ring the Bells, It's Christmas! — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — whoever is wearing the hat stands in the middle for the “HO HO HO” — then:

Thursday's verse: Red, red hat and a big white beard, (pat your head, then stroke the beard)

Ho, ho, ho! — Santa's here! (three belly laughs, then both arms thrown wide)

Do the three “ho”s lower each time, like a big slow drum. By tomorrow they will be doing the voice without you.

3 min Game · Santa says

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Simon says, played in a red hat — and every instruction is one of the week's words.

Teacher“Santa says… ring the bells! Santa says… hang a star! … wrap a present!” (the last one has no “Santa says” — freeze and look round)

Actions: ring the bells (shake both hands) · hang a star (reach up high) · wrap a present (wrap in the air) · be a tree (branch arms) · HO HO HO (pat your belly). Nobody is ever out; a child who moves just does it again with you.

Last round, the hat goes to a Big and they give the orders while you play in the circle like everybody else.

Five instructions, no more — the same five, over and over, faster and faster. That repetition is the English lesson; new instructions would only make it a listening puzzle.

1 min Close

Everybody's hands on their bellies, ready. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Tree… star… PRESENT! MERRY CHRISTMAS!” (arms out · point up high · hands held out · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Before home time: draw the giving pairs for tomorrow — every child's name pulled out of the stocking, so that everybody gives one present and everybody receives one. Check the list twice tonight; a child holding a present with nowhere to take it is the one thing that can spoil Friday.

Got 3 spare minutes? Dear Santa (Rod Campbell) — lift-the-flap, one flap per child, and they will shout “too big!” at you every time.

Day 5 · Give the Gift + Big Review

Today's words: bell — and tree · star · present · Santa, the whole week

Grab: the bell, and every prop from the week packed into the box (or a red sack); the presents the children made, hidden until the game; the giving-pairs list from yesterday; the awards (print pack page 17); the 送 card. Monday is the start of the winter holiday — back on 4 January.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out bulging and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The pine sprig → “Tree!”  The gold star → “Star!”  The wrapped present → “Present!”  The red hat → “Santa!”

Last of all, one small thing left in the bottom. Lift it out and ring it, high. “BELL!”

4 min Teach · Bell, then the whole week

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

Ring the bell — it's Christmas Day! (shake both hands like bells)

A tree, (branch arms) a star, (point right up high)

A present to give away, (hold both hands out to a friend)

And a HO HO HO! (pat your belly)

Merry Christmas — I love you! (hands over your heart, then blow a kiss)

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “I can see a star on the tree.” “This is a present for you.” “Merry Christmas!”

Teacher fails: on “a present to give away”, clutch the present to your chest with both arms and shake your head. Hold on tight. Let them prise it out of you.

Chinese moment: the 送 card joins the row — 树 · 星 · 礼 · 圣 · 送, the whole week on the mat, and 送 is the one that matters: to give. Then tell them: 冬至 falls on the 22nd, while we are all at home, and 北方冬至吃饺子 — families sit down together and eat dumplings. Fold an imaginary dumpling with them, all twenty pairs of hands.

3 min Song · Ring the Bells, It's Christmas! — Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: Bells and stars and presents too, (ring · point up · hands out)

Merry Christmas — I love you! (hands over your heart, then blow a kiss at the tree)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, standing all the way round the tree with the presents piled at its feet and nobody touching them. They learned it one verse a morning without ever noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · Give the gift

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Every child fetches the present they made and sits down holding it. Nobody opens anything until the very end — say so first, and say it twice.

Teacher(ring the bell once) “Whose turn? … Off you go.”

One bell, one child: they walk across the circle, hand it over with two hands and look at the person: “This is for you.” The one who receives it says “Thank you!” Then the bell rings again for the next child. Littles say the one word with the two hands — “Present!” — and that is a full win.

When every present has been given, and only then: open them all together, on the count of three.

Use yesterday's pairs, and hold the list in your hand. Every child gives one and receives one — count it twice before you start. This is the moment of the whole term; it is worth the extra two minutes of preparation.

1 min Close + awards

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home with the holiday news. Then, standing round the tree with their presents, all five gestures:

Everyone“Tree… star… PRESENT! MERRY CHRISTMAS!” (arms out · point up high · hands held out · both arms thrown wide, and everybody rings the bells on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Sing the whole song one more time with the lights off and the tree lit. Then wish them a happy holiday, in both languages, and tell them the date they come back: Monday 4 January.

Theme Song · “Ring the Bells, It's Christmas!”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The three chords

C
F
G7
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“ring — ring — ring-a”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the bells.

Tempo: bright and bouncy, like a sleigh, and a big rest before “HO HO HO” so they can hear it coming. This is the easiest song of the term — three chords, no Am anywhere, and every verse is just C and G7. Chant it without the uke first; add the chords once they know the actions.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CRing, ring, ring the Fbells! C(shake both hands like bells)

FChristmas CDay is G7here! C(arms wide)

CA star on Ftop, a present down beClow, (reach up, then right down to the floor)

FMerry CChristmas, everyone — G7HO HO CHO! (pat your belly and laugh)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who shakes the bells, reaches up for the star and does the belly laugh has understood the week, whether or not a single word comes out.

One verse a day — every verse is just C and G7

Monday · Tree

CA little green tree, a little green tree, (branch arms, small then big)

G7Lights and stars — Ccome and see! (twinkling hands, then beckon)

Tuesday · Star

COne gold star right on the top, (reach up high and hold it)

G7Twinkle, twinkle — Cit will not stop! (twinkling hands, faster and faster)

Wednesday · Present

CA present, a present, tied with a bow, (trace the bow in the air)

G7It's not for me — Cit's for YOU, you know! (hands to your chest, then out to a friend)

Thursday · Santa

CRed, red hat and a big white beard, (pat your head, stroke the beard)

G7Ho, ho, ho! — CSanta's here! (three belly laughs, then arms wide)

Friday · Merry Christmas

CBells and stars and presents too, (ring · point up · hands out)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — and Friday's finale, sung standing round the tree with the presents at its feet, doubles as the review of the whole week.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those three shapes — there is no Am this week at all, so if last week's verses defeated you, this is the one to come back on. C is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the real bell and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.

Theme Shelf · Christmas

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Decorate the little tree
from MONDAY's Magic Box		A small felt tree on a stand and five felt ornaments — star, bell, bauble, candy cane, present. Control: each ornament's outline is sewn or drawn on the tree, so a decoration in the wrong place looks wrong before anybody says so. Language: “on · under · on the top.” Bigs: “I can see a star on the tree.”
Tray 2 · Wrap a present
practical life, from WEDNESDAY		A small box, a square of paper cut to size, a tape dispenser, a length of ribbon. Wrap it, tape it, tie the bow. Control: when it is right, the box has completely disappeared — no corner showing. This is the tray the whole week is really for; leave it out and let them wrap the same box thirty times.
Tray 3 · Christmas 3-part cards
pages 10–11		tree 圣诞树 · star 星星 · present 礼物 · bell 铃铛 · Santa 圣诞老人, Chinese and English. Littles match picture→picture on the whole control card; Bigs lay the labels underneath. Control: the uncut control cards. Beside it the 12 sorting cards (page 14) and the two signs (pages 12–13): 在树上 or 在树下? And the action cards (page 15) face down: draw one, do it, say it — “I can wait!”
Tray 4 · Bell sound pairing
sensorial, from WEDNESDAY's bell		Six small bells in six identical opaque pots — three matching pairs, different pitches. Shake, listen, and pair by sound alone. Control: coloured dots on the bottoms of the pots. Beside it, crayons and the “Decorate MY tree” sheets (page 16). Sheets go home Friday with the award (page 17).

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can — the cards get handled all week and then they go in the box for next December. The row of 汉字 cards the class built up (树 · 星 · 礼 · 圣 · 送) stays at the foot of the tree all week — that is the display, no extra work needed. Everything comes down on Friday afternoon; the tree is the last thing to go.

Dec 14–18 · Theme: Christmas · Sound of the week: h

What we did

Christmas is a giving festival, and that was the whole week. On Monday a real pine branch came out of the Magic Box and went round the circle to be smelled, and then the children decorated the bare classroom tree themselves, one card each. On Tuesday the gold star went on the top — counted down from three — and we hunted twenty little gold stars round the room and counted every one of them out loud. On Wednesday a bell rang, a present came out, and we learned the sentence the week is built on: a present is a thing you give away. We played pass-the-present, which is really a lesson in waiting. On Thursday the red hat came out and we laughed until we fell over. And on Friday every child gave away a present they had made with their own hands, saying “This is for you.” — and nobody opened anything until every single present had been given.

Sentences to listen for at home

“Merry Christmas!” · “This is a present for you.” · “Thank you!” · “I can see a star on the tree.” · “It's not for me — it's for you!”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Tree!” with branch arms, or “Ho ho ho!” with a hand patted on the tummy, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Ring the Bells, It's Christmas!”

“Ring, ring, ring the bells! Christmas Day is here! A star on top, a present down below, Merry Christmas, everyone — HO HO HO!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — tree, star, present, Santa, Merry Christmas. Ask your child to sing the chorus and watch their hands: they shake the bells, reach right up for the star, right down for the present, and it ends in a belly laugh.

Dark Phonics · the h sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met h — a little puff of breath with no voice in it at all, like a panting puppy: hat, hen, hop. Our silly line for it is “ha-ha, hairy hippo!” It could not have landed in a better week: hat, ho ho ho, holly, hang it up, happy. Hold your hand in front of your mouth and feel the puff — if the hand doesn't feel it, it isn't an h.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · Let your child point at your own tree and name what they see — in English: “star · bell · present · lights.” That is the whole week's assessment, done in ten seconds.
2 · Wrap one small thing together. Let them do the tape and the bow, however it turns out.
3 · When anything is handed over at home this holiday, use the two lines: “This is for you.” and “Thank you!” — and 谢谢 · 不客气.
4 · Hunt for the h sound with a hand in front of your mouth: hat, hen, hop, hot, happy, home.
5 · Sing the chorus in the car. It has three actions and no hard words.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
