Week of Nov 9–13 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

Five words they'll own by Friday

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “House!” (both hands make a roof over your head), “Door!” (push an imaginary door open — knock, knock), “Bed!” (hands to the cheek, head tips over).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “This is my house.” · “I sleep in my bedroom.” · “We cook in the kitchen.” · “The key opens the door.”

Day 1 · A House With Rooms

Today's words: house

Grab: a little wooden doll's house with the roof lifted off in the Magic Box — you can see every room at once; a basket of blocks for the middle of the circle; a small doll to walk round the rooms; the 家 character card.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box is big today, and heavy. Carry it in with two hands and put it down as if it might break. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tip it a little — one soft wooden knock inside. Peek in, and stare for a long moment.

Teacher(whisper) “There is a whole HOUSE in this box.” (lift the doll's house out slowly, roof off, and turn it round so everyone sees inside)

Both hands over your head to make a roof. “House!”

4 min Teach · House

Put the house in the middle where every child can see down into it. Point into each room, one at a time, and say what happens there.

A roof on the top… (both hands make a roof over your head)

A floor down below. (pat the floor twice)

A door to go in… (push an imaginary door open)

That's my HOUSE — in you go! (sweep both arms in)

Now the little doll walks the rooms. Kitchen — we cook. Bedroom — we sleep. Bathroom — we wash. Living room — we sit together. Every child gets to walk the doll into one room and the class names it with you.

Littles say the one word and make the roof: “House!” Bigs take the full frame: “This is my house.”

Teacher fails: put the little bed in the kitchen and the pot in the bathroom, stand back and look extremely pleased with yourself. Wait. They will move it all back for you, loudly.

Chinese moment: 家 is the character of the week, and one of the loveliest in the language — a roof 宀 with a pig 豕 underneath. A house was only a house once there was an animal in it. Draw the roof. Draw the pig. Then draw 家. Lay the card on the mat — one character a day, no drilling.

3 min Song · This Is My House — chorus + Monday verse

This is my house, my house, my house, (hands make a roof)

Big enough for me and a little mouse! (arms wide, then a tiny pinch)

A door to open, a bed to keep, (push open, then hands to the cheek)

A kitchen to eat and a room to sleep! (eat, then snore)

Monday's verse: A roof on the top and a floor below, (roof hands, then pat the floor)

That's my house — in you go! (sweep both arms in)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. The chorus is a finger-play: they will do the actions long before they sing the words.

3 min Game · Build my house

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The basket of blocks comes to the middle and everybody sits round the empty floor.

Teacher“One block each. What room shall we build first?”

Round the circle, one child at a time, one block each. As each part appears you name it and the class says it back: walls, “Door!”, “Window!”, and last of all the roof — everybody makes the roof over their heads as the last block goes on.

Bigs get a room each: “This is the kitchen.” Littles just place their block and name it: “House!”

If it falls over — and it will — cheer, and build it again. A house that falls down twice is more fun than a house that stands, and they get twice the language out of it.

1 min Close

Roof hands over the head, then fling both arms wide on the shout. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Door… key… KITCHEN! This is MY HOUSE!” (roof hands, then arms wide on the last word)

Got 3 spare minutes? A House Is a House for Me (Mary Ann Hoberman) — a hill is a house for an ant, a hive is a house for a bee. By page four they will be shouting the next one at you.

Day 2 · Knock, Knock!

Today's words: door (house comes back every day)

Grab: a real brass door handle and a doorbell that actually rings in the Magic Box; the classroom door itself; the 门 character card. It is just past 立冬 — cold outside, so the door gets closed properly behind us today.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out ordinary. Chant it as always — then, halfway through, press the doorbell that is hiding inside it.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

DING… DONG! Stop dead. Look at the door of the classroom. Look at the box. Look at the door again.

Teacher“Somebody is at the door… but the door is over THERE!” (ring it once more, then lift out the bell and the brass handle)

Push an imaginary door open, knock twice on the air. “Door!”

4 min Teach · Door

Take the whole circle to the real classroom door and stand round it. Open. Closed. Open. Closed.

Knock, knock, knock! (three knocks on the real door)

Open! (pull it wide, arms open)

Closed. (shut it gently, both hands, no bang)

Come in, come in! (wave everybody through)

Then the brass handle goes round the circle: hold it, turn it, feel how cold and heavy it is. Littles say the one word: “Door!” Bigs take the frame: “The door is open.” / “Please close the door.”

Teacher fails: knock politely on the window, then wait, puzzled, and knock again harder. “Nobody is answering!” Let them march you to the door.

Chinese moment: 门 joins 家 on the mat — two doors of a gate, drawn in two strokes. We have just had 立冬: 天冷了，回家关门 — it is cold now, so we come home and we close the door. From today, every child closes the door behind them properly, with two hands and no bang.

3 min Song · This Is My House — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — then yesterday's verse if they shout for it — then today's new one:

Tuesday's verse: Knock, knock, knock — who's at the door? (three knocks on the floor)

Come in, come in — there's room for more! (both arms wave everyone in)

Do the three knocks on the wooden floor, all together, on the beat. Twenty children knocking in time is the loudest and most satisfying moment of the week.

3 min Game · Knock knock, who's there?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” One child goes quietly outside the classroom door and closes it. Everyone else sits still.

Outside(three knocks) “Knock, knock!”
Everyone“Who's there?”

The child outside answers in a silly voice — a squeak, a growl, a whisper — and the class guesses who it is before the door opens. Then the door opens and everybody shouts the name.

Bigs may add a whole line: “It's me — open the door!” Littles just knock and let the class do the rest.

Every child gets a turn; it takes about fifteen seconds each. Keep the door only half-latched so a two-year-old can open it alone — the opening is the best part and they must be the one who does it.

1 min Close

Three knocks on the floor, then the shout. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Door… key… KITCHEN! This is MY HOUSE!” (knock, knock, knock — then arms wide)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Doorbell Rang (Pat Hutchins) — the doorbell goes again, and again, and again. Ring the real doorbell on every page and let them count who has arrived.

Day 3 · Click! The Key

Today's words: key (house · door come back)

Grab: a heavy bunch of keys and three padlocks in the Magic Box — only one key opens each one; a tray to work on; a wooden spoon (for the teacher fail); the 窗 character card.

2 min Magic Box hook

This box rattles harder than any box all year. Do not tell them what it is. Chant, then shake it once, enormously.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

JANGLE! Freeze. Shake it again, softly, right beside one child's ear. Then round the whole circle, one shake each.

Teacher“Something in here is made of metal… and there are LOTS of them.” (lift out the bunch of keys and let them hang and swing)

Turn an imaginary key in the air — click. “Key!”

4 min Teach · Key

One padlock in the middle of the mat, closed. Hold it up. Pull at it. It will not open.

Locked. (tug the padlock, both hands, it holds)

A little key… (hold one key up high so it catches the light)

Turn it — click! (turn it slowly, everyone leans in)

Open! (the shackle springs, hold it up in triumph)

Then the language, in the same order every time: locked → key → turn → click → open. Littles say the one word and turn their hand: “Key!” Bigs take the frame: “The key opens the door.”

Now hand the padlock and the right key to a child and let them do it in front of everybody. Nobody helps. The click is worth waiting twenty seconds for.

Teacher fails: try to unlock the padlock with the wooden spoon. Push. Twist. Try the other end. Then get thoroughly cross with the padlock — “What is WRONG with you today?” — and wait for twenty children to put a key in your hand.

Chinese moment: 窗 joins 家 and 门 on the mat. The door lets you in; the window lets the light in. Stand at the classroom window and say it once, looking out.

3 min Song · This Is My House — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — then today's verse, which is the one that leans on the Am and goes a bit mysterious:

Wednesday's verse: A little key goes click, click, click, (three tiny turns of the wrist)

Turn it round — open, quick! (one big turn, then both hands fly open)

Sing the three “clicks” as three tiny, quiet notes and the last line loud. The Am makes it sound like a secret, which is exactly what a key is to a four-year-old.

3 min Game · Find the key that fits

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Three padlocks on the tray, all locked, and a pile of keys beside them.

Teacher“One key at a time. Ready? Try it…”

One child, one padlock, one key at a time — and the whole circle counts the tries out loud: “One… two… three…” On the click, everybody cheers.

Littles hand the keys over and shout “Key!” with each try. Bigs report back: “This key opens this lock.”

Control of error is the lock itself — nobody has to say “wrong”, the key simply does not turn. Say nothing when it fails; the material has already spoken, and the child just tries the next one.

1 min Close

Everybody turns a key in the air on the word “key”. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Door… key… KITCHEN! This is MY HOUSE!” (one enormous turn of the wrist on “key”)

Got 3 spare minutes? Any lift-the-flap house book — Where's Spot? (Eric Hill) works perfectly: every flap is a door, and every door has something behind it.

Day 4 · In the Kitchen

Today's words: kitchen (house · door · key come back)

Grab: a wooden spoon, a bowl and a small pot in the Magic Box; a placemat with the plate, bowl, spoon and cup drawn on it as outlines (print pack, tray 3) and the four real things in a basket; the 厨房 character card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box smells faintly of dinner and knocks like wood. Chant, then tap the box twice from inside with the spoon.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tock. Tock. Sniff at the lid. Close your eyes and stir the air with one hand, slowly.

Teacher“Mmm… I think somebody is cooking in there.” (lift out the pot, then the bowl, then the wooden spoon — and stir)

Stir a big pot in the air. “Kitchen!”

4 min Teach · Kitchen

The pot goes in the middle. Everything that belongs in a kitchen comes out of it, one thing at a time, named and passed round.

Pot — we cook in it. (hold it up with both hands)

Spoon — we stir with it. (big slow stir)

Bowl — we eat from it. (cup it and pretend to eat)

Cup — we drink from it! (drink, then a satisfied “Ahh”)

Then the whole class cooks together: everybody stirs, everybody blows on the spoon, everybody tastes and says whether it needs more. Littles say the one word: “Kitchen!” Bigs take the frame: “We cook in the kitchen.”

Teacher fails: try to stir the pot with yesterday's padlock key, then try to eat the soup with the door handle. Look completely satisfied. Wait for the corrections — and they will hand you the right thing, which is the whole lesson.

Chinese moment: 厨房 goes down beside 家, 门 and 窗. Ask who cooks at home — 妈妈? 爸爸? 奶奶? — and let three children answer in Chinese and then again in English: “We cook in the kitchen.”

3 min Song · This Is My House — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — today make “a kitchen to eat” enormous — then:

Thursday's verse: Stir the pot and lay the plate, (big stir, then lay four places on the floor)

Dinner's ready — don't be late! (both hands beckon everybody in)

Sing it once sitting and once standing round an imaginary table. The “don't be late!” line gets shouted; let it.

3 min Game · Set the table

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The placemat goes at one end of the room with its four outlines showing; the basket of real things sits with you at the other.

Teacher“Take ONE thing. Walk — don't run — and put it where it belongs.”

One child, one object, one careful walk: plate, bowl, spoon, cup. They lay it on its own outline and walk back. “Plate!”  Bigs: “The spoon goes here.”

When the mat is full, everybody claps, you clear it, and the next four go. Do it three or four times round so every child carries something.

Control of error is the outline — the shape either matches or it doesn't, and the child sees it before you do. Carrying one real, slightly heavy object slowly across a room is practical life, not a game about words — and that is why the language sticks to it.

1 min Close

Everybody stirs a big pot on the word “kitchen”. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Door… key… KITCHEN! This is MY HOUSE!” (one huge stir on KITCHEN)

Got 3 spare minutes? Pancakes, Pancakes! (Eric Carle) — a whole book of kitchen words, and by the end they are stirring with you.

Day 5 · Goodnight, House! + Big Review

Today's words: house · door · key · kitchen · bed — the whole week

Grab: a pillow and a folded blanket in the Magic Box, plus every prop from the week back inside it (doll's house, door handle, doorbell, keys and padlocks, pot and spoon); a floor plan chalked on the mat — four big rooms; the awards (print pack page 17); the 床 character card.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out fat, soft on the top and rattling underneath. Chant it, then press down on the lid — it squashes.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”
Teacher“Today the box is FULL… and something in here is soft.” (pull out the pillow, hug it, and yawn enormously)

Hands to the cheek, head tipped over. “Bed!”

Then everything else comes out one at a time and held up in silence — they will supply the word before you can. Doll's house → “House!”  Handle → “Door!”  Keys → “Key!”  Pot → “Kitchen!”

4 min Teach · Bed, then the whole week

The pillow and the blanket go in the middle. Lie down beside them, right there on the mat, and do it properly.

Pillow — soft. (cheek down on it, eyes closed)

Blanket — warm. (pull it up to your chin)

Lights off… (click an imaginary switch)

Goodnight, house! (one enormous snore — then sit bolt upright)

Littles say the one word and tip their head: “Bed!” Bigs: “I sleep in my bedroom.”

Then everybody stands and we tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

A roof — my house! (roof hands over the head)

Knock knock — the door! (three knocks in the air)

Click — the key! (turn the wrist)

Stir — the kitchen! (big stir)

Shh — my BED! (hands to the cheek, head over, everybody snores)

Teacher fails: put the pillow in the pot and try to stir it, then lie down with your head on the padlock. Sigh happily. Let them rescue you twice.

Chinese moment: 床 completes the row — 家 · 门 · 窗 · 厨房 · 床. Point along the line and let them tell you which one you are standing on. Sing 《小小的家》 if they know it.

3 min Song · This Is My House — Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: Pillow soft and blanket tight, (cheek on hands, then pull the blanket up)

Off go the lights — goodnight, goodnight! (click the switch, then everybody snores)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, top to bottom, with the pillow passed round the circle — whoever is holding it does the actions biggest. Everyone snores on the last chord. They learned it one verse a morning without noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · Which room?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Four big rooms are chalked on the mat — kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, living room — and the week's props are in a heap in the middle.

Teacher“Take one thing. Which room does it live in? — GO!”

Each child grabs one object and runs it to the right room, then says it: “Kitchen!”  Bigs owe you the sentence: “The pot goes in the kitchen.”

Twelve objects, everybody goes twice, and the last round is the whole class at once — total, delighted chaos, and then we tidy it together.

Put one thing in that could go in two rooms — the cup. Watch them argue. Both answers are right; say so out loud, and say why each one is right.

1 min Close + awards

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home with their house drawing. Then, standing, with all five gestures:

Everyone“House… door… key… kitchen… BED! This is MY HOUSE!” (roof · knock · turn · stir · head on hands, then arms wide)

Got 3 spare minutes? Goodnight Moon (Margaret Wise Brown) — goodnight to every single thing in the room. Let them say goodnight to the classroom, object by object, as they leave.

Theme Song · “This Is My House”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The four chords

Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“this — is — my house”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: warm and steady, like rocking — this is a going-home song, not a marching song. The Am in the third line of the chorus is the “a bed to keep” moment; lean on it and go quiet, then lift on “a room to sleep!” Chant it without the uke first; add chords once they know the actions.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CThis is my Fhouse, my house, my Chouse, (hands make a roof)

FBig enough for Cme and a little G7mouse! C(arms wide, then a tiny pinch)

CA door to Fopen, a bed to Amkeep, (push open, then hands to the cheek)

FA kitchen to Ceat and a G7room to Csleep! (eat, then snore)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who makes the roof, pushes the door, tips their head and snores has understood the entire week, whether or not a single word comes out.

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — Friday's finale is the whole thing top to bottom with the pillow going round the circle, and it doubles as your review of the entire week.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. Add Am last: it is one finger on the same string as F, so slide from Am to F and back until it stops feeling like a change. That's the entire song — and four of the five verses are only C and G7. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.

Theme Shelf · My House

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Lock and key matching
from WEDNESDAY's Magic Box		Three padlocks and six keys on a ring, on a small tray. Try a key, turn it, listen for the click. Control: only one key turns each lock — the lock itself is the control, so nobody ever has to be told they are wrong. Real fine motor and real logic in one material. Language: “locked · turn · click · open.” Bigs: “This key opens this lock.”
Tray 2 · Which room?
from FRIDAY's game		The two room signs (pages 12–13) laid out as headings, and the 12 sorting cards (page 14) in a basket. Pick a card, say it, put it under Kitchen 厨房 or Bedroom 卧室. Control: a small orange dot on the back of every kitchen card and a blue dot on every bedroom card — turn them over to check. Beside it, the My House 3-part cards (pages 10–11): Littles match picture→picture on the control card, Bigs lay the labels underneath.
Tray 3 · Laying the table
from THURSDAY's kitchen		A placemat with the plate, bowl, spoon and cup drawn on it as outlines, and the four real things in a basket beside it. Lay them, clear them, do it again. Control: the outlines — the shape either matches or it doesn't. Practical life, and every child in the class will want a turn. Language: “The spoon goes here.”
Tray 4 · My house, drawn
from MONDAY's Magic Box		The little doll's house with its roof off and a basket of blocks, plus crayons and the “This is MY HOUSE” sheets (page 16). Walk the doll through the rooms and name them, build one out of blocks, then draw your own. Add the action cards (page 15) face down: draw one, do it, say it. Sheets go home Friday with the award (page 17).

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can — the room cards get handled twenty times a day. Keep the padlock keys on a single ring so none of them walk off. The row of 汉字 cards the class built up (家 · 门 · 窗 · 厨房 · 床) stays on the shelf edge all week — that is the display, no extra work needed.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Nov 9–13 · Theme: My House · Sound of the week: k

What we did

Last week we learned about the people in a family; this week we moved into the place they live in. On Monday a little doll's house with the roof lifted off came out of the Magic Box and we walked a doll through every room — kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, living room — then built a house out of blocks, one block per child, naming each part as it appeared. On Tuesday a real doorbell rang from inside the box: we opened and closed the classroom door, practised knocking, and played knock knock, who's there? — one child outside doing a silly voice while the class guessed who it was. On Wednesday the box rattled harder than any box all year: a bunch of keys and three padlocks, and the children worked out by trial and error which key opens which lock while the whole circle counted the tries. On Thursday we cooked: a pot, a bowl and a wooden spoon, and then a proper table-laying relay — plate, bowl, spoon, cup, each carried carefully across the room to its own outline. On Friday a pillow and a blanket came out, we ran objects to the right room on a floor plan chalked on the mat, sang the whole song with the pillow going round the circle, and took a star award home.

Sentences to listen for at home

“This is my house.” · “I sleep in my bedroom.” · “We cook in the kitchen.” · “The key opens the door.” · “Please close the door.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “House!” with both hands making a roof overhead, or “Bed!” with their head tipped onto their hands, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “This Is My House”

“This is my house, my house, my house, big enough for me and a little mouse! A door to open, a bed to keep, a kitchen to eat and a room to sleep!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — house, door, key, kitchen, bed. Ask your child to sing the chorus; it is a finger-play, so watch their hands, not just their words.

The character 家

We met one of the loveliest characters in the language this week: 家 — a roof 宀 with a pig 豕 underneath. A house only became a home once there was an animal living in it. We drew the roof, drew the pig, and then drew 家. It is just past 立冬, so we also practised the oldest house rule there is: 天冷了，回家关门 — it's cold, so come home and close the door, with two hands and no bang.

Dark Phonics · the k sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met k — the hard, kicking “kuh” at the front of a word: key, kitchen, kettle. Our silly line for it is “kooky king kicks!” It fitted the week perfectly — nearly every k-thing we met came out of the same rattling box, and Wednesday's whole lesson was a key turning in a lock. Listen for it in: key, kitchen, kettle, king, kick, kit.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · Walk round your home together and name one room at a time: “This is the kitchen.”
2 · Let your child lock and unlock something real with a key — a padlock, a suitcase, the front door — and listen for the click.
3 · Ask them to lay the table for dinner. They know exactly where the spoon goes.
4 · At bedtime: “Goodnight, door. Goodnight, kitchen. Goodnight, bed.”
5 · Hunt for the k sound in your own kitchen: key, kettle, cup… say them hard and quick.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
