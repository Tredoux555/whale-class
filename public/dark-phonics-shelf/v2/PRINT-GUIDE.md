# Dark Phonics · The Writing Shelf — how to print, cut and assemble (v2)

Ten printables for the eight-tray Writing Shelf. Thirteen sheets became ten, twenty-four sheets of paper became sixteen, and every word an adult needs to read now sits on the part of the paper you throw away.

**Three rules that apply to every sheet.** Print at **100%** — choose *Actual size* or *Scale: 100%*, never *Fit to page*, because every size here is a real measured size and 'fit to page' quietly shrinks it by about 4%. Where a sheet says **duplex, flip on SHORT EDGE**: only two jobs can now go wrong, the chain cards and the dictation cards, and they are the same job twice — set the printer once and run both together. Those two sheets are portrait, so their backs are printed upside down on the sheet *on purpose*; that is what short-edge duplex needs, and it comes out the right way up on the cut card. Print one, hold it to the light, check the word is behind its own photograph. And **laminate before you cut, everywhere** — everything laminated is on 250 or 300 gsm, everything the child writes on is plain paper.

**Before Tray 3 runs:** restock the letter tin. Four of v1's six chains could not be built from it at all. The verified tin for the six v2 chains is **18 tiles**: one of each of a b c d e g h i m n o p r t u, plus a second **g**, **p** and **t** — the only letters any one chain needs twice. All fifteen letters are used, so nothing in the tin is dead weight.

**Correction.** The four-frame side of `01-sound-frame-mat.pdf` still carries a printed hint reading 'doubles of … fifteen letters, thirty tiles'. That over-provisions by twelve tiles. The sheet is not being re-rendered for it — **18 tiles is the verified tin**, and this guide is the authority.

**Do the two risky jobs together.** Only `02-chain-cards` and `03-dictation-photo-cards` can come out wrong: portrait duplex with a photograph that must land behind its own word. Same setting, same paper. Set flip on SHORT EDGE once, print one sheet, hold it to the light, then run all five sheets in one go. `01-sound-frame-mat` is duplex too but forgiving — a wrong flip still gives you a mat on each face.

## A. Sound-frame mat — 3-frame / 4-frame
*Tray 1 and 3 · `01-sound-frame-mat.pdf` · 2 pages · A4 landscape, 300 gsm card*

Sheets 01 and 02 of v1 were the same object at two different sizes. They are now one mat: three frames on the front for Tray 1, four on the back for Tray 3, and the fourth frame outlined in amber because it is the spare — the one the child uses when a word gains or loses a sound. Print one sheet double-sided with the flip on SHORT EDGE, at 100% on the heaviest card your printer takes, cut the single dotted rectangle and give it a MATT laminate; gloss throws the ceiling lights straight back at a child bent over it. A wrong flip costs nothing here — it is one mat face on each side of one piece of card, with no picture to land behind a word. Nothing is printed on the mat itself: every word on the sheet is on the paper you throw away. If you want the tiles genuinely recessed, glue the laminated mat onto 3 mm foam board and cut the windows out with a scalpel. **Run this file twice.** The Tray 1 mat and the Tray 3 chain board are the same duplex card, and each tray needs its own — two copies, two cuts, two matt pouches. And before Tray 3 runs, restock its letter tin: one of each of a b c d e g h i m n o p r t u, plus a second g, p and t — **18 tiles**. Note that this sheet's own printed hint on the four-frame side still reads “doubles of … fifteen letters, thirty tiles”. That over-provisions by twelve tiles; 18 is the verified tin. The PDF is not being re-rendered for it — go by this guide.

**Cut & finish.** One cut: trim the mat out on the dotted rectangle. 55 mm frames, 6 mm gutters.

## B. Chain cards ×6
*Tray 3 · `02-chain-cards.pdf` · 4 pages · A4 portrait, 250 gsm card*

Two sheets instead of six, and the card no longer does the child's naming for him. The front is the photograph and nothing else — no word, no chain number, no tray line — so he has to hear the word before he can build it. The back carries the word, then the five-word chain beneath it with the letter that moved printed in amber. All six chains were rewritten so that every word is one a five-year-old owns, every step moves exactly one letter, and every letter is in the tin: tap cap can pan pen · mop hop hot hut hug · peg beg bed bad bag · bin big bug dug mug · nut cut cup cap cat · rat bat bag big dig. Every start word is also a new photograph, so no chain card and no dictation card come out of the printer with the same front. Four A6 cards tile an A4 exactly, so there is no margin and no crop mark: cut the sheet in half and in half again. Print DUPLEX with the flip on SHORT EDGE — the backs are imposed for it and are printed upside down on the sheet on purpose. Print one sheet before you commit the rest and hold a card to the light: the chain must be behind its own photograph. Laminate; this is the tray they handle most. ONE TEMPORARY THING: card 1 ships with the word tap set large in the picture well, because the tap photograph has not arrived. Its chain back is final. A front that names the word is exactly what this card was redesigned to stop, so treat card 1 as a stand-in and reprint sheet 1 of 2 on its own the day the photograph lands — the other five cards are finished. Six cards on two four-up sheets leaves two blank A6 positions on sheet 2 of 2: those are spares, not a printing fault. Cut them out and keep them with the pack.

**Cut & finish.** Cut each sheet in half, then in half again: four A6 cards, 105 × 148.5 mm.

## C. Dictation photo cards ×12
*Tray 4 (and 1, 2) · `03-dictation-photo-cards.pdf` · 6 pages · A4 portrait, 250 gsm card*

Three sheets instead of twelve. Photograph alone on the front, the word alone on the back at 48 pt in the letterforms the children trace, and not one line of instruction on either face — a card whose whole purpose is that the child works without an adult should not be talking to him. cup, sun and net are gone: cup cannot be told from mug in a photograph, sun cannot be a single object on white, and net is a fishing net, a football net or a hairnet depending on who is looking. rug, cot and jam take their places. Same imposition as the chain cards, so set the printer once and run both jobs together: DUPLEX, SHORT EDGE, 100%, cut in half and in half again. This pack is also Tray 1's week-four picture set and Tray 2's object substitute — one set of twelve photographs furnishing four trays.

**Cut & finish.** Cut each sheet in half, then in half again: four A6 cards, 105 × 148.5 mm.

## D. Small objects — 5 heart words, 7 blanks, 3 punctuation tiles
*Tray 4 and 5 · `04-small-objects.pdf` · 1 page · A4 portrait, 300 gsm card*

v1 spent one A4 on five stamp-sized heart cards and another on three tiny tiles. This is both, on one sheet, in one print and one laminate. Five heart words are printed — a, an, I, the, ate — and seven more cards carry the heart and a ruled space, because the guide says add one heart word a week and by half-term the teacher has none left. The heart is not decoration: it means this word has a trick in it and cannot be sounded out, which is why no heart card ever carries a picture. The three punctuation tiles stay deliberately oversized, because they are objects a child places on purpose. Every footer is gone from every piece; the STOP / QUESTION / SHOUT labels stay on the waste between the crop marks. Laminate the whole sheet first and cut afterwards — everyone does this the wrong way round once. Punch the dashed amber circle and ring the heart cards. Note the full stop is a 10 mm dot against the 30 mm red grammar circle of Tray 8: same shape, same hand, same term, and the size is what keeps them apart. **Run this file twice.** The heart-word ring lives on Tray 4 and Tray 5 and one ring cannot be in two places, so each tray gets its own: two copies gives two rings of five, fourteen blanks and six punctuation tiles. Tray 5 takes three tiles; the spares are spares.

**Cut & finish.** 15 pieces: 12 cards at 60 × 35 mm and 3 tiles at 34 × 42 mm. Punch the rings.

## E. Lined sentence strips, 190 × 60 mm
*Tray 5 · `05-lined-sentence-strips.pdf` · 1 page · A4 portrait, plain 100–120 gsm paper*

The strips are 190 mm now, not 200. At 200 mm on a 210 mm sheet the crop ticks sat inside the unprintable margin of most office printers and the old guide had to tell the teacher to trim by eye; at 190 mm the problem simply does not arise, and the grammar control cards were moved to the same 190 mm so a control card and the child's own strip are the same object. “where is your dot?” is gone, and so is the tray footer: a printed nag on a consumable a child uses fifty times is a nag that never goes away, and the punctuation tile in his hand is the reminder. Three guides and no other ink anywhere on the strip — light top line, dashed amber midline at the x-height, solid baseline. Plain paper, print a term's worth, do not laminate.

**Cut & finish.** Cut 4 strips, 190 × 60 mm, on the dotted rectangles.

## F. Picture sequences ×3 (12 cards)
*Tray 6 · `06-picture-sequences.pdf` · 3 pages · A4 portrait, 250 gsm card*

Three sheets where v1 had eight, and no backs at all — four sheets of card and the whole short-edge-flip risk were being spent on printing one letter sixteen times, when the print guide already told you to put each set in its own envelope. Put the letter on the envelope. Set D, sad to playing, is cut: it needs a child's face, which the house style forbids, and it is a different skill anyway — reading an emotion, not ordering an event. In its place, apple to core. SET A IS PHOTOGRAPHIC and normalised as a set: the pot is the same width and stands on the same line in all four frames, so the only thing that changes between the cards is the plant, which is the whole content of the material. SETS B AND C ARE STILL DRAWN — their frames have not been shot yet. Style is uniform inside each set, which is what the child compares across; a set half drawn and half photographed would not be, and that is the only consistency that matters here. The mismatch between sets is visible to an adult and invisible to the work, and it goes away when the eight remaining frames land. Nothing is printed on the picture face.

**Cut & finish.** Cut 12 cards, 90 × 90 mm, four per sheet.

## G. Fold-book template
*Tray 6 · `07-fold-book-template.pdf` · 1 page · A4 landscape, plain 100–120 gsm paper*

The fold and cut geometry is unchanged and still correct, but the book has stopped writing itself for him. “the end” is gone from page 8 — that is his book's last page and if his story finishes on page 5 the book should not contradict him. “spell it the way it sounds” is gone; say it aloud once, it belongs on the teacher script. “draw it here” is gone six times over: a box is already an invitation, and six printed instructions is six things a child who is trying not to read is being asked to read. MY BOOK and the name line stay, because those are his. The p1–p8 fold markers are still there for you but at 5 pt grey at the extreme edge, near-invisible once folded. Print at 100%, fold in half three times, open flat, fold in half the long way and cut ONLY the amber line, then push the ends together until the slit opens into a diamond. Make six in advance.

**Cut & finish.** One cut, along the amber line in the middle of the sheet. Three folds.

## H. Story-dictation sheet
*Tray 7 · `08-story-dictation-sheet.pdf` · 1 page · A4 portrait, plain 100 gsm paper*

Almost untouched, because there was nothing structural to fix. The wide lines are spaced for an adult pen moving fast, and the small amber “acted on” tick is the quiet best idea on the shelf — it is the thing that stops a child being skipped for a month. Two changes only: the pronouns are neutral, because half the class is not a he, and the “HIS DRAWING” caption is gone. The box needs no caption. Print twenty, keep them in one folder with the class list, and read down the acted-on column every Friday.

**Cut & finish.** None.

## I. Teacher script card, 2-up A5
*Tray 7 · `09-teacher-script-card.pdf` · 1 page · A4 landscape, 250 gsm card*

The only adult-facing card in the set, and the one thing that makes the author's chair run the same way when a different adult takes it. Six numbered lines, and it must not grow to eight. The guide tells you to tape it to the back of the author's chair and to keep it in the story folder, and you need both — so it is laid out 2-up and one print gives two identical cards. Pronouns neutral, same as the dictation sheet; otherwise untouched.

**Cut & finish.** One cut down the middle: two identical A5 cards, 148.5 × 210 mm.

## J. Grammar pack — 12 tokens + 4 control cards
*Tray 8 · `10-grammar-pack.pdf` · 2 pages · A4 portrait, 300 gsm card, colour*

v1's token sheet was the best-laid-out sheet in the set and the rest of v2 copies it: every word in the margin, the objects coming out as pure shapes. Its only failure was count — eighteen hand-cut shapes, twelve of them triangles with points that tear, and no sentence a five-year-old builds needs six nouns. Twelve now: four black triangles, four red circles, four dark-blue triangles, which covers any four-word sentence twice over. The colours are the Montessori ones and are not negotiable. Page 2 is rebuilt from the ground up, because v1's six printed sentences contradicted the shelf guide, which says in bold that the child symbolises six sentence strips he wrote himself and never a printed sentence from a book. There is now one symbol key card — black triangle / red circle / blue triangle, with naming · doing · describing and no sentence at all — which lives on the tray permanently, and three worked examples for your first presentation only: the dog ran · a cat sat · the red hen ran. Every verb is one a child can act, which is why “is” is gone, and “a rat digs” is gone with it — do not hand a child learning to encode a word whose last sound is spelled the way he would not spell it. After the presentation the material is his own strips. Both sheets go to the same tray and both need colour, so it is one print job. Laminate first, cut second.

**Cut & finish.** 16 pieces: 12 tokens on page 1, 4 cards at 190 × 60 mm on page 2.

---

**Nothing is printed on the thing the child picks up.** Ten of v1's eleven child-facing sheets carried a footer, a label or a prompt on the piece the child holds. In v2 every instruction, tray name and size note sits outside the trim rectangle, on the paper that gets thrown away. The chain cards and the dictation cards go further: four A6 cards tile an A4 exactly, so those sheets have no waste and therefore carry no mark of any kind — cut them in half and in half again.

**The two colours that are not the accent.** Everything is black on white with one warm amber for the single meaningful thing — the letter that moved, the heart on a heart word, the midline you write on, the spare frame, the one line you cut. The grammar pack is the exception: its red and dark blue are the Montessori grammar colours and mean something specific, so they stay.
