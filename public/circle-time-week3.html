Five words they'll own by Friday

seeheartouchsmelltaste

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “See!” (two fingers to the eyes, then point), “Hear!” (cup both ears, lean in), “Touch!” (wiggle all ten fingers).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “I see a red apple.” · “I hear a bell.” · “I touch something soft.” · “I can ___!”

Day 1 · Two Little Eyes

Today's words: see

Grab: a magnifying glass and one tiny thing to look at in the Magic Box — a seed, a beetle wing, a grain of rice; one red thing and one round thing hidden in plain sight round the room; the 视觉 character card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today we look at the box. Turn it slowly so everyone sees every side. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — the tiniest tick. Peek in, and go completely still: (whisper) “It's so… small. I can hardly SEE it.”

Teacher“I need my eyes. I need two eyes… and this!” (lift the magnifying glass out slowly, hold it over one eye — your eye goes enormous)

They shriek. Two fingers to the eyes, then point. “See!”

4 min Teach · See

Hold up the seed on your flat palm. Everyone leans in.

Two little eyes… (two fingers pointing at your own eyes)

Open them WIDE. (eyes huge, eyebrows up)

I can see! (point out at the room)

I see… a seed! (hold it up)

Now the magnifying glass goes round the circle, one child at a time, ten seconds each: look at the seed, then say what you see. Littles say the one word “See!” or just the thing — “Seed!” Bigs take the full frame: “I see a little seed.”

Then eyes shut, tight, everybody. Move one thing in the circle. Eyes open — what changed? Do it three times. They will out-see you every time.

Teacher fails: hold the magnifying glass against your ear and announce “Oh! Now I can SEE the seed!” Nod, pleased. Wait. Let them move your hand to your eye themselves.

Lay the 视觉 card beside the magnifying glass on the mat. Say it once, in Chinese, and leave it there — one character a day, no drilling.

3 min Song · Two Little Eyes — chorus + Monday verse

Two little eyes that look and SEE, (point to both eyes)

Two little ears that listen to me! (cup both ears)

Ten little fingers that touch and know, (wiggle all ten)

One nose, one tongue — off we go! (tap your nose, poke your tongue out)

Monday's verse: I see the sun, I see the tree, (point up, then across)

Open my eyes — what do I see? (hands over eyes, then fling them open)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. The chorus is a finger-play: they will do the actions before they can sing the words.

3 min Game · I spy with my little eye

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everyone sits tall and gets their “binoculars” up (two curled fists at the eyes).

Teacher“I spy with my little eye… something RED!” (scan the room slowly through your fists)

Hands up, then the child points and names it. “Red!”  Bigs: “I see a red book.”

Six rounds — something red, something round, something blue, something tiny, something with a face on it, something that is YOU. Then two children get to be the spy while the class hunts.

Colours and shapes only, never letters — “something beginning with…” loses every English learner in the room. Point-and-name is the whole game.

1 min Close

Hands over the eyes, then fling them open on the shout. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“See… see… SEE!” (hands fly off the eyes on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See? (Bill Martin Jr & Eric Carle) — they will chant “What do you see?” with you by page three.

Day 2 · Listen! Sh…

Today's words: hear (see comes back every day)

Grab: a little bell and a pair of sound cylinders in the Magic Box; a cloth big enough to hide things behind; a bunch of keys, a sheet of paper, a jug and a cup of water; the 听觉 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today nobody looks. Box chant with your eyes shut — make them shut theirs too.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it. Ting… ting… ting. Shake it again, softer. Hold it to one ear, then the other.

Teacher“I can't see it. But I can HEAR it! What is it?” (shake once more, then lift out the bell and ring it high)

Cup both ears, lean in. “Hear!”

4 min Teach · Hear

Ring the bell right up loud, then so quietly that the back of the circle has to lean forward.

Loud! (ring hard, hands off ears)

Soft… (the tiniest ring, everyone leans in)

Sh… (finger on lips)

I can hear! (cup both ears)

Now the silence game: everybody still, eyes shut, ten whole seconds. Then hands up — what did you hear? A car. A bird. A door. Somebody's tummy. Take every answer; the Littles will just say “Hear!” and point at their ear, and that counts. Bigs: “I hear a bird.”

Finish with the sound cylinders: shake one, shake its partner, shake a stranger. Same or different? Two children come and find the pair.

Teacher fails: hold the bell to your nose and sniff it, hard, twice. “Mmm! I can hear it beautifully.” Wait for them to march your hand up to your ear.

The 听觉 card goes down beside the bell, next to yesterday's 视觉.

3 min Song · Two Little Eyes — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — then yesterday's verse if they shout for it — then today's new one:

Tuesday's verse: Shhh! I hear a little sound, (finger on lips, then cup one ear)

Ears wide open — listen all round! (cup both ears and turn slowly right round)

Sing this verse quietly, then have them sing it in a whisper. A whispered song makes twenty children listen to each other — which is the lesson.

3 min Game · Guess that sound

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Then: “Eyes… CLOSED.” You work behind the cloth.

Teacher“Listen… what is it?” (crumple the paper slowly)

“Paper!”  Bigs: “I hear paper.”

Six sounds: the bell, the keys, crumpling paper, water pouring from the jug into the cup, two hands clapping, and your own shoes walking. Then one child hides behind the cloth and chooses; the class guesses.

Do the water one last and pour it high — it is the sound they all get right and the one they ask for again tomorrow.

1 min Close

Hands cupped at the ears, leaning in. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Hear… hear… HEAR!” (one bright ring of the bell on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Peace at Last (Jill Murphy) — every page is a noise. Make the noise with them; don't read the sentences.

Day 3 · Feel It · Sink or Float

Today's words: touch (see · hear come back)

Grab: a feely bag in the Magic Box — a pinecone, a silk scarf, a square of sandpaper; a basin of water, a stone and a cork, two labelled dishes (沉 sink · 浮 float) and a towel; the 触觉 card; the open window for the poem.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today we reach in without looking. The box comes out with a cloth bag sitting inside it.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Hand in, eyes up at the ceiling, feeling about. Narrate it: “Ooh… it's bumpy… it's pointy… it's… a PINECONE!” Out it comes.

Teacher“I never looked once. My fingers knew.” (hold up all ten and wiggle them)

Everyone wiggles ten fingers. “Touch!”

4 min Teach · Touch

Three things go round the circle, one at a time, everyone gets a feel:

Pinecone — bumpy! (fingers tapping in the air)

Silk — soft… (stroke your own cheek with it)

Sandpaper — rough! (scrub your fingertips across it)

Littles say the feeling word and wiggle their fingers: “Soft!” Bigs take the frame: “I touch something soft.”

Then the bag again, this time for them: reach in, name it before you pull it out. Right or wrong, everybody claps — the bag is its own control of error, you pull it out and you see.

Teacher fails: pull out the sandpaper, stroke it lovingly down your cheek and sigh “Ahhh… so SOFT.” Offer it to a child to stroke. Wait.

Chinese moment (2 min, at the open window): read 《初秋》 孟浩然 — “不觉初秋夜渐长，清风习习重凄凉。” Then everyone goes silent and uses all five senses on early autumn: what can you hear? what can you feel on your face? what does the air smell like now? Lay the 触觉 card with the others.

3 min Song · Two Little Eyes — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — today make “ten little fingers” enormous — then:

Wednesday's verse: Soft and bumpy, wet and dry, (stroke, tap, splash, brush palms together)

My fingers know — let me try! (hold both hands out, palms up)

Sing it once with hands behind backs and once with hands out in front. They notice the difference themselves.

3 min Game · Sink or float?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The basin comes to the middle of the circle, the two dishes either side: 沉 sink and 浮 float.

Teacher“Hold it first. Heavy? Or light? Now guess — sink, or float?”

Each child holds the object, feels the weight, guesses, and then drops it in and watches. “Sink!” “Float!”  Bigs: “The stone sinks. The cork floats.”

Eight objects: stone, coin, key, metal spoon, marble — cork, leaf, feather, apple, wooden block. Wrong guesses are the best bit; nobody is wrong, the water answers.

Guess before the drop, every single time. The touch comes first (heavy / light), then the prediction, then the water tells the truth — that is the whole experiment, and it lives on the shelf all week.

1 min Close

Ten fingers wiggling in the air. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Touch… touch… TOUCH!” (all ten fingers wiggling high on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Any That's Not My… touchy-feely book (Fiona Watt) — pass it round, every child gets one patch to stroke and one word: soft, rough, bumpy.

Day 4 · Sniff, Sniff, Sniff

Today's words: smell (see · hear · touch come back)

Grab: three smelling jars in the Magic Box — orange peel, a mint leaf, fresh ginger (cotton wool in a small jar, lid on); the three real things laid out on a mat across the room; the 嗅觉 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today we sniff the box. Lift the lid a crack and sniff at the gap, eyes closed, for a long moment.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”
Teacher“Mmmm… I can't see it. I can't hear it. But my nose knows!” (tap your nose twice)

Carry the open box round the circle so every child gets one sniff before anything comes out. Then lift out the orange jar.

Tap your nose. “Smell!”

4 min Teach · Smell

One jar at a time, lid off, round the circle. Big sniff, then the face that goes with it.

Orange! (sniff, eyes wide, big smile)

Mint! (sniff, whole face freshens up)

Ginger! (sniff, nose wrinkles, head back)

Littles sniff and say the one word — “Orange!” — or just pull the face, which is also an answer. Bigs: “I smell an orange.” and “I like it.” / “I don't like it!”

Then all three jars with eyes shut: can you tell them apart with your nose alone? Two children at a time.

Teacher fails: hold the jar up to your eye like the magnifying glass and peer into it: “Nothing there! I can't smell a single thing.” Wait for twenty children to shout your own nose at you.

The 嗅觉 card joins the row. Four down, one to go — point along the line and let them tell you which one is missing.

3 min Song · Two Little Eyes — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — big tap on “one nose” — then:

Thursday's verse: Sniff, sniff, sniff — what's in the jar? (three quick sniffs, then hold up an imaginary jar)

My good nose knows near and far! (tap your nose, then point far away)

Three real sniffs on “sniff, sniff, sniff” — loud ones. It is the funniest line of the week and they will do it in the corridor.

3 min Game · Sniff and find

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The three real objects — orange peel, mint, ginger — are waiting on a mat across the room.

Teacher“Sniff the jar… now GO and find it!” (hold a jar under a child's nose)

They run, choose, hold it up. The class calls it: “Orange!”  Bigs bring it back and say “I smell an orange.”

Every child gets a turn — it takes about eight seconds each. Last two rounds, send two children at once and let them race.

If a child hesitates at the mat, let a friend go and stand beside them. Nobody sits down without having held something.

1 min Close

Everybody's finger on their nose. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Smell… smell… SMELL!” (one enormous sniff all together on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Little Mouse, the Red Ripe Strawberry, and the Big Hungry Bear (Don & Audrey Wood) — the whole book is about a smell nobody can resist.

Day 5 · Taste It! + Big Review

Today's words: see · hear · touch · smell · taste — the whole week

Grab: a tasting tray — lemon slices, honey, a plain cracker, one clean teaspoon per child; every prop from the week back in the box (magnifying glass, bell, feely bag, jars); the awards (print pack page 17); the 味觉 card. Check allergies first.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out fat, rattling, and smelling of lemon.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL! Which sense do we need?”

Pull the props out one at a time and hold each up in silence — they will supply the word before you can.

Magnifying glass → “See!”  Bell → “Hear!”  Feely bag → “Touch!”  Jar → “Smell!”

Last of all, the lemon. Everyone waits. “TASTE!”

4 min Teach · Taste, then the whole week

One clean spoon per child, three tastes, faces allowed to be enormous:

Honey — sweet! (eyes closed, big slow smile)

Lemon — sour! (whole face screws up, everyone screams)

Cracker — plain. (shrug, chew, nod)

Littles say the one word and pull the face: “Sour!” Bigs: “I taste a lemon. It is sour!”

Then everybody stands and we tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

Two eyes — I see! (point at eyes, then out)

Two ears — I hear! (cup both ears)

Ten fingers — I touch! (wiggle all ten)

One nose — I smell! (tap your nose)

One tongue — I TASTE! (tongue out, everybody)

Teacher fails: put the lemon slice to your ear and listen hard. “Hmm. It doesn't taste of anything.” Hold the pose. Let them fix you twice.

Allergy check before you start, and one spoon per child — never a shared spoon. A child who won't taste can smell instead and still shout the word.

3 min Song · Two Little Eyes — Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: Sweet and sour upon my tongue, (big smile, then screwed-up face)

Taste it, taste it — yum, yum, yum! (rub your tummy three times)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, top to bottom. They learned it one verse a morning without noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · Which sense?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Props in a heap in the middle. Hold one up; the class shouts which sense it needs and does the gesture.

Teacher“Which sense? … and what do we DO?”

Magnifying glass → “See!” (fingers to eyes)
Bell → “Hear!” (cup ears)
Sandpaper → “Touch!” (wiggle ten fingers)
Jar → “Smell!” (tap nose)
Lemon → “Taste!” (tongue out)

Go round twice, faster the second time, then let a Big hold the props. Bigs owe you a sentence — “I hear a bell.” — Littles owe you a word and a gesture.

Slip one thing in that needs two senses — the orange. Watch them argue. Both answers are right; say so out loud.

1 min Close + awards

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home with the sink-or-float news. Then, standing, with all five gestures:

Everyone“See… hear… touch… smell… TASTE!” (eyes · ears · fingers · nose · tongue out on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Lemons Are Not Red (Laura Vaccaro Seeger) — every page is a “no it isn't!” Perfect for a class that has spent all week correcting you.

Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“sing — song — sing-a”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: gentle and bouncy, like a finger-play — this is a hands song, not a shouting song. Slow right down for the last line of the chorus (the Am), then lift on “off we go!” Chant it without the uke first; add chords once they know the actions.

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who does all four gestures has understood five senses, whether or not a single word comes out.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. Add Am last: it is one finger on the same string as F, so slide from Am to F and back until it stops feeling like a change. That's the entire song — and the five verses are only C and G7. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.

Theme Shelf · My 5 Senses

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Sound cylinders
from TUESDAY's Magic Box		Six shakers, three matching pairs. Shake one, shake another, pair them up. Control: matching colour dots on the bases — turn them over and check. Beside it, the Five Senses 3-part cards (pages 10–11): Littles match picture→picture on the control card, Bigs lay the labels underneath. Language: “Same!” / “Different!” Bigs: “I hear a loud one.”

from WEDNESDAY's Magic Box		Rough → smooth touch tablets to grade, then the feely bag: reach in, name it before you pull it out. Tray 3 · Smelling jars
from THURSDAY's Magic Box		Five pairs of jars — orange, mint, ginger, lemon, coffee. Sniff and match. Control: matching symbols stuck under the lids. Language: “Same smell!” Bigs: “I smell mint.” Refresh the cotton wool on Wednesday or the whole tray goes flat by Friday.
Tray 4 · Sink or float
from WEDNESDAY's experiment		A basin, a towel, eight objects and two labelled dishes: 沉 sink and 浮 float (signs, pages 12–13; the 12 sorting cards, page 14). Hold it, guess, drop it, sort the card. End of Week Wrap-Up

Friday recap — read it at the last circle, then print it for the parent wall or WeChat photo.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Sep 14–18 · Theme: My 5 Senses · Sound of the week: i

What we did

This week the children used all five senses on purpose, one a day. On Monday a magnifying glass came out of the Magic Box and we hunted colours and shapes round the room — “I spy something red!” On Tuesday we shut our eyes and guessed sounds from behind a cloth: a bell, keys, crumpling paper, water pouring. On Wednesday we reached into a mystery bag and named things by touch alone — pinecone, silk, sandpaper — and then ran a real sink-or-float experiment: hold it, feel if it is heavy or light, guess, and only then drop it in the water. On Thursday we matched smelling jars — orange, mint, ginger — and raced to find the real thing across the room. On Friday we tasted honey (sweet!), lemon (sour!) and a plain cracker, sang the whole song, and took a star award home.

Words we learned

seeheartouchsmelltastesoftroughloudsweetsoursinkfloat
视觉听觉触觉嗅觉味觉沉 · 浮

Sentences to listen for at home

“I see a red apple.” · “I hear a bell.” · “I touch something soft.” · “I smell an orange.” · “The stone sinks. The cork floats.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “See!” with two fingers at the eyes, or “Hear!” with both hands cupped behind the ears, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Two Little Eyes”

“Two little eyes that look and see, two little ears that listen to me! Ten little fingers that touch and know, one nose, one tongue — off we go!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning. Ask your child to sing the chorus — it is a finger-play, so watch their hands, not just their words.

Dark Phonics · the i sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met i — the short, quick “ih” right at the front of a word: in, is, it. Our silly line for it is “icky, sticky pig!” It fitted the week perfectly: I spy was Monday's game, and the hand went in the bag on Wednesday — so the letter and the games taught each other. Listen for it in: in, it, is, sit, pig, big, tin.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · At dinner, everybody stops and listens for ten seconds: “What can you hear?”
2 · Put a spoon, a cork and a stone in a bowl of water in the sink. Guess first — “Sink? Or float?” — then drop it in.
3 · Hold something behind your back and let your child feel it without looking: “I touch something soft.”
4 · One sniff of something in the kitchen with eyes closed: “I smell an orange!”
5 · Hunt for the i sound: in, it, is, pig, sit — say them short and quick.

Thank you for a lovely week — the Whale Class 🐳

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
