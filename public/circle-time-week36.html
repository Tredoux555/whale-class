Whale Class · Weekly Circle Time

Graduation

Week of Jun 14–18 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

graduationfriendthank yougrowgood-bye

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Bye-bye!” (a big slow wave), “Thank you!” (hands together, a little bow), “Big!” (stretch tall on tiptoe).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “I am big now.” · “Thank you, teacher.” · “Good-bye, my friends — I will come back!”

The one big idea: look how small we were — and look at us now. This is the last week of the whole year, so this week is the review. The four trays become a memory shelf, and on Friday the class sings its graduation song and then one chorus of every song we sang all year. Parents will cry. That is the point.

Still exactly one song. “We Grew So Big” is the week's song — same chorus daily, one new verse a morning, the whole thing on Friday. The old choruses are not new material: they come back on Thursday as a memory game (two bars, shout the title) and on Friday as the finale medley. Never teach a second song this week.

Before Monday: ask every family for their child's first-day-of-school photograph and print one copy each. Write the date on the back. Everything on Monday depends on them, and they become Tray 1 for the rest of the week.

Day 1 · Look How We Grew

Today's words: grow

Grab: every child's first-day-of-school photograph, printed, one each, with the date written on the back, in the Magic Box; a long paper strip per child for the growth timeline (Tray 1); the 长大 card; the picture cards from print pack page 11.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is light and flat and it does not rattle at all. Hold it up. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a soft paper slither. Lift the lid a crack, peek in for a long time, and let your face go slowly amazed.

Teacher“There are some very small people in here… and I think I know them.” (draw out the first photograph and hold it against your chest, face hidden)

Turn it round. One photograph at a time, held high, all the way round the circle. Do not name them — let the class do it. Then stretch up tall together: “Big!”

4 min Teach · Grow

Everybody crouches down as small as they can go. We grow up twice — slow, then quick:

When I came here I was THIS small, (crouch right down, head tucked in)

I couldn't talk, I couldn't run, (shake your head, hands over your mouth)

Now look at me — I'm THIS tall, (rise slowly and stretch up on tiptoe)

And I can do it ALL! (one big jump with both arms up)

Then the photographs, properly. Hold one up beside the real child standing next to you and say the plain true thing, slowly: this was you, in September. You could not say “hello” in English. You are the same person, and you are not the same size, and nobody in this room stopped growing for one single day.

Littles crouch and stretch and say “Big!” Bigs take the frame: “I am big now.” “I was little in September.”

Teacher fails: hold up the smallest photograph, look hard at it, then hold it next to your own face. “Ah. This is me.” Nod seriously. Hold it. Wait for the roar.

Chinese moment: lay the 长大 card in the middle of the mat and say 你们长大了！ once, all together. Leave the card there; the row grows by one card a day and on Friday the whole year is on the mat: 长大 · 毕业 · 朋友 · 谢谢 · 再见.

3 min Song · We Grew So Big — chorus + Monday verse

We were so small, and now we're SO big! (crouch tiny, then stretch tall)

Look how we grew — let's dance a jig! (everybody jumps)

Thank you, teachers, thank you, friends, (hands together, bow one way then the other)

Sing our song — it never ends! (arms wide)

Monday's verse: Little, little, little me, (crouch smaller on each “little”)

Now I'm big as big can be! (shoot up onto tiptoe, arms right up)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole thing on Friday. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. Sing it standing in a ring, holding hands, every single day.

3 min Game · Then & now

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The photographs are face down in a basket in the middle of the circle.

Teacher“Take one… look at it… now find that person and give it to them.”

One child at a time: pick, look, walk round the circle, and hand the photograph to the child in it with two hands. Littles get shown two photographs and point at the right friend — a full win. Bigs say it as they hand it over: “This is you. You were little!”

Nobody gets their own. That is the whole game: to find your photograph you have to be found, by a friend.

Keep the photographs out afterwards — they go straight onto Tray 1, the growth timeline, with the date on the back as the control. They stay on the shelf all week and go home in Friday's folder.

1 min Close

Standing in a ring, crouched right down and ready. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“We were so small… now we are SO BIG!” (crouch tiny on the whisper · rise on the normal voice · jump up with both arms on the SHOUT)

Got 3 spare minutes? Leo the Late Bloomer (Robert Kraus) — Leo can't do anything at all, until one day he can. It is the kindest book in the world for the child who is still catching up, and this is the week to read it.

Day 2 · Cap and Certificate

Today's words: graduation (grow comes back every day)

Grab: one graduation cap with a tassel and one blank certificate (print pack page 17), in the Magic Box; a chair or a mat square that marks “the front”; the caps for the whole class if you have them, still in their bag; the 毕业 card; yesterday's photographs on Tray 1.

2 min Magic Box hook

There are exactly two things in the box today: one is flat and one is square with something dangling off it. Chant first:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tip the box gently — a slide, then a swing. Lift the lid and take out the certificate first, holding it flat in both hands like something precious.

Teacher“This is a paper… and it has somebody's name on it. Whose name do you think?” (take out the cap, put it on your head, and let the tassel swing)

Both hands on their own heads: “Graduation!”

4 min Teach · Graduation

Everybody stands. We put the cap on twice — slow and careful, then proud:

Cap on my head, (both hands lower an imaginary cap onto your head)

Tassel on the side, (flick two fingers beside your ear, swinging)

Walk to the front, (four big marching steps on the spot)

and stand up with PRIDE! (stop, feet together, back straight, chin up)

Then explain it once, plainly, because most of them have never heard the word: graduation is the day we say you are finished. You came, you stayed the whole year, you learned all of it — and on Friday everybody watches you walk to the front on your own and take your paper. Nobody has to be brave. We are going to practise it until it is easy.

Littles pat their heads and say “Graduation!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “I am big now.” “On Friday I walk to the front.”

Teacher fails: put the cap on backwards, with the tassel hanging straight down over your nose, and try to read the certificate through it. Blow the tassel out of the way. Try again. Wait.

Chinese moment: the 毕业 card joins 长大 on the mat — 毕业啦！ shouted once at full volume, both arms up. Then 毕业帽 as the cap goes round the circle and every single child wears it for three seconds.

3 min Song · We Grew So Big — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus first, with the actions — today on “thank you, teachers” everybody bows to a real teacher — then the new verse:

Tuesday's verse: Cap on my head and a smile so wide, (hands on head, then draw a big smile with one finger)

Walk to the front with a little pride! (four proud marching steps, then stand tall and still)

Sing this verse marching, the whole class walking the circle in a slow line, and stop dead on the last word. The stopping is the bit they need for Friday; the song is just how we practise it without anybody feeling watched.

3 min Game · Graduation parade practice

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Mark “the front” with a chair. Four steps, and the class says them out loud with you each time: walk · stop · bow · cap on.

Teacher(call one name at a time, exactly as you will on Friday) “…and now — Mei-mei!” (everybody claps until she is back in her place)

Every child goes once. Littles walk it holding a friend's hand and that is how they will do it on Friday too — decide it now, not on the day. Bigs go alone and take the pretend certificate with two hands: “Thank you!”

Clap for every single child, exactly the same amount. They are counting, and they will remember for years who got the loudest clap.

1 min Close

Standing in a ring, caps on (real or imaginary). Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“We were so small… now we are SO BIG!” (crouch tiny · rise · jump up with both arms on the SHOUT, tassels flying)

Got 3 spare minutes? Oh, the Places You'll Go! (Dr Seuss) — read two spreads only, not the whole thing, and read them slowly. Save the rest for Friday.

Day 3 · Our Memory Box

Today's words: friend (grow · graduation come back every day)

Grab: the memory box — a globe, a dinosaur, a rocket, a bird's nest, a red envelope 红包 and a pinwheel — one object per big stretch of the year; a cloth to cover it; the month cards for Tray 2; the 朋友 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is heavy and it rattles like a whole cupboard. Carry it in with both arms. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it once — an enormous clatter — and look alarmed at the noise you just made. Then lift out one thing at a time and say nothing at all.

Teacher(hold up the globe, then the dinosaur, then the rocket, in complete silence) “…we did all of this.”

Every object goes into the middle of the mat. Hands to the temples, remembering: “I remember!”

4 min Teach · Friend · remember

Everybody sits round the six objects. Say it twice, and the second time they say it with you:

Do you remember? (both hands to your temples, eyes shut)

Yes — I do! (eyes open wide, one finger up)

A star, a dinosaur, a seed that grew… (point up · stomp · hands opening like a flower)

and best of all — YOU! (point at the friend beside you)

Now take each object round the circle and let them tell you: the globe is when we found all seven continents and sat on the map; the dinosaur is when we dug for fossils; the rocket is blast off; the nest is where the bird lives; the red envelope is 春节; the pinwheel is the windy day. Then say the true thing: not one of those days happened on your own. You did every single one of them with your friends.

Littles hold one object and name it — “Rocket!” Bigs give the sentence: “You are my friend.” “I remember the dinosaurs!”

Teacher fails: pick up the bird's nest, look at it blankly and guess: “A hat? Is it a little hat?” Put it on your head. Wait to be corrected, loudly, by everybody at once.

Chinese moment: the 朋友 card joins the row — 好朋友. Then everybody turns to the child beside them, takes both their hands and says it: 你是我的好朋友。 Tomorrow we thank the teachers; today we thank each other.

3 min Song · We Grew So Big — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus with the actions — today sung holding hands right round the ring — then the new verse:

Wednesday's verse: Stars and dinosaurs, seeds and snow, (point up · stomp · hands opening · fingers falling like snow)

All the things we got to know! (both hands sweep round the whole circle)

Four gestures, four whole months of the year in one line. Do them very slowly the first time and let somebody shout out what each one was — that shouting is the review, and it costs you nothing.

3 min Game · Memory-box guessing

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The six objects go back in the box under the cloth. One child at a time, eyes closed, hand in.

Teacher“No looking… feel it… what is it — and when did we do it?” (the whole circle waits; nobody shouts the answer)

Naming it before seeing it is the work; naming the month is the extra step for the bigs. Littles feel it and give one word: “Rocket!” Bigs give both: “A rocket — that was May!” Then out it comes and everybody sees.

Put the same object back in twice on purpose. A child who feels the nest a second time and names it instantly, without stopping to think, has just shown you that the word is theirs for good.

1 min Close

Standing in a ring, holding the hands of the friends on either side. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“We were so small… now we are SO BIG!” (crouch tiny together · rise together · all the arms go up together on the SHOUT, hands still joined)

Got 3 spare minutes? My Friend Is Sad (Mo Willems) — two friends, almost no words, and every child in the room can read the faces. Let them do the voices.

Day 4 · Thank You + Our Songs

Today's words: thank you (grow · graduation · friend come back every day)

Grab: the ukulele itself, in the Magic Box if it fits and beside it if it doesn't, plus the year's song-title cards (Tray 3); the thank-you card materials for Tray 4 — folded card, a model, glue, paper flowers; the 谢谢 card; the memory box still out on Tray 2.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box will not shut, because the ukulele is sticking out of it. Carry it in that way, seriously, as if you had not noticed. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — and the strings go brrrring all by themselves. Look at the box, astonished. Shake it again.

Teacher“This box is SINGING.” (lift the ukulele out and hold it up; strum one slow chord and say nothing)

Then the song-title cards, fanned out like a hand of cards. Hands together, a little bow: “Thank you!”

4 min Teach · Thank you

Everybody stands. Say it twice — once quietly to one person, once out loud to the whole room:

Thank you for the songs, (hands together, bow to the ukulele)

Thank you for the days, (bow to the window)

Thank you, all my friends, (turn on the spot, bowing all the way round)

in a hundred different ways! (both arms thrown wide)

Then the real thing, and take your time over it. Ask them who they want to thank — and take every answer: the cleaning 阿姨, the cook, the driver, the teacher who plaits their hair, a friend who shared a bike. Write the names down as they say them; that list is who Tray 4's thank-you cards get made for. Say it plainly: thank you is two words that you say to somebody's face, and this week we are going to say it to everybody who helped us.

Littles put their hands together and bow: “Thank you!” Bigs owe you the whole line: “Thank you, teacher.” “Thank you for the year with you.”

Teacher fails: hold the ukulele the wrong way round, strum the back of it with great feeling, and look puzzled when nothing happens. Turn it upside down and try again. Wait.

Chinese moment: the 谢谢 card joins the row — 谢谢老师，谢谢朋友, said with the bow. Then tell them about tomorrow: 李白 wrote a poem for a friend who came to sing him good-bye — 《赠汪伦》 — and we will say its last line at the ceremony: 桃花潭水深千尺，不及汪伦送我情。

3 min Song · We Grew So Big — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus with the actions — today one child holds the ukulele with you and strums the last chord — then the new verse:

Thursday's verse: Thank you, teacher, thank you true, (hands together, a deep bow)

Thank you for the year with you! (both hands out to the whole room, slowly)

Sing this one quietly. It is the only quiet verse in the song and it is the one the parents will remember on Friday, so do not let it get shouted. Straight back into the big chorus afterwards.

3 min Game · Name that song

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Ten song-title cards face down. You play two bars only — no words — and they shout the title.

Teacher(strum two bars of “Dinosaur Stomp” and stop dead) “…which one?”

Good ones to play: Two Little Eyes · Autumn Is Here! · I Love My Family · A Little Seed · Ring the Bells, It's Christmas! · How's the Weather? · Gong Xi, Happy New Year! · Blast Off! · Dinosaur Stomp · Summer, Summer, Sunny Day. Whoever gets it turns the card over and the class sings one chorus, standing.

This is a memory game, not new material. One chorus each, no verses, no teaching, and no correcting — the words come back on their own or they don't. The week's own song is still “We Grew So Big”, and it is the only one we are still learning.

1 min Close

Standing in a ring, hands together ready to bow. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“We were so small… now we are SO BIG!” (crouch tiny · rise · jump with both arms up on the SHOUT, then one last bow to each other)

Got 3 spare minutes? Thank You, Omu! (Oge Mora) — everybody takes a little of the stew, and then everybody brings something back. Read it just before the thank-you cards go on the shelf.

Day 5 · The Graduation Ceremony + the Whole Year

Today's words: good-bye — and graduation · friend · thank you · grow, the whole week, and the whole year

Grab: the certificates, names written in, one per child (print pack page 17); the caps; the class photograph plan (who stands where); every prop of the year laid out along the mat in order, September on the left; the ukulele; the thank-you cards the children made; the 再见 card; tissues for the parents. Today runs long — the ceremony is not squeezed into 13 minutes.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out bulging and will not close, and the whole year is laid out on the mat behind it.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it has our whole YEAR inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The photograph → “Little me!”  The cap → “Graduation!”  The memory box → “I remember!”  The ukulele → “Thank you!”

Last of all, one small thing left in the bottom: a certificate with a real name on it. Read the name out. Then wave it, slowly, high. “GOOD-BYE!”

4 min Teach · Good-bye, then the whole year

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

I was so little, (crouch right down)

Now I've grown, (rise onto tiptoe)

Graduation day — (both hands lower the cap onto your head)

Thank you, friends, (hands together, bow all the way round)

Good-bye — but not for long! (a big slow wave, then both arms wide)

Then walk the mat, September to June, and let them name it: I'm special · my body · five senses · feelings · autumn · food · family · my house · plants · helpers · Christmas · winter · Beijing · China · 春节 · the continents · the oceans · Africa · spring · habitats · the Earth · the universe · the solar system · space · dinosaurs · summer. Say the true thing at the end of the walk: you did all of that, and in September you could not say “hello”.

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “I am big now.” “Thank you, teacher.” “Good-bye, my friends — I will come back!”

Teacher fails: start handing out the certificates to the parents instead, very solemnly, shaking each of them by the hand. Keep going until the children stop you.

Chinese moment: the 再见 card completes the row — 长大 · 毕业 · 朋友 · 谢谢 · 再见, the whole week on the mat. Say 《赠汪伦》's last line together, slowly: 桃花潭水深千尺，不及汪伦送我情。 A friend came down to the water to sing good-bye, and the poet never forgot it. That is exactly what today is. Then, all together: 老师再见！朋友再见！

3 min Song · We Grew So Big — Friday verse, the WHOLE song, then the medley

Friday's verse: Good-bye, good-bye — but not for long! (a big slow wave with both hands)

We'll come back and sing our song! (point back at the classroom door, then arms wide)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, standing in the ring with the parents behind them. They learned it one verse a morning without ever noticing, and this run-through is the review of the week.

Then the medley — the review of the year. One chorus each, in order, no verses: Where Do You Live? → This Is My Earth → Up the Mountain, Down the River → Love the Earth → The Big Bang Boom → Round and Round → Blast Off! → Dinosaur Stomp → Dig, Dig, Dig → Summer, Summer, Sunny Day — and straight back into the “We Grew So Big” chorus to finish. Hold the song-title cards up as you go so nobody is lost; if a chorus has gone, skip it and keep moving.

3 min Game · The graduation ceremony

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Caps on. Everybody sits in the ring; the parents sit behind. Exactly what we practised on Tuesday: walk · stop · bow · cap on.

Teacher(one name at a time, said clearly and slowly) “…and now — Mei-mei!” (she walks, takes the certificate with two hands, bows, and everybody claps until she is back)

Littles walk it holding a friend's hand, exactly as agreed on Tuesday. Bigs go alone, take it with two hands and say it out loud: “Thank you!” Every child hands their thank-you card to the person they made it for on the way back.

Then the class photograph, everybody in, teachers included — and one silly one straight afterwards.

Same clap for every child, and read every name slowly enough to get it right. Somebody in this room will remember this two minutes for thirty years.

1 min Close + certificates

Standing in the ring, certificates held in two hands, caps on. All five gestures, one last time:

Everyone“We were so small… now we are SO BIG!” (crouch right down on the whisper · rise on the normal voice · every arm and every certificate up on the SHOUT)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Kissing Hand (Audrey Penn) — the good-bye book, and the one that lets a child cry about it without anybody making a fuss. Then send them out of the door with their folder: photographs, timeline strip, worksheet, certificate.

Theme Song · “We Grew So Big”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday, then the year's medley.

The four chords

C
F
G7
Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“we grew — so — big-ger”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — on Friday the parents are watching the children, not your right hand.

Tempo: bright and marching, and a big rest before “SO big!” so the whole room can jump into it together. Four shapes: C, F, G7 and Am — Am is one finger and it carries the two lines that make the parents cry, so it is worth the five minutes. Every verse is only C and G7.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CWe were so small, and Fnow we're SO Cbig! (crouch tiny, then stretch tall)

AmLook how we Cgrew — let's G7dance a Cjig! (everybody jumps)

CThank you, teachers, Fthank you, Cfriends, (hands together, bow both ways)

AmSing our Csong — it G7never Cends! (arms wide)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who crouches tiny, shoots up tall and bows to a friend has understood the week, whether or not a single word comes out.

One verse a day — every verse is just C and G7

Monday · Grow

CLittle, little, little me, (crouch smaller on each “little”)

G7Now I'm big Cas big can be! (shoot up onto tiptoe, arms right up)

Tuesday · Graduation

CCap on my head and a smile so wide, (hands on head, then draw a big smile)

G7Walk to the front Cwith a little pride! (four proud steps, then stand still)

Wednesday · Remember

CStars and dinosaurs, seeds and snow, (point up · stomp · hands opening · falling fingers)

G7All the things Cwe got to know! (sweep both hands round the circle)

Thursday · Thank you

CThank you, teacher, thank you true, (hands together, a deep bow)

G7Thank you for Cthe year with you! (both hands out to the whole room)

Friday · Good-bye

CGood-bye, good-bye — but not for long! (a big slow wave)

G7We'll come back Cand sing our song! (point at the door, then arms wide)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — and Friday's finale, sung in a ring with the parents behind them, doubles as the review of the whole week.

Friday's medley — the one exception of the year. After the whole song, one chorus each, in order: Where Do You Live? · This Is My Earth · Up the Mountain, Down the River · Love the Earth · The Big Bang Boom · Round and Round · Blast Off! · Dinosaur Stomp · Dig, Dig, Dig · Summer, Summer, Sunny Day — then back into the “We Grew So Big” chorus to finish. These are remembered, not taught: no verses, no rehearsing, no correcting.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → Am → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and every verse is just C and G7. C is one finger, Am is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order and leave G7 till last. If your fingers get stuck on Friday of all days, put the uke down, hold the class photograph up instead and do the finger-play: the actions carry the meaning either way, and after a whole year they will sing it without you.

Memory Shelf · Graduation

For one week only the theme shelf becomes a memory shelf. Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language. On Friday afternoon the whole shelf goes home in the children's folders.

Tray 1 · Growth timeline
from MONDAY's Magic Box		Each child's photographs, first day → today, laid out in order along a paper strip. Control: the date written on the back of each photograph — turn them over and check. Language: “first · then · now · little · big.” Bigs: “I was little in September. I am big now.”

Whale Class · Year Wrap-Up 🐳

Jun 14–18 · Theme: Graduation · Sound of the week: sh · the last week of the year

What we did

One whole year. On Monday your child's first-day photograph came out of the Magic Box and went round the circle, and nobody could believe how small everybody was. On Tuesday the cap and the certificate arrived and we practised the walk — walk, stop, bow, cap on — until it was easy. On Wednesday we opened the memory box: a globe, a dinosaur, a rocket, a bird's nest, a 红包 and a pinwheel, one object for each big stretch of the year, and they named every single one with their eyes shut. On Thursday we thanked everybody who helped us and made a card for them, and played name that song — two bars on the ukulele and twenty children shouting the title. And on Friday they put the caps on, walked to the front one at a time, took their certificate with two hands and said “Thank you!” — and then sang their graduation song and one chorus of every song of the year, straight through, without stopping.

Sentences to listen for at home

“I am big now.” · “Thank you, teacher.” · “You are my friend.” · “Good-bye, my friends — I will come back!”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Big!” on tiptoe, or “Bye-bye!” with a big slow wave — and at 2 and 3 that is a very big win indeed.

Our song · “We Grew So Big”

“We were so small, and now we're SO big! Look how we grew — let's dance a jig! Thank you, teachers, thank you, friends, Sing our song — it never ends!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — grow, graduation, remember, thank you, good-bye. Ask your child to sing the chorus and watch their hands: they crouch down tiny, shoot up tall, bow to you, and throw their arms wide at the end. Then ask for “Blast Off!” or “Dinosaur Stomp” — they know ten songs by heart now.

Dark Phonics · the sh sound — and the grand review

Our very last sound is sh — the quiet one, one long finger on the lips: shine, ship, shoe, sheep, and shhhh. Our line for it is “sh! be still — sheep go baba!” It is a lovely one to finish on, because it is the sound the whole room makes when the ceremony is about to begin. And because it is graduation, we sang the grand review as well: the alphabet A–Z straight through, the five short vowels a e i o u, and the double endings ll · ff · ss, each one hooked to a word from the year — big bang, rocket, dinosaur, shell. That is a whole alphabet learned in one year, in a second language.

Try this over the summer (2 minutes)

1 · Put the first-day photograph and Friday's class photograph side by side on the fridge and let them tell you the difference — in English: “little · big.”
2 · Keep the songs going in the car. Ten choruses, no homework, and the words stay.
3 · Hunt the sh sound with a finger on your lips: shoe, ship, shop, sheep, fish, wash.
4 · Let them read you the picture books they know. A child who “reads” from memory is reading.
5 · Say “thank you” out loud to somebody in front of them, every day. That one turned out to be the whole year's real lesson.

That is the end of our year together. They arrived not knowing “hello” and they are leaving able to say “I am big now,” “I found a fossil,” “The bird lives in a nest,” “I love the Earth.” They know ten songs by heart, the whole alphabet, every short vowel and a good handful of digraphs. Today they wore a cap, walked to the front on their own, and sang every song of the year straight through. 夏至 falls on 21 June, the longest day of the summer — a good day to go outside and start the holiday properly.

Thank you for lending them to us. Come back and sing with us any time — 老师再见！朋友再见！🐳

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
