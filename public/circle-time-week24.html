Whale Class · Weekly Circle Time

One Country — South Africa

Week of Mar 22–26 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

South Africazebramountainflaghello (Sawubona)

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Hello!” (a big two-handed wave), “Zebra!” (draw stripes down your body), “Mountain!” (flat hands, flat top).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “This is South Africa.” · “The zebra is black and white.” · “The mountain is flat on top.” · “Sawubona means hello.”

The one big idea: at the very bottom of Africa there is a country with six colours in its flag and a mountain with a flat top — and it is Teacher Tredoux's country. Last week we found the whole continent; this week we zoom right in on one country in it. Say it out loud every single day, and say it in the first person: my flag, my mountain, my country. That is what makes it real to them.

Bring your own things in. Photographs of home, of the road to school, of family — Teacher Tredoux's family are from Newcastle, in KwaZulu-Natal. A photograph of a real person's real house does more in ten seconds than any poster. 春分, the spring equinox, fell on Sunday 21 March, the day before this week begins: day and night exactly equal, and an egg that will stand up on Friday.

Day 1 · Six Colours, One Flag

Today's words: flag

Grab: the real South African flag in the Magic Box (a cloth one you can wave, not a picture); six squares of coloured paper — black, gold, green, white, red, blue; last week's green Africa puzzle piece and a photograph of Teacher Tredoux as a small boy; a long strip of paper pinned up as the week's display; the 旗 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is light and flat, and when you tip it something whispers — cloth sliding on cardboard. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a soft dry whisper, no rattle at all. Sniff it — nothing. Peek in for a long time and let your face go enormous. Then take hold of two corners and pull it out slowly, wide open, so it fills the space in front of you.

Teacher“Look at all the colours… let's COUNT them.” (touch each colour and count out loud together — one… two… three… four… five… SIX!)

Everybody waves an imaginary flag above their head: “Flag!”

4 min Teach · Flag

The flag stays open in your two hands the whole way through. Say it twice; the second time they do the actions with you:

Six bright colours, (hold the flag wide open in both hands)

One big flag, (snap it once so it cracks)

It flies up HIGH… (lift it right above your head)

Wave, wave, wave! (wave it big and slow, side to side)

Then the map. Last week's green Africa piece goes on the floor in the middle of the circle, and your finger travels all the way down it, slowly, to the very bottom point. Stop there. “Right down here, at the bottom — that is South Africa. That is MY country.” Now the photograph goes round the circle: a small boy, in that country, a long time ago.

Littles wave and say “Flag!” Bigs owe you the frame: “This is South Africa.” “The flag has six colours.”

Teacher fails: count the colours again, out loud, confidently — and stop at four. “One, two, three… FOUR! Four colours.” Look delighted with yourself. Wait.

Chinese moment: pin the 旗 card at the top of the week's paper strip — 国旗. Then count the six colours again, in Chinese, touching each one: 一 · 二 · 三 · 四 · 五 · 六. The row of 汉字 grows by one card every morning this week.

3 min Song · Sawubona, South Africa! — chorus + Monday verse

Sawubona, sawubona — South Africa! (a big two-handed wave)

Black and white zebra — ZA-ZA-ZA! (stripe both hands down your body)

A flat-top mountain touching the sky, (one flat hand held high overhead)

Six bright colours flying high! (wave the flag)

Monday's verse: Six bright colours in one flag, (hold up six fingers, then the flag)

South Africa — wave your flag! (wave it right over your head)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole thing on Friday. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. Whoever is holding the real flag stands in the middle and waves it for everybody.

3 min Game · Six colours

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The six paper squares are face down in the middle. The flag hangs open where everybody can see it.

Teacher(turn over one square and hold it up) “Green! — can you find green on the flag?” (everybody points at it, then goes and finds that colour somewhere in the room)

Six rounds, six colours. Littles point and say the colour word; Bigs come back with the thing they found and tell you: “I found green. It is a leaf.”

Do black and white last and leave them out on the mat — they are tomorrow's whole lesson, and the children will spot the join themselves.

1 min Close

Everybody standing, one hand waving an imaginary flag. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Not So Fast, Songololo (Niki Daly) — a small boy and his grandmother go shopping in a South African city, and he gets new red shoes. It is a real street in a real country and the pictures do all the work.

Day 2 · Zebra Stripes

Today's words: zebra (flag comes back every day)

Grab: a toy zebra and a long length of black-and-white striped cloth in the Magic Box; black and white pegs plus a zebra card on the shelf tray; the 马 card; the black and white squares left out from yesterday.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is heavy on one side, and when you tip it something firm thumps from end to end. Chant, then tip it twice — thump … thump — and stop dead.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Take out the cloth first, and keep pulling: it goes on and on and on, far longer than the box could possibly hold, until it is lying right across the circle.

Teacher“Black… white… black… white…” (walk the length of it, tapping each stripe, until you reach the end — then reach back into the box and lift the zebra out)

Both hands draw stripes down their bodies: “Zebra!”

4 min Teach · Zebra

The zebra stands in your open palm the whole way through. Say it twice; the second time everybody does it with you:

Black and white, black and white, (draw stripes down your front with both hands)

Stripes down my back, (reach over your shoulders and stripe all the way down)

Every zebra is different… (point slowly at three different children)

just like YOU! (point at one child, then at your own chest)

Then the true thing, said plainly and once: no two zebras in the whole world have the same stripes. Exactly like the lines on a fingertip; exactly like the faces sitting in this circle. Hold two children's hands up side by side while you say it.

Littles stripe their bodies and say “Zebra!” Bigs owe you a sentence: “The zebra is black and white.” “The zebra lives in South Africa.”

Teacher fails: drape the striped cloth round your shoulders, crouch down and announce, with total confidence, that you are a TIGER. Roar. Hold it. Wait for the correction.

Chinese moment: the 马 card joins 旗 on the strip — 斑马, which is literally stripe-horse, and they will love that. Then the two colours, pointing at the cloth: 黑 · 白.

3 min Song · Sawubona, South Africa! — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — on “ZA-ZA-ZA” everybody now stripes their body with the striped cloth going round the circle — then today's new verse:

Tuesday's verse: Zebra, zebra — black and white, (stripes down your front)

Stripey, stripey — what a sight! (hands wide open, eyes wide, look amazed)

Sing “ZA-ZA-ZA” three times louder each time it comes round. It is the noisiest half-second of the week and it is what they will hum in the corridor.

3 min Game · One long zebra

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody lines up nose to tail, each child's hands on the shoulders in front. You are the head. That is one zebra, and it is very long.

Teacher“Zebras walk… slowly… zebras trot… and zebras — FREEZE!” (everybody stops, hands still on shoulders, completely still)

Walk the whole room. Then turn round so the child at the back becomes the head and do it again — the tail leads, and it is a different animal entirely.

Nobody is left holding nobody. If a child does not want to be touched they walk beside the line as the zebra's foal and nothing is said about it.

1 min Close

Everybody standing, both hands ready to stripe. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? How the Zebra Got Its Stripes — a traditional African tale, in any picture-book edition you can find. Let them do the zebra's kick and the baboon's shriek every time it comes round.

Day 3 · Table Mountain, Flat on Top

Today's words: mountain (flag · zebra come back)

Grab: a big photograph of Table Mountain and a fistful of wooden blocks in the Magic Box; a square of white cloth or a handful of cotton wool for the cloud; a real small table if you have one; the 山 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is heavy and it clonks — wood on wood. Chant, then shake it once, hard, and let everybody hear it.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Reach in and pull out one block. Then another. Then a whole handful, dropped in a heap on the mat. Last of all, the photograph — held up flat against your chest, face hidden, for one long beat before you turn it round.

Teacher(turn the photograph round) “A mountain… but look at the TOP.”

Flat hands held out level, like a tabletop: “Mountain!”

4 min Teach · Mountain

Everybody stands up for this one. Say it twice; the second time they do every action with you:

A mountain goes UP, up, up, (both hands climb, higher and higher)

But this one stops — FLAT! (slap both flat hands out level, hard)

The clouds come down like a tablecloth, (flutter your fingers down over the flat hands)

And that is that! (fold your arms, done)

Then build. Two mountains out of the blocks, side by side: one pointy, one flat on top. Point at each in turn and let the class vote with their bodies — pointy hands over the head, or flat hands out level — for which one is Table Mountain. Then lay the white cloth over the flat one, slowly, so it hangs down the sides: that is the real cloud that sits on it, and in South Africa people call it the tablecloth.

Littles hold flat hands and say “Mountain!” Bigs owe you the frame: “The mountain is flat on top.” “Table Mountain is in South Africa.”

Teacher fails: build a beautiful pointy mountain, announce it is Table Mountain, and try to lay the cloth on the point. It slides off. Try again. It slides off again. Keep going until somebody takes the cloth off you.

Chinese moment: the 山 card joins 旗 · 马 — 高山, and this one is 桌山: table-mountain, the same word in both languages. Draw 山 in the air with one finger, big, all together: it is a mountain with three peaks — and then flatten the middle one with your hand.

3 min Song · Sawubona, South Africa! — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — on “a flat-top mountain touching the sky” everybody now holds one flat hand as high as it will go — then:

Wednesday's verse: Table Mountain, flat on top, (flat hands out level, held high)

Climb up, climb up — then STOP! (climb with both hands, then freeze dead still on STOP)

The freeze on “STOP” is the whole verse. Hold the silence for three full seconds before you go back into the chorus — count it in your head, it is always longer than it feels.

3 min Game · Flat top / pointy top

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” No blocks now — the bodies are the mountains.

Teacher“Pointy top!” (everybody makes a point over their head with both hands) “Flat top!” (flat hands out level) “Pointy… flat… FLAT!”

Faster and faster, and then a trick: call “Table Mountain!” and see who goes flat. Littles copy whoever is nearest, which is exactly how they learn it. Last round, a Big calls the shapes and you play in the circle with everybody else.

Two shapes only, over and over. The listening is the lesson: they are matching a whole body to one English word, which is what they must do to hear the difference between any two words.

1 min Close

Everybody standing, flat hands ready. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Jamela's Dress (Niki Daly) — a South African girl, a length of beautiful cloth, and one very big mistake. It is set in the same country as the mountain, and the cloth ties straight back to yesterday's stripes.

Day 4 · Sawubona!

Today's words: hello (Sawubona) (flag · zebra · mountain come back)

Grab: a Zulu beaded bracelet in the Magic Box; a bowl of big wooden beads and laces on the shelf tray with the red · yellow · blue pattern card; the 好 card; the class name list, so you can greet every single child by name.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is nearly empty and it makes a tiny, hard, seed-like rattle — you have to hold it right up to your ear to hear it. Chant, then hold it to your ear and go completely still.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Hold it to one child's ear. Then another's. Then lift out the bracelet on one finger and turn it slowly so the light runs round the beads.

Teacher“These beads were made by hand — in a place called KwaZulu-Natal, where my family lives.” (hold it out flat on your palm and let it go all the way round the circle)

A big two-handed wave: “Sawubona!”

4 min Teach · Hello · Sawubona

Nothing in your hands for this one. Say it twice; the second time they wave it with you:

Sawubona! (a big two-handed wave, right over your head)

It means HELLO. (wave again, smaller, at one child)

Say it soft… (whisper it, one hand beside your mouth)

say it SLOW. (say it in four slow pieces — sa · wu · BO · na — clapping each one)

Then the round, and this is the work: you go to every child in turn, look at them, and say “Sawubona, <name>!” They answer “Yebo, sawubona!” — yes, I see you too. Say the true thing once, plainly: this is Zulu, one of the twelve languages of South Africa, and it does not really mean “hello” at all — it means “I see you.” In this room we already speak two languages. Now we have three.

Littles wave and say “Sawubona!” Bigs owe you the frame: “Sawubona means hello.” “Sawubona, teacher!”

Teacher fails: greet the shelf with an enormous “SAWUBONA!”, then the door, then the whale on the wall. Shake the door's handle warmly. Wait for them to point you at an actual person.

Chinese moment: the 好 card joins 旗 · 马 · 山 — 你好. Then the three greetings in one breath, round the whole circle: 你好 · hello · sawubona. Three languages, one circle, ten seconds.

3 min Song · Sawubona, South Africa! — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — today the two-handed wave on “sawubona, sawubona” goes to the friend on your left, then the friend on your right — then:

Thursday's verse: Sawubona means hello, (a big wave, then both hands open)

Say it softly — soft and slow! (the whole verse drops to a whisper, waving very slowly)

Let this verse get quieter and slower until the last “slow” is barely a breath and only the hands are still moving — then straight back into the big chorus. The drop is the best moment in the song.

3 min Game · Pass the bracelet

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody in a circle, knees touching. The bracelet goes round hand to hand, two hands to give and two hands to take.

Teacher“Look at the bead under your thumb — what colour is it? Say it… then say sawubona to the friend you give it to.” (they name the colour, turn, and greet the next child by name)

Round the whole circle. Littles just name the colour and wave; Bigs use the name: “Sawubona, Amy!” Nobody wears it and nobody keeps it — it is a thing that travels.

Go slowly enough that every single child is greeted by their own name, out loud, in front of everybody. That is the real lesson of the day and it takes exactly as long as it takes.

1 min Close

Everybody on their feet, both hands up ready to wave. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Before home time: tomorrow everybody greets everybody, three times over, in three languages. Walk the circle once today in silence and practise just the looking — eyes up, find the face, then wave. It makes Friday twice as good.

Got 3 spare minutes? Handa's Surprise (Eileen Browne) — a girl carries a basket of fruit to her friend and one animal after another helps themselves. Let them shout the animal's name before you turn the page.

Day 5 · My Country + Big Review

Today's words: South Africa — and flag · zebra · mountain · hello, the whole week

Grab: every prop from the week packed back into the box, plus Teacher Tredoux's own photographs from home and an African drum; a raw egg for 春分; the awards (print pack page 17); the 南 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out bulging and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The flag → “Flag!”  The zebra → “Zebra!”  The blocks and the photo → “Mountain!”  The bracelet → “Sawubona!”

Last of all, right at the bottom, a small bundle of photographs. Lift them out and hold up the first one. “SOUTH AFRICA!”

4 min Teach · South Africa, then the whole week

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

A flag with six bright colours, (wave it overhead)

A zebra, black and white, (stripes down your body)

A mountain flat on top, (flat hands out level, held high)

Sawubona — that's “hello”! (a big two-handed wave)

This is SOUTH AFRICA — my home! (both arms thrown wide, then both hands over your heart)

Then the photographs, one at a time, slowly, and told as plainly as you can: this is the town I come from — Newcastle, in KwaZulu-Natal. This is the house. This is the road I walked to school on. This is my family. Let them hold the pictures. A real photograph of a real person's real house does more in ten seconds than any poster on any wall.

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “This is South Africa.” “The zebra is black and white.” “Sawubona means hello.”

Teacher fails: on “this is my home”, point confidently at the classroom window and name this city. Look certain. Then look at the photograph, then out of the window, then at the photograph again. Let them sort you out.

Chinese moment: the 南 card completes the row — 旗 · 马 · 山 · 好 · 南, the whole week on the strip, and 南 is the one that matters: 南非, south-Africa, the country at the bottom. Then 春分: it fell on Sunday, and on 春分 the day and the night are exactly the same length. Stand the raw egg on its end on a flat table — 春分立蛋 — and let them take turns until somebody does it.

3 min Song · Sawubona, South Africa! — Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: Zebra, zebra — zig-zag-zoo, (zig-zag one finger through the air, right across the circle)

South Africa says hello to you! (a big two-handed wave to the whole room)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, standing in one circle with the drum going under it and the flag travelling from hand to hand. They learned five verses one morning at a time without ever noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · Sawubona round

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody stands in the circle facing a partner. This is the whole week, performed.

Teacher“Look at them… wave… and say it three times: sawubona… 你好… hello.” (then everybody takes one step to the left and does it again with the next friend)

Round and round until every child has greeted every child. Bigs use the name every time: “Sawubona, Amy!” Littles wave and say one of the three — any one of them — and that is a full win.

Say the names out loud with them as they go. Twenty children hearing their own name twenty times, in three languages, in one circle, is the best ten minutes of language work you will do all term.

1 min Close + awards

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home. Then, standing in the circle with the drum, all five gestures:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave, with the drum, on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Africa, Amazing Africa (Atinuke) — one spread per country, big pictures, short lines. Find South Africa at the very bottom and read only that page. Next week we come back to where we are standing: spring, and everything waking up.

Theme Song · “Sawubona, South Africa!”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The four chords

C
F
G7
Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“sa-wu — bo — na-na”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the children only hear the wave and the stripes.

Tempo: walking pace, warm and swinging, with a big rest before “Six bright colours” so the last line lands on its own. The one new shape this week is Am — one finger, second fret, top string, and it is the easiest shape on the instrument after C. It appears once per chorus, on the mountain line, and the verses do not use it at all.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CSawubona, sawubona — FSouth CAfrica! (a big two-handed wave)

FBlack and Cwhite zebra — G7ZA-ZA-CZA! (stripe both hands down your body)

AmA flat-top mountain Ftouching the Csky, (one flat hand held high overhead)

FSix bright Ccolours G7flying Chigh! (wave the flag)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who waves, stripes their body and holds one flat hand over their head has understood the week, whether or not a single word comes out.

One verse a day — every verse is just C and G7

Monday · Flag

CSix bright colours in one flag, (six fingers, then the flag)

G7South Africa — Cwave your flag! (wave it right over your head)

Tuesday · Zebra

CZebra, zebra — black and white, (stripes down your front)

G7Stripey, stripey — Cwhat a sight! (hands wide, eyes wide)

Wednesday · Mountain

CTable Mountain, flat on top, (flat hands out level, held high)

G7Climb up, climb up — Cthen STOP! (climb, then freeze dead still)

Thursday · Hello

CSawubona means hello, (a big wave, then both hands open)

G7Say it softly — Csoft and slow! (drop to a whisper, wave very slowly)

Friday · Zig-zag

CZebra, zebra — zig-zag-zoo, (zig-zag one finger through the air)

G7South Africa Csays hello to you! (a big wave to the whole room)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — and Friday's finale, sung standing in one circle with the drum underneath it, doubles as the review of the whole week. Friday's verse is also the week's z sound, three times over: zebra, zig-zag, zoo.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the whole verse, and the chorus adds one shape only — Am, one finger on the top string at the second fret, which is the same finger you already use for F, just moved. C is one finger, Am is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the drum and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the wave and the stripes carry the meaning either way.

Theme Shelf · South Africa

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Six-colour flag collage
from MONDAY's Magic Box		Six strips of paper — black, gold, green, white, red, blue — a glue stick, and a flag template with the colour blocks drawn on it. Control: the template's own colour blocks, so a strip in the wrong place looks wrong before anybody says so. Draw the template yourself from the real flag in the Magic Box — it is deliberately not printed in this pack, and drawing it in front of them is half the lesson. Language: “six · colours · black · gold · green · white · red · blue.”
Tray 2 · Zebra stripes
from TUESDAY		A card zebra outline with a printed stripe pattern along its back, plus black and white clip pegs in a bowl. Clip them round the card, alternating, to match the pattern. Control: the printed stripes underneath — a wrong peg covers the wrong colour. Fine motor, pattern, and the whole of Tuesday in one tray. Bigs: “black, white, black, white — what comes next?”
Tray 3 · Table Mountain building
from WEDNESDAY		Wooden blocks and a card with a flat-topped mountain silhouette on it, stood upright behind the work. Build the mountain against the silhouette until it matches. Control: the silhouette — too pointy and it sticks out over the top. Beside it the white cloth for the tablecloth cloud, and the “Draw MY flat-top mountain” sheets (page 16) with crayons.
Tray 4 · Bead a bracelet + 3-part cards
practical life, from THURSDAY		Large wooden beads, laces with a stiffened end, and a pattern card laid beside the string: red · yellow · blue · red · yellow · blue. Control: the pattern card itself — lay the finished bracelet along it and the colours either line up or they do not. Beside it the 3-part cards (pages 10–11): South Africa 南非 · zebra 斑马 · mountain 山 · flag 旗 · hello 你好 — Littles match picture→picture on the whole control card, Bigs lay the labels underneath. And the 12 sorting cards (page 14) under the two signs (pages 12–13): 在山上 or 在草地上? And the action cards (page 15) face down: draw one, do it, say it — “I can zig-zag!”

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can — the cards get handled all week. The row of 汉字 cards the class built up (旗 · 马 · 山 · 好 · 南) stays pinned along the week's paper strip — that is the display, no extra work needed. The real flag, the beaded bracelet and Teacher Tredoux's photographs come down on Friday and go home with him; everything else stays out for next week's spring.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Mar 22–26 · Theme: One Country — South Africa · Sound of the week: z

What we did

We zoomed in from a whole continent to one country: South Africa, where Teacher Tredoux is from. On Monday a real flag came out of the Magic Box and we counted its colours together — there are six — and then a finger travelled all the way down the map to the very bottom point and stopped there. On Tuesday a striped cloth came out of the box and kept coming, longer and longer, and we learned that no two zebras in the world have the same stripes, exactly like the faces in our circle. On Wednesday we built Table Mountain out of blocks and discovered that it is flat on top like a table, with a cloud that lies along it like a tablecloth. On Thursday a beaded bracelet made by hand in KwaZulu-Natal went round the circle, and we learned to say hello in Zulu: Sawubona — which does not really mean “hello” at all, it means “I see you.” And on Friday Teacher Tredoux brought in his own photographs from home — his town, his road, his family — and every child greeted every other child by name, three times over, in three languages.

Sentences to listen for at home

“This is South Africa.” · “The zebra is black and white.” · “The mountain is flat on top.” · “Sawubona means hello.” · “Sawubona, Mama!”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Zebra!” with stripes drawn down the body, or “Sawubona!” with a big two-handed wave, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Sawubona, South Africa!”

“Sawubona, sawubona — South Africa! Black and white zebra — ZA-ZA-ZA! A flat-top mountain touching the sky, six bright colours flying high!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — flag, zebra, mountain, hello, zig-zag. Ask your child to sing the chorus and watch their hands: they wave with both arms, stripe their body, and hold one flat hand right over their head for the mountain.

Dark Phonics · the z sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met z — a buzzing bee sound, made with the voice on, right at the front of the mouth: zip, zoo, buzz. Our silly line for it is “zippy zebra, zzz!” and it could not have landed in a better week — our animal of the week is the zebra, and Friday's verse says it three times: zebra, zig-zag, zoo. Put a finger on your throat and buzz: z buzzes, s does not. That is the whole difference between them.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · Ask your child to greet you in three languages, all in one go: 你好 · hello · sawubona. They know all three by Friday and they love the third one.
2 · Find something in the house that is flat on top and something that is pointy on top, and name them in English.
3 · Count to six in English, on your fingers, and then find six colours in one room.
4 · Hunt for the z buzz with a finger on the throat: zip, zoo, buzz, zebra, zig-zag.
5 · Sing the chorus in the car. It has three actions and one very loud ZA-ZA-ZA.

春分, the spring equinox, fell on Sunday 21 March — day and night exactly the same length — so on Friday we stood a raw egg on its end, 春分立蛋, and it took eleven tries. Next week we come home again: spring, and the life cycle of animals — eggs, chicks, caterpillars and butterflies.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
