W1I'm Special / I Like MyselfSep 1–4

Summary of the week

This week we began our very first topic, "All About Me", with the question that starts everything: who am I? We looked at our own faces in the mirror, said our own names out loud, and found out what makes each one of us different from everybody else. Our five words this week were: me, like, can, happy, smile. The little ones needed only one word and an action — "Me!" with a thumb at the chest. The big ones built whole sentences: "My name is ___. I like ___. I can ___!"

Day by day

On Tuesday we passed a mirror round the circle and clapped each child's name in syllables. On Wednesday we sorted things into yummy and yuck, and everybody said "I like ___". On Thursday we found out what our bodies can do — jump, run, clap, spin — and froze on the beat. On Friday a paper heart went round the circle, and whoever held it got the spotlight while the whole class pointed and chanted their name: "You are SPECIAL!"

Meipian

What a wonderful first week in Whale Class! We started our topic "All About Me" and learned the words me, like, can, happy and smile. The children looked in the mirror, said their own names to the whole class, and showed us what their bodies can do. We sang our new song "Special Me" every morning and added a fresh verse each day. Our favourite game was "Stand up if…". By Friday they were all chanting: "I am ME! I am SPECIAL!"

W2My BodySep 7–11

Summary of the week

This week our topic was "My Body". We looked at ourselves from head to toe, named the parts we could see, and then talked about the busy parts hiding inside us — the heart that beats, the bones that hold us up. Our five words this week were: body, hands, feet, jump, heart. The little ones answered with one word and a big action — "Hands!", wave them; "Heart!", pat your chest. The big ones used full sentences: "This is my ___. I have two ___. My body helps me ___."

Day by day

On Monday one child lay down on a long sheet of paper, we traced right round them, and we labelled every body part together. On Tuesday we ran three stations — jump, bend, stretch — and cheered each other on. On Wednesday we ran hard and then felt our own hearts going boom boom, practised big breaths, and sorted inside from outside. On Thursday every child took the spotlight and told us what they like about their body. On Friday we made our body booklets and raced through "Touch your ___!"

Meipian

This week Whale Class explored our bodies! We learned the words body, hands, feet, jump and heart, and used them in songs, games and Simon Says. The children traced a giant body outline on paper and labelled the head, hands, feet, arms and tummy all by themselves. They ran fast and then felt their own hearts beating, which was very exciting. Every morning we sang "My Body, My Body" and added a new verse. By Friday everyone could shout: "This is my body!"

W3My 5 SensesSep 14–18

Summary of the week

This week our topic was "My 5 Senses". The big idea was a simple one: I have five different ways of knowing the world, and this week I am going to use every single one of them on purpose. Our five words this week were: see, hear, touch, smell, taste. The little ones needed one word and a gesture — "See!", two fingers to the eyes; "Hear!", cup both ears. The big ones spoke in full sentences: "I see a red apple. I hear a bell. I touch something soft."

Day by day

On Monday we hunted colours and tiny things with a magnifying glass and played I spy. On Tuesday we guessed sounds hidden behind a cloth — a bell, keys, crumpling paper. On Wednesday we reached into a mystery feely bag, then guessed sink or float before dropping each thing into the water. On Thursday we sniffed jars of orange peel, mint and ginger and ran to find the real object. On Friday we tasted lemon, honey and a cracker, and shouted out which sense we were using.

Meipian

Whale Class used all five senses this week! We learned the words see, hear, touch, smell and taste, one sense every day. The children hunted colours with a magnifying glass, guessed mystery sounds behind a cloth, reached into a feely bag, and matched jars of orange, mint and ginger. Our favourite activity was the sink-or-float experiment — guess first, then test it! We sang "Two Little Eyes" daily and on Friday sang all five verses together. Ask your child what they can hear right now.

W4My FeelingSep 21–24

Summary of the week

This week our topic was "My Feeling". The message we repeated every single day was that every feeling is allowed: I can name it, I can show it on my face, and then I can let it go again. Our five words this week were: happy, sad, angry, scared, calm. The little ones matched one word to one face — "Happy!" with a huge smile; "Sad!" with a finger drawing a tear. The big ones used whole sentences: "I feel happy today. He is sad — I can help. I am angry, I need a big breath."

Day by day

On Monday we made our happy faces at a hand mirror and copied each other's. On Tuesday a real onion made even the teacher cry, and we passed a sad puppet round the circle so everybody could do one kind thing for it. On Wednesday we blew a balloon up as full as an angry tummy, then let the air out slowly and breathed the anger away. On Thursday we watched glitter settle in a calm jar, walked with lanterns for 中秋节 and shared mooncakes.

Meipian

This week Whale Class talked about feelings. We learned the words happy, sad, angry, scared and calm, and practised showing each one on our faces. The children cried real tears at an onion, blew a balloon up like an angry tummy and let it out slowly, and watched glitter settle in our calm jar. We also celebrated 中秋节 with mooncakes and lanterns. Our song was "How Do You Feel Today?" Ask your child to show you their angry face, and then their big calming breath.

W5Autumn (1)Sep 28 – Oct 9

Summary of the week

This week our topic was "Autumn". The big idea was that the tree outside is changing colour — and that it keeps on changing even while we are away on holiday. Our five words this week were: autumn, leaf, red, yellow, windy. The little ones answered with one word and an action — "Leaf!", flutter a hand down to the floor; "Windy!", whoosh both arms and sway. The big ones used full sentences: "It is autumn. The leaf is red. The wind blows the leaves down."

Day by day

On Monday we photographed a branch that was only just turning gold. On Tuesday a whole basket of leaves was tipped over the circle, and we became leaves ourselves and spun to the floor on the word windy. On Wednesday we hunted five red things, waved the 红旗 and said goodbye to the tree before the 国庆 holiday. On Thursday, back at school, we held Monday's photograph up beside the real branch and named everything that had changed. On Friday we fanned the whole basket of leaves across the mat.

Meipian

Autumn arrived in Whale Class! We learned the words autumn, leaf, red, yellow and windy. Before the holiday we photographed a branch that was just turning gold, and when we came back the children compared the photo with the real tree and named everything that had changed. They sorted leaves by colour, spun to the floor like falling leaves, and fanned a basket of leaves right across the mat. We sang "Autumn Is Here!" every morning. Ask your child what happened to the tree while they were away.

W6Autumn (2)Oct 12–16

Summary of the week

This week we stayed with autumn, and looked at what the season gives away. The tree gives up its leaves, the weather gives us cold mornings, and the field gives us food. Our five words this week were: tree, fall, cold, harvest, climb. The little ones used one word and a big action — "Cold!", hug yourself and shiver; "Fall!", drop your arms. The big ones spoke in sentences: "The tree is bare. It is cold today. I can climb high."

Day by day

On Monday we turned ourselves into trees and dropped our branches one by one. On Tuesday we threw leaves high and caught them before they landed. On Wednesday we raced to dress for a cold morning — scarf, hat, glove. On Thursday we put our hands into the harvest basket and named corn, chestnuts and a persimmon by feel alone. On Friday we climbed high for 重阳节, read 王维's poem at the top, and carried the 重阳糕 down to the grandparents instead of eating it ourselves.

Meipian

Whale Class finished autumn this week. We learned the words tree, fall, cold, harvest and climb. The children became trees and dropped their branches one by one, caught falling leaves in the air, and raced to dress for a cold morning. They reached into a harvest basket and named corn, chestnuts and persimmons just by feeling them. For 重阳节 we climbed high, read a poem at the top, and gave our 重阳糕 to the grandparents. W7Five Food GroupsOct 19–23

Summary of the week

This week our topic was "Five Food Groups". The big idea was that food comes in five families, and that a good plate wants some of each of them. Our five words this week were: fruit, vegetable, grain, milk, egg. The little ones named one food and mimed eating it — "Egg!", crack and stir. The big ones sorted and explained: "An egg is protein. I eat fruit every day. Milk helps my bones grow strong."

Day by day

On Monday a basket of real fruit came round the circle to be smelled, named and passed on. On Tuesday we shelled fresh bean pods into a bowl and counted them in. On Wednesday we hunted rice, corn, bread and noodles hidden all round the room. On Thursday we touched a frost-furred spoon straight from the freezer for 霜降 and ran food cards to the right group. On Friday we built a food pyramid on the mat and voted thumbs up or thumbs down on every food.

Meipian

Food week in Whale Class! We learned the words fruit, vegetable, grain, milk and egg, and met all five food groups with real food in our hands. The children passed a fruit basket around the circle, shelled bean pods into a bowl, hunted for grains around the room, and built a big food pyramid on the mat together. For 霜降 we touched a frosty spoon straight from the freezer. We sang "Five Food Groups" every day. Ask your child which group an egg belongs to!

Summary of the week

This week our topic was "Healthy Food and Healthy Habits". The children learned to sort food into two baskets: food for every day, and food that is only for sometimes. On Wednesday we washed a muddy apple before anybody was allowed to eat it, and scrubbed our hands for a whole twenty seconds. On Thursday we brushed a giant sticky tooth until every surface was clean. On Friday we sliced fruit, gave every piece away, and said one big BOO for Halloween.

W11The Cycle of PlantsNov 16–20

Summary of the week

This week our topic was "The Cycle of Plants". The whole week was really one single idea: a seed becomes a whole plant, and the plant then makes seeds all over again. Our five words this week were: seed, root, leaf, flower, fruit. The little ones used one word and an action — "Seed!", curl up small; "Flower!", open your hands. The big ones put the cycle in order: "The seed grows roots. The flower makes fruit. The fruit has seeds inside."

Day by day

On Monday we sorted seeds from pebbles and buttons, and put a bean in a jar to watch all week. On Tuesday we pulled a whole plant out of its pot so everybody could see the roots, then became trees ourselves. On Wednesday we went on a leaf hunt in the cold and compared what we brought back. On Thursday we opened like flowers, as slowly as we possibly could. On Friday we cut an apple sideways and found the star and the seeds hiding inside.

Meipian

Whale Class watched things grow this week! Our words were seed, root, leaf, flower and fruit. A bean in a jar sat on our shelf and grew a little every day — the children checked it each morning and were amazed by the roots. We pulled a whole plant out of its pot to look underneath, hunted for leaves in the cold, and cut an apple sideways to find the star and the seeds hiding inside. Our song was "A Little Seed". Seed to seed, all week long.

On Wednesday we built a turkey out of feathers, naming something we were thankful for with every feather. On Thursday one snack was shared out in front of everybody — please, thank you, you're welcome, done for real. On Friday we hung leaves on our class gratitude tree; nearly every one said mama or baba.

The children roared like lions, walked heavily like elephants, drummed a big boom-ba-boom rhythm, and fanned themselves in the pretend heat. Every morning they found Africa on the map before we began. Our song was "Africa Is Hot!" Next week we zoom in further, to Teacher Tredoux's own country: South Africa.
