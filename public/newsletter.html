This week our topic was "My House". The big idea was that a house is made of rooms, that every room has a job to do, and that everything in it has a place to go back to. Next week we go outside to the garden: seeds, roots and the life cycle of plants.

This week our topic was "The Cycle of Plants". The whole week was really one single idea: a seed becomes a whole plant, and the plant then makes seeds all over again. Our five words this week were: seed, root, leaf, flower, fruit. The little ones used one word and an action — "Seed!", curl up small; "Flower!", open your hands. Page 1 — Welcome letter (lower wide box) ~600 chars, 10pt

We pulled a whole plant out of its pot so everybody could see the roots, went on a leaf hunt in the cold, and cut an apple sideways to find the star and the seeds hiding inside it. Our song was "A Little Seed". Next week is Thanksgiving: saying thank you, and passing things on.

This week our topic was "Chinese New Year". We brought the biggest festival of the year right into the classroom and spent the whole week saying Happy New Year to each other. Friday was 除夕 and 春节 falls on Saturday. Our song was "Gong Xi, Happy New Year!" We wish every Whale Class family a very happy Spring Festival — see you after the holiday!

Welcome back! This week our topic was "The Seven Continents". The big idea was that our round blue world has seven big pieces of land on it — and that one of them is ours. Our five words this week were: world, map, seven, continent, Asia. The little ones counted and pointed — "Seven!" on seven fingers. The big ones answered the question of the week: "I live in Asia. The world is round. There are seven continents."

Page 1 — Welcome letter (lower wide box) ~600 chars, 10pt

The children spun a globe and stopped it with one finger to call out land or water, sat on a map big enough to hold the whole class, ran to the continent we called out, and counted all seven on their fingers every single morning. By Friday every child could say where we live. Our song was "Seven Big Continents". Next week we look at the blue part of the map: the five oceans.

This week our topic was "The Five Oceans". The big idea was that most of our round world is not land at all but water — salty, blue, and completely full of fish. Our five words this week were: ocean, water, fish, blue, boat. The little ones splashed and named — "Fish!", wiggle a hand away. The big ones used sentences: "The ocean is blue and salty. The fish swims under the water. The boat floats on top."

Page 1 — Welcome letter (lower wide box) ~600 chars, 10pt

The children tasted sea water and pulled a great many faces about how salty it was, spun the globe until a finger landed on water, went fishing with magnets, and worked out all over again what floats and what sinks. We counted the five oceans on five blue ribbons every morning. Our song was "Five Blue Oceans". Next week we choose one continent and zoom right in — and we have chosen Africa.

Theme: One Continent — Africa. This week's words: Africa, lion, elephant, hot, drum. Littles built these into single words and actions: "Africa!" (pat the floor twice); "ROAR!" (big open mouth); "Hot!" (fan your face)

Big Group (4–6 years old kids)

Theme: One Continent — Africa. Bigs practiced full sentences such as: "This is Africa."; "The lion is big."; "It is very hot in Africa."

Weekly Newsletter MAR. 2027. Week 25. 3.15-3.19

Page 1 — Welcome letter (upper box) ~500 chars, 10pt

This week we chose one continent out of the seven and zoomed right in on it. We chose Africa — because Teacher Tredoux comes from Africa, from South Africa, and we said so out loud every single day. Our five words this week were: Africa, lion, elephant, hot, drum. The little ones roared and stamped — "Africa!", pat the floor twice; "Hot!", fan your face. The big ones used sentences: "This is Africa. The lion is big. It is very hot in Africa."

Page 1 — Welcome letter (lower wide box) ~600 chars, 10pt

The children roared like lions, walked heavily like elephants, drummed a boom-ba-boom rhythm on real drums, fanned themselves in the heat, and found Africa on the map every single morning before we began. Knowing that it is their own teacher's home made the whole continent feel close. Our song was "Africa Is Hot!" Next week we zoom in one step further, to Teacher Tredoux's own country: South Africa.
