Wash, Brush, Crunch — Every Day!

Week of Oct 26–30 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

healthywaterwashbrushshare

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Water!” (drink from a cup, long “aaah”), “Wash!” (rub both hands together), “Brush!” (brush up and down on your own teeth).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “An apple is healthy.” · “Sweets are sometimes food.” · “I wash my hands before I eat.” · “I share my food with my friend.”

Day 1 · Every Day or Sometimes?

Today's words: healthy

Grab: one washed apple and one lollipop in the Magic Box; two hoops (or a green plate and a yellow plate); the 12 food cards from print pack page 14; a tea towel to polish the apple.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake it — something rolls and something rattles. Two things! Sniff the lid.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek. Gasp. Peek again. Lift out the apple in one hand and the lollipop in the other and hold them far apart.

Teacher“Two foods. One is for every day… and one is only for sometimes.” (look from one hand to the other, very slowly)

Polish the apple on your jumper. “Apple!”

4 min Teach · Healthy

Apple up high, in two hands, so every child can see it.

An apple is healthy. (hold it up)

Healthy food makes me strong. (strong arms, growl)

I eat it every day. (five fingers, one at a time)

CRUNCH! (take a huge bite — let them hear it)

Now the lollipop. Hold it up the same way, wait, and let them tell you.

Teacher“Every day? … or sometimes?”

Littles say the one word “Healthy!” with strong arms — that is a full win at 2 and 3. Bigs say the frame: “An apple is healthy.” and “Sweets are sometimes food.”

Teacher fails: announce that the lollipop is “a lovely healthy breakfast”, start unwrapping it and look delighted. Do not rush. Let them shout you down twice before you sadly put it away.

3 min Song · Every Day, Every Day — chorus + Monday verse

Crunch, crunch, crunch — an apple a day! (big bite, then CRUNCH)

Water in my cup — hip hip hooray! (drink, then both hands up)

Sweeties are for sometimes, not for every day, (waggle one finger, slowly)

Wash and brush and share — hooray! (rub hands · brush teeth · both hands out)

Monday's verse: Healthy food makes me strong, (strong arms)

Eat it up all day long! (five big pretend bites)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. The last line of the chorus already teaches three of this week's five words with their gestures; do the actions from day one even if they only sing the CRUNCH.

3 min Game · Every day or sometimes?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two hoops on the carpet: a green one — every day — and a yellow one — sometimes. Cards face down in a pile.

Teacher“Take a card. Run it to the right hoop!”

Each child takes one card, names it, and runs it to a hoop. The class calls the verdict together: “Every DAY!” or “Sometimes.”

Twelve cards, so everyone gets a turn or two. Bigs owe you a sentence — “Broccoli is healthy.” Littles just run and drop, and shout the word with the class.

Put one card in the wrong hoop yourself, whistling. They will fix it before you stand up.

1 min Close

Sitting. Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

It is the whole week in one line, and they will chant it on Monday without knowing what half of it means yet. By Friday they will. Got 3 spare minutes? The Very Hungry Caterpillar (Eric Carle) — on the Saturday page, ask each food: “Every day? Or sometimes?”

Day 2 · Water in My Cup

Today's words: water (healthy comes back every day)

Grab: a bottle of water and a bottle of fizzy drink in the Magic Box; a bag of sugar cubes (count ten out beforehand and hide them in your pocket); a clear cup, a small jug, a tray; five bottles for the game — water, milk, juice, fizzy drink, sports drink.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake — this one sloshes. Hold it to your ear and slosh it again.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out the water bottle. Then the fizzy bottle. Stand them side by side on the tray, in the middle, where everybody can see — and beside the fizzy one, the little stack of sugar cubes you counted out this morning.

Teacher“Two drinks. Which one do I drink every day?” (hand hovers over each in turn — wait)

“Water!” Everyone drinks from a pretend cup with a long “aaah”.

4 min Teach · Water

Pour water from the jug into the clear cup, slowly, so they hear it. Hold it up to the window.

Water is clear. (hold the cup up to the light)

Water is cool. (touch the cup to your cheek, shiver)

I drink my water… (one long drink)

Aaaaah! (whole body relaxes)

Every child gets a real sip if you have cups — it takes ten seconds each and it is the whole lesson. Littles say “Water!” and drink; Bigs say “I drink my water.”

Now the fizzy bottle. Take the sugar cubes out of your pocket and stack them one at a time beside it, counting out loud together: “One… two… three…” all the way to ten. Everybody gasps at the tower.

Teacher fails: “I am thirsty! I will just eat these ten sugar lumps for my breakfast.” Lift one towards your mouth. Wait for the roar, then put it down and drink the water instead.

3 min Song · Every Day, Every Day — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — then yesterday's verse if they shout for it — then today's new one:

Tuesday's verse: Water, water, clear and cool, (cup hands, look into them)

Fill my cup at home and school! (pour with one hand, drink with the other)

On “hip hip hooray” in the chorus, everyone's cup hands go straight up over the head. That is the loudest bar of the week.

3 min Game · Count the sugar cubes

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Five bottles in the middle, sugar cubes in a dish, a numbered card face down under each bottle.

Teacher“How much sugar is hiding in this one?”

A child stacks the cubes in front of one bottle while the class counts. Then turn the card over and check together. Water: zero — everybody cheers. Fizzy drink: a tower — everybody gasps.

Five bottles: water, milk, juice, fizzy drink, sports drink. Then everyone drinks a cup of water together.

Littles do the counting with their fingers up in the air, not with the cubes. Bigs read the number card themselves and say “The fizzy drink has ten.”

1 min Close

Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? Water Is Water (Miranda Paul) — don't read every word, just turn the pages and say “Water!” together on each one.

Day 3 · Wash Your Hands

Today's words: wash (healthy · water come back)

Grab: a bar of soap, a bottle of bubbles and one muddy apple in the Magic Box (roll a washed apple in a little soil before circle); a bowl of water, a nail brush, a towel; the five hand-washing cards for the shelf tray; a 20-second sand timer if you have one.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake — a soft, heavy thud. Sniff the lid, close your eyes, sniff again: it smells clean.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes the bar of soap. Pass it round the circle so every child can smell it. Then reach in again — and lift out the muddy apple, holding it away from you between two fingers.

Teacher“Oh dear. Can I eat this?” (hold it up, turn it slowly, let them see the dirt)

“NO!” Ask what we have to do first. Somebody will get there: “Wash!” Everybody rubs both hands together.

4 min Teach · Wash

Bowl of water in the middle, soap beside it, towel on your shoulder. Wash the apple in front of them, properly, taking your time.

Wet the apple. (dunk it, both hands)

Wash, wash, wash! (rub it round and round)

Rinse it, dry it… (towel, slowly)

NOW I can eat it! (huge CRUNCH — the water is dirty and the apple is shining)

Now your hands, the same way, and name the five steps as you go: wet · soap · scrub · rinse · dry. Hold up a finger for each one.

Littles say “Wash!” and rub both hands together — that is the win. Bigs say the frame: “I wash my hands before I eat.”

汉字 洗. And the 儿歌 everybody already half-knows — sing it in Chinese while you scrub: 《洗手歌》搓搓手心，搓搓手背，洗得干干净净。

Teacher fails: put your hands in the water for half a second, flick them, and reach for the apple. “Done!” Let them stop you. Then do it properly while they count.

3 min Song · Every Day, Every Day — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — today lean hard on “wash and brush and share” and do the hand-rub big — then:

Wednesday's verse: Bubbles, bubbles, wash them away, (rub hands, then flick the bubbles off)

Germs go down the drain — bye-bye today! (wave down at the floor)

This verse is also your timer. One chorus + one verse, sung at normal speed, is about twenty seconds — which is exactly how long a pair of hands needs to be scrubbed. Tell them that; they love owning a rule.

3 min Game · Bubble hand-wash

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Blow a cloud of bubbles over the circle. Every child catches one on their palms — and now their hands are “full of germs”.

Teacher“Scrub! Don't stop until the song stops!”

Everybody scrubs — palms, backs, between the fingers, thumbs, nails — for one whole chorus. That is twenty seconds, and that is the rule. When the song stops, hands go up: “CLEAN!”

Three rounds. Round two, a Big leads the scrubbing and calls out the part: “Now the thumbs!” Round three, everybody does it with their eyes shut.

Then walk to the real basin as a class and do it once with real soap and real water, singing. That walk is the lesson — it is the habit they take home.

1 min Close

Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? Wash Your Hands! (Tony Ross) — the Little Princess will not wash, and they will tell her off on every page.

Day 4 · Brush, Brush, Brush

Today's words: brush (healthy · water · wash all come back)

Grab: a new toothbrush in the Magic Box and a giant model tooth (or a big white sponge, or an egg box painted white) smeared sticky with a little jam or honey the night before; a big brush or nail brush to clean it; a mirror; a spare toothbrush per child if you have them; 汉字 card 牙.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake — a light, hollow clatter. Sniff: mint!

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes the toothbrush. Wave it about. Then lift out the giant tooth — sticky, dull, and disgusting — and hold it up in silence until somebody says something.

Teacher“This tooth ate the lollipop on Monday… and nobody brushed it.” (turn it slowly, sadly)

Everybody brushes their own teeth in the air, up and down. “Brush!”

4 min Teach · Brush

Toothbrush up high so everyone can see the bristles. Then your own teeth, hugely, in the mirror.

Up and down… (brush up and down on the top teeth)

and round and round… (little circles)

Front and back and all around! (open wide, get the back ones)

Spit! Rinse! Shiny! (big grin, show every tooth)

Every child brushes their own teeth in the air with you, twice: once in the morning, once at night — hold up one finger, then two.

Littles say “Brush!” with the up-and-down action. Bigs say “I brush my teeth in the morning and at night.”

汉字 牙 — hold the card up beside your own grin.

Teacher fails: brush your ear. Thoroughly, with great concentration. Then your nose. Then your elbow. Keep going until they are screaming at you and pointing at their own mouths.

3 min Song · Every Day, Every Day — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — then today's, which is the only verse that starts on the round little Am:

Thursday's verse: Up and down and round and round, (brush up and down, then circles)

Brush my teeth — what a shiny sound! (huge grin on “shiny”)

Sing this one a shade slower than the rest — Am wants it. Then finish the last chorus at full speed so nobody goes back to work sleepy.

3 min Game · Brush the giant teeth

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The sticky tooth goes in the middle on a tray, with the big brush beside it.

Teacher“Every sticky bit must come clean. Where is there still some?”

One child brushes while the class directs: “The top!” “The BACK!” “In the middle!” When they think it is done, everyone inspects it, and somebody always finds one more sticky patch.

Then all together, in the air, for the length of one verse: up and down, round and round, front and back.

Bigs can count the strokes to twenty. Littles just brush and shout the word. If you have a toothbrush each, this is the day to send them home clean.

1 min Close

Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? Brush Your Teeth, Please (Leslie McGuire) — a pull-tab book; let a different child brush each animal.

Day 5 · Slice & Share 🎃

Today's words: healthy · water · wash · brush · share — the whole week

Grab: the fruit basket to share and one small pumpkin lantern in the Magic Box, plus every prop from the week; a child-safe apple wedger, a board, a jug of water to rinse and a serving dish; the “My Healthy Day” sheets (print pack page 16) and the awards (page 17).

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out fat, heavy and smelling of fruit. Everyone leans in.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL! What did we learn this week?”

Pull the props out one at a time and hold each up in silence — they will supply the word before you can.

Apple → “Healthy!”  Water bottle → “Water!”  Soap → “Wash!”  Toothbrush → “Brush!”  Lollipop → “Sometimes!”

Last out: the fruit basket — and one small pumpkin lantern. Don't explain it yet. Set it at the edge of the mat where they can all see it and carry on.

4 min Teach · Share

Wash your hands first, in front of them, all five steps — Wednesday's habit, used for real. Then the fruit basket in both hands. Don't eat any. Turn to the child beside you and hold it out.

One for me… (hand on your own chest)

and one for you. (hold the basket out with both hands)

I share my food. (both hands push the basket forward)

Thank you! (小手合十 — hands together, small bow)

The basket goes all the way round the circle, hand to hand, and nobody eats yet. Every child says “Share!” as they pass it on; Bigs say “I share my food with my friend.”

Teacher fails: take the basket back, sit down and start eating the whole lot yourself with your back to them. Wait. Being told off for not sharing is the best English lesson of the week.

3 min Song · Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: One for me and one for you, (hand on chest, then hand out)

Share the fruit — that's what we do! (both hands offer the basket)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, top to bottom, with the fruit on the mat and nobody allowed to touch it until the last chord. They learned it one verse a morning without noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · Slice and share

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Board, wedger, jug and dish in the middle. Hands washed. Rinse each apple, then press the wedger down — eight pieces every time, and the click is very satisfying.

Teacher“Take a piece — and give it to somebody else.”

Nobody eats their own piece. Each child takes one, turns to a friend, holds it out with both hands and says “Share!” — or, for a Big, “This is for you.” Then everybody eats at the same moment.

Now the pumpkin. Tomorrow is 万圣节 — Halloween — a festival other children have, where they carve a pumpkin and pretend to be a little bit scary. Light it (or switch it on), turn the lights down, and once — only once, all together:

Everyone“BOO!” (hands up like claws, then everybody laughs and it is over)

One pumpkin, one BOO, and back to the fruit. It is a nod, not a party — the week belongs to washing, brushing and sharing.

1 min Close + the take-home sheet

Hand out the “My Healthy Day” sheets: every child traces the cup, the toothbrush and the soap, draws the every-day food they love on the plate, you scribe their line, and it goes home today with an award.

Everyone holds their sheet up. Whisper → normal → shout, with the actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Doorbell Rang (Pat Hutchins) — more and more friends arrive and the biscuits get shared again and again. Count them out loud together.

Theme Song · “Every Day, Every Day”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The four chords

C
F
G7
Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“crunch — crunch — crunch-a”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: bright and bouncy for the first two lines, then lean back on the Am — that is the “sometimes” line and it wants to sound a little sorry for itself — then drive hard into the last line, which is the one that teaches wash · brush · share. Am is one finger, the same finger C uses, just moved across. Chant it without the uke first; add chords once they know the words.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CCrunch, crunch, crunch — an Fapple a day! C(big bite, then CRUNCH)

FWater in my Ccup — hip hip G7hooray! C(drink, then both hands up)

CSweeties are for sometimes, Fnot for every Amday, (waggle one finger, slowly)

FWash and Cbrush and G7share — hooCray! (rub hands · brush teeth · both hands out)

Sing the chorus twice each time. On every CRUNCH the whole class takes a huge bite of the air — and the last line does three of the week's five words with their gestures, every single circle.

One verse a day — verses use C, G7 and one Am

Monday · Healthy

CHealthy food makes me strong, (strong arms)

G7Eat it up Call day long! (five big pretend bites)

Tuesday · Water

CWater, water, clear and cool, (cup hands, look into them)

G7Fill my cup Cat home and school! (pour with one hand, drink with the other)

Wednesday · Wash

CBubbles, bubbles, wash them away, (rub hands, then flick the bubbles off)

G7Germs go down the drain — Cbye-bye today! (wave down at the floor)

Thursday · Brush

AmUp and down and round and round, (brush up and down, then circles)

G7Brush my teeth — Cwhat a shiny sound! (huge grin on “shiny”)

Friday · Share

COne for me and one for you, (hand on chest, then hand out)

G7Share the fruit — Cthat's what we do! (both hands offer the basket)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — Friday's finale is the whole thing top to bottom with the fruit on the mat, and it doubles as your review of the entire week. One chorus and one verse is also almost exactly twenty seconds, which is Wednesday's hand-washing rule.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the whole chorus, and it is the same three shapes as every Whale Class song so far. The only new one is Am — one finger on the G string, second fret, and it appears exactly twice: once in the chorus and once on Thursday. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and clap the chorus instead: the class will not notice, and the CRUNCH lands either way.

Theme Shelf · Healthy Food & Healthy Habits

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Every day / sometimes sorting
from MONDAY's apple and lollipop		Every day! / Sometimes signs + the food cards (pages 12–14) on a green plate and a yellow plate. Child lays out both signs and sorts every card. Control: a green dot on the back of the six every-day cards, yellow on the six sometimes. Language: “Broccoli is healthy.” Littles: “Every day!” / “Sometimes.”
Tray 2 · Sugar-cube counting
from TUESDAY's Magic Box		Five drink bottles (water, milk, juice, fizzy drink, sports drink), a dish of sugar cubes and a numbered card for each. Stack the right number of cubes in front of each bottle. Control: the number card sits face-down under each bottle — turn it over and check. Bigs say “The fizzy drink has ten.”
Tray 3 · Hand-washing sequence
from WEDNESDAY's soap		Five cards — wet · soap · scrub · rinse · dry — laid out in order beside the real washstand, with a twenty-second sand timer, the soap and a towel. Control: numbers on the card backs; and the timer must run all the way out before you stop scrubbing. Add the action cards (page 15) face-down: draw one, do it, say it.
Tray 4 · Apple slicing to share
practical life — and the word share is built in		A child-safe apple wedger, a board, a small jug of water to rinse the apple and a dish to serve from. Wash hands, rinse, cut — then offer a piece to somebody else. Control: the wedger makes eight equal pieces, and the board must be wiped dry before the tray goes back. Beside it: the healthy habits 3-part cards (pages 10–11), the “My Healthy Day” sheets (page 16) and crayons. Sheets go home Friday with the award (page 17).

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can. Dot the backs of the 12 sorting cards before the tray goes out. Check your allergy list before Wednesday's apple and Friday's fruit basket. The soap, the towel and the sand timer live beside the real basin all week — that is where the habit actually gets built.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Oct 26–30 · Theme: Healthy Food & Healthy Habits · Sound of the week: o

What we did

This week the children sorted food into every day and sometimes: they stacked the sugar cubes that live inside a fizzy drink (there were a lot, and everybody gasped), washed a muddy apple before anyone was allowed to eat it, scrubbed their hands for a full twenty seconds — one whole chorus of our song, which is now the class rule — brushed a giant sticky tooth clean after it had eaten Monday's lollipop, and on Friday sliced fruit and handed every piece to somebody else. Ask them how many sugar cubes were in the fizzy bottle, and ask who they gave their apple slice to. Friday was the day before 万圣节 Halloween — one pumpkin came out of the box and we all said BOO once, and then went back to the fruit.

Sentences to listen for at home

“An apple is healthy.” · “I drink my water.” · “I wash my hands before I eat.” · “I brush my teeth every day.” · “I share my food with my friend.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Wash!” rubbing both hands together, or “Brush!” brushing their own teeth in the air, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Every Day, Every Day”

“Crunch, crunch, crunch — an apple a day! Water in my cup — hip hip hooray! Sweeties are for sometimes, not for every day, wash and brush and share — hooray!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning. Ask your child to sing the chorus — they take a huge bite of the air on CRUNCH! and do all three actions on the last line. One chorus is also twenty seconds, which is exactly how long a pair of hands needs scrubbing.

Dark Phonics · the o sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met o — the short, round vowel in the middle of a word. Open your mouth into a little o shape and say “o-o-o”, like an octopus. Our catchphrase was “hot dog on a log!”, and it was on the taps all week: the soap goes on, the tap goes off. Hot, dog, log, pot, top and on all carry the same little round sound. The trick they had to learn: o is short and quick — not the long “oh” of their own names.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · At the fridge, hold up two things: “Every day? Or sometimes?” Let your child decide.
2 · Wash your hands together and sing the chorus while you scrub — stop when the song stops, not before.
3 · Brush teeth twice, morning and night, and let them count the strokes: up and down, round and round.
4 · Hunt for the o sound: pot, dog, sock, on, off. Say it with a little round mouth.
5 · Ask them how many sugar cubes were hiding in the fizzy bottle — and who they gave their apple slice to.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
