Crunch! Every Day or Sometimes?

Week of Oct 26–30 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

healthywatercrunchysweetshare

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Crunchy!” (huge bite, then CRUNCH with the whole body), “Water!” (drink from a cup, long “aaah”), “Share!” (hold it out with both hands).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “An apple is healthy.” · “Sweets are sometimes food.” · “I share my food with my friend.” · “I can ___!”

Day 1 · Every Day or Sometimes?

Today's words: healthy

Grab: one washed apple and one lollipop in the Magic Box; two hoops (or a green plate and a yellow plate); the 12 food cards from print pack page 14; a tea towel to wipe the apple.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake it — something rolls and something rattles. Two things! Sniff the lid.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek. Gasp. Peek again. Lift out the apple in one hand and the lollipop in the other and hold them far apart.

Teacher“Two foods. One is for every day… and one is only for sometimes.” (look from one hand to the other, very slowly)

Polish the apple on your jumper. “Apple!”

4 min Teach · Healthy

Apple up high, in two hands, so every child can see it.

An apple is healthy. (hold it up)

Healthy food makes me strong. (strong arms, growl)

I eat it every day. (five fingers, one at a time)

CRUNCH! (take a huge bite — let them hear it)

Now the lollipop. Hold it up the same way, wait, and let them tell you.

Teacher“Every day? … or sometimes?”

Littles say the one word “Healthy!” with strong arms — that is a full win at 2 and 3. Bigs say the frame: “An apple is healthy.” and “Sweets are sometimes food.”

Teacher fails: announce that the lollipop is “a lovely healthy breakfast”, start unwrapping it and look delighted. Do not rush. Let them shout you down twice before you sadly put it away.

3 min Song · Every Day or Sometimes? — chorus + Monday verse

Crunch, crunch, crunch — an apple a day! (big bite, then CRUNCH)

Water in my cup — hip hip hooray! (drink, then both hands up)

Sweeties are for sometimes, not for every day, (waggle one finger, slowly)

Healthy food helps me grow and play! (strong arms, then run on the spot)

Monday's verse: Healthy food makes me strong, (strong arms)

Eat it up all day long! (five big pretend bites)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. Sing it twice; the second time they will already CRUNCH with you.

3 min Game · Every day or sometimes?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two hoops on the carpet: a green one — every day — and a yellow one — sometimes. Cards face down in a pile.

Teacher“Take a card. Run it to the right hoop!”

Each child takes one card, names it, and puts it in a hoop. The class calls the verdict together: “Every DAY!” or “Sometimes.”

Twelve cards, so everyone gets a turn or two. Bigs owe you a sentence — “Broccoli is healthy.” Littles just run and drop, and shout the word with the class.

Put one card in the wrong hoop yourself, whistling. They will fix it before you stand up.

1 min Close

Sitting, strong arms on the shout. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Healthy… healthy… HEALTHY!” (both arms up strong on the last one)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Very Hungry Caterpillar (Eric Carle) — on the Saturday page, ask each food: “Every day? Or sometimes?”

Day 2 · Water in My Cup

Today's words: water (healthy comes back every day)

Grab: a bottle of water and a bottle of fizzy drink in the Magic Box; a bag of sugar cubes (count ten out beforehand and hide them in your pocket); a clear cup, a small jug, a tray; the tea towel.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake — this one sloshes. Hold it to your ear and slosh it again.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out the water bottle. Then the fizzy bottle. Stand them side by side on the tray, in the middle, where everybody can see.

Teacher“Two drinks. Which one do I drink every day?” (hand hovers over each in turn — wait)

“Water!” Everyone drinks from a pretend cup with a long “aaah”.

4 min Teach · Water

Pour water from the jug into the clear cup, slowly, so they hear it. Hold it up to the window.

Water is clear. (hold the cup up to the light)

Water is cool. (touch the cup to your cheek, shiver)

I drink my water… (one long drink)

Aaaaah! (whole body relaxes)

Every child gets a real sip if you have cups — it takes ten seconds each and it is the whole lesson. Littles say “Water!” and drink; Bigs say “I drink my water.”

Now the fizzy bottle. Take the sugar cubes out of your pocket and stack them one at a time beside it, counting out loud together: “One… two… three…” all the way to ten. Everybody gasps at the tower.

Teacher fails: “I am thirsty! I will just eat these ten sugar lumps for my breakfast.” Lift one towards your mouth. Wait for the roar, then put it down and drink the water instead.

3 min Song · Every Day or Sometimes? — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — then yesterday's verse if they shout for it — then today's new one:

Tuesday's verse: Water, water, clear and cool, (cup hands, look into them)

Fill my cup at home and school! (pour with one hand, drink with the other)

On “hip hip hooray” in the chorus, everyone's cup hands go straight up over the head. That is the loudest bar of the week.

3 min Game · Count the sugar cubes

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Bottles in the middle, sugar cubes in a dish, a numbered card face down under each bottle.

Teacher“How much sugar is hiding in this one?”

A child stacks the cubes in front of one bottle while the class counts. Then turn the card over and check together. Water: zero — everybody cheers. Fizzy drink: a tower — everybody gasps.

Five bottles: water, milk, juice, fizzy drink, sports drink. Then everyone drinks a cup of water together.

Littles do the counting with their fingers up in the air, not with the cubes. Bigs read the number card themselves and say “The fizzy drink has ten.”

1 min Close

Cup hands to the mouth, then high over the head. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Water… water… WATER!” (both hands fly up on the last one)

Got 3 spare minutes? Water Is Water (Miranda Paul) — don't read every word, just turn the pages and say “Water!” together on each one.

Day 3 · Listen to the Crunch

Today's words: crunchy (healthy · water come back)

Grab: the crunchy tray in the Magic Box — celery, carrot, cucumber and an apple, washed and whole; one soft thing for the contrast (a marshmallow, or a slice of soft bread); a chopping board; wipes.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake — a dry, woody knock. Sniff the lid: it smells green.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek in. Don't show them yet. Reach in and snap the celery inside the closed box, right by their ears.

Teacher“Did you HEAR that?” (snap it again, louder) “That is a crunchy food!”

Everyone takes a huge pretend bite and crunches with their whole body. “Crunchy!”

4 min Teach · Crunchy

One at a time onto the board. Snap each one loudly, right in front of them, and name it.

Celery — SNAP! (break it in two)

Carrot — CRUNCH! (bite it)

Cucumber — CRUNCH! (bite it)

Apple — CRUNCH! (the loudest one of all)

All crunchy. All healthy. (strong arms)

Now the marshmallow. Bite it. Silence. Hold your ear out to the class and look confused.

Teacher“No crunch. That one is soft.”

Littles say “Crunchy!” with the big bite; Bigs say “The carrot is crunchy.” and “The marshmallow is soft.”

Teacher fails: bite the marshmallow and shout “CRUNCH!” with enormous satisfaction. Insist. Chew it again and listen hard. Let them correct you twice.

3 min Song · Every Day or Sometimes? — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — today make the CRUNCH the loudest thing in the building — then:

Wednesday's verse: Carrot, celery, apple too, (three fingers up, one per food)

CRUNCH! goes the food when I chew! (huge bite, then chew with the whole face)

Today's is the verse they will still be crunching in the corridor at home time. Let them shout it.

3 min Game · The crunch game

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody closes their eyes and cups their ears.

Teacher“Listen… what am I eating?” (bite one food, right by the circle)

Eyes open. Hands up. “Carrot!”  Bigs: “I think it is the carrot.”

Six rounds: celery, carrot, cucumber, apple, the soft marshmallow (a trick round — “No crunch!”), and one last mystery. Then a Big does the biting and the class guesses.

Any child who wants a taste gets one — that is the point of the tray. Wipes on standby, and check your allergy list before Wednesday.

1 min Close

Big pretend bite, then chew, then the shout. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Crunch… crunch… CRUNCHY!” (one enormous bite of the air)

Got 3 spare minutes? Eating the Alphabet (Lois Ehlert) — don't read the letters, just point and ask: “Crunchy? Or soft?”

Day 4 · Pack My Lunchbox

Today's words: sweet (healthy · water · crunchy all come back)

Grab: an empty lunchbox in the Magic Box; the food cards from print pack page 14 plus last week's five-food-group cards; one wrapped sweet; a basket for the relay.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake — nothing. Not a sound. Look worried and shake it again.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out the lunchbox. Open it. Turn it upside down. Nothing falls out.

Teacher“My lunchbox is empty! Who will help me fill it?” (hold it open towards the circle)

Every hand goes up. Then reach into your pocket and hold up the one wrapped sweet. Everybody says the word: “Sweet!”

4 min Teach · Sweet, and what goes in the box

The lunchbox sits open in the middle. Last week's five food groups, one at a time.

Something from the fruit… (a child puts an apple card in)

Something green and crunchy… (celery goes in)

Some bread or rice… (in it goes)

Something to make me strong… (egg or fish)

And my water! (the bottle goes in last, with a cheer)

Now hold the sweet over the open box, and wait.

Teacher“Every day? … or sometimes?”

Littles shout “Sweet!” and rub their tummy; Bigs say the frame: “Sweets are sometimes food.”

Teacher fails: fill the whole lunchbox with sweets, snap the lid shut and pat it proudly — “Perfect lunch!” Hold the pose. Wait for them to unpack you.

3 min Song · Every Day or Sometimes? — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — then today's, which is the only verse that starts on the sad little Am:

Thursday's verse: Sweeties are sweet but not every day, (waggle one finger, slowly)

Sometimes food — put it away! (both hands push something gently away)

Sing this one quietly. Four loud crunchy days need one soft verse, and Am does the work for you — you barely have to explain “sometimes”.

3 min Game · Pack my lunchbox relay

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Food cards spread at one end of the carpet, the open lunchbox at the other. Two teams, or one long line if that is calmer.

Teacher“Fetch me ONE food — and tell me its group!”

Each child runs, chooses a card, brings it back and names it: “Apple — fruit!” Bigs add the sentence “An apple is healthy.” Littles name the food and the class supplies the group.

Anyone who brings a sometimes food has to hold it up while the whole class chants “Some-TIMES!” — then it goes back in the basket, not the box.

Finish by closing the lid together and everyone shouting “Lunchtime!” — the lunchbox stays on the shelf all week.

1 min Close

Rub the tummy, then the finger waggle, then strong arms. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Sweet… sometimes… HEALTHY every day!” (both arms up strong on the last word)

Got 3 spare minutes? Lunch (Denise Fleming) — guess the food from the colour on each page before you turn it.

Day 5 · Climb High & Share · 重阳节

Today's words: healthy · water · crunchy · sweet · share — the whole week

Grab: 重阳糕 cut into small pieces on a plate, a chrysanthemum in a jar and a walking stick, all in (or beside) the Magic Box; every prop from the week; 王维's poem printed big; the “My Healthy Lunchbox” sheets (print pack page 16) and the awards (page 17); somewhere to climb — steps, a slope, the little hill outside.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out fat, rattling, and smelling of cake. Everyone leans in.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL! What did we learn this week?”

Pull the props out one at a time and hold each up in silence — they will supply the word before you can.

Apple → “Healthy!”  Water bottle → “Water!”  Celery → “Crunchy!”  Lollipop → “Sweet — sometimes!”

Last out: the chrysanthemum, the walking stick, and the 重阳糕. “重阳节!”

4 min Teach · Share

Hold the plate of cake in both hands. Don't eat any. Turn to the child beside you and hold it out.

One for me… (hand on your own chest)

and one for you. (hold the plate out with both hands)

I share my food. (both hands push the plate forward)

Thank you! (小手合十 — hands together, small bow)

The plate goes all the way round the circle, hand to hand, and nobody eats yet. Every child says “Share!” as they pass it on; Bigs say “I share my food with my friend.”

Today 重阳节 is also 老人节, Elders' Day. So the cake is not for us — we are taking it to 爷爷奶奶. Wrap a piece for each grandparent while they watch.

Teacher fails: take the plate back, sit down and eat the whole lot yourself with your back to them. Wait. Being told off for not sharing is the best English lesson of the week.

3 min Song · Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: One for me and one for you, (hand on chest, then hand out)

Share the cake — that's what we do! (both hands offer the plate)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, top to bottom, with the 重阳糕 on the mat and nobody allowed to eat it until the last chord. They learned it one verse a morning without noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · 登高 — climb high, then share

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Coats on, walking stick at the front, and up we go — the steps, the slope, or the little hill outside.

Everyone“Climb, climb, climb — up we go!” (big marching steps all the way up)

At the top, everybody out of breath, sit down and read 王维's poem together:

独在异乡为异客，

每逢佳节倍思亲。

遥知兄弟登高处，

遍插茱萸少一人。

Then ask, quietly: “谁不在这里？Who do you wish were here?” Let them answer in whichever language comes. Then down the hill, and the 重阳糕 goes round: “Share!”

Raining? Climb the school stairs instead — up two flights counting each step, and read the poem on the landing. The climb is the point, not the view.

1 min Close + lunchbox finale

Hand out the “My Healthy Lunchbox” sheets: every child draws the food they would pack, you scribe their line, and it goes home today with an award.

Everyone holds their sheet up. Whisper → normal → shout, with the actions:

Everyone“Crunchy… healthy… WATER! And I SHARE!” (big bite · strong arms · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Doorbell Rang (Pat Hutchins) — more and more friends arrive and the biscuits get shared again and again. Count them out loud together.

Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“crunch — crunch — crunch-a”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: bright and bouncy for the first two lines, then lean back on the Am — that is the “sometimes” line and it wants to sound a little sorry for itself — then drive hard into the last line. If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the whole chorus, and it is the same three shapes as every Whale Class song so far. The only new one is Am — one finger on the G string, second fret, and it appears exactly twice: once in the chorus and once on Thursday. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and clap the chorus instead: the class will not notice, and the CRUNCH lands either way.

End of Week Wrap-Up

Friday recap — read it at the last circle, then print it for the parent wall or WeChat photo.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Oct 26–30 · Theme: Healthy Food · Sound of the week: o

What we did

This week the children sorted food into every day and sometimes. On Monday an apple and a lollipop came out of the Magic Box and we ran every food card to the right hoop. On Tuesday we stacked the sugar cubes that live inside a bottle of fizzy drink — there were a lot, and everybody gasped — then we all drank a cup of water. On Wednesday we shut our eyes and listened to celery snap, and guessed each food by its crunch. On Thursday we packed a real lunchbox together, one food from each of last week's five groups, and worked out that the sweet only goes in sometimes. On Friday, for 重阳节, we climbed high (登高), read 王维's poem out of breath at the top, and shared 重阳糕 — 重阳 is also 老人节, so the cake went to the grandparents.

Words we learned

healthywatercrunchysweetshareevery daysometimessoftlunchboxstrong

Sentences to listen for at home

“An apple is healthy.” · “I drink my water.” · “The carrot is crunchy!” · “Sweets are sometimes food.” · “I share my food with my friend.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Crunchy!” with a huge bite, or “Share!” holding something out with both hands, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Every Day or Sometimes?”

“Crunch, crunch, crunch — an apple a day! Water in my cup — hip hip hooray! Sweeties are for sometimes, not for every day, healthy food helps me grow and play!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning. Ask your child to sing the chorus — they take a huge bite of the air on CRUNCH!

Dark Phonics · the o sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met o — the short, round vowel in the middle of a word. Open your mouth into a little o shape and say “o-o-o”, like an octopus. Our catchphrase was “hot dog on a log!”, which fitted this week perfectly: hot is what we say about soup, and dog, log, pot, top and on all carry the same little round sound. The trick they had to learn: o is short and quick — not the long “oh” of their own names. We read our new decodable words pot and dog in our book The ___ Has a Dog!

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · At the fridge, hold up two things: “Every day? Or sometimes?” Let your child decide.
2 · Pour a cup of water together and drink it with a long “aaah!”
3 · Give them something crunchy and listen to it together: “Crunchy!” Then something soft: “No crunch — soft.”
4 · Hunt for the d sound: drink, dinner, dog, dad. Tap it like a little drum.
5 · Ask them how many sugar cubes were hiding in the fizzy bottle — and who they gave their apple slice to.

Thank you for a lovely week — the Whale Class 🐳

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
