Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a face is a win: “Happy!” (huge smile, both hands to the cheeks), “Sad!” (one finger draws a tear down), “Angry!” (stamp once, then blow it out).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “I feel happy today.” · “He is sad. I can help.” · “I am angry — I need a big breath.” · “I can ___!”

Day 1 · Happy Faces

Today's word: happy

Grab: a hand mirror in the Magic Box (an unbreakable one if you have it); a scarf over the box; the five feeling posters with happy on top.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box wears the scarf today. Shake it — one soft clunk. Hold it to your ear. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift the lid a crack, look in, and gasp. Shut it. Look in again. Gasp again.

Teacher“There is somebody in this box… and they are looking straight at me!” (pull the mirror out slowly, hold it to your own face, and grin enormously at it) “Oh! It's ME. And I am HAPPY!”

Pass the mirror round the circle. Every child makes their happy face at it and the class says it with them. “Happy!”

4 min Teach · Happy

Your face first, big and slow. They copy you before they say a word.

Happy! (huge smile, both hands to the cheeks)

My mouth goes wide, (draw the smile with two fingers)

My eyes go crinkle, (squeeze both eyes shut, then open)

Happy inside! (both hands flat on your own chest)

Say it three times — bigger each time. Then the mirror goes round again and each child checks their own happy face in it: make it, look, laugh.

Littles say the one word “Happy!” with hands to the cheeks — that is a full win at 2 and 3. Bigs say the frame: “I feel happy today.”

Teacher fails: pull the biggest grin you own, hold it, and announce very seriously, “I am so sad today.” Nod sadly with the grin still on. Wait. Let them fix you twice — the correction shout is the loudest English of the day.

汉字 on the wall today: 开心. Point at it as you say “happy” and they will start pointing at it for you.

3 min Song · How Do You Feel Today? — chorus + Monday verse

How do you feel, how do you feel today? (open both hands to the circle)

Happy or sad — it's okay either way! (thumb up · thumb down)

Show me your face and show me your hands, (huge face, hands up high)

Whale Class listens — Whale Class understands! (hand flat on your own chest)

Monday's verse: When I am happy my mouth goes wide, (draw the smile with two fingers)

Sunshine, sunshine right inside! (both hands open on the chest, then out like rays)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. Sing it twice; by the second time the thumbs are already going up and down.

3 min Game · Feelings mirror

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everyone kneels up facing you.

Teacher“I am the face. You are my mirror.” (make one huge slow happy face)

They copy it exactly and hold it while you count to three. “Happy!” Then a silly one, then a sleepy one, then happy again.

Now swap: one child makes a face, you and the whole class are their mirror. Every child gets a turn — ten seconds each. Bigs name theirs first: “I feel happy today.”

A child who won't make a face will still watch everyone copy someone else. Give them the mirror to hold instead — holding is a turn.

1 min Close

Hands to the cheeks, whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Happy… happy… HAPPY!” (the biggest smile in the building on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Colour Monster (Anna Llenas) — don't read every word, just turn the pages and name the colour and the feeling together.

Day 2 · Sad, and Kind

Today's word: sad (happy comes back every day)

Grab: one onion and a box of tissues in the Magic Box; a chopping board and a knife you keep well above small hands; a sad-looking puppet or soft toy; yesterday's mirror.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake it — nothing rattles at all. So sniff the box instead. Sniff again. Pull a face.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Hold the box out and let three children sniff it too. Then lift out the onion and hold it up like a jewel.

Teacher“An onion. And this…” (lift out the tissues) “…is for what happens next.”

Everyone leans in. “Sad!”

4 min Teach · Sad

Cut the onion in half on the board, well away from the circle, and hold the cut side up. Let your eyes water honestly — they will spot it in about four seconds.

Teacher“Look! Teacher is crying! And I am not even sad — onions do that to grown-ups too.” (take a tissue, dab your eyes, hand a tissue to the nearest child to hold for you)

Now the real thing, slow and quiet:

Sad. (shoulders down, mouth turns down)

My mouth goes down, (draw the frown with two fingers)

A tear rolls down, (one finger draws a tear down the cheek)

Sad… sad… sad. (both hands still in the lap)

Littles say “Sad!” with the tear finger. Bigs say the two-part frame — the important one this week: “He is sad. I can help.”

Teacher fails: hunt everywhere for a tissue — in the box, under the mat, up your sleeve — while a tissue sits balanced on your head. Ask them, sadly, if anyone has seen a tissue. Wait.

汉字 on the wall today: 难过. Sad is allowed in this room — that is the whole lesson. Never rush a child out of it, just sit beside it.

3 min Song · How Do You Feel Today? — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — then Monday's verse if they shout for it — then today's new one:

Tuesday's verse: When I am sad a tear rolls down, (one finger down the cheek)

Hug me, hug me — turn round my frown! (hug yourself, then turn the frown up into a smile with both thumbs)

Sing this verse quietly, then the chorus loudly straight after. Sad, then okay — that shape is the point of the song.

3 min Game · Sad to glad

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The puppet sits in the middle of the circle with its head right down.

Teacher“Oh no. Look. He is sad. What can we do?”

The puppet goes round the circle and every child does one kind thing for it: a pat, a hug, a blanket, a song, a whisper. Then they hand it on.

Littles do the kind thing and say “Sad!” Bigs say it as they do it: “He is sad. I can help.”

When it has been all the way round, hold the puppet up — its head comes slowly up too. Whole class: “Happy!”

Watch who is gentlest. Name it out loud by name at the end — that thirty seconds does more for the class than the whole rest of the game.

1 min Close

Tear finger, then both hands out to the circle. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Happy… sad… I can HELP!” (hands to the cheeks · finger down the cheek · both hands reach out)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Rabbit Listened (Cori Doerrfeld) — the whole book is “he is sad, I can help”. Turn the pages slowly and let them say who is helping.

Day 3 · Angry — Big Breath

Today's word: angry (happy · sad come back)

Grab: a balloon (plus three spares) in the Magic Box; a two-pan balance with one black stone and one white stone for 秋分; a strip of tape on the floor for the balloon race.

2 min Magic Box hook

Box chant. Shake — a papery flutter. Peek, frown, and pull out one flat, floppy, uninflated balloon.

Teacher“This is my tummy when I am not angry.” (hold the flat balloon on one fingertip, calm face, shoulders down)

Then, slowly, one enormous breath into it.

Teacher“And this… is my tummy when I am.” (the balloon swells, your face goes tight)

4 min Teach · Angry, and the big breath

Blow the balloon bigger in stages. The class grows with it, arms opening each time. Then the whole thing, on your own body:

Angry! (stamp one foot, fists down by your sides)

My face goes hot, (both hands on your cheeks)

My tummy goes tight, (arms crossed tight, squeeze)

Big breath IN… (huge breath, arms rise all the way up)

…and let it go. (blow out slowly, arms float down, shoulders drop)

Now let the balloon's air out slowly, squealing, right down to floppy again — that squeal is the sound they will remember the breath by. Three times together.

Littles stamp once and blow once: “Angry!” Bigs say the whole frame: “I am angry — I need a big breath.”

Teacher fails: take an enormous breath IN… and then shout even louder than before. Look pleased. Wait for them to tell you what you forgot to do with it.

秋分 · Autumn Equinox is this week. Two spare minutes? Bring out the balance: the black stone is night, the white stone is day. Let a Big put one in each pan — the beam sits dead level. “Today, night and day are the same.” The balance and its cards live on Tray 3 all week.

汉字 on the wall today: 生气, and 秋分 beside the balance.

3 min Song · How Do You Feel Today? — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — then:

Wednesday's verse: When I am angry I stamp and blow, (one stamp, then blow out hard)

Big breath IN and let it go! (arms up on IN, float them down on GO)

Sing “let it go” slower than the rest of the line, every single time. That slowing-down is a breathing lesson hidden inside a song.

3 min Game · Volcano breath, then the balloon race

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everyone squats down tiny with their arms wrapped round their knees.

Teacher“The angry is down here… it's coming up… up… UP…” (rise with them, hands climbing over your head)
Everyone“…and BLOW IT OUT!” (one enormous slow breath out, arms flopping down, then flop to the floor)

Three rounds — slower each time, not faster. Round three, hold the top for two whole seconds before the blow.

Then the balloon race: blow two balloons up, let them go, and everyone watches them squeal across the room and drop. Twice each side of the tape line.

Pop-shy child? Nothing pops today at all — the whole point this morning is that the air comes out slowly. Keep it that way.

1 min Close

Stamp, then the breath. Whisper → normal → shout, and the breath is the loudest thing:

Everyone“Happy… sad… angry… BIG BREATH!” (cheeks · tear finger · one stamp · one huge slow breath out — then everybody is quiet)

Got 3 spare minutes? When Sophie Gets Angry — Really, Really Angry (Molly Bang) — don't read it, show it: point at Sophie's face on every page and ask “Angry? Or calm?”

Day 4 · Scared → Calm · 中秋 · the whole song

Today's words: scared · calm — and happy · sad · angry all come back. (Thursday is this short week's Friday: the whole song, the review, and the parent wrap-up all land today.)

Grab: the calm jar (water + glitter + a spoon of glue, lid glued shut), a mooncake cut into small pieces, a paper lantern (one each if you can, one for the class if you can't), a small red flag, and every prop from the week back in the box.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out fat, rattling and glinting. Box chant, whispered.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL. Who remembers?”

Lift each prop out in silence and hold it up — they will supply the word before you can.

Mirror → “Happy!”  Onion → “Sad!”  Balloon → “Angry!”

Then, last and slowest, the calm jar. Shake it hard and hold it up. Everybody watches the storm without a word.

4 min Teach · Scared, then calm

Scared first, on your own face — eyes wide, shoulders up by your ears.

Scared! (both hands up by the face, eyes wide, shoulders up)

My heart goes fast, (pat your chest quickly)

I hold a hand, (take the hand of the child next to you)

Slow… slow… breath. (one long breath together, shoulders dropping)

Now I am calm. (hands still in the lap, everything soft)

Then set the shaken calm jar down in the middle and watch it — no talking — until every speck of glitter has settled. It takes about ninety seconds and it is the quietest the room will be all week.

Littles say “Scared!” with their hands up, and “Calm.” with their hands in their lap. Bigs say both: “I was scared. Now I am calm.”

Teacher fails: shake the jar hard and immediately announce, “There! All calm!” while the glitter is still a blizzard. Insist. Let them make you wait.

汉字 today: 害怕 and 平静. The calm jar goes into the 情绪角 (calm corner) this afternoon and stays there for the rest of the year.

3 min Song · Thursday's verse, then the WHOLE song

Thursday's verse: When I am scared I hold a hand, (hold your neighbour's hand)

Slow, slow breath — now I'm calm, I can. (one long breath, then hands into the lap)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday → chorus, top to bottom, with the lanterns in their hands. They learned it one verse a morning without noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · Feelings quick-fire, then the lantern walk

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The five feeling cards face-down in your hand.

Teacher“I show. You be it. Ready?” (flip one card up)

The whole class makes that face instantly and holds it. “Angry!” Faster and faster, then let a Big be the card-holder. Bigs owe you the sentence, Littles the word and the face.

Then 中秋节: lanterns up, walk slowly all the way round the room singing the chorus, and come back to the circle to share the mooncake sitting down.

Teacher“但愿人长久，千里共婵娟。” (read it slowly, then say it in English) “The moon is far away. The people we love are far away. And we can both look at the same moon.”

Last of all, the little red flag: 国庆节. Not yet — we still have school next week; the holiday is 1–7 October, and we come back on Thursday 8 October.

Mooncake is rich and the pieces should be tiny. Check your allergy list before circle, not during it.

1 min Close · the whole week in one line

Everyone sitting, one action each. Whisper → normal → SHOUT — and then the very last word drops to nothing:

Everyone“Happy… sad… angry… scared… BIG BREATH — calm.” (cheeks · tear finger · stamp · hands up · one enormous breath out · hands into the lap, silence)

The loudest word is the breath, and then the room goes quiet on “calm”. That is the joke, and it works every single time. Send the wrap-up home today with the awards and the drawing sheets.

Got 3 spare minutes? Thanking the Moon (Grace Lin) — a whole Mid-Autumn family under one moon. Read it with the lanterns still lit.

Friday · No class — 中秋节

There is no circle time today. This week is four days: Monday 21 to Thursday 24 September.

Nothing to grab. 中秋快乐 — school is closed today for 中秋节 and we are back on Monday 28 September.

Why The short week

中秋节 falls on the Friday, so the whole of this week's finale moved forward one day:

  • Thursday carried Friday's job — the whole song top to bottom, the week's review, the lantern walk, the mooncake and the parent wrap-up all happened on Day 4.
  • Four words on four days, five words owned: happy · sad · angry, then scared → calm together on Thursday.
  • The song has four verses, not five. Nothing is missing — that is the whole song.

Before you go What stays out over the long weekend

  • The calm corner (情绪角) stays exactly where it is — the calm jar, the breathing-star card and the soft cloth. It is not a theme tray; it stays for the rest of the year.
  • The feeling cards stay on the shelf. Children coming back after the break will reach for the face they recognise before they will reach for a word.
  • Leave the lanterns hanging. They are the first thing the class will see when they walk back in on Monday, and they will tell you about their moon — and they can stay up right through the 国庆 holiday.
  • Take the onion home. Trust us.

If your Friday does run (a make-up day, or a class that is in): sing the whole song once more, let every child choose the tray they liked best, and finish with the week's line — “Happy… sad… angry… scared… BIG BREATH — calm.” Nothing new; the week is already complete.

Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“boom — boom — boom-chick”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Am is new this week and it is the easiest shape on the instrument: one finger on the top string, second fret, everything else open. It is the sad-and-soft chord — it lands on “Happy or sad” and it opens Tuesday's verse, which is exactly why the song needs it.

Tempo: gentle and rocking, not fast. Slow right down on “slow, slow breath” and let the room breathe with you.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4) → Am (4) → G7 (4) → back to C. That is the entire song. C is one finger and Am is one finger, so two of the four shapes cost you nothing. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and sing it unaccompanied with the actions — the class will not notice, and the actions are doing most of the teaching anyway.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Sep 21–24 (four days) · Theme: My Feeling · Sound of the week: n

What we did

A short week, but a big one. The one big idea was this: every feeling is allowed — I can name it, I can show it, and I can let it go. On Monday a hand mirror came out of the Magic Box and every child made their happy face at it. On Tuesday we cut an onion and teacher cried in front of the whole class, honestly, which made sad a safe thing to look at — then a sad puppet went round the circle and every child did one kind thing for it. On Wednesday a balloon grew tight and full like an angry tummy, and we let the air out slowly, together, three times. On Thursday we shook a calm jar and sat completely silent until every speck of glitter had settled, sang the whole song with our lanterns, read the last line of 《水调歌头》 and shared mooncake for 中秋节.

Words we learned

Sentences to listen for at home

“I feel happy today.” · “He is sad. I can help.” · “I am angry — I need a big breath.” · “I was scared. Now I am calm.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a face — “Happy!” with both hands on the cheeks, or “Angry!” with one stamp and a big blow, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “How Do You Feel Today?”

“How do you feel, how do you feel today? Happy or sad — it's okay either way! Show me your face and show me your hands, Whale Class listens — Whale Class understands!”
We sang it on the ukulele every morning and added a new verse each day. Ask your child to sing it — thumb up on happy, thumb down on sad.

秋分 · 中秋节 · 国庆节

Three things landed in one short week. On 秋分 we put one black stone and one white stone on a balance and watched the beam sit level: on that day, night and day are exactly the same length. For 中秋节 we walked with lanterns, shared mooncake, and read 苏轼's line slowly — 但愿人长久，千里共婵娟 — the moon is far away, and the people you love are far away, and you can both look at the same moon. That is a feelings poem, which is why it belongs in this week. Then the little red flag: 国庆节 — the holiday runs 1–7 October, school stops after Wednesday 30 September and we are back on Thursday 8 October.

Dark Phonics · the n sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met n — “nnnnn”, made with the tongue right up behind the top teeth: no, nose, nap, and our chant, no-no, nanny goat! “No” was the most useful word of the whole week — it is allowed, it is said out loud rather than kicked or bitten, and it starts with n. Put a finger on your nose and hum nnnnn; you can feel it buzz.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · At dinner, everyone answers one question: “How do you feel today?” Grown-ups go first and give a real answer.
2 · Show me your angry face… now show me your big breath. That pair is the whole week — practise it when nobody is cross, so it is there when somebody is.
3 · Make a calm jar together: a jar of water, a spoonful of glitter, a spoonful of glue, and glue the lid shut. Shake it, then watch it settle without talking.
4 · Hunt for the n sound: no, nose, nap, night, moon.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
