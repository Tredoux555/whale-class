Tools & Transportation

Week of Dec 7–11 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

toolwheelcarbustrain

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Wheel!” (both hands turn a big circle), “Car!” (steer, brrrm), “Train!” (elbows pumping, choo-choo).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “A hammer is a tool.” · “The bus has six wheels.” · “I go to school by car.” · “The train goes on the track.”

The tools are real. A real hammer, a real screwdriver, a real pair of pliers — heavy, cold, and handed over handle first, one child at a time, and put straight back in the box. That ceremony is the lesson: a tool is a serious thing that helps your hands. A plastic toy hammer teaches nothing and they know it.

Monday is 大雪, Major Snow. It threads through the whole week — slippery roads on Monday, and Friday's helmet: 下雪了，慢慢走，慢慢骑。

Day 1 · Tools in My Hands

Today's words: tool

Grab: a real hammer, a real screwdriver and a real pair of pliers in the Magic Box; one block of soft wood with a nail started in it, and one loose screw; the 工具 character card. Today is 大雪 — have the snow line ready.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is the heaviest of the whole year. Carry it in with two hands and let it land on the mat with a thud. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Let two children come and try to lift it. They can't do it easily — that is the point. Shake it: a deep, serious CLUNK. Sniff it: it smells of metal.

Teacher“It's heavy… it's cold… and it smells like metal. These are not toys.” (lift out the hammer with both hands and hold it flat across your palms)

Hands up like a hammer. “Tool!”

4 min Teach · Tool

One tool at a time, out of the box, used for its real job, then straight back in. Nothing else is on the mat.

The hammer goes BANG. (three real taps on the nail — let them hear it)

The screwdriver goes round and round. (turn the screw slowly, wrist twisting)

The pliers go squeeze. (open and shut them right in front of your nose)

A tool helps my hands do a job. (hold both empty hands up, then take the hammer again)

Littles do the gesture and say the one word: “Tool!” “Bang!” Bigs take the frame: “A hammer is a tool.” “The hammer hits the nail.”

Then it goes round: each child holds one tool for ten seconds, in both hands, sitting down, while everybody watches. You pass it handle first and you take it back yourself. Nobody swings anything.

Teacher fails: try to bang the nail in with the screwdriver. Hard. Twice. Look genuinely baffled at the nail, then at the screwdriver, then at the children. Wait for the shouting.

Chinese moment: lay the 工具 card beside the three tools. 工具 = tool. Say it once, point along the row, and leave it there. No drilling.

3 min Song · Round and Round the Wheels Go — chorus + Monday verse

Round and round and round they go, (both hands turn big circles)

Wheels on the road, fast and slow! (fast circles, then very slow)

A hammer, a spanner, a helping hand, (hammer / twist / show your open palm)

Tools and wheels all over the land! (arms wide)

Monday's verse: Bang goes the hammer, twist goes the screw, (bang, then twist)

A tool in my hand helps me do! (hold up one hand, close it round an imaginary handle)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Do the chorus as a pure finger-play the first time, no ukulele: the hands teach the words. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab.

3 min Game · What does this tool do?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The three tools lie on the mat in front of you, out of reach.

Teacher“What does this one do?” (hold one up high, then mime its job hugely — big slow bang, big slow twist, big slow squeeze)

Everybody copies the mime with empty hands and says the sound: “BANG!” “Round and round!” “Squeeze!” Bigs give the sentence: “The hammer hits the nail.”

Then a Big holds a tool up and the class mimes it back — six children get a turn. The tool never leaves their two hands.

Mime is safer than practice and just as good: twenty pairs of hands hammering the air is twenty children rehearsing the word without a single bruised thumb.

1 min Close

Hands out flat, ready to turn. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Car… bus… TRAIN! Round and round the WHEEL!” (both hands turning enormous circles over their heads on the shout)

大雪 today. One sentence, no lesson: “Today is 大雪 — the big snow. When it snows, the road is slippery. 下雪了，慢慢走。” Walk two exaggerated slow-motion steps and let them copy you out of the door.

Got 3 spare minutes? 《谁的本领大》 — who is more use, the wind or the sun? Read it and ask them which tool they'd pick.

Day 2 · Round and Round the Wheel

Today's words: wheel (tool comes back every day)

Grab: a real bicycle wheel in the Magic Box (or leaning beside it — it will not fit); a plank for a ramp and two books to prop it; a basket of things that roll and things that don't (ball, marble, tin, orange, wooden block, book, shoe, key); the 轮 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is far too small and the lid won't go on — something round is sticking out of it. Chant anyway:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Try, seriously, to shut the lid. Fail. Try again. Then lift the whole wheel out and spin it in your hands, close to their ears.

Teacher“Listen… ticka-ticka-ticka. It's round, and it never stops being round.” (hold it flat, then stand it up and let it roll across the mat to a child)

Big circles with both hands. “Wheel!”

4 min Teach · Wheel

The wheel stands on the mat. Turn it with one finger while you say it:

A wheel is a circle. (draw a circle in the air, then round the rim with one finger)

A circle has no corners. (run your finger round and round — nowhere to stop)

So it rolls… (let it roll away and catch it)

and it takes you with it! (walk two steps beside it)

Now the corners. Put a wooden block beside the wheel. Push the block — it scrapes, it stops. Push the wheel — it goes. Do it twice more without saying a word.

Littles turn their hands and say “Wheel!” Bigs: “A wheel is round.” “Round things roll.” “The bus has six wheels.”

Teacher fails: announce that you have found a much better wheel, and hold up the book. Try to roll it. Try harder. Ask them, sincerely, what is wrong with your wheel.

Count the wheels on things: a bicycle has two, a car has four, a bus has six. Count them on fingers. That is Tuesday's maths and they will do it all week without being asked.

The 轮 card goes down beside yesterday's 工具. 轮 = wheel. Point at the wheel, point at the card. Once.

3 min Song · Round and Round the Wheels Go — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — then yesterday's verse if they call for it — then today's new one:

Tuesday's verse: A wheel is a circle, round and true, (draw a big circle with one finger)

Give it a push — off it flew! (one sharp push, then both hands fly away)

On “round and round” in the chorus, everybody's hands should be turning at the same speed as yours — go fast, then drop to almost stopped on “slow”. Two-year-olds who only ever do the hands have understood the entire week.

3 min Game · Rolls or doesn't roll?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The ramp is propped on two books. The basket of objects sits beside it.

Teacher“Predict first. Will it roll… or will it stop? Hands up for ROLL.” (hold the object up high and wait for every hand to commit before anyone tests it)

Then one child sends it down the ramp and the whole class watches. Littles shout the answer: “Roll!” “Stop!” Bigs: “The tin rolls because it is round.”

Eight objects, eight predictions. The two baskets at the bottom of the ramp fill up as you go: rolls and doesn't roll.

The ramp is the control of error — it either rolls or it doesn't and nobody can argue with it, including you. Never say “no” to a prediction; just send the object down. Guessing wrong out loud and then seeing is the whole tray.

1 min Close

Everybody standing, hands turning. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Car… bus… TRAIN! Round and round the WHEEL!” (spin right round on the spot on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Wheels on the Bus — do it as a finger-play only, no singing, so it doesn't compete with this week's song. The wipers, the doors, the horn, the babies.

Day 3 · Cars & Buses on the Road

Today's words: car · bus (tool · wheel come back)

Grab: a toy car and a Beijing 公交卡 bus card in the Magic Box; three big circles of card — red, amber, green — on a stick; a line of chairs set up as a bus with one driver's chair at the front; the 车 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box rattles twice and quite differently each time — one little wooden knock, one flat plastic tap. Chant, shake, tip your head, shake again.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”
Teacher“Two things. One goes knock… and one goes tap.” (out comes the little car — roll it along your arm — then the 公交卡, held up flat between two fingers)

“A card? What do you do with a card?” Beep it on your palm. “Bus!”

4 min Teach · Car and bus

The car runs along the floor in front of them while you talk. Count its wheels first: one, two, three, four.

A car is small. (hands close together, steer, brrrm)

It takes my family. (four fingers up)

A bus is BIG. (arms right out wide)

It takes everybody! (sweep both arms round the whole circle)

Now the chairs. You are the driver; six children get on, each one beeping the 公交卡 on your hand as they board — 滴! Everybody else is waiting at the bus stop.

Littles steer and say “Car!” or wave the card and say “Bus!” Bigs: “I go to school by car.” “The bus is big.” “The bus has six wheels.”

Teacher fails: try to get on your own bus by beeping the toy car on your hand. Nothing happens. Beep it harder. Look at the driver's seat, which is empty, and ask it what's wrong.

Chinese moment: the 车 card joins the row — 工具 · 轮 · 车. Then name Beijing's own three: 汽车 · 公交车 · 地铁. Sing one line of 《小汽车》 — 小汽车，嘀嘀嘀，开到东，开到西 — one line, then stop.

3 min Song · Round and Round the Wheels Go — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — then today's verse, which has a full stop in the middle of it:

Wednesday's verse: Brrrm goes the car down the busy street, (steering, leaning round the corners)

Red light — STOP! Green light — beep! (freeze completely on STOP, then two beeps on the nose)

Actually stop. Everybody freezes, hands in the air, and you count two silent beats before “green light”. The silence is the funniest part of the whole song and it sets up today's game perfectly.

3 min Game · Red light, green light

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody lines up at one wall. You stand at the other with the three card circles. You are the traffic light. They are the cars.

Teacher“GREEN!” (green circle up — everybody drives forward, steering and brrrming) “AMBER…” (slow motion) “RED!” (freeze)

Anyone still moving on red goes back three steps, cheerfully. Littles just run and stop — that is the whole skill. Bigs call the colours for the second round while you drive with the class.

Three rounds. On the last one, hold amber for a very long time.

This is a listening game wearing a car costume: stopping fast when the word comes is the same self-control they need for “one, two, three — eyes on me”. Bank it.

1 min Close

Everybody sitting, hands on an imaginary steering wheel. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Car… bus… TRAIN! Round and round the WHEEL!” (two big beeps on their own noses on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Park the chairs as a bus again and let two Bigs run it — one driver, one conductor with the 公交卡. They will play it all afternoon.

Day 4 · The Train on the Track

Today's words: train (tool · wheel · car · bus come back)

Grab: two pieces of train track that click together in the Magic Box, plus one wooden train; a printed photo of the 高铁; three station signs on chairs round the room (write them with the children's own names for places); the 火车 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box makes one hard wooden CLACK and then nothing. Chant, shake once, then open it in front of one child's face.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”
Teacher“Two pieces of wood. That's all. Just two boring bits of wood…” (hold one in each hand, look disappointed, then click them together loudly)

Click. “Oh! They join.” Stand the train on them and push. Elbows pumping. “Train!”

4 min Teach · Train

The two track pieces stay joined on the mat, with the train on top.

A car can go anywhere. (drive the toy car in wild loops all over the floor)

A train goes here. (run the train along the track — and off the end, where it stops)

On the track, on the track, (finger along the rails)

chuff, chuff, chuff — and chuff back! (elbows pumping, then reverse)

Then the 高铁 photo. Hold it up. “This train goes faster than a car. Faster than a bus. Faster than anything you have ever sat in.” Let them tell you if they have been on one; several will have.

Littles pump their elbows and say “Train!” “Choo-choo!” Bigs: “The train goes on the track.” “The train is long.”

Teacher fails: put the wooden train on the carpet, well away from the track, and push it. Push it again. Announce that the train is broken and look very sad until somebody drags the track over.

The 火车 card joins the row: 工具 · 轮 · 车 · 火车. Point out that 火车 has 车 inside it — they will spot it before you finish saying it. Then 地铁 and 高铁, once each, pointing at the photo.

3 min Song · Round and Round the Wheels Go — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — then today's verse, the one that opens on the Am:

Thursday's verse: Chuff, chuff, chuff on the shiny track, (elbows pumping, slow then faster)

All aboard! And chuff, chuff back! (one hand cupped to your mouth, then everybody reverses)

Start this verse very slowly and speed up as it goes — a train leaving a station. “All aboard!” is shouted, not sung, with a hand cupped round your mouth. By Friday they shout it before you do.

3 min Game · The classroom train

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody lines up behind you, each child's hands on the shoulders in front. You are the engine. The three station chairs are already out.

Teacher“All aboard! Next stop… the window!” (chuff off round the room and stop dead at the first sign)

At each station: everybody stops, the whole train says the station's name, one child gets off and one gets on at the front. Three stations, then back to the mat, which is the depot.

Littles just hold on and chuff. Bigs announce the next station themselves: “Next stop, the book corner!”

Let the last child be the engine on the second run. The train is a beautiful accidental lesson in staying joined — the moment somebody lets go, the train breaks, and they fix it themselves without you saying a word.

1 min Close

Still in the train, hands on shoulders. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Car… bus… TRAIN! Round and round the WHEEL!” (the whole train chuffs one step forward on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Freight Train (Donald Crews) — one colour per carriage. Read it once slowly, then again fast, and let the train go through a tunnel with the lights off.

Day 5 · How I Get to School + Big Review

Today's words: tool · wheel · car · bus · train — the whole week

Grab: a bicycle bell and a helmet in the Magic Box with every prop from the week underneath; four column headings taped on the floor — walk 🚶 · car 🚗 · bus 🚌 · bicycle 🚲; the awards (print pack page 17); the 路 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out fat and bulging and won't quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each prop out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The hammer → “Tool!”  The bicycle wheel → “Wheel!”  The little car → “Car!”  The 公交卡 → “Bus!”  The track → “Train!”

Last of all, right at the bottom: a bell. Ring it once. Then the helmet — put it on your own head and do the strap up under your chin, slowly, in front of all of them.

4 min Teach · Ride safely, then the whole week

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

Tool — bang, twist, squeeze! (hammer, screwdriver, pliers)

Wheel — round and round. (both hands turning)

Car — brrrm! (steering)

Bus — everybody on! (beep the card, arms wide)

And the TRAIN goes chuff, chuff, chuff! (elbows pumping)

Then the helmet goes round the circle: every child puts it on, does one ring of the bell, and hands it on. While it travels, the sentence: “Helmet on. Slowly, slowly. 下雪了，慢慢走，慢慢骑。”

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a sentence each: “A hammer is a tool.” “The bus has six wheels.” “The train goes on the track.”

Teacher fails: put the helmet on backwards, or better, on your knee, and do up the strap with enormous satisfaction. Announce that you are ready to ride. Wait.

Chinese moment: the 路 card completes the row — 工具 · 轮 · 车 · 火车 · 路, the whole week on the mat. Point along it and let them say what they can.

3 min Song · Round and Round the Wheels Go — Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: Ring, ring, ring goes my bicycle bell, (three rings on the real bell)

Helmet on tight — I ride so well! (both hands pat the top of your head, then pedal)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, with the bell rung once on the very last word. They learned it one verse a morning without ever noticing, and this run-through is the review of the entire week.

3 min Game · How do you get to school?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Four columns are taped on the floor: walk · car · bus · bicycle, with a picture at the top of each.

Teacher“Go and stand in your column. How do you come to school?”

Everybody moves. Then, column by column, count the children out loud, touching each head: one, two, three… Littles just stand and be counted, and shout their word: “Car!” Bigs: “I go to school by bus.”

Which column is the longest? Which is the shortest? Say the numbers again in Chinese while you're standing there.

This is a real graph made of children. Take a photo from the doorway looking down the columns — that photo is Friday's parent report, done, and the class will read it off the wall all next week.

1 min Close + awards

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home. Then, standing in their columns, with all five gestures:

Everyone“Car… bus… TRAIN! Round and round the WHEEL!” (the bell rung once, hard, on the very last word)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Wheels on the Bus again — but this time the children choose what goes on the bus. It will end up with a hammer, a helmet and a train on it, and that is exactly right.

Theme Song · “Round and Round the Wheels Go”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The four chords

C
F
G7
Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“sing — song — sing-a”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: rolling and steady, like a bicycle on a flat road — not a march. The Am turns up twice in the whole song: on “a helping hand” at the end of the chorus's third line, and at the top of Thursday's train verse. Everything else is C, F and G7. Chant it without the uke first; add chords once they know the actions.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CRound and round and Fround they Cgo, (both hands turn big circles)

FWheels on the Croad, fast and G7slow! C(fast circles, then very slow)

CA hammer, a Fspanner, a helping Amhand, (hammer / twist / open palm)

FTools and Cwheels all G7over the Cland! (arms wide)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who turns both hands fast on “fast” and almost stops on “slow” has understood the week, whether or not a single word comes out.

One verse a day — four of the five use only C and G7

Monday · Tool

CBang goes the hammer, twist goes the screw, (bang, then twist)

G7A tool in my hand Chelps me do! (hand closed round an imaginary handle)

Tuesday · Wheel

CA wheel is a circle, round and true, (draw a big circle with one finger)

G7Give it a push — Coff it flew! (one sharp push, then both hands fly away)

Wednesday · Car

CBrrrm goes the car down the busy street, (steering round the corners)

G7Red light — STOP! CGreen light — beep! (freeze completely, then two beeps on the nose)

Thursday · Train

AmChuff, chuff, chuff on the shiny track, (elbows pumping, slow then faster)

G7All aboard! CAnd chuff, chuff back! (hand cupped to your mouth — shouted, not sung)

Friday · Ride

CRing, ring, ring goes my bicycle bell, (three rings on the real bell)

G7Helmet on tight — CI ride so well! (pat the top of your head, then pedal)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — and Friday's finale, sung standing in the four columns of the how-do-you-get-to-school graph with the bell rung on the last word, doubles as your review of the whole week.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is nearly the whole chorus. Add Am last — it is one finger on the same string as F, so slide Am → F → Am until it stops feeling like a change; you only need it for “a helping hand” and the first line of Thursday. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the turning hands carry the meaning either way.

Theme Shelf · Tools & Transportation

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Nuts and bolts
from MONDAY's Magic Box		Five bolts of different widths and their five nuts, in a segmented tray. Screw each one on, take it off, put it back. Control: only one nut fits each bolt — nobody has to be told. Practical life, fine motor, and the wrist twist that Monday's screwdriver taught. Language: “twist · tight · loose · it fits!”
Tray 2 · Rolls / doesn't roll
pages 12–14 · from TUESDAY		A small ramp, the two printed signs, and a basket of a dozen real objects — ball, marble, tin, orange, block, book, shoe, key. Predict, then send it down. Control: the ramp itself — it either rolls or it doesn't, and nobody can argue, including you. The 12 sorting cards (page 14) live beside the real objects so the card and the thing are always together.
Tray 3 · Tool-to-job matching
from MONDAY's Magic Box		hammer↔nail · screwdriver↔screw · saw↔wood · spanner↔bolt · brush↔paint, as five pairs of cards or five pairs of real objects. Control: matching symbols on the card backs. Beside it, the action cards (page 15) face down: draw one, do it, say it — “I can fix!”
Tray 4 · Transport 3-part cards
pages 10–11 · from WED & THU		汽车 car · 公共汽车 bus · 火车 train · 自行车 bicycle · 飞机 aeroplane, Chinese and English. Littles match picture→picture on the whole control card; Bigs lay the labels underneath. Control: the uncut control cards. Beside it the two track pieces, the wooden train and the 公交卡 — and the crayons and the “My Ride” sheets (page 16). Sheets go home Friday with the award (page 17).

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can — the ramp tray gets handled all week. The real tools do NOT live on the shelf: they come out of the Magic Box with you, are used sitting down, and go back in the box before you stand up. That is not fussiness, it is the reason the children treat them as serious. The row of 汉字 cards the class built up (工具 · 轮 · 车 · 火车 · 路) stays on the shelf edge all week — that is the display, no extra work needed.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Dec 7–11 · Theme: Tools & Transportation · Sound of the week: r

What we did

This week the children handled real tools. On Monday the Magic Box was the heaviest of the whole year: a real hammer, a real screwdriver and a real pair of pliers, passed handle first, one child at a time, held in two hands sitting down, and put straight back — and that ceremony was half the lesson. On Tuesday a bicycle wheel came out and we found out what a wheel is for, by rolling one across the room and then trying, unsuccessfully, to roll a book. We built a ramp and tested a dozen things: predict first, hands up, then send it down and watch. On Wednesday a toy car and a 公交卡 turned the chairs into a bus, and we played Red light, green light with me as the traffic light. On Thursday two pieces of track clicked together, we became one long train and stopped at three stations round the room. And on Friday we made a graph out of ourselves — walk, car, bus, bicycle — and counted which column was tallest. Monday was 大雪, Major Snow, so the helmet and “慢慢走，慢慢骑” ran right through the week.

Sentences to listen for at home

“A hammer is a tool.” · “A wheel is round.” · “The bus has six wheels.” · “I go to school by car.” · “The train goes on the track.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Wheel!” with both hands turning a big circle, or “Train!” with the elbows pumping, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Round and Round the Wheels Go”

“Round and round and round they go, wheels on the road, fast and slow! A hammer, a spanner, a helping hand, tools and wheels all over the land!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — tool, wheel, car, train, ride. Ask your child to sing the chorus and watch their hands: they turn fast on “fast” and almost stop on “slow”.

Dark Phonics · the r sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met r — Rrr Goes the Engine. It is the growl you make with the engine running before the car pulls away: rrrrr. Our silly line for it is “run, run, red rat!” It could not have landed in a better week, because every wheel we touched went round, on the road, and we ride and we roll. Listen for it in: rat, rug, run, red, road, ride, roll.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · On the way home, name what you are travelling in — in English: “We go home by bus.” That is the whole week's assessment, done on the journey.
2 · Count wheels together: a bicycle has two, a car has four, a bus has six.
3 · Find one round thing and one thing with corners, and roll them both across the table. Ask “why did that one stop?”
4 · Let them help with one real job with a real tool — holding the screwdriver while you turn it counts.
5 · Hunt for the r sound: rat, rug, run, red, road — growl it like an engine.

Monday was 大雪, the Major Snow. When the road is icy, everything on wheels slows down: 下雪了，慢慢走，慢慢骑。 If your child suddenly insists you do up their helmet strap properly — that was us, and we are not sorry.

Thank you for a lovely week — the Whale Class 🐳

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
