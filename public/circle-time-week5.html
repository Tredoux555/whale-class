Five words they'll own by Friday

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Leaf!” (flutter one hand down to the floor), “Red!” (point at something red), “Windy!” (whoosh both arms sideways and sway).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “It is autumn.” · “The leaf is red.” · “The wind blows the leaves down.” · “The tree changed while we were away.”

Day 1 · Monday 28 September · Autumn

Today's words: autumn

Grab: a branch that is only just turning — mostly green, with two leaves going gold at the tip. And your phone: you must photograph that branch today. Thursday's whole lesson depends on the photo. Also: two hoops or two big dishes, a pile of real leaves (half green, half gold), and the 秋 character card.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box is longer than usual today — something is sticking out of the lid. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a dry, papery rustle, nothing else. Sniff the lid: (eyes closed) “It smells like… outside.”

Teacher“It came off a tree. This morning. And the tree is changing.” (lift the branch out slowly and hold it up above their heads so they look up into it)

Turn it. Two leaves at the tip are gold; the rest are still green. Let them touch one gold leaf each, one at a time.

4 min Teach · Autumn

Hold the branch across your two open hands, low, so everyone can see both colours at once.

Green… (touch a green leaf)

Green… (touch another)

GOLD! (touch the gold tip — big surprised face)

It's autumn. (both arms open wide, slow)

Say the word three times, getting quieter: autumn, autumn, autumn. Littles just say it with you — “Autumn!” Bigs take the frame: “It is autumn.”

Now the photograph. Stand the branch up where everybody can see it, get the whole circle to say “cheese” to it, and take the picture in front of them — ceremonially, like it matters, because on Thursday it will. Tell them plainly: “I am keeping this picture. When we come back after the holiday we will look at it again and see what the tree did without us.”

Teacher fails: hold up the gold tip, look at it with total confidence and announce “Ah — green.” Nod. Wait. They will shout you down, and the shouting is the lesson.

Lay the 秋 card beside the branch on the mat. Say it once, in Chinese — 秋天到了 — and leave it there. One character a day, no drilling.

3 min Song · Autumn Is Here! — chorus + Monday verse

Autumn is here — the leaves come down, (flutter both hands to the floor)

Red and yellow all over the town! (big circle arms, point out of the window)

Whoosh goes the wind and away they go, (whoosh sideways)

Round and round and down below! (spin one finger, then crouch)

Monday's verse: Summer's packed her bags and gone, (mime lifting a heavy bag, wave goodbye)

Autumn's here — put your jumper on! (pull on an imaginary jumper, hug yourself)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, right through the holiday and out the other side. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. The chorus is a finger-play: they will do the actions before they can sing the words.

3 min Game · Green or gold?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two hoops on the floor, well apart. A green leaf in one, a gold leaf in the other — those are the labels.

Tip the pile of leaves out in the middle. One child at a time: pick up one leaf, hold it high so the circle can see, and run it to the hoop it belongs in. The circle calls the colour as it goes.

Everyone“GREEN!” … “GOLD!”

Go round twice. Then hold up a leaf that is half and half and let them argue about it — there is no right answer, and that is exactly the point: it is changing.

Real leaves, never paper ones. The dry crackle is half of what they are learning.

1 min Close

The week's line, every day. Whisper → normal → shout, with the leaves fluttering down on the last word:

Everyone“Red… yellow… WINDY! It's AUTUMN!” (fingers flutter down, then both arms fly up on AUTUMN)

Got 3 spare minutes? Leaf Man (Lois Ehlert) — every page is real leaves, and they will start seeing faces in the pile outside.

Day 2 · Tuesday 29 September · Leaf

Today's words: leaf (autumn comes back every day)

Grab: a basket of just-fallen leaves, as full as you can carry; yesterday's branch, still standing on the shelf; the 叶 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is heavy and light at the same time. Let two children lift it. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it hard — a huge dry rustle, like rain on a window. Peek in, and make your face enormous.

Teacher“There are hundreds. And they all fell off the tree by themselves.” (stand up, lift the basket high over the middle of the circle and tip it slowly)

Leaves come down over everybody. Let the shrieking run for five whole seconds before you say anything. Then, quietly: “Leaf!” — one hand fluttering down to the floor.

4 min Teach · Leaf

Everyone picks up one leaf from the floor in front of them. One each, no swapping, and hold it flat on the palm.

One little leaf… (hold yours up between two fingers)

Let go… (drop it, and everyone watches it all the way down)

Down, down, down. (follow it with your finger)

It landed on me! (catch one on the top of your head)

Now go round the circle: each child holds their leaf up and says one thing about it. Littles say the word — “Leaf!” Bigs take a frame: “My leaf is big.” “The leaf is red.”

Then all together: hold the leaf as high as you can reach, and on three, everybody lets go at once. Twenty leaves falling in silence is the best three seconds of the week.

Teacher fails: try to make your leaf fall upwards. Throw it at the ceiling, twice, and look genuinely puzzled when it comes back down.

The 叶 card goes down beside yesterday's 秋 — 树叶变黄了。The row is starting to build.

3 min Song · Autumn Is Here! — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — then Monday's verse if they shout for it — then today's new one:

Tuesday's verse: One little leaf let go of the tree, (pinch fingers, then open them)

Down, down, down — it landed on me! (flutter down, then tap the top of your head)

Sing this one sitting inside the fallen leaves. Nobody tidies until after the game — the mess is the material.

3 min Game · Leaf, leaf, drop

Everybody stands up. “You are not children any more. You are leaves, and you are still on the branch.” Arms up high, fingers spread, completely still.

Teacher(quietly) “The tree is very still… very still… and then — WINDY!”

On the word windy, every leaf spins slowly down to the floor and goes absolutely still where it lands. Then back up on the branch, and again. Four or five rounds — and make them wait for it, longer each time.

The stillness is the game, not the spinning. If they land and stay down for three silent seconds, you have taught self-regulation and the word windy in the same breath — and Friday's word is already in the room.

1 min Close

Everyone holds one leaf. Whisper → normal → shout, and throw the leaves up on the last word:

Everyone“Red… yellow… WINDY! It's AUTUMN!” (leaves go up on AUTUMN)

Now they tidy — every leaf back into the basket, and the basket goes onto Tray 2 for the shape-matching work.

Day 3 · Wednesday 30 September · Red — and goodbye to the tree

Today's words: red (the last day before the holiday)

Grab: a small 红旗 and one red maple leaf 枫叶, side by side in the box; five red things hidden in plain sight round the room; the 红 card; and Monday's photograph, printed if you can — you will need it on Thursday either way.

2 min Magic Box hook

Two things in the box today, and they are the same colour. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out the maple leaf first, high, and turn it slowly. Then the little flag beside it, and hold them together.

Teacher“Red… and red. One came off a tree. One belongs to the whole country. Tomorrow is 国庆节.”

Point at something red on your own clothes. Then they hunt their own: “Red!”

4 min Teach · Red

The maple leaf goes round the circle, one child at a time, held up against something of theirs. Same or not the same?

A red, red leaf… (hold it up)

A red, red flag… (wave it gently)

Red on my jumper! (point at a child's red thing)

RED! (everyone points at anything red at once)

Littles point and name: “Red!” Bigs take the frame: “The leaf is red.” “I see a red flag.”

Lay the red leaf beside a green one and a gold one from Tuesday's basket. Three colours, one week, one tree.

Teacher fails: hold the bright red maple leaf up, admire it, and call it green — with your whole chest. Say it twice. Let them physically take it off you.

The 红 card joins 秋 and 叶 on the shelf edge. 国庆节快乐！

3 min Song · Autumn Is Here! — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus, then Monday's and Tuesday's verses if they want them, then today's:

Wednesday's verse: A red, red flag and a red, red leaf, (wave the flag, then hold up the leaf)

Goodbye, tree — we'll be back next week! (big slow wave at the window)

Sing this one at the window, facing the actual tree. It is a goodbye song today, and they should be able to see who they are saying goodbye to.

3 min Game · Red hunt

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Then: “Find me five red things — before the song ends. GO!”

Sing the chorus, slowly, on the ukulele while they scatter. They bring red things back to the middle and pile them up. When the chorus ends, everybody sits, and you count the pile together — out loud, in English and in Chinese.

Last round: everybody holds one red thing, and the 红旗 goes to whichever child has been waiting most patiently, to wave for the class.

Cap it at five, count it properly, and stop. A hunt that never ends turns into a room-wrecking, and today ends with a ceremony.

1 min Close

Standing at the window, flag in one hand. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Red… yellow… WINDY! It's AUTUMN!”

Before you go 再见, tree — keep changing without us 🇨🇳

国庆节 holiday · 1–7 October — no circle time. We stop here on Wednesday and come back on Thursday 8 October. This is still one week: same five words, same song, same four trays on the shelf right through. The hole in the middle is part of the lesson.

Take the whole class to the window, or outside to the tree itself if you have five spare minutes. Hold up Monday's branch. Then say it, slowly, and mean it:

Teacher“We are going away for a week. You are not. Keep changing without us. When we come back, we are going to look very carefully and find out exactly what you did.”

Everybody waves the 红旗 once. 再见，树！ Then check the photograph is safe on your phone — that photograph is Thursday's whole lesson.

What stays out over the holiday: all four trays, exactly as they are. The row of 汉字 (秋 · 叶 · 红) stays on the shelf edge, and the branch stands beside it. Nothing gets packed away — the room should look like it is still mid-week when they walk back in, because it is.

Day 4 · Thursday 8 October · Yellow — what changed?

Today's words: yellow (first day back — and 寒露)

Grab: gold ginkgo leaves 银杏, picked on your own way in this morning — not last week's; Monday's photograph, on the biggest screen you can find; a jar of cold, wet grass for 寒露; the 黄 card. Two minutes before circle, take them outside to touch the railing: 凉不凉？
Welcome back. 国庆节快乐 — the class has been away for seven days (1–7 October) and this is the second half of the same week. Do not start a new theme. Same words, same song, same trays, same branch on the shelf. Today is the day the hole in the middle pays off — and it is also 寒露 · Cold Dew, the third autumn solar term, the morning the dew turns cold to touch.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box has been sitting on the shelf, shut, for a week. Say so. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a rustle, and something sloshes. Peek, and go very still.

Teacher(whisper) “These were not this colour when you left.” (lift out a handful of gold ginkgo leaves and let them fall through your fingers)

Then the jar of cold wet grass. Everyone touches it. “Cold!” That is 寒露 — 寒露到，露水凉。

4 min Teach · Yellow — and What changed?

This is the big one. Hold Monday's photograph up in one hand and the real branch in the other, right next to each other, and say nothing at all for five seconds.

We went away… (walk two fingers off to one side)

and the tree turned GOLD. (hold up a ginkgo leaf, high)

Yellow, yellow — (everyone points at the gold)

look, behold! (hands open wide)

Now round the circle: each child names one thing that is different between the photograph and the branch. Littles point at the gold and say the word — “Yellow!” Bigs take the full frame: “The tree changed while we were away.” “The leaf is yellow now.”

Then the honest part, said plainly: “Nobody watched it. It did it anyway.” Beijing's 银杏 trees all turned at once while they were on holiday, and every single child walked past it.

Teacher fails: look from the photo to the branch and back, frown, and announce “Hmm. Exactly the same.” Hold your ground for a good long moment.

The 黄 card joins 秋 · 叶 · 红 on the shelf edge. Ask what colour the trees outside their own homes are — every answer is right and every answer is evidence.

3 min Song · Autumn Is Here! — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus first — and they will remember it, a week later, better than you expect. Then Monday's, Tuesday's and Wednesday's, then today's:

Thursday's verse: We went away and the tree turned gold, (walk fingers away, then both hands up like branches)

Yellow, yellow — look, behold! (point, then open both hands wide)

This verse starts on Am — the one darker chord in the song, and it lands on the day they come back to a changed tree. Slow right down for it.

3 min Game · What changed?

Monday's photograph propped up in the middle of the circle; the real branch lying beside it; the gold ginkgo leaves in a pile.

One child at a time comes into the middle, points at one difference and names it. The circle answers together: “It CHANGED!” Nobody may repeat somebody else's difference — which forces them to look properly.

Second round, harder and quieter: everyone shuts their eyes, you move or turn one thing on the mat, and they open them and find it. Three times.

This is the sensorial memory work the whole week was built around, and the holiday is what made it possible. Take every answer, even a wrong one — “the pot moved” counts, they are looking.

1 min Close

A gold leaf in every hand. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Red… yellow… WINDY! It's AUTUMN!” (gold leaves fly up on AUTUMN)

Got 3 spare minutes? Fletcher and the Falling Leaves (Julia Rawlinson) — a small fox worries about a tree losing its leaves. Read it today, of all days.

Day 5 · Friday 9 October · Windy + the whole song

Today's words: windy (+ all five, all week, one last time)

Grab: a paper fan and every leaf the week collected, in one basket; two baskets or hoops labelled red and yellow; the 风 card; the star awards (page 17), signed and ready.

2 min Magic Box hook

Last box of the week, and it is completely silent. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it. Nothing. Shake it harder. Still nothing. Look worried.

Teacher“It's empty… no, wait.” (lift out the folded paper fan, open it with one snap, and fan the whole front row's hair)

Whoosh both arms sideways and sway. “Windy!”

4 min Teach · Windy

Tip the week's whole basket of leaves onto the mat in one heap. Then kneel down and fan it, hard.

The windy, windy wind… (fan, big sweeps)

goes whoosh and blow! (everyone whooshes with you)

And all the leaves… (hands high)

let go, LET GO! (hands open, everything flutters)

The fan goes round the circle, one child at a time: three fans each, and the rest of the class makes the wind sound. Littles say the word with the whoosh — “Windy!” Bigs take the frame: “The wind blows the leaves down.”

Then blow with your mouths instead: one big class breath, all together, on three. The leaves barely move. Try the fan again. Which is stronger? That is a real question with a real answer, and they can see it.

Teacher fails: fan yourself elegantly and wonder aloud why the leaves are not moving. Point the fan at your own face for a good ten seconds before somebody turns your wrist round.

The 风 card completes the row: 秋 · 叶 · 红 · 黄 · 风. Read it left to right with them once — that row is the week, and it is also your display.

3 min Song · the WHOLE song — this is the review

Chorus → all five verses → chorus. Outside, standing in the fallen leaves, if the weather allows it.

Monday: Summer's packed her bags and gone…   Tuesday: One little leaf let go of the tree…

Wednesday: A red, red flag and a red, red leaf…   Thursday: We went away and the tree turned gold…

Friday: The windy, windy wind goes whoosh and blow…

This run-through IS the week's review. Do not add a second review activity — five verses in order is the five words in order, and they will sing it without knowing they are being assessed. Watch for the two-year-olds doing all four chorus actions: that is a pass.

3 min Game · Red basket, yellow basket

Two baskets, far apart. One leaf at a time, one child at a time: pick it up, hold it high, name the colour, run it to the right basket. The circle calls the colour with them.

Everyone“RED!” … “YELLOW!”

Go until the mat is clear. Then count both baskets out loud — which one won? Fan the winner over the floor and watch it all come down again, and let them groan.

Then the awards: one star each, name read out, on the spot. And the leaf worksheets (page 16) go home with them.

1 min Close

Everyone holding a leaf, fan in the middle. Whisper → normal → biggest shout of the week:

Everyone“Red… yellow… WINDY! It's AUTUMN!” (the whole basket goes up on AUTUMN)

Next week we stay in autumn — the tree finishes giving its leaves away, and the field gives us food.

Theme Song · “Autumn Is Here!”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle, on both sides of the holiday. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The four chords

C
F
G7
Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“leaves — come — down-now”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: unhurried and swaying, like something drifting down — this is a falling song, not a marching song. Slow right down on the Am in the third line, then lift again for “down below!” Chant it without the uke first; add chords once they know the actions.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CAutumn is here — the Fleaves come Cdown, (flutter both hands to the floor)

FRed and yellow Call over the G7town! C(big circle arms, point out)

AmWhoosh goes the wind and Faway they Cgo, (whoosh sideways)

FRound and round Cand G7down beClow! (spin one finger, then crouch)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who flutters, whooshes and crouches has understood autumn, whether or not a single word comes out.

One verse a day — the verses are C and G7, except Thursday's Am

Monday 28 Sep · Autumn

CSummer's packed her bags and gone, (lift a heavy bag, wave goodbye)

G7Autumn's here — Cput your jumper on! (pull on a jumper, hug yourself)

Tuesday 29 Sep · Leaf

COne little leaf let go of the tree, (pinch fingers, then open them)

G7Down, down, down — Cit landed on me! (flutter down, tap your head)

Wednesday 30 Sep · Red

CA red, red flag and a red, red leaf, (wave the flag, hold up the leaf)

G7Goodbye, tree — Cwe'll be back next week! (big slow wave at the window)

Thursday 8 Oct · Yellow

AmWe went away and the tree turned gold, (walk fingers away, arms up like branches)

G7Yellow, yellow — Clook, behold! (point, then open both hands)

Friday 9 Oct · Windy

CThe windy, windy wind goes whoosh and blow, (big fanning sweeps)

G7And all the leaves Clet go, let go! (hands high, then open)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. The holiday sits between Wednesday's verse and Thursday's, and the song is what carries the week across it — open Thursday with the chorus before you say anything else and watch them remember. Friday's finale is the whole thing top to bottom, and it doubles as the review of the entire week.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. Add Am last: it is one finger on the same string as F, so slide from Am to F and back until it stops feeling like a change. That is the entire song — and four of the five verses are only C and G7. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.

Page 18 · Theme shelf guide (for you — tape inside the cupboard)

Theme Shelf · Autumn (1)

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Present all four on Monday and leave every one of them out through the 国庆 holiday — the room should still look mid-week when the children walk back in on Thursday 8 October. What comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Leaf colour sorting
from MONDAY's branch		A green dish, a yellow dish and a red dish, and twenty real pressed leaves. Sort them by colour. Control: a dot of the matching colour painted on the back of each leaf's card — turn it over and check. The Red / Yellow signs (pages 12–13) label the dishes and the 12 sorting cards (page 14) run the same work on card. Language: “Red!” / “Yellow!” Bigs: “The leaf is red.”
Tray 2 · Leaf-shape matching
from TUESDAY's basket		Five pairs of real leaves — ginkgo, maple, poplar, willow, oak — laid face-down. Turn two, find the two that match. Control: matching outlines drawn on the mat underneath. Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Sep 28–30 · Oct 8–9 · Theme: Autumn (1) · Sound of the week: m

What we did

A week with a holiday in the middle of it — and we used the hole on purpose. Before 国庆节 we photographed a branch that was only just turning gold, and asked the tree to carry on changing without us. When we came back on 寒露 morning we held that photograph up next to the real thing, and the children named everything that had changed. In between they sorted real leaves into red and yellow, became leaves themselves and spun to the floor on the word windy, hunted five red things for the 红旗, and on Friday blew the whole basket across the mat with a paper fan.

Words we learned

Sentences to listen for at home

“It is autumn.” · “The leaf is red.” · “The wind blows the leaves down.” · “The tree changed while we were away.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Leaf!” with a hand fluttering down to the floor, or “Windy!” with both arms whooshing sideways, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Autumn Is Here!”

“Autumn is here — the leaves come down, red and yellow all over the town! Whoosh goes the wind and away they go, round and round and down below!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — three before the holiday, two after it. Ask your child to sing the chorus; it is a finger-play, so watch their hands, not just their words.

国庆节 · 寒露 · the tree that kept going

We stopped on Wednesday 30 September, waved the 红旗 and said 再见 to the tree, and came back on Thursday 8 October — which was 寒露, Cold Dew, the morning the dew turns cold to touch. We went out and felt the railing first thing: 凉不凉？ Then we looked at the photograph. Beijing's 银杏 trees all turned gold at once over the holiday, and every child in this class walked past it without being told to look. Now they have been told.

Dark Phonics · the m sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met m — a long, humming “mmm” with your lips shut: mmm, mist, maple. Our silly line for it is “mmm, muddy monkey!” Put your lips together, hum, and touch the side of your nose — it buzzes, and children love that it does. It fitted the week: the maple leaf was Wednesday's whole lesson, and every foggy autumn morning is a mist. Listen for it in: mmm, me, my, mud, map, mist, maple.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · On the way home, find one red leaf and one yellow leaf and bring them back to school on Monday.
2 · Ask: “What happened to the tree while you were away?” — and wait for the whole answer.
3 · Stand outside for ten seconds and feel whether it is windy today. Whoosh with your arms if it is.
4 · Put a leaf under a sheet of paper and rub over it with a crayon lying flat. It appears like magic.
5 · Hunt for the m sound: me, my, mud, map, mist, maple — lips together, hum it long.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
