Whale Class · Weekly Circle Time

Chinese New Year

Week of Feb 1–5 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

📖 Guide Book

Print · laminate · ring-bind — your in-circle guide

Five words they'll own by Friday

red packetlanterndumplinglionvisit

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Red!” (hold up a red packet), “Boom!” (one big clap — firecracker), “Lantern!” (both hands make a round shape).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “Happy New Year!” · “I have a red packet.” · “We eat dumplings together.” · “Thank you!”

The one big idea: the New Year is coming, and we get ready together. This is not a week about a festival that has passed — it is the countdown to it. Every day we make one more thing ready: the red packets, the lanterns, the dumplings, the lion, and then we say it to each other. 年 is coming, and we are ready.

The dates, so you can say them out loud all week. 除夕, New Year's Eve, is this Friday, 5 February — the night families sit down together. 春节 itself is Saturday 6 February, and it opens the Year of the Goat 羊年. 立春, the first day of spring, falls on Thursday 4 February. Our last day is Friday, the holiday runs 8–26 February, and we are back in class on Monday 1 March.

Day 1 · The Red Packet

Today's words: red packet

Grab: one red packet with a sweet inside for the Magic Box, and one for every child, kept out of sight until the end; music you can start and stop; a long strip of red paper to pin up as the week's display; the 红 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is light, and something inside it slides when you tip it. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a small dry rattle. Sniff it — nothing. Peek in and let your eyes go enormous. Then lift out one red packet, very slowly, held in both hands, the way it is properly given.

Teacher“It's RED… and there is something inside it.” (hold it up, shake it once beside your ear)

Everybody holds up an imaginary one: “Red!”

4 min Teach · Red packet

The red packet stays in your two hands the whole way through. Say it twice; the second time they do the actions with you:

A little red packet, (hold it up in both hands)

Held in two hands, (offer it out flat, both palms up)

“Thank you, thank you!” (fist in palm, one small bow)

The New Year is grand! (both arms thrown wide)

Then the whole thing, done properly, once, with one child: you offer it with two hands and a small bow; they take it with two hands, bow back, and say “Thank you!” Nobody snatches, nobody opens it. Two hands is the lesson.

Littles hold theirs up and say “Red!” Bigs owe you the frame: “I have a red packet.” “Thank you!”

Teacher fails: take the red packet back with one hand, snatched, and stuff it straight in your pocket. Look pleased with yourself. Wait for the outrage.

Chinese moment: pin the 红 card at the top of the red strip — 红包. Say 恭喜发财 once, all together, with the fist-in-palm. The row of 汉字 grows by one card every morning this week.

What is inside is not the point. One sweet in each is plenty, and it keeps every packet the same. If a family sends money in, it goes home in the bag unopened.

3 min Song · Gong Xi, Happy New Year! — chorus + Monday verse

Gong xi, gong xi — Happy New Year! (fist in palm, one bow)

Red and gold and lanterns here! (both hands make a round lantern shape)

Boom! Boom! Boom! The firecrackers go, (three big claps)

Happy New Year — hello, hello! (wave, then bow to the friend beside you)

Monday's verse: A little red packet in my hand, (hold both hands out flat)

“Thank you, thank you!” — the New Year's grand! (fist in palm, bow; then arms wide)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole thing on Friday. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. The three claps are the hook; teach them before the words.

3 min Game · Pass the red packet

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody in a circle, knees touching. One red packet goes round hand to hand while the music plays — two hands to give, two hands to take.

Teacher“Music on — pass it. Music STOPS — whoever is holding it stands up!” (they bow, fist in palm, and say it)

The child standing says “Happy New Year!” and the whole class bows back. Littles just say “Red!” and hold it up — that is a full win. Then the music starts again.

Stop the music so that everybody gets one turn. Watch who has not stood up yet and stop it on them; this game is only fun if it is fair, and they are counting.

1 min Close

Everybody's hands held out flat, ready to receive. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Red packet… lantern… DUMPLING! GONG XI FA CAI!” (hands out · round lantern hands · fold a dumpling · fist in palm and bow on the shout)

At the door: every child gets their own red packet to take home, handed over with two hands, one at a time, and a bow. It takes four minutes and they will remember it all year.

Got 3 spare minutes? Bringing In the New Year (Grace Lin) — one family getting ready, day by day, ending in a dragon that unfolds across the last spread. It is this exact week in a book.

Day 2 · Lanterns Everywhere

Today's words: lantern (red packet comes back every day)

Grab: one red paper lantern in the Magic Box, folded flat; a torch; string already hung across the room at your own head height, empty; the red paper, safe scissors and glue on the shelf tray; the 灯 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is completely flat and completely silent. Chant, then shake it — nothing at all. Press the lid: it does not spring back.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out a flat folded lantern and hold it up. Look disappointed. Turn it over. Then open it, slowly, with both hands, until it is round.

Teacher(the lantern opens out round) “Ohhhh — it was hiding!”

Both hands make the round shape: “Lantern!”

4 min Teach · Lantern

The lantern hangs from one finger while you say it — twice, and the second time they say it with you:

Lantern, lantern, round and red, (both hands make a big round shape)

Hanging, hanging over my head. (hold the round hands up high and let them sway)

The light goes on… (open both hands, twinkling)

and the dark goes away! (throw both arms wide)

Now the room. Lights off, torch inside the lantern, and hold it up: the whole ceiling goes red. Every child gets to hold it for a count of three. Then it goes up on the string, first of many — the rest are made on the tray this week and go up as they are finished.

Littles make the round hands and say “Lantern!” Bigs owe you a sentence: “I can see a red lantern.” “The lantern is over my head.”

Teacher fails: try very hard to hang the lantern on the floor. Crouch down. Pat it. Stand back and admire it. Wait to be corrected.

Chinese moment: the 灯 card joins 红 on the red strip — 灯笼. Ask them where they have seen red lanterns going up this week: the shops, the gate, the street. They will all have seen them; this is the week the whole city turns red.

3 min Song · Gong Xi, Happy New Year! — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice with the actions — on “lanterns here” everybody now points at the real one on the string — then today's new verse:

Tuesday's verse: Lantern, lantern, round and bright, (round hands, held up high)

Shining, shining in the night! (twinkling fingers, slowly turning round)

Sing this verse with the lights off and the torch in the lantern. It takes ten seconds to arrange and it turns a song into something they talk about at home.

3 min Game · Lantern parade

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody takes a lantern — a paper one, a hand-made one, or two round hands if there are not enough — and we walk the room in one long line while the music plays.

Teacher“Walking feet, lanterns up… and when the music stops — FREEZE!” (everything stops, lanterns still held high)

Walk, freeze, walk, freeze. On the last freeze, everybody holds the pose while you count to five out loud and they count with you.

Slow the music each round until the parade is moving at half speed. Walking slowly while holding something up is real work for a three-year-old, and it looks beautiful.

1 min Close

Lanterns up, or round hands up. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Red packet… lantern… DUMPLING! GONG XI FA CAI!” (hands out · round lantern hands · fold a dumpling · fist in palm and bow on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Lights off, every lantern lit from one torch passed round the circle, and sing the chorus once, quietly. Nobody talks in the dark; they just watch.

Day 3 · Folding Dumplings

Today's words: dumpling (red packet · lantern come back)

Grab: a lump of dough and a small bowl of filling in the Magic Box; a rolling pin, a spoon and a floured board; a steamer basket or a tray with five hollows; wooden spoons and five paper dumplings for the relay; the 饺 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box is heavy, and when you tip it something soft thumps from one side to the other.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it: thump. Sniff it: something is in there. Lift out the dough in both hands and hold it up like treasure, then squeeze it and let everybody hear it.

Teacher“It's soft… it's cold… and on Friday night every family in this city will be making these.” (lift out the little bowl of filling and hold it under one child's nose)

Everybody folds an imaginary one: “Dumpling!”

4 min Teach · Dumpling

You make one dumpling, in front of them, slowly, saying each step. They do it in the air with you, twice:

Roll it flat, (two flat hands, rolling)

Spoon it in, (one hand a bowl, the other a spoon)

Fold it over… (bring both palms together)

and pinch, pinch, PINCH! (pinch along the edge with both thumbs and fingers)

Then say the true thing, plainly, because it is the heart of the week: on 除夕 — this Friday night — families sit down together and fold dumplings together, and that is the point of them. Not the eating. The sitting down together.

Littles pinch the air and say “Dumpling!” Bigs owe you the frame: “We eat dumplings together.” “I can fold a dumpling.”

Teacher fails: fold your dumpling with the filling on the outside, hold it up proudly and take a big bite of air. Wait for the shrieking.

Chinese moment: the 饺 card joins 红 · 灯 — 饺子. Say it with the mouth-shape exaggerated, and count five together into the steamer: 一 · 二 · 三 · 四 · 五.

Dough, not food. The circle dough is for folding, not eating — say so once, at the start. Real cooking, if you do it, happens later in the morning with clean hands and an adult at the steamer.

3 min Song · Gong Xi, Happy New Year! — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — today the three claps are done as three pinches instead, high in the air — then:

Wednesday's verse: Fold it, fold it — a dumpling for me, (palms together, then pinch along the edge)

Jiaozi, jiaozi — one, two, three! (hold up one finger, two, three)

Count the “one, two, three” on your fingers and hold them up for the whole class to see. Half the room is learning to count in English and this verse does it without anybody noticing.

3 min Game · Dumpling relay

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two short lines, a wooden spoon each, a paper dumpling balanced on the spoon, and the “steamer” (the tray with five hollows) at the far end.

Teacher“Carry it… slowly… into the steamer — and run back with the spoon!” (dropped it? pick it up, put it back on, carry on — nobody starts again)

Five dumplings in each steamer and the whole class counts them in out loud as they land. Littles carry theirs with a hand steadying it; that is fine and nobody says otherwise.

There is no winning line. When both steamers are full you count them together — five and five — and that is the end of the game. Racing turns it into tears before Friday.

1 min Close

Everybody's palms together, ready to pinch. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Red packet… lantern… DUMPLING! GONG XI FA CAI!” (hands out · round lantern hands · fold a dumpling · fist in palm and bow on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Runaway Wok (Ying Chang Compestine) — a New Year story where the food ends up shared with everybody in the town. Read the wok's song out loud and let them join in on the second time round.

Day 4 · The Lion Dance

Today's words: lion (red packet · lantern · dumpling come back)

Grab: a cardboard lion head and a small drum in the Magic Box; a big cloth or sheet for the lion's body; one green shoot, bulb or twig with a bud on it for 立春; the 狮 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Today the box will not shut properly — something big is inside it — and when you move it, it booms.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Take out the drum first and hit it once, hard. Everybody jumps. Hit it again, softly. Then reach back in with both arms and lift the lion head out over your own head, and make it blink at them.

Teacher(the lion's mouth opens and shuts, once, right at the circle) “He is not a real lion. He is a dancing lion.”

Everybody claws the air: “Lion!”

4 min Teach · Lion — and 立春

The drum sets the speed for everything. Say it twice, the second time with all of them moving:

The big lion dances, (big stamping steps on the spot)

He jumps, jump, JUMP! (three jumps, arms up)

The drum goes boom… (one slow, heavy beat)

and the lion goes STOP. (everything freezes, completely still)

Then two children under the cloth — one is the head, one is the back — and the drum tells them what to do: fast, slow, STOP. Everybody else claps the beat. Swap so that as many pairs as possible get a turn; nobody is made to go under the cloth.

Littles claw the air and say “Lion!” Bigs owe you a sentence: “The lion is dancing.” “The drum goes boom!”

Teacher fails: put the lion head on backwards and walk off in completely the wrong direction, banging into the shelf. Keep going until they turn you round.

Chinese moment: the 狮 card joins the row — 舞狮. And today is 立春, the first day of spring in the old calendar: pass round the shoot with the bud on it and say the plain true thing — it is still cold, but spring has officially started, and the lion is out to say so. 立春了！

The drum is loud. Tell them the rule before you start: when the drum stops, everybody stops. A child who finds it too loud sits with you and holds the beater — they are the one who gets to end it.

3 min Song · Gong Xi, Happy New Year! — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — today the drum plays the three “Boom! Boom! Boom!” and everybody claps with it — then:

Thursday's verse: The big lion dances — jump, jump, jump! (three jumps on the spot)

The drum goes boom — thump, thump, thump! (three big slow beats on your knees)

Do the three “thump”s slower and heavier each time, then freeze dead still. The freeze at the end is what makes them ask to sing it again.

3 min Game · Lion dance

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody is a lion now, not just the pair under the cloth: hands up like paws, knees bent, and the drum runs the whole room.

Teacher(fast drumming) “Fast lions!” (slow, heavy beats) “Slow lions…” (silence) “STOP.”

Fast, slow, stop — and then hand the beater to a Big and let them run the room while you dance in it. That swap is the best thirty seconds of the week.

Three speeds only, over and over. The listening is the lesson: they are matching their whole body to a sound, which is exactly what they must do to hear the difference between two English words.

1 min Close

Everybody on their knees, paws up, ready. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Red packet… lantern… DUMPLING! GONG XI FA CAI!” (hands out · round lantern hands · fold a dumpling · fist in palm and bow on the shout)

Before home time: tomorrow every child bows to every other child. Walk the circle once today and practise it in silence — fist in palm, small bow, eyes up — so that Friday is smooth and nobody is embarrassed.

Got 3 spare minutes? Lion Dance or any picture book with a real photograph of a lion troupe. Find the drummer on the page and let them point at him.

Day 5 · Happy New Year! + Big Review

Today's words: visit — and red packet · lantern · dumpling · lion, the whole week

Grab: every prop from the week packed into the box, plus a tray of sweets (糖果) to share; the finished lanterns on the string; the awards (print pack page 17); the 拜 card. Tonight is 除夕 and the holiday starts on Monday — back on Monday 1 March.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out bulging and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The red packet → “Red packet!”  The lantern → “Lantern!”  The dough → “Dumpling!”  The lion head → “Lion!”

Last of all, the tray of sweets. Hold it out to the child beside you with two hands and a bow. “VISIT!”

4 min Teach · Visit, then the whole week

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

A red packet in my hand, (both hands out flat)

A lantern up above, (round hands, held high)

A dumpling that we fold, (palms together, pinch)

A lion and a drum — (paws up, then three beats)

We VISIT — Happy New Year! (fist in palm, one big bow to a friend)

Then the word itself, done not explained: to visit is to go to somebody's house, knock, bow, and say the words. You knock on the floor, they say “come in”, you bow. Do it once with one child in front of everybody, then everybody has a go on Friday's game.

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “Happy New Year!” “I have a red packet.” “We eat dumplings together.”

Teacher fails: “visit” a child by walking straight past them, out of the door, and shutting it. Come back in looking confused. Let them tell you what you forgot.

Chinese moment: the 拜 card completes the row — 红 · 灯 · 饺 · 狮 · 拜, the whole week on the red strip, and 拜年 is the one that matters: to go and greet people at New Year. Then the three lines everybody says tonight: 新年快乐 · 恭喜发财 · 万事如意. And tomorrow the 羊年 begins — the Year of the Goat. Everybody makes goat horns with two fingers and says 咩.

3 min Song · Gong Xi, Happy New Year! — Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: We visit Grandma, we visit you, (bow one way, then the other)

Happy New Year — I love you too! (hands over your heart, then both arms wide)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, standing under the lanterns with the drum on the last three booms. They learned it one verse a morning without ever noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · New Year visiting 拜年

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody stands in the circle facing a partner. This is the whole week, performed.

Teacher“Fist in palm… small bow… look at them… and say it.” (then everybody takes one step to the left and does it again with the next friend)

Round and round until every child has greeted every child. Bigs use the name: “Happy New Year, Amy!” Littles bow and say “Happy New Year!” — and a bow with no words at all is still a greeting.

When the circle closes, the sweets go round: two hands to give, two hands to take, “Thank you!” every single time.

Say the names out loud with them as they go round. Twenty children hearing their own name twenty times, in English, on the last day before a three-week break, is worth more than any worksheet.

1 min Close + awards

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home with the holiday news. Then, standing under the lanterns, all five gestures:

Everyone“Red packet… lantern… DUMPLING! GONG XI FA CAI!” (hands out · round lantern hands · fold a dumpling · fist in palm and a big bow, with the drum, on the shout)

Say the dates at the door, twice. Tonight is 除夕; tomorrow is 春节 and the Year of the Goat begins; the holiday runs 8–26 February; we are back on Monday 1 March. Send the lanterns home with them — they were made to be hung up.

Got 3 spare minutes? Sing the whole song one more time with every lantern lit and the drum going, and let them leave the room loud. It is the last minute of the term; do not spend it tidying.

Theme Song · “Gong Xi, Happy New Year!”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The three chords

C
F
G7
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“gong — xi — gong-xi”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the class is listening for the three claps, not for you.

Tempo: bright and marching, like a lion troupe coming down the street, with a big rest before “Boom! Boom! Boom!” so the claps land clean. Three chords, no Am — every verse is just C and G7. Chant it without the uke first; add the chords once they know the claps and the bow.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CGong xi, gong xi — FHappy New CYear! (fist in palm, one bow)

FRed and Cgold and G7lanterns Chere! (both hands make a round lantern)

CBoom! Boom! Boom! The Ffirecrackers Cgo, (three big claps)

FHappy CNew Year — G7hello, helClo! (wave, then bow to a friend)

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who bows, makes the lantern and claps the three firecrackers has understood the week, whether or not a single word comes out.

One verse a day — every verse is just C and G7

Monday · Red packet

CA little red packet in my hand, (both hands out flat)

G7“Thank you, thank you!” — Cthe New Year's grand! (fist in palm and bow, then arms wide)

Tuesday · Lantern

CLantern, lantern, round and bright, (round hands, held up high)

G7Shining, shining Cin the night! (twinkling fingers, turning slowly)

Wednesday · Dumpling

CFold it, fold it — a dumpling for me, (palms together, then pinch)

G7Jiaozi, jiaozi C— one, two, three! (one finger, two, three)

Thursday · Lion

CThe big lion dances — jump, jump, jump! (three jumps on the spot)

G7The drum goes boom C— thump, thump, thump! (three big slow beats)

Friday · Visit

CWe visit Grandma, we visit you, (bow one way, then the other)

G7Happy New Year — CI love you too! (hands to heart, then arms wide)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — and Friday's finale, sung standing under the lanterns with the drum on the booms, doubles as the review of the whole week.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those three shapes — there is no Am this week at all. C is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the drum and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the claps and the bow carry the meaning either way.

Theme Shelf · Chinese New Year

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is a circle-time prop that stayed out — what comes out of the Magic Box in the morning goes on the shelf the same day, already loaded with language.

Tray 1 · Fold a red packet
practical life, from MONDAY		Squares of red paper printed with fold lines, a bone folder or a lolly stick, and a small paper “coin” card to slip inside the finished packet. Control: the printed fold lines — a fold that misses them shows immediately. Language: “fold · in · thank you.” Bigs: “I have a red packet.”
Tray 2 · Cut-and-paste lantern
from TUESDAY		Red paper folded in half with cutting lines printed to a stop line, child scissors, a glue stick and a strip for the handle. Cut, open, roll, glue — then it goes up on the string. Control: a finished lantern standing beside the template to copy. This tray fills the room over the week; that is the display, no extra work needed.
Tray 3 · Make dumplings
from WEDNESDAY		Play dough or real dough, a small rolling pin, a spoon, a bowl of “filling” (dried beans work), and a five-hole steamer tray. Roll, spoon, fold, pinch, and set five in the tray. Control: five holes, five dumplings — the tray tells them when they are done. Counting 一二三四五 out loud every time.
Tray 4 · Spring Festival 3-part cards
pages 10–11		red packet 红包 · lantern 灯笼 · dumpling 饺子 · lion 狮子 · visit 拜年, Chinese and English. Littles match picture→picture on the whole control card; Bigs lay the labels underneath. Control: the uncut control cards. Beside it the 12 sorting cards (page 14) and the two signs (pages 12–13): 挂起来 or 吃? And the action cards (page 15) face down: draw one, do it, say it — “I can bow!”

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can — the cards get handled all week and then they go in the box for next February. Everything comes down on Friday afternoon and the lanterns go home with the children — they were made to be hung up, and the holiday starts on Monday.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Feb 1–5 · Theme: Chinese New Year · Sound of the week: v

What we did

We spent the week getting ready for the New Year, together. On Monday a red packet came out of the Magic Box and we learned to give it and take it with two hands and a bow — and every child took one home. On Tuesday a flat piece of paper opened into a lantern, we lit it with a torch in the dark, and the first one went up on the string; more went up every day as they were made. On Wednesday we rolled, spooned, folded and pinched a dumpling, and we said the true thing about it — that on 除夕 families sit down and fold them together, and the sitting down together is the point. On Thursday the drum came out and the lion danced: fast, slow, STOP — and it was 立春, so we passed round a shoot with a bud on it and said that spring had officially started. And on Friday we went round the whole circle bowing to each other, one friend at a time, and said it properly: “Happy New Year!”

Sentences to listen for at home

“Happy New Year!” · “I have a red packet.” · “We eat dumplings together.” · “I can see a red lantern.” · “Thank you!”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Red!” holding up a packet, or “Lantern!” with two round hands, is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Gong Xi, Happy New Year!”

“Gong xi, gong xi — Happy New Year! Red and gold and lanterns here! Boom! Boom! Boom! The firecrackers go, Happy New Year — hello, hello!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — red packet, lantern, dumpling, lion, visit. Ask your child to sing the chorus and watch their hands: they bow, they make the lantern, and they clap the three firecrackers. Then they will want you to clap too.

Dark Phonics · the v sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met v — the buzzing one you make with your top teeth on your bottom lip, and it does not stop: van, vet, very. Our silly line for it is “vroom-vroom van!” It landed in exactly the right week: we visit our family, and we wish them a very happy new year. Put a finger on your throat and say it — v buzzes, f does not. That is the whole difference between the two.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · When the red packets are handed round this weekend, let your child do it properly: two hands, a small bow, and “Thank you!” — then ask them for the English: “Happy New Year!”
2 · Point at the lanterns in the street and let them name them in English — “red lantern”. That is the week's assessment, done in ten seconds.
3 · Let them fold one dumpling on 除夕, badly, and let it go in the pot anyway.
4 · Hunt for the v sound with a finger on your throat: van, vet, very, five, love.
5 · Sing the chorus in the car. It has a bow, a lantern and three claps, and no hard words.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
