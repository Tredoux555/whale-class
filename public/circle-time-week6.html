Week of Oct 12–16 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

Five words they'll own by Friday

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Fall!” (one hand drops from high to the floor), “Cold!” (hug yourself and shiver, brrr), “Climb!” (both hands pulling up a ladder).

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “The leaves fall from the tree.” · “It is cold in autumn.” · “We climb up high.” · “I can ___!”

Day 1 · Tree · One by One

Today's words: tree

Grab: two twigs for the Magic Box — one completely bare, one still leafy; the 树 character card; a space to stand up in.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box is light today, and it rattles like dry sticks. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — tick, tick, tick. Sniff it. Peek in and frown, puzzled.

Teacher“Two sticks. Just… two sticks?” (lift both twigs out together, one in each hand, and hold them high)

Wait. Let them see it themselves: one hand is full, one hand is empty.

Arms up like branches. “Tree!”

4 min Teach · Tree

Both twigs go round the circle, one child at a time — hold the leafy one in one hand and the bare one in the other, and feel the difference.

A tall, tall tree… (stand up, arms wide and high)

Leaves on every branch! (wiggle all ten fingers up high)

One… lets go. (one finger flutters down)

And one… and one… (two more)

Now the tree is BARE. (arms up, every finger still)

Littles say the one word and put their arms up: “Tree!” Bigs take the frame: “The leaves fall from the tree.”

Then take them to the window. Which tree outside is finished? Which one is still going? Nobody has to be right — they only have to look.

Teacher fails: hold up the bare twig and admire it. “Ohhh… look at ALL these beautiful leaves.” Start counting them, seriously. Wait for the roar.

Lay the 树 card beside the two twigs on the mat. Say it once, in Chinese, and leave it there — one character a day, no drilling.

3 min Song · Down, Down, Down the Leaves Come — chorus + Monday verse

Down, down, down the leaves come down, (both hands flutter to the floor)

The tree is bare and the field is brown! (arms up bare, then sweep low)

Cold on my nose and cold on my toes, (touch nose, touch toes)

That's how the autumn goes! (big shrug, both hands out)

Monday's verse: A tall, tall tree with a hundred leaves, (arms up, fingers wiggling)

One by one the autumn takes! (fingers fold away one at a time)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. The chorus is a finger-play: they will do the actions long before they sing the words.

3 min Game · Tree, tree, bare tree

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody stands up and becomes a tree: arms up as branches, ten fingers spread as leaves.

Teacher“The wind is coming…” (one slow clap)

On every clap, one finger folds away. Count them down out loud together — ten, nine, eight… The last clap drops both arms to your sides.

“Bare!”  Bigs: “The tree is bare.”

Play it twice: once slow and sad, once fast and silly. Then let a child be the wind and do the clapping.

Going down from ten is a real counting exercise hiding inside a game. Say the numbers with them, never at them — the Littles join in around six.

1 min Close

Arms up like branches, then everything drops on the shout. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Fall… cold… CLIMB! Happy AUTUMN!” (flutter down · shiver · hands pull up a ladder · both arms fly open)

Got 3 spare minutes? Leaf Man (Lois Ehlert) — every picture is made of real leaves. Stop on each page and ask what it turned into.

Day 2 · Fall · Down They Come

Today's words: fall (tree comes back every day)

Grab: a basket of fallen leaves in the Magic Box — as many as you can gather on the way in, dry ones; the 秋 card; floor space for twenty children to spin and land.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box is fat today, and it rustles. Chant it, then hold it up over your head.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a huge dry rustle. Sniff it — it smells of outside.

Teacher“Ready? Look UP…” (tip the whole basket out from as high as you can reach)

Leaves land on every head. Let the shrieking run for five whole seconds before you say anything.

One hand drops from high to the floor. “Fall!”

4 min Teach · Fall

Everyone picks up one leaf and holds it up as high as their arm goes.

Up on the branch… (arm high, hand still)

Hold on… hold on… (everyone frozen)

Let… go. (fingers open)

Fall, fall, fall… (watch it all the way down, nobody moves)

DOWN! (flat hand on the floor when it lands)

Do it three times. The waiting is the lesson — twenty children watching one leaf come down is the quietest the room will be all week.

Littles say the one word and drop a hand: “Fall!” Bigs: “The leaf falls from the tree.”

Teacher fails: throw your leaf up and announce brightly, “Look! It's falling!” while it is clearly going up. Nod, satisfied. Wait for them to fix you.

The 秋 card goes down beside yesterday's 树. Autumn is the season that takes the leaves off — say it once, in Chinese, and move on.

3 min Song · Down, Down, Down the Leaves Come — chorus + Tuesday verse

Chorus twice — today the flutter-down is enormous and slow — then yesterday's verse if they shout for it, then today's:

Tuesday's verse: Fall, leaf, fall — spin and sway, (turn slowly on the spot, arms loose)

Down to the ground you go today! (sink all the way to the floor and go still)

Sing this one standing. Everybody is a leaf; the last word puts them on the floor. It is the easiest transition to sitting you will ever get.

3 min Game · Leaf catch

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everyone gathers a small handful of leaves and stands in a circle, well spread out.

Teacher“Throw them UP… and catch ONE before it lands!” (throw your own handful straight up)

Almost nobody catches one the first time. That is the joke and they love it. “Fall!”  Bigs: “I caught one leaf!”

Three rounds. Then the quiet version: throw them up and don't catch — freeze, and watch every single one land. Count how long the last one takes.

Finish by sweeping them all back into the basket together, everybody, no exceptions. The tidy-up is part of the game — announce it that way and it stays fun.

1 min Close

Both hands high, fluttering down as the voice comes up. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Fall… cold… CLIMB! Happy AUTUMN!” (flutter down · shiver · hands pull up a ladder · both arms fly open)

Got 3 spare minutes? Fletcher and the Falling Leaves (Julia Rawlinson) — a little fox worries about his tree losing every leaf. Stop before the last page and ask what they think happens.

Day 3 · Cold · Brrr! Dress Warm

Today's words: cold (tree · fall come back)

Grab: a scarf, a woolly hat and one glove in the Magic Box (leave the box by an open window for ten minutes first so it is genuinely cold to touch); a sun hat and a pair of sandals for the sort; the two sorting signs (print pack pages 12–13); the 冷 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

Before the chant, the box goes round the circle and everybody puts one hand flat on it. Say nothing. Watch the faces.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”
Teacher“Ooh… the box is cold.” (shiver from head to toe, hug yourself, teeth chattering)

Peek in, then pull the scarf out slowly and wind it round your neck, twice, with a huge sigh of relief.

Everyone hugs themselves and shivers. “Cold!”

4 min Teach · Cold

Hat, scarf, glove — each one goes on you, in the wrong order, while they watch.

Cold on my nose! (touch your nose, shiver)

Cold on my toes! (touch your toes)

Hat… scarf… glove… (mime each one going on)

Now I am WARM! (big hug of yourself, whole body relaxes)

Littles shiver and say the one word: “Cold!” Bigs take the frame: “It is cold in autumn.” and “I wear a hat.”

Then out to the window or the door: everybody touches the railing or the glass with one finger. 凉不凉? Come back in and touch a friend's warm hand. Cold, warm, cold, warm.

Teacher fails: put the woolly hat carefully on your foot. Sit back, pleased. “Ahh. That's better now, isn't it?” Wait for twenty children to move it for you.

The 冷 card joins 树 and 秋 on the shelf edge. Three down, two to go.

3 min Song · Down, Down, Down the Leaves Come — chorus + Wednesday verse

Chorus twice — today the “cold on my nose, cold on my toes” line gets real shivers — then:

Wednesday's verse: Hat on my head and a scarf round me, (hands on head, then wind a scarf)

Brrr, it's cold — as cold can be! (whole-body shiver, teeth chattering)

Sing it once sitting and once standing with the real scarf being passed from child to child on the beat. Whoever is holding it at “Brrr” does the biggest shiver.

3 min Game · Dress for the cold relay

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The scarf, hat and glove go on a mat at the far end of the room. Two lines, or one line racing the song.

Teacher“Run… put one thing on… run back… and give it to your friend!”

Each child runs, puts on one item, runs back, hands it over. “Cold!”  Bigs, as they hand it on: “It is cold. Put your hat on!”

Then the sort: the two signs (秋天 Autumn and 夏天 Summer) go down at either end of the circle, and the cards come out one at a time. Sandals — which end? Woolly hat — which end?

The sorting cards from page 14 go straight onto the shelf after this, still warm from the game. What they sorted standing up in the circle, they will do again kneeling at a mat — that is the whole trick of this week.

1 min Close

Everybody hugging themselves and shivering, voices growing. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Fall… cold… CLIMB! Happy AUTUMN!” (flutter down · shiver · hands pull up a ladder · both arms fly open)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Mitten (Jan Brett) — one lost mitten in the snow and every animal climbs inside. They will predict who comes next by the third page.

Day 4 · Harvest · The Autumn Basket

Today's words: harvest (tree · fall · cold come back)

Grab: the harvest basket in the Magic Box — corn 玉米, a sweet potato 红薯, chestnuts 栗子 and a persimmon 柿子; a cloth to cover the basket for the game; a chrysanthemum 菊花 and the 菊 card.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box is heavy today. Ask two children to lift it together, and let them fail once.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a solid, dull thud, nothing rattles. Sniff it — it smells sweet and earthy.

Teacher“Something heavy. Something you can eat…” (lift the whole basket out with both arms, slowly, like it weighs a ton)

Both arms out as if carrying a full basket. “Harvest!”

4 min Teach · Harvest

One thing at a time, right round the circle. Everybody holds it, feels the weight, says the word.

Corn — bumpy rows! (run a thumb down the cob)

Sweet potato — heavy and dusty. (two hands, feel the weight)

Chestnut — hard and shiny! (tap it on the floor, tock tock)

Persimmon — soft… careful. (cup it in both palms, very gently)

Littles name the food or just say the week word: “Harvest!” Bigs: “The field gives us food.” and “The persimmon is soft.”

Then the counting: how many chestnuts? Lay them in a line and count together. Put them back in the basket one at a time and count down again.

Teacher fails: take an enormous bite out of the raw sweet potato. Chew. Beam. “Mmm! Delicious!” Offer it round. Wait for the outrage.

The 菊 card and the real 菊花 go down beside the basket. The chrysanthemum is the flower that opens just as everything else is finishing — and it is waiting for tomorrow, when we hold our 重阳节 (the festival itself falls on Sunday 18 October).

3 min Song · Down, Down, Down the Leaves Come — chorus + Thursday verse

Chorus twice — then the only verse of the week that starts on Am, so it sounds different and they notice:

Thursday's verse: Corn and chestnuts, sweet and gold, (hold both hands out, cupped and full)

The autumn field gives all it holds! (big low sweep, then both arms open wide)

Sing this one with the basket in the middle of the circle. On “gives all it holds” everybody's arms open outward — away from themselves, not towards. Tomorrow they give the cake away; this is where that starts.

3 min Game · Feel the harvest

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The cloth goes over the basket. Nobody looks in again.

Teacher“Hand in… no peeking… name it before you pull it out!” (eyes up at the ceiling, hand under the cloth)

Narrate your own turn first: “Ooh… it's bumpy… it's got little rows… it's CORN!” Out it comes.

“Corn!”  Bigs: “I have the sweet potato. It is heavy.”

Every child gets a turn — about eight seconds each. Right or wrong, everybody claps: the basket is its own control of error, you pull it out and you see.

Put the persimmon in last, and warn them it is soft before the first hand goes in. One squashed persimmon is a good story; four is a floor to mop.

1 min Close

Arms out as if carrying a heavy basket, voices growing. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Fall… cold… CLIMB! Happy AUTUMN!” (flutter down · shiver · hands pull up a ladder · both arms fly open)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Little Red Hen — a whole harvest from seed to bread, and nobody helps. They will be furious on her behalf, which is exactly right.

Day 5 · Climb 登高 + Big Review

Today's words: tree · fall · cold · harvest · climb — the whole week

Grab: 重阳糕 (or any steamed cake) wrapped up, a chrysanthemum 菊花 and a walking stick; every prop from the week back in the box (the two twigs, the leaf basket, the scarf, the harvest basket); 王维's poem printed big; the awards (print pack page 17); the 高 card; coats on — we are going outside.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box comes out full, heavy, rustling, and smelling faintly of cake.

Teacher“Today the box has the WHOLE WEEK in it. What's this?”

Pull the props out one at a time and hold each one up in silence — they will supply the word before you can open your mouth.

Bare twig → “Tree!”  A handful of leaves, dropped → “Fall!”  Scarf → “Cold!”  Corn → “Harvest!”

Last of all, the walking stick and the cake. Everyone waits. Hands pull up a ladder. “CLIMB!”

4 min Teach · Climb, then the whole week

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

A tall, tall TREE! (arms up wide and high)

The leaves FALL down! (flutter all ten fingers to the floor)

Brrr — it's COLD! (hug yourself and shiver)

The HARVEST is in! (both arms out, carrying a full basket)

Up we CLIMB! (both hands pulling up a ladder, higher and higher)

Then coats on, and out. Climb the steps, or the little hill, or the slope by the gate — whatever you have. At the top, everybody stops and looks out at everything. Out of breath is the right way to arrive.

Read 《九月九日忆山东兄弟》王维 up there: “独在异乡为异客，每逢佳节倍思亲。遥知兄弟登高处，遍插茱萸少一人。” Then ask, quietly: 你想谁? Who do you wish were here with us?

Teacher fails: set off down the steps announcing “Up we go! Up, up, UP!” Keep going down, cheerfully. Wait to be turned round.

The 高 card completes the row: 树 · 秋 · 冷 · 菊 · 高. Point along it and let them tell you what each one was.

3 min Song · Friday verse, then the WHOLE song

Friday's verse: Up we go, up so high, (climb an imaginary ladder, all the way up)

Look at the world — and wave goodbye! (shade your eyes, look out, then wave big)

The finale: chorus → Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday → chorus, sung at the top of the climb with the 重阳糕 waiting at the bottom. They learned it one verse a morning without noticing, and this run-through is the review of the whole week.

3 min Game · 登高 — climb high, then give the cake away

The climb is the game. Up together, look out together, and come down together holding the rail.

Teacher“The cake is not for us. Who is it for?” (hold up the wrapped 重阳糕)

At the bottom, unwrap it — and then don't eat it. Every child carries a piece to the grandparents at the gate. “For you!”  Bigs: “Happy 重阳节! This is for you.”

If the weather is against you, climb the indoor stairs to the top landing and look out of the highest window you have. It still counts. The cake still goes to the gate.

重阳节 is also 老人节, Elders' Day. Giving the cake away instead of eating it is the whole festival in one thirty-second act — don't explain it, just do it and let them feel it.

1 min Close + awards

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home with a leaf from the basket. Then, standing, with all five gestures:

Everyone“Fall… cold… CLIMB! Happy AUTUMN!” (flutter down · shiver · hands pull up a ladder · both arms fly open on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? We're Going on a Bear Hunt (Michael Rosen) — a whole-body chant about going over and under and up. Do it with the actions, standing, after a week of climbing.

Theme Song · “Down, Down, Down the Leaves Come”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The four chords

C
F
G7
Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“leaves — come — down-a”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: slow and swaying, like something drifting down — this is not a marching song. Let the Am in the third line sit for a beat longer than feels comfortable, then lift into “That's how the autumn goes!” Chant it without the uke first; add chords once they know the actions.

Sing the chorus twice each time. The actions are the whole point — a two-year-old who flutters both hands to the floor and touches their nose and toes has understood autumn, whether or not a single word comes out.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. Add Am last: it is one finger on the same string as F, so slide from Am to F and back until it stops feeling like a change. That's the entire song — and four of the five verses are only C and G7. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.

from THURSDAY's basket		Chestnuts and acorns, a pair of tongs, a bowl and a segmented tray. Control: five hollows, five chestnuts — the tray tells you when you are finished. Crayons and the “My Autumn Tree” sheets (page 16) go here too; sheets go home Friday with the award (page 17).

Laminate pages 10–15 if you can. Pin-punching needs a mat and a rule: one child, sitting down. Keep the week's real leaves in a flat basket at the end of the shelf — by Friday every tray on this shelf came out of the Magic Box first, and they know it.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

Oct 12–16 · Theme: Autumn (2) · Sound of the week: d

What we did

The full autumn week. On Monday two twigs came out of the Magic Box — one bare, one still leafy — and the children made themselves into trees and dropped their branches one finger at a time, counting down from ten. On Tuesday a whole basket of leaves came down over their heads from above, and we held one leaf up high, let go, and watched it all the way to the floor without a sound. On Wednesday the box was genuinely cold to touch: we raced to dress for a cold morning with a scarf, a hat and one glove, and sorted autumn clothes from summer ones. On Thursday hands went into a covered harvest basket to name corn, a sweet potato, chestnuts and a persimmon by feel alone. On Friday we climbed high for 重阳节, read 王维's poem at the top out of breath, and carried the 重阳糕 down to the grandparents at the gate instead of eating it ourselves.

Sentences to listen for at home

“The leaves fall from the tree.” · “It is cold in autumn.” · “We climb up high.” · “The field gives us food.” · “This is for you.”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Fall!” with a hand dropping to the floor, or “Cold!” with a big shiver, is a real win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Down, Down, Down the Leaves Come”

“Down, down, down the leaves come down, the tree is bare and the field is brown! Cold on my nose and cold on my toes, that's how the autumn goes!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning, then sang the whole thing at the top of Friday's climb. Ask your child to sing the chorus — it is a finger-play, so watch their hands, not just their words.

Dark Phonics · the d sound

In our Dark Phonics work we met d — a quick tap of the tongue right behind the top teeth: down, dry, dig. Our silly line for it is “dirty dog, dig dig dig!” It fitted the week perfectly: every dry leaf this week went down. Listen for it in: down, dry, dig, dad, mud, red, cold.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · Find one bare branch and one that still has leaves on your walk home: “Which tree is finished?”
2 · Hold a leaf up as high as you can, let go together, and watch it all the way down without talking.
3 · Getting dressed in the morning: “Is it cold today? What do we need?”
4 · Let your child put a hand in the shopping bag and name one thing by feel before pulling it out.
5 · Hunt for the d sound: down, dry, dig, dad, red — a short, quick tap, not “duh”.

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
