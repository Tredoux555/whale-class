Our Solar System! Round and Round the Sun

Week of May 10–14 · 13 min a day · ages 2.5–6, English learners

Five words they'll own by Friday

sunmoonEarthroundhot

Littles (2.5–3)

One word + a gesture is a win: “Sun!” and draw a big circle with both arms, “Hot!” and fan your face, “Round!” and spin.

Bigs (4–6)

Sentence frames: “The sun is hot.” · “The moon is round.” · “Earth is my home.” · “It goes round and round.”

Day 1 · The Sun

Today's words: sun · hot · round · light

Grab: a torch and a big yellow ball (or a yellow balloon) in the Magic Box; a warm water bottle and an ice pack in a bag behind you; a black cloth on the floor for the sun to sit on all week.

2 min Magic Box hook

Shake the box, hold it to your ear. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek in. Gasp. Lift out the yellow ball with both hands, high above your head, and switch the torch on behind it.

Teacher“A ball? … No! It's the SUN!” (hold it up, turn slowly so everyone gets the light) “Big. Round. And HOT!” (fan your face)

Everyone fans their face. “Hot!”

4 min Teach · Sun, hot, round

Set the sun down on the black cloth in the middle of the circle. Say and do each line three times, getting faster:

The sun is a big ball! (draw a huge circle with both arms)

The sun is round! (circle again, smaller and faster)

The sun is HOT! (fan your face, blow out)

The sun gives us light! (open both hands like a starburst)

Littles say the one word and do the gesture — “Sun!”, “Hot!”, “Round!”. Bigs give the whole frame: “The sun is hot.”

Teacher fails: pick up the ice pack, hold it like the sun and say “Mmm, the sun is nice and cold.” Wait. Let them fix you twice — the correction shout is the loudest English of the day.

3 min Song · Round and Round — chorus + Monday verse

Round and round and round we go! (everyone spins on the spot)

Round the sun — go slow, go slow! (walk one slow circle round the yellow ball)

The sun is hot, the moon is cold, (fan your face, then shiver)

And Earth is my home, I am told! (hug yourself)

Monday's verse: The sun is hot! The sun is bright! (fan, then starburst hands)

It keeps me warm and gives me light! (arms crossed and warm, then starburst again)

The one song of the week — same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning. Chords and the full sheet are on the Song tab. Sing it twice.

3 min Game · Hot or Cold?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Bring out the warm water bottle and the ice pack. Pass them round the circle in opposite directions while everyone chants “Hot or cold? Hot or cold?”

Teacher“Bao-Bao — hot or cold?”

“Hot!”  Bigs: “The bottle is hot, like the sun.”

Every few passes, point at the yellow sun on the cloth and ask “And the sun?” — the whole class fans and shouts “HOT!”

Littles who won't speak still choose — they fan their face or shiver. That is the answer; accept it and cheer it. The bottle and the ice pack go straight onto Tray 2 this afternoon.

1 min Close

Everyone puts both hands up like a sun, whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“The sun is hot… the sun is hot… THE SUN IS HOT!”

Got 3 spare minutes? The Sun Is My Favorite Star (Frank Asch) — don't read it, do it. Every page, the class turns their face up to the sun and smiles.

Day 2 · Our Earth

Today's words: Earth · home · blue · green

Grab: a globe (or any blue-and-green ball) in the Magic Box; yesterday's yellow sun still on the black cloth; nothing else.

2 min Magic Box hook

Shake the box, hold it to your ear. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek in. Gasp. Lift out the globe and hold it against your chest like something precious.

Teacher“A ball? … It's EARTH. It's where we live.” (turn it slowly) “Blue is water. Green is land. And here —” (put one finger on China) “— is us.”

Everyone points at the floor. “Earth!”

4 min Teach · Earth is my home

Turn the globe as you chant. Every child copies the gesture:

Earth is round! (big circle with both arms)

Earth is blue — that's the water! (wavy swimming hands)

Earth is green — that's the land! (pat the floor twice)

Earth is my home! (hug yourself tight)

Now set Earth on the black cloth, a little way from the sun, and walk it one slow lap round. “Round and round!”

Littles: one word plus the hug — “Earth!”. Bigs: “Earth is my home.”

Teacher fails: hold up the globe and announce “This is the sun!” with total confidence. Let them shout you down, then look amazed and say it properly.

3 min Song · Round and Round — chorus + Tuesday verse

Round and round and round we go! (spin on the spot)

Round the sun — go slow, go slow! (one slow circle)

The sun is hot, the moon is cold, (fan, then shiver)

And Earth is my home, I am told! (hug yourself)

Tuesday's verse: Earth, Earth, my home is Earth — (pat the floor on each “Earth”)

Blue and green, the best on Earth! (wavy hands, then pat the floor, then arms wide)

Sing the chorus, then the verse, then the chorus again. Same shape every single day — that repetition is the lesson.

3 min Game · Pass the Earth

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Sit in a tight circle. Pass the globe hand to hand while the class sings the chorus. You stop the singing whenever you like — whoever is holding Earth stands up and says their line.

Teacher“You've got the whole world in your hands! What is it?”

“Earth!”  Bigs: “Earth is my home.”

That child then gets to be the one who walks Earth one lap round the sun before passing it on.

Pass with two hands and say “careful — it's our home” every time. They will start saying it to each other, and the globe goes onto Tray 1 looking after itself.

1 min Close

Everyone hugs themselves, whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Earth is my home… Earth is my home… EARTH IS MY HOME!”

Got 3 spare minutes? Here We Are (Oliver Jeffers) — read only the two Earth pages, then close it. Short and enormous.

Day 3 · The Moon

Today's words: moon · night · round · cold

Grab: a white ball poked all over with a pencil end so it is full of little craters, in the Magic Box; the torch; a room you can make dark (curtains or the light switch); the sun and Earth still on the cloth.

2 min Magic Box hook

Shake the box, hold it to your ear. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek in. Whisper this time — the moon is a quiet thing. Lift out the cratered white ball.

Teacher(whispering)“The moon… white… round… and full of little holes.” (run one finger over the craters, then pass it so every child can feel three craters)

Whispered back, all together: “Moon!”

4 min Teach · The moon comes at night

The moon is round! (circle with both arms)

The moon is white. (hands flat on cheeks)

The moon is cold — brrr! (shiver, arms crossed)

The moon comes out at night! (hands together beside your cheek, eyes closed)

Now the comparison, sun in one hand, moon in the other, three times fast:

Teacher“Sun — hot! Moon — cold!”

“Hot! Cold!”  Bigs: “The moon is round and cold.”

Set the moon on the cloth and walk it one lap round Earth — not round the sun. Littles just say “Moon!” and spin.

Teacher fails: hold the moon up and say “Careful — it's HOT!” and pretend to burn your fingers. They will love telling you it's cold.

3 min Song · Round and Round — chorus + Wednesday verse

Round and round and round we go! (spin on the spot)

Round the sun — go slow, go slow! (one slow circle)

The sun is hot, the moon is cold, (fan, then shiver)

And Earth is my home, I am told! (hug yourself)

Wednesday's verse: The moon is white, the moon is round, (hands on cheeks, then a circle)

It comes at night without a sound! (sleepy hands, then finger on lips — sing this line in a whisper)

Wednesday's verse starts on Am — the sad little moon chord. Play it once on its own first and ask “happy or sleepy?” They hear it instantly.

3 min Game · Day and Night

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Lights on is day; lights off with the torch is night.

Teacher(lights on)“DAY! The sun is up!”

“Sun!” — everyone stands, stretches tall and wiggles.

Teacher(lights off, torch on the white ball)“Night… the moon is up…”

“Moon!” — everyone curls up on the floor and snores. Bigs whisper: “The moon comes at night.”

Speed it up. Day, night, day-night-day-night. Finish on night, whispering.

This is the fastest way to calm the wildest circle you will ever have — end on night and they are already quiet for the close.

1 min Close

Still curled up, whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“The moon is round… the moon is round… THE MOON IS ROUND!”

Got 3 spare minutes? Papa, Please Get the Moon for Me (Eric Carle) — open the big fold-out page slowly and say nothing at all.

Day 4 · The Planets

Today's words: planet · round · big · small

Grab: fruit planets in a basket — a peppercorn, a grape, a small apple, an orange, a grapefruit and a watermelon; the yellow sun on the black cloth; the Magic Box (the watermelon will not fit — that's the joke).

2 min Magic Box hook

Shake the box, hold it to your ear. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tip out the peppercorn — one tiny thing. Look disappointed. Then heave the watermelon out from behind your back with a groan.

Teacher“One small…” (hold up the peppercorn between two fingers) “…and one BIG!” (stagger under the watermelon) “Both round. Both planets!”

Everyone makes a tiny circle with two fingers, then a huge one with both arms. “Small! BIG!”

4 min Teach · Every planet is round

Hand one fruit to each of six children. One at a time they bring it to the cloth and place it in a line beside the sun, smallest nearest you:

Round like a pepper! (tiny circle, two fingers)

Round like a grape! (circle, thumb and finger)

Round like an apple! (one hand)

Round like a watermelon — ROUND, ROUND, ROUND! (both arms, and everyone spins)

Point along the line: “Planet. Planet. Planet. Planet.” Littles chorus “Round!” on each one. Bigs: “The planet is big.” · “The planet is small.”

Teacher fails: put the watermelon at the small end and the peppercorn at the big end. Stand back and admire it. They will physically get up and fix it — let them.

3 min Song · Round and Round — chorus + Thursday verse

Round and round and round we go! (spin on the spot)

Round the sun — go slow, go slow! (one slow circle)

The sun is hot, the moon is cold, (fan, then shiver)

And Earth is my home, I am told! (hug yourself)

Thursday's verse: Eight round planets in a row, (count eight on your fingers)

Round and round the sun they go, go, GO! (three big spins, faster each time)

Don't teach the eight planet names — that is not this week's job. “Eight” and “round” are plenty.

3 min Game · The planet parade

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Six children each carry one fruit planet. The rest of the class are the judges and chant “Small to big! Small to big!” while the six shuffle themselves into a line by size.

Teacher“Is that right? Small… to… BIG?”

“YES!”  or  “NO!” — and the judges point at who must swap.

Then the parade walks one slow lap round the yellow sun while everyone sings the chorus. Swap the six and run it again.

The whole line-up moves straight onto Tray 3 this afternoon with balls or pom-poms instead of fruit — the fruit becomes snack.

1 min Close

Everyone holding a circle shape in the air, whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Every planet is round… every planet is round… EVERY PLANET IS ROUND!”

Got 3 spare minutes? Me and My Place in Space (Joan Sweeney) — show the pictures, name only sun, Earth and moon, skip the rest.

Day 5 · Round and Round

Today's words: sun · moon · Earth · round · hot — all five

Grab: everything, laid out on the black cloth before they come in — sun, Earth, moon, the fruit planets; the planet sheets (page 16) and crayons on the table; the awards (page 17) signed and hidden.

2 min Magic Box hook

The box sits on the cloth, empty and open. Chant anyway:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”
Teacher“Nothing! Because it's all out here!” (whip the cloth's corner back — the whole solar system is laid out) “Look what we made this week!”

Point at each one and let them name it, fast: “Sun!” “Earth!” “Moon!” “Planet!”

4 min Teach · Quick-fire review

Hold each object up, one second each, round and round the circle until it's a game:

Sun — hot or cold? “HOT!” (fan)

Moon — hot or cold? “COLD!” (shiver)

Earth — whose home? “MY home!” (hug)

All of them — what shape? “ROUND!” (spin)

Littles need only the shout and the gesture. Bigs, one each, get a full sentence: “The sun is hot.” · “The moon is round.” · “Earth is my home.”

Teacher fails: ask “Moon — hot or cold?” and answer it yourself with a confident “HOT!”. By Friday they will correct you before you finish the word.

3 min Song · Round and Round — the whole song, top to bottom

Chorus → Monday's sun verse → chorus → Tuesday's Earth verse → chorus → Wednesday's moon verse (whisper it) → chorus → Thursday's planet verse → chorus → Friday's verse → chorus!

Friday's verse: Round and round and round and round, (spin, spin, spin, spin)

Everybody spin around! (everyone spins and flops down)

Friday's finale is the review. Five verses, five topics, no worksheet — they will sing back the whole week without noticing they are being tested.

3 min Game · Walk the orbit

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Put the yellow sun in the middle of the floor. Everybody holds hands in one big ring around it.

Teacher“We are Earth. Earth goes round the sun — slowly, slowly…”

Walk one whole lap singing the chorus, slowly. Then: “Faster!” — one lap running. Then “Stop! Let go! SPIN!” and everyone spins on the spot and falls over laughing.

“Round and round!”  Bigs: “Earth goes round the sun.”

Then to the table: each child colours their own planet sheet, you scribe their sentence, and they get their award. Sheets go on the theme wall, awards go home.

1 min Close

One last ring, hands joined, whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Round and round the sun… round and round the sun… ROUND AND ROUND THE SUN!”

Got 3 spare minutes? Go outside, find the sun (don't look at it!), find your shadow, and jump on it. Then say “h-o-t… hot!” in the sunshine — this week's sound, free.

Theme Song · “Round and Round”

The one song of the week — sung at every circle. Same chorus daily, one new verse each morning, full song on Friday.

The four chords

C
F
G7
Am
Strum: Down · Down · Down-Up  (“round — and — round-round”). Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the kids only hear the fun.

Tempo: a slow, rolling waltz-y walking pace — this is an orbit, not a march. Am is one finger, the same finger as F's first shape, and it appears only in Wednesday's moon verse. If it scares you, play C there instead; nobody will mind.

Chorus — sing it every single day

CRound and round and Fround we Cgo! (everyone spins on the spot)

FRound the Csun — go slow, go G7slow! (the whole class walks one slow circle)

CThe sun is hot, the Fmoon is Ccold, (fan your face, then shiver)

FAnd Earth is my G7home, I am Ctold! (hug yourself tight)

Sing the chorus twice each time. On the last “told!” everybody hugs themselves and holds still — that stillness is how you get the next instruction heard.

One verse a day — verses use C and G7 (and one Am on Wednesday)

Monday · The Sun

CThe sun is hot! The sun is bright! (fan your face, then starburst hands)

G7It keeps me warm and Cgives me light! (arms crossed and cosy, then starburst)

Tuesday · Our Earth

CEarth, Earth, my home is Earth — (pat the floor on every “Earth”)

G7Blue and green, the Cbest on Earth! (wavy water hands, then arms wide)

Wednesday · The Moon

AmThe moon is white, the moon is round, (hands on cheeks, then a big circle)

G7It comes at night Cwithout a sound! (sleepy hands — sing this whole line in a whisper)

Thursday · The Planets

CEight round planets in a row, (count to eight on your fingers)

G7Round and round the sun they Cgo, go, GO! (three spins, faster each time)

Friday · Round and Round

CRound and round and round and round, (one spin per “round”)

G7Everybody Cspin around! (everyone spins, then flops down)

The build: each day sing chorus → today's verse → chorus. By Friday they know five verses without ever “learning” the song — Friday's finale is the whole thing top to bottom, and it doubles as your review of the entire week.

If you're brand new to ukulele

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That's the entire chorus — the same three shapes you already used in the last two weeks. Am is a bonus: one finger on the top string, second fret. Play it slowly and let the kids hear how different it sounds.

Four trays, left → right, easiest → hardest. Every tray is something that came out of the Magic Box at circle — present each one the afternoon of the day it appeared, then it's free-choice work for the rest of the week.

from Wednesday's circle (the cratered moon ball)		The cratered white ball + the torch + 4 moon discs (white paper circles: new · crescent · half · full) against a control strip, and crayons with the planet sheets (page 16, one per child) + a stapler. End of Week Wrap-Up

Friday recap — read it at the last circle, then print it for the parent wall or WeChat photo.

Whale Class · Week Wrap-Up 🐳

May 10–14 · Theme: Our Solar System — Round and Round the Sun · Sound of the week: short o

What we did

This week the Magic Box gave us a whole solar system. On Monday a torch and a big yellow ball became the sun — hot, round and full of light — and we passed a warm water bottle and an ice pack round the circle shouting “Hot!” and “Cold!”. On Tuesday a globe came out and we found out that Earth is blue for water, green for land, and home for us; we passed it round very carefully. On Wednesday we felt the little craters on a white moon ball, turned the lights off, and played Day and Night until the whole class was asleep on the carpet. On Thursday we made planets out of fruit — a peppercorn, a grape, an apple, an orange, a grapefruit and a watermelon — and paraded them from small to big. On Friday we held hands, walked one whole orbit round the sun, and every child drew their own planet.

Words we learned

sunmoonEarthroundhotcoldplanetorbitnighthome

Sentences to listen for at home

“The sun is hot.” · “The moon is round.” · “Earth is my home.” · “It goes round and round.” · “The ice is cold!”
Our littlest friends say one word with a gesture — “Sun!” with two arms drawing a big circle is a big win at 2 and 3.

Our song · “Round and Round”

“Round and round and round we go! Round the sun — go slow, go slow! The sun is hot, the moon is cold, and Earth is my home, I am told!”
We sang it on the ukulele every day and added a new verse each morning — sun, Earth, moon, planets, and one for spinning. Ask your child to sing the chorus; they will spin on the first line.

Sound of the week · short o (“hot!”)

In our Dark Phonics work we met the short o — open your mouth into a little round o and say it quick: “o-o-o”. Our catchphrase was “hop on a hot log!”. It suited the week: the sun is hot, and everything up there goes round like the letter itself. Our words: hot, hop, log, on, pot, top. The trick they had to learn: short o is short — not the long “oh” they say in their own names.

Try this at home (2 minutes)

1 · At bedtime, look out of the window: “Where is the sun?” — “Sleeping!” — “Where is the moon?” — “Up!”
2 · Give your child two things from the kitchen, one warm and one from the fridge: “Hot or cold?” Then let them test you and get one wrong on purpose.
3 · Find three round things in the house and line them up small to big — that's Thursday's planet parade, at home.
4 · Say “o-o-o” with a little round mouth, then hunt for one hot thing and one thing on top of something.

Thank you for a lovely week — the Whale Class 🐳

Weekly rituals (same every week, only the theme changes)
  • Attention grabber: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” → kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”
  • The Magic Box: any box with a lid. The chant, the shake, the sniff, the dramatic peek. Whatever the week's theme is, its key prop lives in the box. By week 3 the chant alone will get you silence.
  • The two-tier rule: littles succeed with one word + gesture; bigs get a sentence frame. Never make a little produce a sentence — they'll get there by watching.
  • Whisper → shout closes: end every day chanting the theme line quiet-to-loud. Calms them, then releases them.
  • Teacher fails on purpose: once a day, get something wrong so they can correct you. ESL kids who correct the teacher are producing language without noticing.
