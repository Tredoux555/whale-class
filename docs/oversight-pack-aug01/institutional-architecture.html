1

The flow — one pencil mark, seven steps, one gate

Everything a teacher records travels the same road. The single most important thing on this page is the choke point in the middle: every write to a child's progress passes through writeProgress(). Clean at the source, or not at all — aggregation never fixes dirty data, it amplifies it.

Why the gate is the architectural heart: 15+ independent write paths exist today — some rank-gated, some overwriting, some duplicating, some able to downgrade a mastered work back to presented. They converge into one primitive; direct upserts are deleted. Every cleanliness rule lives at the write path, never at the report layer — which is what keeps institutional data clean forever after.
2

The hierarchy — and who sees what

Four levels, two new roles (org_admin, org_viewer). Each level sees one level down in detail, two levels down on demand. Every org query filters by the institution's school set.

LevelSees in detailCan drill toNever sees
Institutionorg_admin / org_viewer Every school in the institution — SPI, coverage, areas, flags A child's progress, via the school drill-down Observation notes, photos, schools outside the institution
Schoolprincipal All classrooms and all children in the school Work-level detail for any child Any other school
Classroomteacher Own classroom's children Every observation and work for those children Other classrooms
Observation notes and photos stay school-scoped by default. Institutions see progress, not surveillance — a privacy posture that matters to AMI-affiliated buyers.
Enforced in code, not in the UI: verifyOrganizationRequest() mirrors the existing verifySchoolRequest(), the JWT carries organizationId, and every org query filters by the institution's school set.
3

The curriculum spine — what makes School A comparable to School B

Today every classroom owns an independent curriculum copy with its own sequence numbers. That was right for single classrooms; it makes cross-school comparison impossible. The single most important structural change is one master spine with stable work IDs.

Today — each classroom its own copy

Three different meanings of “60%”.
Progress is keyed by work_name TEXT, not an ID — rename a work and its history orphans; a Chinese name becomes a separate row. Cross-school joins are string matching. There is no join path at all.

Tomorrow — one master spine

Two children, two schools, one line — finally comparable.
One row per work in montree_master_works; classroom rows become overlays via master_work_id. Backfilled by the Jaro-Winkler matcher that already exists. Custom classroom works stay local — and stay out of cross-school comparison.

The reading doctrine — visuals first, numbers second, analysis third

Every dashboard page, at every level, is the same three stacked bands. No exotic chart types. Nothing appears in a bar that can't be verified in the table underneath.

1

BARS — understood in three seconds

Simple horizontal bars. Institution: schools ranked by SPI plus an area heatmap. Principal: classes side by side. Teacher: a child's five areas.

Sample: Casa 2, average area progress.

Bars are thin, the grid is a hairline, and the value rides the tip of each bar — the area name carries identity, so the colour never has to. The same five area colours are used everywhere in the product, so a reader learns them once.
Institution level swaps the subject for schools ranked by Progress Index; the shape of the page does not change.

3

AI ANALYSIS — Guru says what the bars mean, and what to do

Written to the level being read, cached nightly with the rollup, regenerated on demand.

Sample narrative · school level “Casa 2's Language average fell 4 points this month; 6 of the 9 stalled children are in the same classroom; coverage there dropped to 40% after the teacher change — this is a data gap, not a performance drop. Suggested action: re-establish weekly sheet capture in Casa 2 before reading these numbers again.”
Same page shape for teacher, principal, institution — only the subject changes: child → class → school
5

Coverage before judgment

Every metric at every level carries a coverage tier — the share of active children with at least one observation in the last 14 days. This protects trust in the numbers, and protects schools from unfair comparison while they are still adopting.

Reliable
≥ 80% of active children observed in the last 14 days. Read the number as written; compare freely.
Partial
50–79% observed. Directional only — show the number, but never rank a school on it.
Low
< 50% observed. The metric is suppressed and the school reads Unknown.
A school that isn't logging data is Unknown — never failing.
Onboarding schools carry an informational flag that suppresses judgment entirely. Every attention flag ships with an evidence sentence and a suggested action, never a bare red dot.
