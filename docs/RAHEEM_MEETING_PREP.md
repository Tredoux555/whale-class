## 2. What YOU need from HIM (this is what makes you look prepared)

Bring these as your questions — the person with a list of questions runs the meeting:

1. **Website:** Where exactly does the logo go — partner strip, dedicated page, blog post? Do they want a short "About Montree" section? (Offer the approved copy.)
2. **Promotion:** What channels (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, newsletter?), what cadence, and when does the first post go out? Offer the ready-made captions and story template — the 1080×1920 story template works for WhatsApp status too.
3. **Tracking:** Ask if he wants a dedicated link for their audience so they can see the response. (You can set up a montree.xyz welcome code — offer it; techies love this and it quietly signals your platform is real.)
4. **Flagship school basics:** Name, location, number of classrooms, rough student count, ages, and language of instruction (English/Swahili/bilingual). Montree supports 12 languages — check before the meeting whether Swahili is one of them, and only mention what you've confirmed.
5. **The humans:** Who's the principal/decision-maker at the school? Who's the most enthusiastic teacher? (Start the pilot with the enthusiast, not the skeptic.)
6. **Devices:** Do teachers use their own phones, or school devices/tablets?
7. **Timeline:** When do they want the school live? Propose a concrete date for a setup call with the school.
8. **Payment practicalities (quietly important):** Montree bills by card via Stripe. In Tanzania, mobile money (M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa) is often how schools actually pay for things and card access varies. The school is free for six months, so nothing is urgent — but ask now how they'd prefer to pay later, so month six is a formality, not a scramble. If card is a problem, "we'll sort a payment route that works for you" is a fine answer in the room.

## 3. What NOT to agree to in the room

- **No custom feature promises.** If they ask "can it also do X?" — *"Let me look at that and come back to you with a yes/no and a date."* You've made custom-feature promises in outreach emails before; in person, keep it to "I'll confirm in writing."
- **No commission numbers.** "Referral partner program, terms in writing this week."
- **No launching all classrooms at once.** One classroom first, nail the week-one wow, then roll out. This is your stated onboarding method, and it's also just true — a botched first week at the flagship school is the one real risk here.
- **No printed prices beyond the founding offer.** For everything else: "pricing's on montree.xyz/pricing, always current."
