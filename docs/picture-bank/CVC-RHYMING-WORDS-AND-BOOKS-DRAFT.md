## The approach (why word families, not random CVC words)

The standard progression for a CVC rhyming reader: pick ONE word family (rime) at a
time — e.g. -at, -an, -ig — so every word on the page rhymes and only changes its
first letter. That's what makes it decodable AND rhyming at the same time. A full
program eventually cycles through every family per vowel; this draft picks a handful
of the most common, most concrete (picturable) families per vowel to start from.

Words in **bold** are concrete, single-object nouns — good candidates for a picture
alongside the text. Plain words are still real, decodable CVC words but are verbs,
function words, or less concrete (sat, ran, wet) — fine inside a sentence, not
necessarily worth their own photo.

## Short A

- **-at**: **cat**, **hat**, **bat**, **mat**, rat, sat, pat
- **-an**: **van**, **fan**, **man**, ran, can, tan
- **-ap**: **cap**, **map**, lap, nap, tap
- **-ag**: **bag**, **tag**, wag, rag
- **-am**: **jam**, **ham**, **ram**, **yam**

**Starter rhyme (-at family):**
> The **cat** sat on the **mat**,
> and wore a floppy **hat**.
> A **bat** flew past — imagine that! —
> and landed on the **mat**.

**Starter rhyme (-an family):**
> A little **van** can go so far,
> it beeps just like a tiny car.
> A **fan** blows wind across the **man**,
> as fast, as fast as **van**s can!

## Short E

- **-ed**: **bed**, fed, led, red, wed
- **-en**: **hen**, **pen**, **ten**, den, men
- **-et**: **jet**, **net**, **pet**, wet, vet, let
- **-eg**: **leg**, **peg**, beg
- **-em**: **gem**, hem

**Starter rhyme (-en family):**
> A little **hen** sat down to write
> with a tiny **pen**, held just right.
> She counted up to **ten** — and then,
> put down her **pen** and rested again.

**Starter rhyme (-et family):**
> A **jet** flew fast across the sky,
> a **net** caught fish that swam nearby.
> My **pet** sat down beside the **net**,
> the happiest **pet** you've ever met.

## Short I

- **-ig**: **pig**, **wig**, **fig**, dig, jig, big
- **-in**: **pin**, **fin**, bin, win, tin
- **-ip**: **lip**, zip, dip, sip, tip
- **-id**: **lid**, kid, bid
- **-ix**: **six**, mix, fix

**Starter rhyme (-ig family):**
> A **pig** put on a curly **wig**,
> then ate a purple, juicy **fig**.
> He tried to dance a happy jig,
> but tripped — that silly, wiggly **pig**!

**Starter rhyme (-in family):**
> A shiny **pin** upon my **fin**,
> a coin fell deep into the **bin**.
> Who do you think is going to win?
> The fish still wears his shiny **fin**.

## Short O

- **-og**: **dog**, **log**, fog, jog
- **-ot**: **pot**, dot, hot, not, got
- **-op**: **mop**, **top**, hop, pop, stop
- **-ox**: **box**, **fox**, ox
- **-ob**: **cob**, job, mob, rob

**Starter rhyme (-og family):**
> A little **dog** sat on a **log**,
> and watched the morning turn to fog.
> He liked to jump and run and jog,
> that happy, hoppy little **dog**.

**Starter rhyme (-ox family):**
> A clever **fox** hid in a **box**,
> beside a big and heavy **ox**.
> "Please don't peek," the **fox** politely begs —
> the **ox** just nods and shakes its legs.

## Short U

- **-ug**: **bug**, **mug**, **rug**, **jug**, dug, hug, tug
- **-un**: sun, bun, run, fun
- **-ub**: **tub**, **cub**, rub, sub
- **-ut**: nut, cut, hut, but
- **-um**: **gum**, **drum**, hum, sum

**Starter rhyme (-ug family):**
> A little **bug** sat on my **mug**,
> then hopped along the woolly **rug**.
> He climbed inside a cold **jug**,
> that tiny, sneaky, wandering **bug**.

**Starter rhyme (-ub family):**
> A fuzzy **cub** climbed in the **tub**,
> and gave his muddy paws a scrub.
> "Time to rub-a-dub-a-dub!"
> said the happy, splashy little **cub**.

## Sample fuller "mini reader" page (short A, -at family)

*(One page, illustrating what an actual future book page could look like —*
*picture-book pacing, one line per page, ending on a rhyme.)*

> Page 1: The **cat** sat on a **mat**.
> Page 2: She wore a big, red **hat**.
> Page 3: A **bat** flew by — imagine that!
> Page 4: It landed right beside the **cat**.
> Page 5: They shared the **mat**. They shared the **hat**.
> Page 6: The end — and that was that!
