sound basket from WEEK_02_T / "A Tiger in the Taxi": turtle · tomato · teddy · toothbrush ·
tiger · taxi. Art run completed 2026-08-02 — all nine pages passed first submission on the

**Placement:** sits at week 4 (p) or later — `Spat` needs s, a, t, **p** all taught. The *cast* is
the /t/ basket, so the book doubles as a letter-t vocabulary review inside a p-week decode.
Runs alongside the existing wk4 *SPAT!* (the sap book) as the low-floor hybrid version.

**Format:** hybrid (teacher narration + one child-owned decodable word per spread).
**Child's owned word:** `Spat` — s,p,a,t. First consonant-blend hero word.
**Frame:** teacher reads "The turtle…", child completes by decoding the big word **`Spat!`**.
Nouns are picture-supported oral vocabulary from the /t/ sound basket; the child names the noun
from the art, then decodes `Spat!`.
**Layout:** teacher narration small grey type up top; `Spat!` BIG; noun never printed (not
decodable — the picture IS the noun).

**Running gag:** the same white porcelain sink on every page. Only the spitter changes — and the
escalation lives in **how much foam is in the basin**, not in the act of spitting.

## THE PROMPT RULES — read this before touching the prompts

Four rounds failed before this run. All four failed the same way: **the characters looked like
they were throwing up.** Diagnosed by inspecting the actual MJ output, 2026-08-02. The causes,
and the rules that fix them:

1. **NEVER draw the spit in flight.** "spitting a stream", "foam caught in mid-air", "blob
   falling into the basin" all produce a hunched, head-down, often quadruped pose with a
   continuous rope of fluid from the mouth to the floor. That IS the vomiting composition —
   no adjective beats it. **Draw the aftermath instead.** The word is `Spat`, past tense:
   mouth shut, chin up, proud, foam already resting in the basin. The picture confirms the
   decode via the foam's *presence*, not its motion.
2. **NEVER use a `--no` on this.** `--no gaping open mouth, vomiting, sick` made it measurably
   worse — naming the concept primes it. Confirmed across a full job of four tiles, every one
   a textbook sick pose.
3. **Anchor the sink with a named fixture.** "plain white background" will strip the room and
   delete the sink, leaving a hunched creature dribbling into a void — which is the whole
   problem. Say "white porcelain bathroom sink" with a faucet/pedestal/basin named. Every
   passing tile in the final run had a fully rendered, physically anchored sink.
4. **Separate the foam clause from the mouth clause.** "a neat dollop of white toothpaste foam
   resting in the sink basin below its chin" keeps the foam contained; any phrasing that links
   mouth and foam in one clause re-opens the stream.
5. **Keep every character proud.** Mood words backfire — "looking sheepish" produced a wide-eyed
   distressed bear. Proud/pleased/deadpan only. Combined with the style's big googly eyes, any
   hunched or open-mouthed pose reads as alarm rather than triumph.
6. **State "upright pose" and "side view at eye level".** Head below shoulder height is the
   single strongest vomit cue.
7. **Escalate the basin, not the act.** "neat dollop" → "heaped and brimming over" → "full of
   foamy water" carries the escalation with zero vomit risk.
8. **Faceless characters are free wins.** The toothbrush and taxi have no mouth, so no queasy
   read is possible — both passed first try.
9. **Foam is white, never red.** Red glossy liquid photographs as blood in this style. Matters
   most on the tomato page.
10. **Taxi pages leak text.** "TAXI" renders on the roof sign despite the no-text clause. Add
    "no text on any vehicle signage" or pick a tile that avoids it.
11. **V8.2, no orefs.** V8.2 does not take `--oref`; consistency comes from repeating the
    character wording verbatim page to page.

## Failed approaches — do not repeat (2026-08-02)

- Detailed mid-air version ("one tidy blob … caught in mid-air falling down into the basin"):
  foam rendered as a disconnected cloud near the faucet, action illegible.
- Simplified version ("a green turtle spitting toothpaste into a basin"): sink dropped entirely.
- "Cheerful / pursed lips / neat stream" + `--no gaping open mouth, vomiting, sick`: worst of
  the lot — hunched quadruped, continuous rope of fluid to a floor puddle, alarmed eyes.
- Teddy with "looking sheepish" + "foam soaking into his cheek fur": brush still in mouth,
  distressed expression, empty sink.
