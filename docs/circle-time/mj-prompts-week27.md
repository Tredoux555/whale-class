# Midjourney prompts — Circle Time, site Week 27 · “The Earth” (Apr 12–16)

Sheet week 27 in the principal's plan. 37 images → `public/circle-time-images/week27/`.
Runbook: `docs/circle-time/mj-prompts-README.md`. Style is LOCKED — do not paraphrase the suffix.

- **8 posters** `--ar 3:4`, saved 928×1232 · **28 cards + 1 badge** `--ar 1:1`, saved 1000×1000
- Flags on every prompt: `--raw --stylize 50 --v 8.2`
- Submit one prompt per 30 s through the authenticated `midjourney.com/imagine` tab.
- Cards are hyper-literal and singular — one object, centred, nothing else in frame. A three-year-old
  has to be able to name the card from across the mat.
- **Every prompt that shows the Earth, a globe or land-and-sea from above** carries *no writing, no
  place names, no flags* on top of the locked negative list. Midjourney letters a globe with
  hallucinated country names the moment you let it, and a card with fake writing on it is unusable
  in a Montessori 3-part-card set. Never ask for a named country, a continent outline you could
  identify, a compass rose, a grid or a legend — the shapes are decorative, the *words* are the
  lesson and they are printed on the page, not baked into the art.
- **Every prompt that shows a person** (six posters and all eight action cards) carries
  *clothed, flat cartoon illustration, children's book art, no photo-realism*: the AI Moderator flags
  people-heavy prompts and this is the wording that clears it. Never give an age in years, never
  describe a body, never mention skin.
- Land and water cards are **plain pieces of the world**, not scenes with people, animals, houses or
  boats in them. The child has to name *the land itself*.
- Save each upscale as the exact `.png` filename below; `mj_convert.sh 27` turns them into the
  `.jpg` the page asks for.
