Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

MONDAY
Thank You!
Today's words: thank you
Grab: a real handwritten thank-you card in an envelope, with one child's name on the front, in the Magic Box; a basket of small things to hand out (a leaf, a pine cone, a card each); the thanks character card. Minor Snow was Sunday — the harvest is in.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is thin and light, and something slides about inside when you tilt it. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — one soft paper slither, nothing more. Peek in for a long time, then look up, astonished.

Teacher“There's a letter in here… and it has somebody's name on the front.” (read the name out slowly; that child comes and fetches it)

They open it, you read it out loud to everybody: “Dear ___, thank you for helping me carry the chairs on Friday. Love, Teacher.”

Flat hands from chins, forward. “Thank you!”

4 MINTEACH · THANK YOU

Teach the gesture first, before the words: a flat hand at your chin, moving forward and down, as if you are handing your words over to somebody.

Somebody grew it… (hold up an apple in two hands)

somebody picked it… (mime picking, high up)

somebody carried it here to me… (carry it across the circle)

so I say THANK YOU! (flat hand from the chin, forward)

Littles the gesture and the one word, “Thank you!” Bigs owe you the name on the end of it: “Thank you, Lily.” Never let the name drop off — that is the whole difference between a manner and a habit.

Then the basket. Go round the circle putting one small thing into each pair of hands; it does not let go until the thank-you comes. Ten seconds each, all the way round.

Teacher fails: hand somebody the apple and immediately say “You're welcome!” yourself, very warmly, before they have said one word. Beam at them. Wait.

Chinese moment: lay the thanks card down. Everybody says thank you once, with the same gesture. Then tell them: Sunday was Minor Snow — the food is picked, the vegetables are salted away, the store is full. That is exactly why this week we say thank you.

3 MINGAME · THANK-YOU BALL TOSS

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody sits in a tight circle with their legs out. One soft ball.

Teacher“Roll it to a friend — but you may not let go until you have said their name.” (roll it slowly to one child: “Thank you, Austin!”)

Littles say the two words and roll: “Thank you!” Bigs must land the name: “Thank you, Stella!”

Keep it going until every single child has had the ball at least once — keep a quiet tally on your fingers so nobody is missed.

The rule that makes this work is no name, no roll. Hold the ball gently in place and wait; the name always arrives, and after two days they start using each other's names all day long.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody's hand flat under their chin, ready. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Thank you… thank you… THANK YOU!” (all twenty hands fly forward from their chins on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Bear Says Thanks (Karma Wilson) — Bear has nothing to give, and by the last page they will have worked out what he gave anyway.

TUESDAY
Please · the Corn
Today's words: please (thank you comes back every day)
Grab: an ear of corn still in its husk in the Magic Box (two or three if you can get them); a bowl for the kernels; music you can start and stop; the please card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is long and heavy, and whatever is inside rolls when you tilt it. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a dry, papery rustle, like leaves. Sniff the opening. Peek in, and frown, puzzled.

Teacher“It's long… it's heavy… and it's wearing a coat.” (lift out the corn in its husk and turn it slowly; peel ONE leaf back in total silence)

Palms together in front of you. “Please!”

4 MINTEACH · PLEASE

Palms flat together, a small bow of the head. Then the four-line exchange, which is the real lesson of the whole week:

Please may I have some corn? (palms together, small bow)

Yes — here you are. (hand it over with both hands)

Thank you! (flat hand from the chin)

You're welcome. (open both palms, warm)

Do it once with one Big, slowly, while everybody watches. Then peel the husk right down, leaf by leaf, and rub a handful of kernels off into the bowl so they can hear them drop.

Littles say the one word with the hands: “Please!” Bigs take the whole frame: “Please may I have some?” and, when they are the one holding it, “You're welcome.”

Teacher fails: snatch the corn straight out of a child's hands with a big delighted grin and no word at all. Freeze exactly there. Wait for the shouting.

Chinese moment: the please card goes down beside yesterday's thanks. please and thank you are the first two words of politeness in any language on earth — say each one once, together, and leave them on the mat.

3 MINGAME · PASS THE CORN

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The corn goes round the circle hand to hand while the music plays.

Teacher“When the music stops — whoever is holding it peels one leaf, and asks the next friend nicely.” (stop the music)

The holder peels one leaf, drops it in the middle, and passes on with “Please!” Bigs give the whole line: “Please may I have some?” and the receiver answers “Here you are.”

The game ends when the corn is completely naked and everybody cheers. The kernels get rubbed off into the bowl and go straight onto Tray 2 for the rest of the week.

Nobody is ever “out”. The corn just keeps going round losing its coat — and because the leaves come off one at a time, the game lasts almost exactly three minutes on its own.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody's palms pressed together. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Please… please… PLEASE!” (palms thrown wide open on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Little Red Hen — who helped plant it? who helped grind it? who gets to eat it? They will have very strong opinions.

WEDNESDAY
Turkey Feathers
Today's words: turkey (thank you · please come back)
Grab: a big hand-print turkey on card in the Magic Box (draw round your own hand — the thumb is the head, the fingers are the tail) and a handful of real feathers, five of them cut shortest to longest; a marker pen; the feather card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box weighs almost nothing and gives the softest dry whisper when you tip it. Chant, shake twice, then peek in for a very long time.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out one feather. Hold it right up above your head, let go, and everybody watches it float all the way down to the floor in silence.

Teacher“Whose feather is that? There's a whole tail in here… and here is the bird it belongs to.” (lift out the hand-print turkey and hold it up)

Elbows out, wobble them. “Turkey!” and the whole class gobbles.

4 MINTEACH · TURKEY

Stand up for this one. Everybody copies, one line at a time:

A turkey is fat, (round arms out in front of your tummy)

a turkey is brown, (pat yourself all over)

a turkey says GOBBLE — (wobble a hand under your chin)

and his tail fans ROUND! (one big fan opened out behind you)

Now show them the trick: your hand is the turkey. Thumb is the head, four fingers are the tail. Everybody makes one in the air and gobbles with their thumb.

Then the five feathers, laid out in a fan shortest → longest, counted along the row. Littles “Turkey!” with the gobble. Bigs “The turkey has a big tail.” “This feather is the longest.”

Teacher fails: push the feathers carefully into the turkey's beak, one at a time, standing back to admire it. “What a beautiful tail.” Hold it. Wait.

Chinese moment: the feather card joins thanks · please on the mat — feather, a feather. Then tell them a turkey in Chinese is turkey, a fire chicken. That is worth exactly one enormous laugh and no drilling whatsoever.

3 MINGAME · THE TURKEY'S TAIL

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The hand-print turkey is propped up where everybody can see it, with a bare tail and a basket of feathers beside it.

Teacher“Take a feather. Put it in his tail — and tell us one thing you are thankful for.” (do the first one yourself: “I am thankful for Whale Class.”)

Littles give one word and it counts completely: “Mama!” “Milk!” “Dog!” Bigs owe the sentence: “I am thankful for my baby brother.”

No feather goes into the tail silently. By the end the turkey has a full fan of feathers and the whole class's week is written on it.

Write each one on its feather as they say it, in front of them, in big letters. Friday's gratitude tree is built out of these — do not skip the writing or Friday loses half its material.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody's hands fanned out behind them like tails. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Gobble… gobble… TURKEY!” (the tail-fan hands thrown right open on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? 10 Fat Turkeys (Tony Johnston) — count them down on ten fingers and let them do the falling-off noises.

THURSDAY
Share the Pie
Today's words: share · pie (thank you · please · turkey come back)
Grab: a real pie (or a loaf, or a plate of steamed buns) in the Magic Box — enough for one small piece each; a cloth tablecloth; one serving plate, one serving spoon, a napkin per child; a knife you keep hold of; the share card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is warm, and it smells. Do not shake it. Carry it slowly round the circle and hold it under three or four noses on the way.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Teacher“Sniff… sniff… oh. Oh my goodness.” (lift the pie out with both hands and hold it high, saying nothing at all for one long beat)

Then the tablecloth: two children take a corner each, shake it right out, and lay it in the middle of the circle. The pie goes in the middle of the cloth.

Hands passing sideways. “Share!”

4 MINTEACH · SHARE & PIE

The whole four minutes is the real thing, done slowly, with the food in your hands.

One pie… (hold it up on two flat hands)

and lots and lots of children! (sweep one arm right round the circle)

So we cut it, and we pass it, (cut once, then pass sideways)

and we SHARE it! (hand a piece to the child beside you)

Cut it where they can all see, and count the pieces out loud until there are enough for every person in the room, you included. Then the exchange, one child at a time, and nobody eats until everybody has some:

Everyone“Please may I have some?” — “Here you are.” — “Thank you.” — “You're welcome.”

Littles do “Please!” and “Thank you!” with the two gestures, and that is a full turn. Bigs give all four lines, and the child serving gives the answers.

Teacher fails: take the biggest piece first, sit straight down and start eating it happily while nineteen children stare at you. Chew. Wait.

Chinese moment: the share card goes down — sharing, to share. Then count the pieces round the circle in Chinese, one piece each, one person one piece, until it comes out exactly right.

3 MINGAME · THE SHARING PLATE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” One plate, one snack, one serving spoon, for the whole class.

Teacher“You do not serve yourself. You serve the friend next to you — and then they serve the next one.” (hand the plate to the first child)

The plate goes right round: each child holds it for their neighbour, who says “Please may I have some?”, takes one piece, and answers “Thank you.” The server says “You're welcome.” Littles hand over a napkin instead of the spoon and say “Please!”

Bigs may hold the serving spoon on their own. The last child in the circle serves you, and you say the words properly too.

Slow it right down. Leave a whole silent second between each line — the silence is what makes them listen for the words. This is Montessori grace and courtesy, and it works precisely because it is real food and a real plate.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody sitting round the cloth, hands cupped as if holding something to pass. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Please… thank you… SHARE!” (hands pushed out flat, offering, on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Stone Soup — everybody puts one thing in the pot and everybody eats. Read it sitting on the tablecloth.

FRIDAY
Gratitude Tree + Big Review
Today's words: thank you · please · share · turkey · pie — the whole week
Grab: the folded gratitude-tree cloth (a big bare tree, no leaves) in the Magic Box with every prop from the week underneath it; Wednesday's written-on feathers; a basket of paper leaves, a pot of pegs and a marker; the awards (print pack page 17); the heart card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box comes out fat and bulging and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it has our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each prop out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word long before you can.

The thank-you card → “Thank you!”  The corn → “Please!”  The feathered turkey → “Turkey!”  The empty pie plate → “Share!” “Pie!”

Last of all, a folded cloth. Shake it right out — a big bare tree with no leaves on it at all. Everybody waits. Hands over hearts: “Thank you!”

4 MINTEACH · THE WHOLE WEEK, THEN THE TREE

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slowly, then fast:

Thank you — for everything! (flat hand from the chin, forward)

Please — may I have some? (palms together, small bow)

Turkey — gobble, gobble! (wobble your chin, fan your tail)

Pie — mmm! (round shape in the air, then rub your tummy)

And we SHARE it, every bit! (hand it to the person beside you)

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe a whole sentence each: “Thank you, Teacher.” “Please may I have some?” “I want to share with you.”

Teacher fails: on “and we share it”, sweep everything on the cloth back into your own lap with both arms and hold on tight. Look pleased. Wait for it.

Chinese moment: the heart card joins the row — thanks · please · feather · share · heart, the whole week laid out on the mat. Read along it together, pointing, and finish on thank you said to the person sitting next to you.

3 MINGAME · THE GRATITUDE TREE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The tree cloth is pegged up on the wall at child height. Wednesday's feathers are already on one branch.

Teacher“Come and hang your leaf on our tree — and tell us who it is for.”

One child at a time: peg a leaf on and say it out loud. Littles give the one name: “Mama!” Bigs give the sentence: “I am thankful for my grandma.” Write each one on its leaf as they say it.

Add the families' own thank-yous from the notes you asked for, read out one by one. By the end the bare tree is covered and the whole room's gratitude is hanging on the wall.

Leave it up. Parents find their own name on it at pick-up time and read the entire week off the wall — and nearly every leaf says mama or baba. That is your parent report done, for free.

1 MINCLOSE + AWARDS

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home. Then, standing in front of the finished tree, with all five gestures:

Everyone“Please… share… THANK YOU, EVERYBODY!” (all twenty hands fly forward from their chins at the tree on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Read the whole tree out loud, leaf by leaf, with the class chorusing “thank you!” after every single one.
