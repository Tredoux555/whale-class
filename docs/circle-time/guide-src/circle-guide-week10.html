Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

MONDAY
Every Day or Sometimes?
Today's words: healthy
Grab: one washed apple and one lollipop in the Magic Box; two hoops (or a green plate and a yellow plate); the 12 food cards from print pack page 14; a tea towel to polish the apple.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Box chant. Shake it — something rolls and something rattles. Two things! Sniff the lid.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek. Gasp. Peek again. Lift out the apple in one hand and the lollipop in the other and hold them far apart.

Teacher“Two foods. One is for every day… and one is only for sometimes.” (look from one hand to the other, very slowly)

Polish the apple on your jumper. “Apple!”

4 MINTEACH · HEALTHY

Apple up high, in two hands, so every child can see it.

An apple is healthy. (hold it up)

Healthy food makes me strong. (strong arms, growl)

I eat it every day. (five fingers, one at a time)

CRUNCH! (take a huge bite — let them hear it)

Now the lollipop. Hold it up the same way, wait, and let them tell you.

Teacher“Every day? … or sometimes?”

Littles say the one word “Healthy!” with strong arms — that is a full win at 2 and 3. Bigs say the frame: “An apple is healthy.” and “Sweets are sometimes food.”

Teacher fails: announce that the lollipop is “a lovely healthy breakfast”, start unwrapping it and look delighted. Do not rush. Let them shout you down twice before you sadly put it away.

3 MINGAME · EVERY DAY OR SOMETIMES?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two hoops on the carpet: a green one — every day — and a yellow one — sometimes. Cards face down in a pile.

Teacher“Take a card. Run it to the right hoop!”

Each child takes one card, names it, and runs it to a hoop. The class calls the verdict together: “Every DAY!” or “Sometimes.”

Twelve cards, so everyone gets a turn or two. Bigs owe you a sentence — “Broccoli is healthy.” Littles just run and drop, and shout the word with the class.

Put one card in the wrong hoop yourself, whistling. They will fix it before you stand up.

1 MINCLOSE

Sitting. Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

It is the whole week in one line, and they will chant it on Monday without knowing what half of it means yet. By Friday they will. Got 3 spare minutes? The Very Hungry Caterpillar (Eric Carle) — on the Saturday page, ask each food: “Every day? Or sometimes?”

TUESDAY
Water in My Cup
Today's words: water (healthy comes back every day)
Grab: a bottle of water and a bottle of fizzy drink in the Magic Box; a bag of sugar cubes (count ten out beforehand and hide them in your pocket); a clear cup, a small jug, a tray; five bottles for the game — water, milk, juice, fizzy drink, sports drink.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Box chant. Shake — this one sloshes. Hold it to your ear and slosh it again.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out the water bottle. Then the fizzy bottle. Stand them side by side on the tray, in the middle, where everybody can see — and beside the fizzy one, the little stack of sugar cubes you counted out this morning.

Teacher“Two drinks. Which one do I drink every day?” (hand hovers over each in turn — wait)

“Water!” Everyone drinks from a pretend cup with a long “aaah”.

4 MINTEACH · WATER

Pour water from the jug into the clear cup, slowly, so they hear it. Hold it up to the window.

Water is clear. (hold the cup up to the light)

Water is cool. (touch the cup to your cheek, shiver)

I drink my water… (one long drink)

Aaaaah! (whole body relaxes)

Every child gets a real sip if you have cups — it takes ten seconds each and it is the whole lesson. Littles say “Water!” and drink; Bigs say “I drink my water.”

Now the fizzy bottle. Take the sugar cubes out of your pocket and stack them one at a time beside it, counting out loud together: “One… two… three…” all the way to ten. Everybody gasps at the tower.

Teacher fails: “I am thirsty! I will just eat these ten sugar lumps for my breakfast.” Lift one towards your mouth. Wait for the roar, then put it down and drink the water instead.

3 MINGAME · COUNT THE SUGAR CUBES

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Five bottles in the middle, sugar cubes in a dish, a numbered card face down under each bottle.

Teacher“How much sugar is hiding in this one?”

A child stacks the cubes in front of one bottle while the class counts. Then turn the card over and check together. Water: zero — everybody cheers. Fizzy drink: a tower — everybody gasps.

Five bottles: water, milk, juice, fizzy drink, sports drink. Then everyone drinks a cup of water together.

Littles do the counting with their fingers up in the air, not with the cubes. Bigs read the number card themselves and say “The fizzy drink has ten.”

1 MINCLOSE

Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? Water Is Water (Miranda Paul) — don't read every word, just turn the pages and say “Water!” together on each one.

WEDNESDAY
Wash Your Hands
Today's words: wash (healthy · water come back)
Grab: a bar of soap, a bottle of bubbles and one muddy apple in the Magic Box (roll a washed apple in a little soil before circle); a bowl of water, a nail brush, a towel; the five hand-washing cards for the shelf tray; a 20-second sand timer if you have one.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Box chant. Shake — a soft, heavy thud. Sniff the lid, close your eyes, sniff again: it smells clean.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes the bar of soap. Pass it round the circle so every child can smell it. Then reach in again — and lift out the muddy apple, holding it away from you between two fingers.

Teacher“Oh dear. Can I eat this?” (hold it up, turn it slowly, let them see the dirt)

“NO!” Ask what we have to do first. Somebody will get there: “Wash!” Everybody rubs both hands together.

4 MINTEACH · WASH

Bowl of water in the middle, soap beside it, towel on your shoulder. Wash the apple in front of them, properly, taking your time.

Wet the apple. (dunk it, both hands)

Wash, wash, wash! (rub it round and round)

Rinse it, dry it… (towel, slowly)

NOW I can eat it! (huge CRUNCH — the water is dirty and the apple is shining)

Now your hands, the same way, and name the five steps as you go: wet · soap · scrub · rinse · dry. Hold up a finger for each one.

Littles say “Wash!” and rub both hands together — that is the win. Bigs say the frame: “I wash my hands before I eat.”

Chinese character wash. And the rhyme everybody already half-knows — sing it in Chinese while you scrub: The Hand-Washing Song — rub your palms, rub the backs of your hands, wash them clean.

Teacher fails: put your hands in the water for half a second, flick them, and reach for the apple. “Done!” Let them stop you. Then do it properly while they count.

3 MINGAME · BUBBLE HAND-WASH

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Blow a cloud of bubbles over the circle. Every child catches one on their palms — and now their hands are “full of germs”.

Teacher“Scrub! Don't stop until the song stops!”

Everybody scrubs — palms, backs, between the fingers, thumbs, nails — for one whole chorus. That is twenty seconds, and that is the rule. When the song stops, hands go up: “CLEAN!”

Three rounds. Round two, a Big leads the scrubbing and calls out the part: “Now the thumbs!” Round three, everybody does it with their eyes shut.

Then walk to the real basin as a class and do it once with real soap and real water, singing. That walk is the lesson — it is the habit they take home.

1 MINCLOSE

Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? Wash Your Hands! (Tony Ross) — the Little Princess will not wash, and they will tell her off on every page.

THURSDAY
Brush, Brush, Brush
Today's words: brush (healthy · water · wash all come back)
Grab: a new toothbrush in the Magic Box and a giant model tooth (or a big white sponge, or an egg box painted white) smeared sticky with a little jam or honey the night before; a big brush or nail brush to clean it; a mirror; a spare toothbrush per child if you have them; Chinese character card tooth.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Box chant. Shake — a light, hollow clatter. Sniff: mint!

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes the toothbrush. Wave it about. Then lift out the giant tooth — sticky, dull, and disgusting — and hold it up in silence until somebody says something.

Teacher“This tooth ate the lollipop on Monday… and nobody brushed it.” (turn it slowly, sadly)

Everybody brushes their own teeth in the air, up and down. “Brush!”

4 MINTEACH · BRUSH

Toothbrush up high so everyone can see the bristles. Then your own teeth, hugely, in the mirror.

Up and down… (brush up and down on the top teeth)

and round and round… (little circles)

Front and back and all around! (open wide, get the back ones)

Spit! Rinse! Shiny! (big grin, show every tooth)

Every child brushes their own teeth in the air with you, twice: once in the morning, once at night — hold up one finger, then two.

Littles say “Brush!” with the up-and-down action. Bigs say “I brush my teeth in the morning and at night.”

Chinese character tooth — hold the card up beside your own grin.

Teacher fails: brush your ear. Thoroughly, with great concentration. Then your nose. Then your elbow. Keep going until they are screaming at you and pointing at their own mouths.

3 MINGAME · BRUSH THE GIANT TEETH

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The sticky tooth goes in the middle on a tray, with the big brush beside it.

Teacher“Every sticky bit must come clean. Where is there still some?”

One child brushes while the class directs: “The top!” “The BACK!” “In the middle!” When they think it is done, everyone inspects it, and somebody always finds one more sticky patch.

Then all together, in the air, for the length of one verse: up and down, round and round, front and back.

Bigs can count the strokes to twenty. Littles just brush and shout the word. If you have a toothbrush each, this is the day to send them home clean.

1 MINCLOSE

Whisper → normal → shout, with all four actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? Brush Your Teeth, Please (Leslie McGuire) — a pull-tab book; let a different child brush each animal.

FRIDAY
Slice & Share 🎃
Today's words: healthy · water · wash · brush · share — the whole week
Grab: the fruit basket to share and one small pumpkin lantern in the Magic Box, plus every prop from the week; a child-safe apple wedger, a board, a jug of water to rinse and a serving dish; the “My Healthy Day” sheets (print pack page 16) and the awards (page 17).
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box comes out fat, heavy and smelling of fruit. Everyone leans in.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL! What did we learn this week?”

Pull the props out one at a time and hold each up in silence — they will supply the word before you can.

Apple → “Healthy!”  Water bottle → “Water!”  Soap → “Wash!”  Toothbrush → “Brush!”  Lollipop → “Sometimes!”

Last out: the fruit basket — and one small pumpkin lantern. Don't explain it yet. Set it at the edge of the mat where they can all see it and carry on.

4 MINTEACH · SHARE

Wash your hands first, in front of them, all five steps — Wednesday's habit, used for real. Then the fruit basket in both hands. Don't eat any. Turn to the child beside you and hold it out.

One for me… (hand on your own chest)

and one for you. (hold the basket out with both hands)

I share my food. (both hands push the basket forward)

Thank you! (hands together, small bow)

The basket goes all the way round the circle, hand to hand, and nobody eats yet. Every child says “Share!” as they pass it on; Bigs say “I share my food with my friend.”

Teacher fails: take the basket back, sit down and start eating the whole lot yourself with your back to them. Wait. Being told off for not sharing is the best English lesson of the week.

3 MINGAME · SLICE AND SHARE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Board, wedger, jug and dish in the middle. Hands washed. Rinse each apple, then press the wedger down — eight pieces every time, and the click is very satisfying.

Teacher“Take a piece — and give it to somebody else.”

Nobody eats their own piece. Each child takes one, turns to a friend, holds it out with both hands and says “Share!” — or, for a Big, “This is for you.” Then everybody eats at the same moment.

Now the pumpkin. Tomorrow is Halloween a festival other children have, where they carve a pumpkin and pretend to be a little bit scary. Light it (or switch it on), turn the lights down, and once — only once, all together:

Everyone“BOO!” (hands up like claws, then everybody laughs and it is over)

One pumpkin, one BOO, and back to the fruit. It is a nod, not a party — the week belongs to washing, brushing and sharing.

1 MINCLOSE + THE TAKE-HOME SHEET

Hand out the “My Healthy Day” sheets: every child traces the cup, the toothbrush and the soap, draws the every-day food they love on the plate, you scribe their line, and it goes home today with an award.

Everyone holds their sheet up. Whisper → normal → shout, with the actions:

Everyone“Wash… brush… WATER! And I SHARE!” (rub hands · brush teeth · cup hands up high · both hands offered out)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Doorbell Rang (Pat Hutchins) — more and more friends arrive and the biscuits get shared again and again. Count them out loud together.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the whole chorus, and it is the same three shapes as every Whale Class song so far.
