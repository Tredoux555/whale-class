Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

MONDAY
Find Africa
Today's words: Africa
Grab: the green Africa piece from the continent puzzle map and a photo of Teacher Tredoux as a child, both in the Magic Box; the puzzle map itself (or the world map cloth from Week 21) spread on the floor; a long strip of paper pinned up for the week's Chinese character row; the Africa card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is flat and almost weightless, and something slides from side to side when you tip it. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tip it — slide. Sniff it — nothing at all. Peek in for a long time and let your face go serious. Then lift out the green piece with two hands and hold it up above your head.

Teacher“It's green… and it is a piece of the WORLD.” (turn it slowly so everybody sees the shape)

Now reach back in for the photograph and hold it beside the green piece: “Teacher Tredoux was a little boy — in Africa.” Let them look for as long as they want. Everybody pats the floor twice: “Africa!”

4 MINTEACH · AFRICA

The green piece stays in your hands. Say it twice; the second time they do the actions with you:

Africa, Africa — big and green, (draw one enormous shape in the air with both hands)

The hottest place that I have seen! (fan your face)

My teacher lived there, long ago… (point at the teacher, then hold both hands low, like a small child)

Africa, Africa — now you know! (pat the floor twice)

Then the map. The green piece goes home into its own hole — let a child push it in and feel it drop. Now two fingers, one on each place: “We live HERE” (Asia, China, this room) “… and Teacher lived THERE.” Trace the distance between them slowly with one finger while everybody watches.

Littles pat the floor and say “Africa!” Bigs owe you the frame: “This is Africa.” “Teacher Tredoux is from Africa.”

Teacher fails: try to force the Africa piece into Australia's hole. Push harder. Turn it round. Push again, huffing. Wait to be rescued.

Chinese moment: pin the Africa card at the top of the strip — Africa. Then the two together: China is in Asia; our teacher comes from Africa. The row of Chinese characters grows by one card every morning this week.

3 MINGAME · FIND AFRICA

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The map is on the floor in the middle of the circle and everybody can reach it.

Teacher“Walking feet… go and stand on Africa.” (one child at a time; the rest sit and watch)

The child walks round, finds the green shape, stands on it (or lays one hand flat on it) and everybody else points at them and says it together: “Africa!” Then they sit down and the next one goes.

Bigs get the harder version: stand on Africa, then walk to Asia and stand where we live. Two continents, two names, one journey.

Go round until every single child has stood on it. This is a naming game, not a race — nobody is wrong and nobody is quick, and by Friday they will run to it.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody kneeling round the map, hands flat on the floor. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Africa… lion… ELEPHANT! BOOM-BA-BOOM!” (pat the floor twice · roar · one arm swings as a trunk · three big pats on your knees)

Got 3 spare minutes? Handa's Surprise (Eileen Browne) — a girl carries a basket of fruit on her head and the animals take it one by one. Let them shout the animal's name on every page.

TUESDAY
The Lion Roars
Today's words: lion (Africa comes back every day)
Grab: a model lion and a tuft of wool for his mane in the Magic Box; one small mouse or a mouse puppet; card lion faces, wool and a blunt needle or a punched card for the shelf tray; the lion card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is heavier, and something in it does not slide — it thumps. Chant, then shake it once and let a low growl come out of you, not the box.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Sniff the lid, jump back from it, then peek in with one eye shut. Lift out the lion very slowly and stand him on your open palm, facing the circle.

Teacher“He has big feet… a gold coat… and a very big mouth.” (hold the wool tuft round the lion's face — the mane)

Everybody opens their mouth wide: “ROAR!”

4 MINTEACH · LION

The lion sits on your palm the whole way through. Say it twice; the second time they do it with you:

The lion has a gold mane, (both hands make a big circle round your own face)

He walks so soft… and walks again, (soft padding hands on your knees, very quietly)

He opens up his big, big mouth… (both hands open wide in front of your mouth)

and ROAR comes out! (one enormous roar, then instant silence)

Then the volume dial: your hand low on the floor is a mouse squeak, your hand up high is a full roar, and everything in between is theirs to follow. Move your hand slowly up and down and let twenty voices slide with it. End with the hand on the floor.

Littles just roar — “ROAR!” — and that is a full win. Bigs owe you a sentence: “The lion is big.” “The lion says roar.”

Teacher fails: hold the wool mane round your own face, look enormously fierce, and say “meow”. Very quietly. Wait.

Chinese moment: the lion card joins Africa on the strip — lion. And remember the New Year lion dance, the dancing lion? That one is a costume with two people inside; this one is a real animal, asleep in the hot grass in Africa. Same word, two very different lions.

3 MINGAME · LION AND MOUSE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two children in the middle: one is the lion, one is the mouse. Everybody else is the echo.

Teacher“Lion… ROAR!” (the whole class roars it back at the same size) “Mouse… squeak.” (the whole class squeaks it back, tiny)

Swap the pair every two turns so everybody who wants a go gets one. Littles copy the volume and nothing else; Bigs must say which one is coming before the class answers: “The lion is big!”

Always finish on the mouse. A game that ends loud leaves twenty roaring children; a game that ends in a squeak leaves twenty children listening for the next thing you say.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody up on their knees, mane hands round their faces. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Africa… lion… ELEPHANT! BOOM-BA-BOOM!” (pat the floor twice · roar · one arm swings as a trunk · three big pats on your knees)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Lion Inside (Rachel Bright) — a tiny mouse goes to ask the lion how to roar and finds out the lion is frightened too. It is the loud/soft game in a story.

WEDNESDAY
The Elephant
Today's words: elephant (Africa · lion come back)
Grab: a model elephant and a length of soft tubing — a trunk that really squirts — in the Magic Box; a basin of water and a towel (or take the squirting outside); the animal size-grading mat on the shelf tray; the elephant card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is heavy — make a real effort lifting it — and when you set it down it lands with a thump.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it: one slow, heavy roll. Peek in and say nothing at all. Then lift out the elephant with both hands, like something precious and enormously heavy, and walk him slowly round the inside of the circle.

Teacher“Grey… big… and look at his nose.” (pull the soft tubing out of the box and hold it to your own face)

One arm swings down from every face: “Elephant!”

4 MINTEACH · ELEPHANT

Your arm is the trunk from here on. Say it twice; the second time everybody swings with you:

The elephant's nose is long and grey, (one arm hangs from your face and swings)

He picks the leaves and walks away, (scoop with the arm, then four slow heavy steps)

He fills it at the waterhole… (dip the arm down and sniff up loudly)

and SQUIRT — all over! (throw the arm up over your head)

Then the real thing: the tubing goes in the basin, you suck up a mouthful of air and squirt water out of it. Do it once, badly, all over your own feet. Then every child gets a turn to make the trunk pick something up — a scarf, a bean bag — and put it down again.

Littles swing the trunk arm and say “Elephant!” Bigs owe you a sentence: “The elephant is big and grey.” “The elephant has a long nose.”

Teacher fails: try to pick up a block with your ear. Lean right over. Try the other ear. Look genuinely puzzled that it will not work.

Chinese moment: the elephant card joins Africa · lion — elephant, and the big is the whole joke: the big animal. Then nose, and everybody points at their own nose and then at the elephant's, which is very much longer.

3 MINGAME · ELEPHANT TRUNK WALK

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody stands, one arm hanging as a trunk, knees soft.

Teacher“Heavy feet… slow, slow, slow… and SQUIRT!” (the whole herd walks the room at half speed, then throws the trunk up together)

Walk, squirt, walk, squirt. Then the hard bit, which they love: the herd walks in one line, each child's trunk arm holding the hand of the child in front, all the way round the room without breaking.

Half speed is the lesson. Ask them to walk so heavily that you can hear it, then so heavily you can hear it and nobody bumps — that is body control dressed up as an elephant.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing, trunk arms hanging, ready to swing. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Africa… lion… ELEPHANT! BOOM-BA-BOOM!” (pat the floor twice · roar · one arm swings as a trunk · three big pats on your knees)

Got 3 spare minutes? Elmer (David McKee) — the patchwork elephant who is not grey. Hold the book open at the last page and let them find Elmer in the crowd.

THURSDAY
Boom, Boom, Drum
Today's words: drum (Africa · lion · elephant come back)
Grab: a hand drum in the Magic Box — the biggest one you own; the rhythm dot-cards for the shelf tray; a second drum or an upturned tub so two can play at once; the drum card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box booms. Do not shake it — tap the side of it once with one finger and let the sound do the work.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tap it again, twice. Put your ear right against it. Tap it a third time and let your eyes go wide. Then lift the drum out and hold it flat on your palm without playing it at all — wait until the room is completely silent — and hit it once, hard.

Teacher(one enormous beat, then total stillness) “That is a drum. It talks with your hands.”

Everybody pats their knees twice: “Drum!”

4 MINTEACH · DRUM

Hands, never sticks. Say it twice; the second time everybody plays their knees with you:

Boom, boom — the big drum calls, (two slow, heavy pats)

Ba-da-boom — it fills the walls, (three quick pats)

Fast, fast, fast — now everybody run… (patting as fast as you can, running feet on the spot)

and STOP — the drum is done! (both hands flat on the drum, everything freezes)

Then every child gets the real drum for three beats, counted out loud by the whole class — one, two, three — and passes it on with two hands. Loud is allowed. Longer than three is not, and they will police it for you.

Littles pat their knees and say “Drum!” Bigs owe you the frame: “I can hear the drum.” “The drum is loud.”

Teacher fails: try to play the drum with your elbow. Then with your head. Then put it on like a hat and look round for it.

Chinese moment: the drum card joins Africa · lion · elephant — drumming, and the sound in Chinese: boom boom boom. Africa has drums and China has drums, and both of them say the same thing: come here, something is happening.

3 MINGAME · DRUM SAYS

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody stands with space round them. The drum gives the orders; you say nothing at all after the rules.

Teacher“Fast drum — run on the spot. Slow drum — tiptoe. No drum — FREEZE.” (then play, and let the drum do all the talking)

Fast, slow, silence, over and over, and the silences get longer and longer. Last round, the drum goes to a Big and they run the room while you play in it like everybody else.

Three speeds only, all week, no new instructions. Matching a whole body to a sound is the same skill as hearing the difference between two English words — which is what the Friday phonics sound needs from them.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody sitting, hands raised over their knees, ready to play. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Africa… lion… ELEPHANT! BOOM-BA-BOOM!” (pat the floor twice · roar · one arm swings as a trunk · three big pats on your knees, with the real drum on the shout)

Before home time: hide the animals for tomorrow's safari walk — one per child, some in plain sight at knee height for the littlest ones. Count them into the basket tonight so that tomorrow's count comes out right.

Got 3 spare minutes? Bringing the Rain to Kapiti Plain (Verna Aardema) — a cumulative rhyme that grows a line at a time. Beat the drum softly under it and let them join in on the repeats.

FRIDAY
Hot Yellow Land + Big Review
Today's words: hot — and Africa · lion · elephant · drum, the whole week
Grab: every prop from the week packed into the box, plus a piece of African printed cloth and a woven basket; a jar of warm yellow sand and a bowl of cold water; the animals hidden round the room from yesterday; the awards (print pack page 17); the hot card. This Sunday, 21 March, is Spring Equinox.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box comes out bulging, wrapped in the printed cloth, and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The green map piece → “Africa!”  The lion → “ROAR!”  The elephant → “Elephant!”  The drum → “Drum!”

Last of all, the jar of sand. Open it, pour a little into every open hand, and let them feel it. “HOT!”

4 MINTEACH · HOT, THEN THE WHOLE WEEK

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

Yellow sun and yellow sand, (one big circle overhead, then flat hands sweeping)

Africa is a HOT, hot land! (fan your face twice)

A lion, (mane hands and a roar) an elephant, (swing the trunk arm) a drum, (three pats on your knees)

YES! — to Africa we come! (both arms thrown wide)

Then hot and cold, done with hands and not words: one bowl of warm sand, one bowl of cold water, and every child puts a hand in both. “Hot!” “Cold!” Africa in the day is the first one; Africa at night is the second one, which is the surprise of the week.

Littles fan their faces and say “Hot!” Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “It is very hot in Africa.” “The sun is hot.” “This is Africa.”

Teacher fails: get ready for Africa by putting on a winter coat, a scarf, a woolly hat and gloves. Announce that you are ready. Start fanning yourself. Wait.

Chinese moment: the hot card completes the row — Africa · lion · elephant · drum · hot, the whole week on the strip, and Africa is hot is the sentence. Then this Sunday: Spring Equinox, 21 March — day and night in equal halves, day and night exactly the same length, here in China and in Africa, on the same round world we found on Monday's map.

3 MINGAME · SAFARI WALK

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The animals are hidden round the room, one for every child, and the woven basket sits in the middle of the circle.

Teacher“Quiet feet… find one animal… bring it back and tell us what it is.” (one animal each — the rest stay hidden for the next child)

Everybody comes back, sits down, and one at a time holds theirs up and names it: “Lion!” “Elephant!” Bigs give the sentence — “I found a big elephant.” Then it goes in the basket and we count the whole basket out loud together.

Quiet feet, on purpose. A safari is the one hunt where you find more by being silent, and after four loud days they can feel the difference.

1 MINCLOSE + AWARDS

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home. Then, standing round the map with the drum, all four gestures:

Everyone“Africa… lion… ELEPHANT! BOOM-BA-BOOM!” (pat the floor twice · roar · one arm swings as a trunk · three big pats on your knees, and the drum on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Sing the whole song one more time with the drum, then tell them what is coming: next week we zoom right in from the whole green continent to one country at the very bottom of it — Teacher Tredoux's own country. Do not name it yet. Let them wonder all weekend.

Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the drum carries the beat anyway.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → Am (4 strums) → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those four shapes. C is one finger, Am is one finger, F is two, G7 is three — learn them in that order, and notice that C and Am are the same finger on two different strings, so the swap is almost free. Every verse is only C and G7. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the real drum and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.
