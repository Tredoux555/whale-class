Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

Week 21 · China
MONDAY
This Is China
Today's words: China · red
Grab: a small five-star red flag in the Magic Box; a big map of China that will lie on the floor in the middle of the circle; one little paper flag or red sticker to plant on Beijing; the middle card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is flat and light and something inside it slides. Hold it up. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Tip it slowly from side to side — a soft slither, no rattle. Peek in for a long time and pull out something folded. Unfold it once. Twice. Then let it fall open all at once and hold it up above your head.

Teacher“Look at this. It's red… it has little gold stars… and it belongs to the whole country we live in.” (let it hang still for one long beat, then wave it)

Arms wide, then hug yourself: “China!”

4 MINTEACH · CHINA & RED

Everybody stands. Say it twice — the second time they say it with you:

This is my country, (both arms out wide)

big and wide. (sweep the arms right round the circle)

The flag is RED… (wave the flag over your head)

and I live inside! (hug yourself tight)

Now the map goes down on the floor in the middle of the circle and everybody kneels round it. Walk one finger over it. Find our city, and plant the little flag on it. “We are here. This dot is us.” Then sweep your whole hand over the map: “and ALL of this is China.” Let each child put one finger on the map.

Littles hug themselves and say “China!”, wave and say “Red!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “I live in China.” “The flag is red.”

Teacher fails: point at the far blue edge of the map, out in the sea, and say “So this is our kindergarten, just here.” Nod, satisfied. Wait to be corrected.

Chinese moment: lay the middle card on the map and say China once, all together, then again in a whisper. The card stays on the map all week — the row grows by one character a day.

3 MINGAME · FLAG WAVE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” You hold the flag. The rule is one sentence long:

Teacher“Flag UP — shout CHINA! Flag DOWN — not one sound.”

Then play with it: up fast, down fast, up and hold it there while they run out of breath, a tiny lift that is nearly nothing, then a huge one. The silence is the half they love.

Finish by giving the flag to one child to be the flag-holder while the rest of you shout and stop, shout and stop.

This is the whole week's volume control and you will use it every day from now on — the flag comes out again on Friday for the quick-fire round.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing round the map. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Round Is a Mooncake (Roseanne Thong) — shapes found in ordinary Chinese things, a rice bowl, a lantern, a moon. Point at the round things in your own room afterwards.

TUESDAY
Panda and Bamboo
Today's words: panda (China · red come back every day)
Grab: a panda toy and a few real bamboo leaves or a short bamboo stalk in the Magic Box; the map with yesterday's flag still on it; the panda cards from print pack page 11; the bear card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box rustles. Chant, shake it gently — a dry papery whisper, nothing hard.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift the lid a crack and sniff: green, grassy, like the garden after rain. Pull out the bamboo first and pass it round to be smelled and felt — every child.

Teacher“Somebody in here eats this. All day. Who eats leaves all day?” (wait, then lift the panda out slowly by its ears)

Hands to your eyes like two black patches: “Panda!”

4 MINTEACH · PANDA

The panda sits in your lap facing the circle. Twice, and the second time they do it with you:

Panda, panda, black and white, (hands over the eyes, then pat your tummy)

Sitting up so still. (sit up very tall, hands in your lap)

Munch, munch, munch the bamboo, (both hands to your mouth, chewing hard)

Munch until you're full! (rub your tummy, slow and happy)

Two colours only: black and white. Everybody find black on their own clothes and hold it up; now find white. The panda has both, always, in the same places — ears, eyes, arms, legs.

Littles do the patches and say “Panda!” Bigs take the frame: “The panda eats bamboo.” “The panda is black and white.”

Teacher fails: offer the panda a banana. Then a biscuit. Then your shoe. Look genuinely puzzled each time it is refused. Wait for the outrage.

Chinese moment: the bear card joins middle on the map — panda. Pandas live in Sichuan; put a finger on the map roughly there, west of us, and leave it at that.

3 MINGAME · PANDA MUNCH

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody sits in the circle. The bamboo goes round, hand to hand, while you clap a steady beat.

Teacher“Pass… pass… pass… STOP.” (the clapping stops dead)

Whoever is holding the bamboo is the panda: three enormous munches — the whole circle counts them, one, two, three — and then FREEZE, panda and everybody, absolutely still, for as long as you can hold them.

Then the clapping starts again. Go round until every child has been the panda at least once; slow the pass down near the ones who have not.

The freeze is the real work — it is the quietest game of the week, and it teaches them that stopping is part of playing.

1 MINCLOSE

Sitting up tall like pandas, hands over your eyes. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Panda Bear, Panda Bear, What Do You See? (Bill Martin Jr & Eric Carle) — the whole class can join in on “What do you see?” by the second page.

WEDNESDAY
The Red Dragon
Today's words: dragon (China · red · panda come back)
Grab: a long red silk ribbon in the Magic Box — two or three metres, long enough for the whole class to hold at once; a simple dragon head (a paper plate with eyes, or a small box on a stick); a clear floor to wind round; the dragon card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is full — the lid will not sit down flat. Chant, then shake it: almost no noise at all, which is wrong for something this fat.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Peek in. Gasp. Shut the lid. Peek again. Now take hold of one end and pull, hand over hand, and keep pulling — and keep pulling — until the red ribbon is longer than the circle and lying in coils on the floor.

Teacher“It's red… and it's long… and in China, when this dances, it is a DRAGON.” (lift the middle of it and make one big wave run down its whole length)

Both arms wiggling: “Dragon!”

4 MINTEACH · DRAGON

Everybody stands, the ribbon on the floor in front of them. Twice, second time all together:

A dragon in China is a friendly thing, (both arms wiggling from the shoulders)

He wiggles and he waggles, (wiggle low, then high)

He does not bite — (shake your head, hands up, no)

he DANCES! (one enormous wave with both arms)

This is the part worth saying slowly: the Chinese dragon is lucky and kind. He brings the rain. He is not a monster to be frightened of — and he is never danced by one person on their own. It takes a whole class of people under one long body to make a dragon move.

Littles wiggle both arms and say “Dragon!” Bigs take the frame: “The dragon is long.” “The dragon dances.”

Teacher fails: hold both ends of the ribbon yourself and wiggle it feebly on the spot. “There you are. A dragon.” Look pleased. Wait until somebody tells you it takes everybody.

Chinese moment: the dragon card joins middle and bear on the map. Say dragon dance once, doing the arm wave on the word dance.

3 MINGAME · DRAGON TRAIN

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody takes hold of the ribbon with one hand, spaced out along it. You are the head, with the dragon head held up in front.

Teacher“The dragon never breaks. If it breaks, we stop, and we join it up again.”

Wind slowly round the room: round the shelf, under the window, in a big curl and out of it. Then faster. Then high — everybody's hand up — then low, ducking. Then FREEZE, dead still, dragon and all.

Finish by curling the dragon into a spiral on the floor and everybody sitting down exactly where they are.

Put your steadiest child at the tail, not the head — the tail is where a dragon breaks. Littles who will not hold the ribbon can hold your hand and be the head with you.

1 MINCLOSE

Sitting in the ribbon spiral, everybody holding a piece of red. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Great Race: The Story of the Chinese Zodiac (Dawn Casey) — the dragon is in it, and so are twelve animals they will meet again next week.

THURSDAY
The Great Wall
Today's words: wall (China · red · panda · dragon come back)
Grab: a big photo of the Great Wall and one wooden block in the Magic Box; the whole block basket standing by; a long curved line taped or chalked on the floor with a hill in the middle of it (the shelf tray's printed base-line works); the city card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is heavy and it clonks. Chant, then shake it once, hard: one solid knock.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes… one wooden block. Hold it up. Turn it over. Let the disappointment sit there for a second.

Teacher“One block. That's all. — Unless…” (lift out the photo and hold it up beside the block) “…you have about a million of them.”

Arms stretched as wide as they will go: “Wall!”

4 MINTEACH · WALL

The photo goes round the circle, every child holding it. Then, standing:

Up the hill and down the hill, (one hand climbs, then drops)

the wall goes on and on. (roll both hands forward, away from you)

Longer than the longest thing — (stretch both arms as wide as they go)

the GREAT WALL of China! (shout it, arms still wide)

Say the size in things they know: it is longer than our room, longer than our whole school, longer than our whole street, longer than our city — you could walk all day and all night and not come to the end of it. It climbs over the mountains instead of going round them. And people built it with their hands, stone by stone, a very long time ago.

Littles stretch wide and say “Wall!” Bigs owe you: “The wall is very long.” “I can build a wall.”

Teacher fails: lay two blocks end to end, dust off your hands and step back. “Finished. The Great Wall of China.” Fold your arms. Wait.

Chinese moment: the city card joins the row — the Great Wall, the long wall. Say grow stretching your arms wide on the word, then city.

3 MINGAME · BUILD THE WALL

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The curved line is on the floor with its hill in the middle. The block basket sits at one end.

Teacher“One block each. On the line. And we count it as it grows.”

One child at a time walks a block down and lays it on the line, and the whole class counts out loud — one, two, three… When the wall reaches the hill it has to climb: the blocks go up the slope, which is the moment the photo suddenly makes sense.

If it falls, nobody is in trouble and we rebuild that bit together. Count the whole wall once more at the end, all together, pointing.

Leave the wall standing until Friday if you possibly can. It goes on the shelf as Tray 3 and it is the thing they will show their parents.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing along your own wall, arms stretched wide. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“China… China… I LIVE IN CHINA!” (hug yourself · point down at the floor · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Grandfather Tang's Story (Ann Tompert) — a tangram story; put the seven shapes out on the shelf afterwards and let them make one animal.

FRIDAY
Jiaozi, Chopsticks and All of China
Today's words: all five — China · red · dragon · wall · panda
Grab: child chopsticks, two small bowls and a handful of pom-poms; a lump of dough and a small rolling pin (play dough is fine); one jade pendant or a smooth cool green stone; every prop from the week back in the Magic Box; the dumpling card; the awards from print pack page 17.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the whole week is in the box and it is the fullest it has been. Chant, then take the things out one at a time — and the class names each one before it touches the floor.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Flag… “Red!” Panda… “Panda!” Bamboo, ribbon, block, photo — round they go. Then, last, with the lid nearly shut, one thing left.

Teacher“This one you have to feel, not look at. Eyes closed. Two hands.” (the jade goes round the circle with every child's eyes shut — cool, smooth, heavy for its size)

Then everybody opens their eyes at once and sees what they were holding.

4 MINTEACH · JIAOZI + THE WHOLE WEEK

Fold a dumpling in the air, all together, twice — and then really do it with the dough:

A little round wrapper, (one flat hand held out)

a spoon of the middle, (the other hand spoons something in)

fold it, and pinch it, (fold the flat hand over, pinch along the edge)

pinch, pinch, PINCH! (three fast pinches, the last one shouted)

Jiaozi are the food a whole family makes together, everybody at one table, everybody's dumplings in the same pot — the ugly ones too. Let each child fold one real one out of dough and put it on the tray with the others.

Then the quick-fire round: hold up each prop, the class names it in English — and read the Chinese character row back off the map together: middle · bear · dragon · city · dumpling. Littles give one word per prop; Bigs give the whole sentence for each.

Teacher fails: try to pick up a pom-pom with the chopsticks yourself. Drop it. Try again. Drop it. Hold the chopsticks like a spoon and look hopeful.

Chinese moment: the dumpling card finishes the row — dumplings. As the bowls go round, every single hand-over gets hello and thank you, out loud.

3 MINGAME · CHOPSTICK RACE + QUICK-FIRE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Two bowls in front of each pair, pom-poms in one of them.

Teacher“Move them across — one at a time. Not fast. Neatly.”

Littles use their fingers or a small spoon and nobody says a word about it. Bigs use the chopsticks. It is not really a race — the child who moves three carefully beats the child who tips the bowl.

Then the flag comes back for quick-fire: flag UP and you hold up a prop — everybody shouts its name; flag DOWN — silence, and the next prop comes up. Finish on the flag itself: “CHINA!”

Awards. Print page 17, one half per child, and give them out here by name, one at a time, with the whole class clapping. Then the “Colour MY lantern” sheets go home in their bags.

Got 3 spare minutes? Dim Sum for Everyone! (Grace Lin) — a family sharing little dishes, which is exactly what the chopstick tray on the shelf is for.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those three shapes — there is no Am this week at all. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the red flag to wave on the beat and do the actions instead: the class will not notice, and the arms carry the meaning either way.
