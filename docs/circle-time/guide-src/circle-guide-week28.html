MONDAY
No class — Qingming Festival
There is no circle time today. This week is four days: Tuesday 6 to Friday 9 April.
Nothing to grab. School is closed for Qingming Festival and we start the new theme on Tuesday 6 April — with a real bird's nest, first thing out of the Magic Box.
WHYTHE SHORT WEEK

Qingming falls on the Monday, so the week starts a day late and every day after it does a little more:

OUTDOORSWHAT TO LOOK AT OVER THE LONG WEEKEND

Qingming is the day families sweep the graves and then walk out into the green — a spring walk, treading the new grass. Everything this week is out there today, for free. Tell the parents on Friday afternoon:

If your Monday does run (a make-up day, or a class that is in): don't start the theme. Go outside and do the looking. Then Tuesday opens exactly as written and they arrive already full of it.

NEXTCOMING BACK

We start on Tuesday 6 April with Animal Habitats. First circle back: the box is heavier than it looks and smells faintly of the outside, and inside it is a real bird's nest. Every child holds it with two hands before a single word is taught.

TUESDAY
Nest and Den
Today's words: nest and den (two homes today — this is the four-day week)
Grab: a real bird's nest in the Magic Box (or one you build from twigs and moss the night before); a shoebox den with a flap for a door and a little fox tucked inside it; five small eggs (wooden, or stones wrapped in wool) hidden round the room before the children come in; the nest and burrow cards; the picture cards from print pack page 11.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is light and it smells faintly of outside. Hold it up. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a dry little rustle, nothing solid. Lift the lid a crack and sniff, loudly, eyes shut. Sniff again. Let your face go amazed.

Teacher“Something in here is made of sticks… and grass… and somebody lived in it.” (lift the nest out on two flat palms, very slowly, and hold it at their eye level)

The nest goes right round the circle on two hands and every child holds it. Hands cupped into a bowl: “Nest!”

Say it once, plainly: nobody is at home in this nest, which is why we may hold it. A nest with eggs in it is looked at from far away and never touched.

4 MINTEACH · NEST, THEN DEN

Everybody makes a nest with their own two hands. We say it twice — small and slow, then big:

Twigs and moss and a bit of my hair, (cup both hands into a bowl)

Round and round till the nest is there. (turn the cupped hands round and round)

Up in the tree, where the wind can blow — (lift the nest hands right up high and sway)

That's where the bird lives. Now you know! (one finger points at the real nest)

Then the second thing. The box is not empty — there is a flap in the side of a shoebox and the room goes quiet while you open it. Inside, curled up, a fox.

Teacher“The bird's home is high and open. The fox's home is low… and dark… and it is called a den.” (both hands make a low arch on the floor and everybody peeps into it)

Littles do the two gestures and the two words: “Nest!” “Den!” Bigs owe you the frame: “The bird lives in a nest.” “The fox lives in a den.”

Teacher fails: try to put the fox into the nest. Push. Wedge him in. Stand back, delighted with yourself. Wait. They will take him off you and put him back where he belongs, and they will tell you why.

Chinese moment: lay the nest card beside the nest and the burrow card beside the den, and say bird's nest and fox's den once each. Leave both cards out; the row grows by one card a day and stands finished on Friday.

3 MINGAME · NEST HUNT

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The empty nest sits in the middle of the mat. Five eggs are hidden round the room, two of them in plain sight at knee height for the littlest ones.

Teacher“The eggs have rolled out of the nest! Walking feet… find one egg… and carry it home.” (two hands, held against the chest, all the way back)

One egg per child, carried in two hands and laid in the nest — not dropped. Littles say “Nest!” as they lay it down; Bigs give the sentence: “The egg lives in the nest.” Count them into the nest out loud together: one, two, three, four, five.

Then, with the last two minutes, animal-home charades: you call a home, they do the way in. Den — crawl in low. Nest — fly up and land. Pond — swim down. (Pond is tomorrow; let them meet the word here first.)

Five eggs, five and no more — the nest on the shelf tray is built to hold exactly five, and that is its control of error all week.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody's hands cupped into a nest. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Where do you live? — HOME!” (open hands questioning · cup the ear · both arms thrown wide into a roof on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? A Nest Is Noisy (Dianna Hutts Aston) — page after page of nests that are nothing like each other. Or just take the nest to the window and look for the real one outside.

WEDNESDAY
The Jar Pond
Today's words: pond (nest · den come back)
Grab: a jar pond in the Magic Box — a big clear jar with water, a strand of pond weed and a model fish in it, screwed shut and carried flat; a shallow basin, paper fish with a paperclip on each nose, and a stick-and-string rod with a magnet on the end; a towel on the floor before the children sit down; the pond card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is heavy, and you carry it with two hands and a worried face. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Do not shake it. Tell them you can't. Tip it a few degrees instead — and something inside sloshes. Do it again. Let them hear it.

Teacher“It's wet… it's got weed in it… and somebody in here is swimming.” (lift the jar out and hold it up to the window so the light comes through the water)

The jar goes round the circle, held in two hands, and every child looks through it. One hand swimming like a fish: “Pond!”

4 MINTEACH · POND

Everybody's hand becomes a fish. We say it twice — slow and deep, then quick:

Down in the water, cool and green, (one hand swims down low, past your knees)

The quietest home you've ever seen. (finger on lips, eyes wide)

No twigs, no moss, no door, no bed — (shake your head at each one)

Just water over the fish's head! (flat hand passes right over your own head)

Now the true thing, said slowly, with the jar in your hands: a nest is no good to a fish and a pond is no good to a bird. Hold the nest up beside the jar so they can see both at once. The right home for the right animal — that is the whole week in one picture.

Littles swim one hand and say “Pond!” Bigs owe you the frame: “The fish lives in the pond.” “The fish does not live in a nest!”

Teacher fails: take the little fox from yesterday's den and lower him, very carefully, into the jar. Look pleased. Hold it. Wait for the roar.

Chinese moment: the pond card joins nest and burrow on the mat — pond. Then one line of “The Tadpole Looks for His Mother”: the little tadpole swimming round the pond asking everybody Are you my mother? One line only, then stop — the tadpole is looking for exactly what we are: the right home.

3 MINGAME · MAGNET FISHING

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The basin is the pond, the paper fish are floating in it, and there is one rod. Towel down first.

Teacher“Fish for one… lift it out… and tell us where it lives.” (hand the rod on to the next child, never fish twice yourself)

Every catch is said out loud before it is put down: “The fish lives in the pond.” Littles hold the fish up and say “Pond!” — a full win. Everybody gets one turn; the ones waiting count the catches out loud.

One rod, one turn, and the rest of the class watching is the point — this game is really a lesson in waiting, and the fishing is the sugar. If the water goes everywhere, that is what the towel is for; do not stop the game to mop.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody's hand swimming. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Where do you live? — HOME!” (open hands questioning · cup the ear · both arms thrown wide into a roof on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Leave the jar pond on the window sill and look at it again at the end of the day. Ask what has moved. Something always has.

THURSDAY
A Silky Web
Today's words: web (nest · den · pond come back)
Grab: a wool web in the Magic Box — white wool wound across a bent wire hoop or a forked stick, with a small spider sitting in the middle of it; one big ball of wool for the circle game (a whole ball, not a scrap); a water spray bottle; the web card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box makes no sound at all, however hard you shake it. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake — nothing. Shake harder — still nothing. Sniff — nothing. Now lift the lid and go completely still.

Teacher“It's so light… it's made of string… and it was made by somebody with eight legs.” (lift the hoop out by its edges and hold it up against the light; then spray it once, and it glitters)

The web goes round the circle held by the frame, never touched in the middle. Ten fingers spread wide: “Web!”

4 MINTEACH · WEB

Ten fingers up, spread as wide as they will go. Twice — slowly, then fast:

Round and round and round I go, (wind one finger round and round)

Out of my body the silk does grow! (pull an invisible thread out of your tummy)

Sticky and shiny and strong as string, (pinch and stretch the thread between two hands)

My house is a web — and I made the thing! (ten fingers wide, very proud)

Then the plain true thing: the bird collected her nest, the fox dug his den — but the spider makes her whole house out of herself, in one night, and if it breaks she makes another one. Say it once. Let it sit there.

Littles spread ten fingers and say “Web!” Bigs owe you the frame: “The spider lives in a web.” “The spider made her own home.”

Teacher fails: announce that you will now make a web, out of yourself, exactly like a spider. Strain. Go red. Produce nothing whatsoever. Ask them, wounded, what you are doing wrong.

Chinese moment: the web card joins the row — spider's web. Four cards now: nest · burrow · pond · web, and tomorrow the fifth one finishes it. Say all four together, pointing, quickly.

3 MINGAME · WEAVE THE WEB

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody sits in a circle, cross-legged, knees nearly touching. You hold the ball of wool.

Teacher(hold the end of the wool tightly) “I'm sending it to… Mei. Hold your thread and don't let go. Now Mei sends it to somebody else.”

The ball rolls across the circle, child to child, each one keeping hold of their bit. Say the name every time. Slowly the whole class becomes one enormous web, and then everybody lifts it together, gently, off the floor. Look at it. Bounce the spider on it if you have a light one.

Littles just hold their thread and say “Web!” Bigs say who they are sending it to: “I send my thread to Leo.”

Wind the ball back up together at the end, child by child, in reverse — it takes one extra minute and it is the calmest minute of the day. Do not cut the wool.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody's ten fingers spread wide. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Where do you live? — HOME!” (open hands questioning · cup the ear · both arms thrown wide into a roof on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Very Busy Spider (Eric Carle) — every animal asks her to come and play and she just keeps building. It is the same lesson as the whole week and they can feel the web on the page.

Thursday

FRIDAY
Where Do You Live? + Big Review
Today's words: live — and nest · den · pond · web, the whole week
Grab: everything from the week packed back into the box — the nest, the den, the jar pond, the wool web — plus a basket of small animal figures (bird, fox, fish, spider, bee, and two or three more); the awards (print pack page 17); the home card. Next week is The Earth.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box comes out bulging and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got everybody's HOUSE inside it!”

Pull each home out and stand it on the mat in silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The nest → “Nest!”  The shoebox → “Den!”  The jar → “Pond!”  The hoop → “Web!”

Four homes in a row on the mat. Then tip the basket of animals out in the middle, in a heap. Nobody lives anywhere yet.

4 MINTEACH · LIVE, THEN THE WHOLE WEEK

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

The bird lives in a nest, (cup both hands into a bowl, lifted high)

The fox lives in a den, (both hands into a low arch)

The fish lives in the pond, (one hand swims down low)

The spider in her web — (ten fingers spread wide)

And I live in my HOME! (both arms up into a big roof over your own head)

Then, one at a time, round the circle: every child picks one animal out of the heap and walks it to the home it belongs in. Nobody is corrected out loud — if it goes in the wrong place, ask the class: “Is the fish happy in there?”

Littles carry the animal and say the one word: “Pond!” Bigs owe you the whole sentence as they set it down: “The fish lives in the pond.” Then the last question, to every single child by name: “Where do you live?”

Teacher fails: pick up the spider and announce that she is going to live in the jar pond from now on, because it is nicer. Start unscrewing the lid. Wait.

Chinese moment: the home card finishes the row — nest · burrow · pond · web · home. home is the one that matters: home. Point at each child and say my home, and let them say it back. Then tell them the whole row is going up on the wall.

3 MINGAME · WHO LIVES HERE?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Quick-fire, and fast is the whole point. You hold up one animal; they shout the home. Then you hold up one home; they shout the animal.

Teacher“Bird!” → “NEST!”  “Fox!” → “DEN!”  “Fish!” → “POND!”  “Spider!” → “WEB!”  “Bee!” → “HIVE!”

Then throw in one wrong one on purpose — hold up the fish and say “nest?” with a hopeful face — and let them shout you down. Finish on the one that is not a trick: “Where do you live?” and every child gets to answer, in any language, in any number of words.

Keep it under two minutes. The moment it slows down, stop it and do the awards — a quick-fire that outstays its welcome loses everything it just proved.

1 MINCLOSE + AWARDS

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home with the wrap-up. Then, standing round the four homes with the animals all in their right places:

Everyone“Where do you live? — HOME!” (open hands questioning · cup the ear · both arms thrown wide into a roof on the shout, as loud as it has been all week)

Got 3 spare minutes? Leave the four homes out over the weekend and tell them the nest stays up in the classroom all month. Next week is The Earth — the biggest home of the lot, and it is the one everybody in this room shares.

Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — it is a question-and-answer song and the children are listening to the question.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those three shapes — there is no Am this week at all. C is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, hold the nest up in both hands and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.
