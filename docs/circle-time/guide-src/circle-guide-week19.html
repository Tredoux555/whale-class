Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

Week 19 · Weather
MONDAY
Sunny Day
Today's words: sunny
Grab: a pair of sunglasses in the Magic Box (grown-up ones, the bigger and sillier the better); the blank class weather chart and its five symbol cards, hung where the smallest child can reach it; the sunny card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box is light and something inside it clicks. Hold it up. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — one small clack. Sniff — nothing at all. Peek in for a long time, then say, very puzzled, “There are two little windows in here.”

Teacher(put the sunglasses on, slowly, and look right round the circle at every single child through them) “Now… I can only see you when the sun is out.”

The sunglasses go round the circle and every child tries them on. Big circle drawn with both arms: “Sun!”

4 MINTEACH · SUNNY

Everybody stands. Say it twice — the second time they do it with you:

The sun is up, (both arms draw a big circle overhead)

The sky is blue, (one flat hand sweeps across in front of you)

Sunglasses ON — (mime putting them on with both hands)

I can see YOU! (point at one child after another, all the way round)

Then to the window, the whole class, and look. Is it sunny? One child hangs today's symbol card on the chart while everybody watches. This is the moment the week is built on: the sky, the word, the card, in that order.

Littles draw the sun circle and say “Sun!” Bigs owe you the answer: “It is sunny today.” Ask it properly first — “How is the weather today?”

Teacher fails: put the sunglasses on the back of your head, face the circle and complain that it has gone very dark. Hold it. Wait for them to march you round.

Chinese moment: lay the sunny card under the chart and say today is sunny once. One Chinese character a day joins the row all week — sunny · rain · wind · cloud · snow.

3 MINGAME · WEATHER CHARADES

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” You go first, big and silly: shade your eyes, fan yourself, walk very slowly like a hot afternoon.

Teacher“What am I? What's the weather?” (no words, only the acting — let it run long enough to be funny)

Then one child at a time acts and the class guesses. Littles act with you, holding your hand, and the class still guesses. Bigs answer in the frame: “It is sunny!”

Only two answers are possible today — sunny or rainy — and that is deliberate. Guessing succeeds all week because the choice grows by exactly one word a day.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing at the window. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Sunny… rainy… windy… cloudy… SNOWY! LOOK OUTSIDE!” (sun circle · fingers patter · sway · big round arms · flutter fingers down · both arms thrown at the window on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Sun Up, Sun Down (Gail Gibbons) — or simply lie on the floor in the one patch of sunlight on the carpet and be warm together, saying nothing.

TUESDAY
Rainy Day
Today's words: rainy (sunny comes back every day)
Grab: an umbrella in the Magic Box (or beside it — it will not fit) and a water spray bottle, filled and set to a fine mist; a towel; the rain card; the weather chart.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box will not shut properly and a long handle is sticking out. Chant anyway:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Before you lift it out, hold up the spray bottle and give one small pssst over your own hand. Then over your face. Look astonished.

Teacher“Hands out… here comes the rain.” (one tiny mist over each child's open palms, going right round the circle)

Fingers pattering down: “Rain!” Then out comes the umbrella and it goes up.

4 MINTEACH · RAINY

Everybody stands. Twice, the second time with you:

Drip, drop, drip, (fingers pattering down from high to low)

The rain comes down, (both hands falling all the way to the floor)

Up goes my umbrella — (push both hands up over your head)

All around the town! (turn slowly right round on the spot)

Now the real thing: open the umbrella, hold it over three children and spray the rain onto it so they can hear it landing above their heads and stay dry underneath. Everyone gets a turn to listen.

Littles patter their fingers and say “Rain!” Bigs take the frame: “It is rainy today.” “I need my umbrella.”

Teacher fails: hold the umbrella upside down like a bucket and admire how much rain you are catching. Get wetter and wetter. Wait.

Chinese moment: the rain card joins sunny under the chart — it is raining · umbrella. Point at the two characters and let them see the little strokes in rain falling like the rain itself.

3 MINGAME · RUN IN THE RAIN

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” You stand in the middle holding the open umbrella. Music plays: everybody walks about in the rain. Music stops:

Teacher“It's RAINING! Under the umbrella!” (everybody squeezes in underneath — then count the heads out loud together)

Play it four or five times. Walking feet only, and the last round is a slow-motion round so nobody gets flattened.

Littles who will not leave your leg can hold the umbrella handle with you and be the shelter instead of running for it. Same game, same words, no crying.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing at the window, umbrella up. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Sunny… rainy… windy… cloudy… SNOWY! LOOK OUTSIDE!” (sun circle · fingers patter · sway · big round arms · flutter fingers down · both arms thrown at the window on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Rain Came Down (David Shannon) — the rain falls, everyone squabbles, the sun comes out and everybody is friends again.

WEDNESDAY
Windy Day
Today's words: windy (sunny · rainy come back)
Grab: a paper pinwheel and a fan in the Magic Box; a feather and a few tiny scraps of tissue paper; the wind card; the weather chart.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box makes no sound at all today. Chant, shake — nothing. Sniff — nothing.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Open the lid a crack and blow into it. Something inside starts to whirr. Open it properly and lift out the spinning pinwheel.

Teacher“I can't see it… but I can see what it does.” (hold the pinwheel out and blow it round for one long breath)

Swaying from side to side, whoooosh: “Wind!”

4 MINTEACH · WINDY

Everybody stands. Twice, the second time with you:

Whoosh goes the wind, (sway from side to side, arms loose above your head)

It blows my hair, (both hands through your hair, messing it up)

It blows my hat — (grab your head, look up in alarm)

Up in the AIR! (both hands thrown straight up)

Then the real work. Wind is air, and air is invisible — so we watch what it moves. The feather across the mat. The tissue scraps off your palm. The pinwheel, close up and far away. Every child blows something and every child sees it move.

Littles sway and say “Wind!” Bigs take the frame: “It is windy today.” “The wind blows my hat!”

Teacher fails: stand a metre back from the pinwheel and blow at it very hard indeed. Nothing happens. Blow harder. Get cross with the pinwheel. Wait for somebody to come and move you closer.

Chinese moment: the wind card joins the row — it is windy. Fan one child with the fan and ask them is it cold? — wind on the skin is the same lesson in either language.

3 MINGAME · BLOW THE PINWHEEL

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Sitting in the circle, the pinwheel is passed from child to child. Whose can keep spinning the longest?

Teacher“Big breath in through your nose… and blow slowly out through your mouth.” (count the spins out loud together — one, two, three, four…)

Littles hold it while you blow and count with their fingers up. Bigs count their own spins and report: “I blew six!”

A long slow breath spins it further than a short hard puff, and they discover that themselves in about four turns. Do not tell them — just let the counting say it.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing at the window, swaying. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Sunny… rainy… windy… cloudy… SNOWY! LOOK OUTSIDE!” (sun circle · fingers patter · sway · big round arms · flutter fingers down · both arms thrown at the window on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Wind Blew (Pat Hutchins) — the wind takes an umbrella, a balloon, a hat, a kite, and gives every one of them to the wrong person.

THURSDAY
Cloudy Day
Today's words: cloudy (sunny · rainy · windy come back)
Grab: cotton-wool clouds stuck on grey card in the Magic Box, plus a spare bag of loose cotton wool; a rug or coats if you can get outside; the cloud card; the weather chart.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box weighs nothing at all today. Let one child hold it and be surprised. Chant:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a soft nothing. Lift out the grey card with its cotton-wool clouds and hold it high above everybody's heads, so they have to look up at it.

Teacher“Soft… grey and white… and it lives up there.” (let every child reach up and touch one cloud)

Both arms round in a big fat shape: “Cloud!”

4 MINTEACH · CLOUDY

Everybody stands. Twice, the second time with you:

Big fat clouds, (both arms make a huge round shape)

Grey and white, (one hand, then the other, sweeping across the sky)

They cover up the sun — (one hand slides across in front of the other)

and hide the light! (both hands over your eyes, then peep through your fingers)

Then the loose cotton wool. Pull a piece thin and wispy; roll a piece thick and lumpy. Both are clouds. Hold them up against the window and compare them with the real ones. Count the real clouds together — none, a few, or a sky so full you cannot count at all, which is also an answer.

Littles make the round arms and say “Cloud!” Bigs take the frame: “It is cloudy today.” “I can see three clouds.”

Teacher fails: reach right out of the window and try to catch a cloud in your two hands. Look at your empty hands. Try again, higher. Wait.

Chinese moment: the cloud card joins the row — cloudy. Say sunny day · cloudy · rain in order, pointing at the three cards, and let them chorus it back.

3 MINGAME · CLOUD WATCHING

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Coats on and outside if you possibly can — flat on our backs on the ground, looking straight up. If you cannot get out, everybody lies on the mat with their heads at the window.

Teacher(very quietly) “Find one cloud… just one… and tell me what it looks like.”

Go round one at a time and everybody gets to say their cloud. Littles point and say one word — “Dog!” Bigs give it a sentence: “That cloud looks like a big dog.”

This is the quietest three minutes of the year and it is worth protecting. No corrections, no “are you sure?” — a cloud looks like whatever the child says it looks like.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing at the window, big round cloud arms. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Sunny… rainy… windy… cloudy… SNOWY! LOOK OUTSIDE!” (sun circle · fingers patter · sway · big round arms · flutter fingers down · both arms thrown at the window on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Little Cloud (Eric Carle) — one cloud turns into a sheep, an aeroplane, a hat, and then rains on everybody.

FRIDAY
Snowy + The Weather Reporter
Today's words: snowy (and all five come back for the report)
Grab: everything from the week back in the Magic Box — sunglasses, umbrella, spray bottle, pinwheel, fan, cotton-wool clouds — plus a handful of loose cotton wool for snow; the class weather chart with all five days now filled in; the snow card; the awards from print pack page 17.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box is full and heavy and everything rattles. Chant it big:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes one prop at a time, fast, and the class shouts the word before you can say it — sunglasses, umbrella, pinwheel, cloud. Then, last, hold up a handful of cotton wool and let it fall through your fingers over somebody's head.

Teacher“Cold… white… and it falls very, very slowly.” (everybody flutters their fingers down from as high as they can reach)

Fluttering fingers falling: “Snow!”

4 MINTEACH · SNOWY, AND THE WHOLE WEEK

Everybody stands. The last new rhyme of the week, twice:

Snowy, snowy, white and cold, (flutter fingers down, then hug yourself and shiver)

It falls so slow… (one hand drifting down, very slowly indeed)

and the whole world's gold! (no — wait for them to correct you)

Teacher fails — this is the one: the world is white, not gold, and they will tell you so before the line is finished. Look amazed. Do it again wrong. Let them fix it twice.

Then the review. Hold up each prop and the class gives the word and the gesture: sunglasses → “Sunny!” umbrella → “Rainy!” pinwheel → “Windy!” cloud → “Cloudy!” cotton wool → “Snowy!” Bigs answer the real question every time: “How is the weather today?” “It is snowy today.”

Chinese moment: the snow card completes the row — it is snowing. Read all five together, pointing: sunny · rain · wind · cloud · snow. Then Great Cold — the coldest days of the whole year arrive on the 20th, next week: Great Cold is here — the coldest days of the year.

3 MINGAME · LITTLE WEATHER REPORTER

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The chart is on the wall with all five days filled in. Each child in turn comes up, takes the pointer, points at one day and reports it to the class like the television.

Teacher“And now… the weather. Over to you!” (hand over the pointer and step right back — the reporter runs the room)

Littles point at a card and say the one word: “Sunny!” Bigs give the whole report: “On Monday it was sunny.” “Today it is snowy.” Everybody claps every reporter.

Give out the “You are a star” awards (page 17) here, one per child, as they finish their report. Then the sheets and the awards go home together in the bag.

Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the children are watching your hands do the weather, not your strumming.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those three shapes — there is no Am this week at all. C is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the pinwheel to spin and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.
