Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

MONDAY
A Round, Round World
Today's words: world
Grab: a globe — too big for the Magic Box, so it waits under a cloth beside you; the empty box anyway, because the chant is the ritual; the world card; one red Chinese New Year envelope, empty, for the welcome-back.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Before anything else, the welcome back. Hold up the little red envelope, look round the whole circle, and take your time:

Teacher“Happy New Year! Happy New Year, Whale Class — it's Year of the Goat, the Year of the Goat!” (two fingers up beside your head for horns, and one enormous “meh-eh-eh”)

Everybody does the goat horns and the noise, once, as loud as they like. Then straight into the ritual — the box comes out, and today it is light and empty.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it: nothing. Open it: nothing. Look genuinely worried. Then let your eyes travel slowly sideways to the lump under the cloth — “...it was too BIG for the box.” Pull the cloth off. Hug the globe with both arms: “World!”

4 MINTEACH · WORLD

The globe sits in your lap. Everybody hugs a big invisible ball and we say it twice, the second time together:

The world is round, (both arms round a big invisible ball)

The world is BLUE, (wavy hands all around the ball)

I hold the world… (hold the imaginary ball out in front of you)

and so do you! (point at one child, then all round the circle)

Now the globe goes right round the circle, one child at a time, held in two hands. Every child gets one turn to hold the whole world and say one word. Littles hug it and say “World!” Bigs take the frame: “This is the world.” “The world is round.”

Then the first real looking. Spin it slowly with one finger and name only two things all day: blue is water (wavy hand), green is land (flat, still hand). Nothing else. Not one country name.

Teacher fails: try to make the globe stand still on your flat palm, like a tray. It rolls off. Try again, more carefully. It rolls off again. “But it's ROUND!” — wait for them to tell you so.

Chinese moment: lay the world card at the foot of the globe. One card a day joins it, and by Friday there are five on the mat. Say world once, and round with the arms.

3 MINGAME · SPIN THE GLOBE — LAND OR WATER?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The globe on the floor in the middle, everybody's knees around it.

Teacher“Spin it… and STOP it with one finger. Now look. Land or water?” (spin it slowly; a child reaches in and stops it)

Whatever is under the finger, the whole class calls it: “Water!” with wavy hands, or “Land!” with flat still hands. Go round until every child has stopped the world at least once.

Keep a tally on the board with two columns, drawn as a wavy line and a flat line. At the end, count them and say the true thing out loud: there is far more water than land. That is Tuesday's map and next week's oceans, planted eleven days early.

Spin gently. A globe going fast is a globe on the floor in two pieces, and it is the only one you have.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing, arms round their own invisible world. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“One world… seven continents… I LIVE IN ASIA!” (round arms · seven fingers up · both hands flat on your own chest on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Me on the Map (Joan Sweeney) — it zooms out from one bedroom to the whole world, one page at a time, and it is exactly the move this week is trying to make.

TUESDAY
A Map to Sit On
Today's words: map (world comes back every day)
Grab: the world-map cloth, folded very small into the Magic Box (a printed map shower curtain or a bedsheet you painted at the weekend); the globe from yesterday, beside you; the map card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is heavy and soft, and it rustles. Chant, then squeeze the box in both hands so everyone hears the crumple.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Pull out one corner of the cloth. Then more. Then more — keep pulling, far longer than seems reasonable, until it is obvious the box could never have held it.

Teacher“It's the whole world… and somebody has squashed it FLAT.” (flatten both palms hard together, then open them out)

Two children help you spread it on the floor. Flat palms: “Map!”

4 MINTEACH · MAP

Everybody kneels round the edge of the cloth, nobody on it yet. Say it twice; the second time they say it with you:

The world is round — (arms round the ball)

the map is FLAT. (both palms flattened on the floor)

The same blue water, (wavy hands over the blue)

and the same green land — just like that! (both hands flat on a green piece)

Hold the globe over the map and do it slowly, in silence: point at a green shape on the globe, then find the same green shape on the cloth and lay your whole hand on it. Do it three times. Say nothing at all until the third one, then: “Same world. One is round, one is flat.”

Littles put both palms on the map and say “Map!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “This is a map of the world.” “The map is flat.”

Teacher fails: try very hard to fold the globe in half so it will lie flat on the floor. Push down on it with both hands. Grunt. “Why won't it…?” Let them explain the whole thing to you.

Chinese moment: the map card joins world on the mat. Kneel and trace China's outline on the cloth with one finger while the class watches, and say China once. Only once — Thursday is for that.

3 MINGAME · SIT ON YOUR CONTINENT

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody walks around the outside of the map cloth while you sing the chorus.

Teacher“When the singing stops — sit down on a green piece! Any green piece. GO.” (stop mid-line; everyone drops onto the nearest land)

Once they are down, go round and lay your hand on each group: “You are sitting on a continent.” Say the name once, quietly, and move on — no drilling, no testing. Then up, walk, and stop again somewhere new.

Last round: “Sit on the blue.” They cannot — you cannot sit on water. Big laugh, everybody floats and swims on the spot, and that is the whole land/water lesson done a second time without a single explanation.

Littles will sit wherever their friend sat, and that is fine. The word arrives through their bottom on the green cloth long before it arrives through their ears.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing on the map, wherever they are. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“One world… seven continents… I LIVE IN ASIA!” (round arms · seven fingers up · both hands flat on your own chest on the shout)

Leave the cloth down all week. It becomes the road to the door, the place stories are read, and the thing they walk over forty times a day. That is the lesson doing its own work while you make coffee.

WEDNESDAY
Seven Big Pieces
Today's words: seven (world · map come back)
Grab: one piece of the Montessori continent puzzle map in the Magic Box — the other six hidden round the room before the children come in; the empty puzzle frame, on the mat; the map cloth, still down; the seven card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box knocks — one hard wooden clonk when you tip it. Chant, then tip it once, deliberately, and let them hear it.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift out one coloured puzzle piece and hold it up, turning it slowly. Then put the empty frame down beside it where everyone can see all seven empty holes.

Teacher“One piece… and how many holes? Let's count them.” (point at each hole; the whole class counts to seven, out loud, slowly)

Seven fingers up: “Seven!” And then, quietly: “So six pieces are lost.”

4 MINTEACH · SEVEN

Everybody stands with both hands up. We count them onto our fingers, twice — slowly, then fast:

One, two, three, (three fingers up, one at a time)

four, five, six… (three more, big and clear)

and SEVEN! (the seventh finger, held right up high)

Seven big continents — that's the lot! (both hands out wide, done)

Then the piece in your hand goes into the frame — it only fits one hole, and it fits with a satisfying little drop. Do it once yourself, in silence, and let that noise do the teaching.

Littles hold up seven fingers and say “Seven!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “There are seven continents.” “I can count to seven.”

Teacher fails: count the holes wrong on purpose — “one, two, three, five, six…” — with total confidence. Keep going until somebody stops you. Then look astonished and let them do it properly.

Chinese moment: the seven card joins world · map. Count to seven in Chinese with the fingers: one two three four five six seven. Then in English. Same fingers, two languages, thirty seconds.

3 MINGAME · PUZZLE PIECE HUNT

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The six missing pieces are hidden round the room, some in plain sight at knee height for the littlest ones.

Teacher“Walking feet… find the missing pieces… GO!” (everything found comes straight back to the frame on the mat)

As each piece arrives, the finder tries it in the frame themselves. It only goes in one hole, so nobody can be wrong for long — that is the control of error doing the correcting instead of you.

When the last piece drops in, count the whole frame together, touching each one: one…two…three…four…five…six…SEVEN. Littles count with you and hold seven fingers up at the end; Bigs tell you: “There are seven continents.”

Hide exactly six, and count them into your own hand before you hide them. If one goes missing under a shelf, the count comes out at six on the mat and the whole point of the game quietly collapses.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing round the finished puzzle, seven fingers ready. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“One world… seven continents… I LIVE IN ASIA!” (round arms · seven fingers up · both hands flat on your own chest on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Lay the seven pieces out on the map cloth, on top of their own shapes. They match. Say nothing; just do it, and let whoever notices tell everybody else.

THURSDAY
I Live in Asia
Today's words: Asia (world · map · seven come back)
Grab: seven small coloured flags on sticks and one model animal per continent in the Magic Box — panda (Asia), lion (Africa), penguin (Antarctica), kangaroo (Oceania), bear (Europe), bison (North America), llama (South America); the map cloth; the Asia card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is full of small hard things and it rattles like a treasure chest. Chant, then shake it hard, twice.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out come the seven little coloured flags, one at a time, planted upright in a line in front of you. Then, from right at the bottom, one animal — the panda — held up in two hands.

Teacher“Where does he live? … He lives where WE live.” (walk the panda across the map cloth and sit him down on the biggest green piece)

Both hands flat on your own chest: “Asia!”

4 MINTEACH · ASIA

Everybody kneels at the edge of the map cloth. Say it twice, the second time all together:

Asia, Asia — that's my home, (both hands flat on your own chest)

China is in Asia — (one finger down on the map)

now you KNOW! (both hands out wide)

Then the important thirty seconds, done slowly and only once: put one finger on our city, then open your hand over China, then open both arms over the whole of Asia. Small inside bigger inside biggest. Then plant the yellow flag on it and leave it standing there for the rest of the week.

Littles pat their own chest and say “Asia!” Bigs owe you the whole frame: “I live in Asia.” “China is in Asia.”

Teacher fails: put the panda down on Antarctica, brush your hands and say “There. Done.” Watch him shiver. Wait for the shouting, then let a child carry him home.

Chinese moment: the Asia card joins world · map · seven — and China is in Asia, said once, all together, standing on the map. Children in the class from somewhere else get their continent named out loud too, with a flag planted on it, on the same breath.

3 MINGAME · ANIMAL GOES HOME

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The seven animals in a heap in the middle of the map cloth, the seven flags standing on their continents.

Teacher“Take the lion… and walk him home to Africa.” (the child walks the animal across the cloth to the flag and sits him down)

One child, one animal, one journey. The flags are the control of error — the animal goes where the matching colour is, so a child who has forgotten the name can still get it right by looking. Whole class names the continent out loud as the animal lands.

Last one is always the panda, and the whole class walks him home together: “Asia!”

Littles do the walking with you holding the animal too. The naming is the Bigs' job; the journey across the floor belongs to everybody.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing on Asia, hands flat on their chests. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“One world… seven continents… I LIVE IN ASIA!” (round arms · seven fingers up · both hands flat on your own chest on the shout)

Before home time: make the passports for tomorrow — one folded paper booklet each, seven blank pages, the child's name on the front. Cut a potato stamp or find seven different stickers, one per continent. Ten minutes tonight buys you the best Friday of the term.

FRIDAY
Round the World + Big Review
Today's words: continent — and world · map · seven · Asia, the whole week
Grab: everything from the week in the box — globe, a folded corner of the map cloth, a puzzle piece, a flag, the panda; the passports you made last night, one per child; seven stamps or sticker sets, one per continent; the awards (print pack page 17); the continent card. Tomorrow is Awakening of Insects.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box comes out bulging and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The globe → “World!”  The corner of the cloth → “Map!”  The seven fingers you hold up → “Seven!”  The panda → “Asia!”

Last of all, one puzzle piece left in the bottom. Lift it out, hold it flat on your palm, and give them the last word of the week: “CONTINENT!”

4 MINTEACH · CONTINENT, THEN THE WHOLE WEEK

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

A round, round world, (arms round the big ball)

a flat, flat map, (both palms flattened)

Seven big continents — (seven fingers up high)

just like that! (one clap)

And the one I live on… is ASIA! (both hands flat on your own chest)

Then name all seven, once, walking round the map cloth and touching each one: Asia · Africa · Europe · North America · South America · Oceania · Antarctica. Whole class chants each name as your hand lands on it. Nobody is tested on this, ever — it is a chant, not a list.

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “This is the world.” “There are seven continents.” “I live in Asia.”

Teacher fails: hold up eight fingers on “seven big continents” and look very pleased with yourself. Count them yourself, out loud, still getting it wrong. Let them fold your extra finger down for you.

Chinese moment: the continent card joins the row — world · map · seven · Asia · continent, the whole week on the mat — and the seven continents said together, all seven fingers up. Then tell them: tomorrow is Awakening of Insects, the day the spring thunder wakes the sleeping insects. Everybody makes one roll of thunder with their feet, then wiggles ten fingers up out of the ground like waking worms.

3 MINGAME · PASSPORT STAMPS

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Every child gets their passport and holds it shut with both hands. Say the rule first: we travel to all seven, in one long line, and nobody stamps their own book.

Teacher“Next stop… AFRICA!” (the whole line walks round the cloth to the green shape with the lion on it)

At each continent: everybody stands on it, the whole class chants its name three times, and you go down the line stamping one page per child. Then on to the next. Seven stops, seven stamps, one lap of the world.

Littles just walk and chant — the stamp is the reward, the word is a bonus. Bigs count their pages at the end and tell you: “I have seven stamps.” “I went round the whole world.”

Last stop is always Asia, so the week ends where the children actually are. Say it as you stamp the last page: “Welcome home.”

1 MINCLOSE + AWARDS

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home with their passport. Then, standing all round the map cloth, all five gestures:

Everyone“One world… seven continents… I LIVE IN ASIA!” (round arms · seven fingers up · both hands flat on your own chest, shouted right across the whole map)

Got 3 spare minutes? Lie down on the map cloth, everybody on a different continent, lights low, and sing the chorus once, quietly, flat on your backs looking at the ceiling. Then tell them next week we go where the blue is: the five oceans.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the entire chorus, and the whole song is those three shapes — there is no Am this week at all. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, spin the globe with one hand and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.
