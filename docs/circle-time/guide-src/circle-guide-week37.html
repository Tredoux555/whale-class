Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

MONDAY
Summer Is Here
Today's words: summer
Grab: a sun hat and a pair of sunglasses in the Magic Box; two baskets, one red one blue, and a pile of real clothes — T-shirts, shorts, a woolly hat, a scarf, mittens, a swimming costume, a winter coat; the summer card; the sun-safety cards from print pack page 14.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is light and it rattles softly, like something plastic sliding about. Hold it up. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a soft slither. Lift the lid a crack and sniff: nothing. Sniff again, harder. Peek in, and let your whole face light up.

Teacher“Ooooh. There is something in here for a very, very HOT day.” (pull out the sun hat first, then the sunglasses, one at a time, very slowly)

Put them both on and turn round once so everybody can see. Big sun circle overhead with both arms: “Summer!”

4 MINTEACH · SUMMER

Everybody stands up. We do the whole summer with our bodies, twice — small and slow, then big:

The sun comes up — it's SUMMER! (both arms make a big circle overhead)

Off with my coat, (peel off an imaginary coat and throw it down)

On with my hat, (both hands press a hat onto your head)

And out we go to play! (run on the spot, faster and faster)

Now the three sun rules, in this order, every day this week — say them, do them, and make them say them back: hat (hands on head) · water (drink from a bottle) · shade (both hands up over your eyes).

Littles do the big sun circle and say “Summer!” Bigs take the frame: “It is summer.” “It is hot in summer.”

Teacher fails: put the sun hat on and then pull on the woolly scarf and the mittens as well, shivering, complaining that you are freezing. “Brrr. What a cold day.” Hold it. Wait. Let them undress you.

Chinese moment: lay the summer card where everyone can see it and say Summer is here! once, all together. Leave it there; the row grows by one card a day. Grain in Ear was on Saturday — Grain in Ear is the busy planting time, the busiest planting week the farmers have.

3 MINGAME · SUMMER OR WINTER?

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The pile of clothes goes in the middle of the mat, the red basket at one end of the room and the blue basket at the other.

Teacher“Take one thing… is it for a hot day or a cold day? … RUN it to the right basket!” (red = hot, blue = cold — point at both before anybody moves)

One child at a time, then two at a time as they get the idea. Littles just run it and say the one word — “Hot!” “Cold!” Bigs owe you the sentence as they drop it in: “I wear a hat in the summer.”

At the end, tip the red basket out and hold each thing up: this is what we wear now. Everything in the blue basket is going away until winter.

Put the swimming costume in the pile on purpose. It causes an argument, the argument is the language, and it sets up Thursday.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing, hats on, ready to run. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Hot… sun… SWIM! Hello, SUMMER!” (fan your face · big sun circle · two front-crawl strokes · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? The Very Hungry Caterpillar (Eric Carle) read again, out in the sun this time — they know every page, so let them tell it to you and just turn the pages.

TUESDAY
Hot, Hot, Hot
Today's words: hot (summer comes back every day)
Grab: a bowl of ice cubes straight from the freezer in the Magic Box — carry it fast; three identical saucers with one ice cube each; a sand timer; a paper fan; a cup of warm water; the hot card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today you come in running with the box, because what is inside will not wait. Whole class chants while you jog on the spot:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a hard clatter. Lift the lid, look in, and pull your hand back as if it hurt.

Teacher“Ow! It's COLD. It's so cold it feels HOT.” (lift out the bowl and pass one cube along the whole circle, fast — it is going to disappear)

Every child holds one cube for two seconds and passes it on. Fan your face: “Hot!”

4 MINTEACH · HOT

Everybody sits. We say it twice, and the second time they do the actions with you:

Hot, hot, hot — I fan my face, (flap a flat hand in front of your face)

Puff! Puff! (blow out, cheeks full)

I drink my water, (long drink from a bottle, head right back)

And find a shady place! (both hands up over your eyes, then flop down in the shade)

Then the two cups, side by side: warm water in one, an ice cube in the other. Every child touches both with one finger and tells you which is which — hot and cold are the two ends, and everything else lives in between.

Littles fan and say “Hot!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “It is hot in summer.” “The water is cold.”

Teacher fails: fan yourself furiously with the fan held backwards, blowing air at the wall, and complain loudly that it is not working at all. Wait. Somebody will turn your hand round for you.

Chinese moment: the hot card joins summer — it is very hot, said with the fan going. Ask them what people drink at home when it is this hot: herbal tea? mung bean soup? Let three children answer; there is no wrong one.

3 MINGAME · MELT THE ICE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Three identical saucers, one ice cube in each, held up so everybody sees they are the same.

Teacher“This one goes in the shade. This one goes in the sun. And this one goes in warm water. Which one wins?” (vote with hands up — count the votes out loud and write the numbers on the board)

Place all three, then walk away. Come back at snack time, at lunch and at home time. Littles say “Gone!” and point. Bigs give you the whole thing: “The ice is melting.” “The sun one won.”

This is the real science of the week and its control of error is the ice itself — the answer arrives on its own and nobody can argue with it, including you. Do not tell them which will win. Let the water do it.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing, fanning. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Hot… sun… SWIM! Hello, SUMMER!” (fan your face · big sun circle · two front-crawl strokes · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Lie on the floor in the coolest corner of the room with the lights off and the fan on, and just breathe together for two minutes. Then ask them, quietly, what the hottest thing they have ever touched was.

WEDNESDAY
The Sun and the Dragon Boat · Dragon Boat Festival
Today's words: sun (summer · hot come back)
Grab: a rice dumplings, a bundle of mugwort and a little dragon boat in the Magic Box; a hand drum and a beater; the sun card; the wrapping tray (soaked bamboo leaves, cooked rice, a spoon, string) ready on the shelf; a big clear space down the length of the room.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box smells, and it smells of something they have all met before at home. Chant, then hold the closed box under one child's nose.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Lift the lid a crack and sniff long and slow, eyes shut. Then take the mugwort out and walk it right round the circle so every single child gets a sniff of it — this is the smell of Dragon Boat Festival and it is the whole hook.

Teacher“Today is Dragon Boat Festival.” (lift out the rice dumplings in two hands, then the dragon boat, and hold the boat up over your head so it sails)

Big sun circle overhead with both arms: “Sun!”

4 MINTEACH · SUN

Everybody stands. Say it twice; the second time they do it with you:

Up comes the sun, (rise slowly from a crouch, arms opening into a big circle overhead)

Big and round and HOT, (hold the circle, then fan your face)

It shines on the river, (both hands ripple out in front of you)

And the boats go ROW ROW ROW! (sit down and row, both arms together)

Now the story of Qu Yuan, told plainly and short: a poet who loved his country, and who was so sad that he went into the river. The people who loved him rowed out fast to look for him, and threw rice into the water so the fishes would eat that and not him. That is why the boats race, and that is why the rice is wrapped. Hold up the dragon boat on “rowed out fast”, and the rice dumplings on “wrapped”.

Littles make the sun circle and say “Sun!” Bigs owe you the sentence: “The sun is hot.” “I wear a hat in the sun.”

Teacher fails: try to eat the rice dumplings with the leaves still on, chewing hard, saying how tough it is. Hold it. Wait. They will unwrap you.

Chinese moment: the sun card joins summer · hot — the sun is out. Then the whole class, twice: At Dragon Boat Festival we eat rice dumplings and race dragon boats. And hang mugwort — hang the bundle over the classroom door and leave it there until Friday.

3 MINGAME · DRAGON-BOAT ROW

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody sits in one long line down the room, legs apart, each child behind the one in front. You sit at the back. One child sits at the front with the drum.

Teacher“Hands on your oars… and we row all together — One, two! One, two!” (the drummer keeps the beat; nobody rows faster than the drum)

Row slowly, then faster, then freeze on one drum hit. Chant One, two! One, two! the whole way. Littles just row and shout the numbers; Bigs count in both languages and call the changes.

Swap the drummer twice, so three different children hold the beat.

Everybody rows together or the boat goes nowhere — say that out loud, once, and then let the game prove it. A boat where one child races ahead is the best teaching moment you will get all week.

1 MINCLOSE

Still in the boat, oars up. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Hot… sun… SWIM! Hello, SUMMER!” (fan your face · big sun circle · two front-crawl strokes · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? “The Dragon Boat Race” — any picture-book version, or just the pictures with your own words over the top. Then the rhyme “Dragon Boat Festival”: The fifth day of the fifth month is the Dragon Boat Festival.

THURSDAY
Splash! I Can Swim
Today's words: swim (summer · hot · sun come back)
Grab: swimming goggles, a water bottle and a paper fan in the Magic Box; a long blue cloth or sheet; music you can start and stop; the water card; the action cards from print pack page 15.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box has three things in it and they all belong to the same hot afternoon. Chant, then shake — one soft slosh from the bottle.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Out comes the water bottle: drink. Out comes the fan: shade and air. And last, held up in two hands and put straight onto your own face, the goggles.

Teacher(goggles on, looking round the circle through them, very seriously) “I can see… underwater.”

Two big front-crawl strokes: “Swim!”

4 MINTEACH · SWIM

The blue cloth goes down the middle of the mat and two children hold each end and make it ripple. Say it twice, the second time everybody joins in:

Splash goes the water, (both hands slap the floor)

Kick go my feet, (lie back and kick, or kick sitting down)

Arms go round and round, (two big front-crawl strokes)

Swimming is SWEET! (both arms thrown wide)

Then the water rules, said as plainly as the sun rules and just as often: never go near water without a grown-up, and in the sun you keep your hat on and your T-shirt on, even at the pool. Say it once, clearly, then get straight back to the kicking.

Littles do the two strokes and say “Swim!” Bigs owe you the frame: “I can swim in the water.” “I can kick my feet.”

Teacher fails: put the goggles on over your ears, or upside down over your mouth, and announce that you are ready. Hold it. Wait. Somebody will fix your face for you.

Chinese moment: the water card joins summer · hot · sun — I can swim! shouted once, all together, with the strokes. Then drink plenty of water — and everybody really does take a drink.

3 MINGAME · SWIM ON THE MAT

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody lies on their front at one end of the room. The blue cloth is the water and it goes the whole way across.

Teacher“Music on — SWIM! Music stops — freeze like a starfish.” (front crawl on your tummy all the way across; on the stop, arms and legs out wide and completely still)

Three or four crossings. Change the stroke each time: front crawl, then back stroke on your back, then the slowest swim in the world. Littles just wriggle and freeze; Bigs call out the stroke before each crossing.

The starfish freeze is the whole game — it is a stop signal disguised as fun, and you will use it in the corridor, in the playground and on Friday with a slice of watermelon in every hand.

1 MINCLOSE

Standing, dripping, goggles pushed up. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Hot… sun… SWIM! Hello, SUMMER!” (fan your face · big sun circle · two front-crawl strokes · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Froggy Learns to Swim (Jonathan London) — Froggy is frightened of the water and does it anyway. Let them do all the “Frrrooogggyyy!” shouts for you.

FRIDAY
Ice, Watermelon + Big Review
Today's words: ice — and summer · hot · sun · swim, the whole week
Grab: a whole watermelon, a knife and a big plate (cut it out of sight, bring it in whole); one ice cube per child in a bowl; every prop from the week packed into the box; the awards (print pack page 17); the rice dumpling card. Next week is graduation — say so at the end.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box comes out bulging and will not quite shut, and something on top of it is a great deal too big for it: the whole watermelon.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The sun hat → “Summer!”  The fan → “Hot!”  The dragon boat → “Sun!”  The goggles → “Swim!”

Last of all, one small thing left in the bottom of the bowl, already half gone. Hold it up so the light comes through it. “ICE!”

4 MINTEACH · ICE, THEN THE WHOLE WEEK

Every child gets one ice cube in their own hand and closes their fist round it. Nobody puts it down. We say the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

It's summer! (big sun circle overhead)

It's hot, (fan your face)

Here comes the sun, (both arms rise into the circle again)

I swim and I splash, (two strokes, then slap the floor)

And my ice is… all GONE! (open your fist and show the empty wet hand)

Open every hand together and look: the ice has melted, and the water is still there. Nothing was lost; it only changed. Then the three ice cubes from Tuesday's dishes get their verdict, out loud, from the children.

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “It is hot in summer.” “I can swim in the water.” “The ice is melting.”

Teacher fails: on “my ice is all gone”, hunt for it on the floor, under the mat, behind a child, insisting somebody has taken it. Let them explain melting to you. That explanation, in their words, is the assessment.

Chinese moment: the rice dumpling card joins the row — summer · hot · sun · water · rice dumpling, the whole week on the mat. Then the ice melted, said with the open wet hand. And tell them about Summer Solstice on the 21st: the longest day of the whole year — and after that day the daylight starts getting shorter again. Watch their faces.

3 MINGAME · WATERMELON SHARE

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The watermelon comes in whole. Hold it up, let three children feel how heavy it is, and knock on it so they hear it is hollow. Then it goes out and comes back cut, on the big plate.

Teacher“Everybody gets one slice… and nobody eats until everybody has one.” (say it twice, and say it again while you hand them out)

Hand a slice to every child, two hands, looking at them: “Here you are.” and they answer “Thank you!” Then count round the circle out loud to check nobody has been missed — the counting is what makes the waiting bearable.

On three, everybody bites at once. Littles say “Cold!” Bigs tell you: “The watermelon is cold and sweet.”

The waiting is the lesson, exactly as it was in December. Twenty children holding a slice of watermelon and not eating it is a harder and better piece of work than anything on a shelf.

1 MINCLOSE + AWARDS

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home. Then, standing, sticky, all five gestures:

Everyone“Hot… sun… SWIM! Hello, SUMMER!” (fan your face · big sun circle · two front-crawl strokes · both arms thrown wide on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Sing the whole song one more time outside, in the shade, with the mugwort bundle from Wednesday passed round for one last sniff. Then tell them what next week is: graduation.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → Am → G7 → C. That is the whole chorus, and the whole song is those four shapes. C is one finger, Am is one finger, F is two, G7 is three — learn them in that order, and if Am still frightens you, play C in its place and nobody in the room will ever know. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the paper fan and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the actions carry the meaning either way.
