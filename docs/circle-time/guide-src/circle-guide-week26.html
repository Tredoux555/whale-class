Weekly rituals — use these every single day

Attention grabber: Teacher: “One, two, three — eyes on me!” · Kids: “One, two — eyes on you!”

The Magic Box: any box with a lid. Chant it, shake it, sniff it, peek dramatically — the day's key prop lives inside.

The two-tier rule: never make a little produce a sentence, and never let a big get away with one word.

Whisper → shout close: calms them down, then releases them — every single closing.

Teacher-fails-on-purpose: once a day, get it wrong and let them correct you — the correction shout is the loudest language of the day.

One song a week: same chorus at every circle, one new verse each morning, the whole song on Friday.

MONDAY
Six Colours, One Flag
Today's words: flag
Grab: the real South African flag in the Magic Box (a cloth one you can wave, not a picture); six squares of coloured paper — black, gold, green, white, red, blue; last week's green Africa puzzle piece and a photograph of Teacher Tredoux as a small boy; a long strip of paper pinned up as the week's display; the flag card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is light and flat, and when you tip it something whispers — cloth sliding on cardboard. Whole class chants:

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Shake it — a soft dry whisper, no rattle at all. Sniff it — nothing. Peek in for a long time and let your face go enormous. Then take hold of two corners and pull it out slowly, wide open, so it fills the space in front of you.

Teacher“Look at all the colours… let's COUNT them.” (touch each colour and count out loud together — one… two… three… four… five… SIX!)

Everybody waves an imaginary flag above their head: “Flag!”

4 MINTEACH · FLAG

The flag stays open in your two hands the whole way through. Say it twice; the second time they do the actions with you:

Six bright colours, (hold the flag wide open in both hands)

One big flag, (snap it once so it cracks)

It flies up HIGH… (lift it right above your head)

Wave, wave, wave! (wave it big and slow, side to side)

Then the map. Last week's green Africa piece goes on the floor in the middle of the circle, and your finger travels all the way down it, slowly, to the very bottom point. Stop there. “Right down here, at the bottom — that is South Africa. That is MY country.” Now the photograph goes round the circle: a small boy, in that country, a long time ago.

Littles wave and say “Flag!” Bigs owe you the frame: “This is South Africa.” “The flag has six colours.”

Teacher fails: count the colours again, out loud, confidently — and stop at four. “One, two, three… FOUR! Four colours.” Look delighted with yourself. Wait.

Chinese moment: pin the flag card at the top of the week's paper strip — national flag. Then count the six colours again, in Chinese, touching each one: one · two · three · four · five · six. The row of Chinese characters grows by one card every morning this week.

3 MINGAME · SIX COLOURS

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” The six paper squares are face down in the middle. The flag hangs open where everybody can see it.

Teacher(turn over one square and hold it up) “Green! — can you find green on the flag?” (everybody points at it, then goes and finds that colour somewhere in the room)

Six rounds, six colours. Littles point and say the colour word; Bigs come back with the thing they found and tell you: “I found green. It is a leaf.”

Do black and white last and leave them out on the mat — they are tomorrow's whole lesson, and the children will spot the join themselves.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing, one hand waving an imaginary flag. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Not So Fast, Songololo (Niki Daly) — a small boy and his grandmother go shopping in a South African city, and he gets new red shoes. It is a real street in a real country and the pictures do all the work.

TUESDAY
Zebra Stripes
Today's words: zebra (flag comes back every day)
Grab: a toy zebra and a long length of black-and-white striped cloth in the Magic Box; black and white pegs plus a zebra card on the shelf tray; the horse card; the black and white squares left out from yesterday.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is heavy on one side, and when you tip it something firm thumps from end to end. Chant, then tip it twice — thump … thump — and stop dead.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Take out the cloth first, and keep pulling: it goes on and on and on, far longer than the box could possibly hold, until it is lying right across the circle.

Teacher“Black… white… black… white…” (walk the length of it, tapping each stripe, until you reach the end — then reach back into the box and lift the zebra out)

Both hands draw stripes down their bodies: “Zebra!”

4 MINTEACH · ZEBRA

The zebra stands in your open palm the whole way through. Say it twice; the second time everybody does it with you:

Black and white, black and white, (draw stripes down your front with both hands)

Stripes down my back, (reach over your shoulders and stripe all the way down)

Every zebra is different… (point slowly at three different children)

just like YOU! (point at one child, then at your own chest)

Then the true thing, said plainly and once: no two zebras in the whole world have the same stripes. Exactly like the lines on a fingertip; exactly like the faces sitting in this circle. Hold two children's hands up side by side while you say it.

Littles stripe their bodies and say “Zebra!” Bigs owe you a sentence: “The zebra is black and white.” “The zebra lives in South Africa.”

Teacher fails: drape the striped cloth round your shoulders, crouch down and announce, with total confidence, that you are a TIGER. Roar. Hold it. Wait for the correction.

Chinese moment: the horse card joins flag on the strip — zebra, which is literally stripe-horse, and they will love that. Then the two colours, pointing at the cloth: black · white.

3 MINGAME · ONE LONG ZEBRA

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody lines up nose to tail, each child's hands on the shoulders in front. You are the head. That is one zebra, and it is very long.

Teacher“Zebras walk… slowly… zebras trot… and zebras — FREEZE!” (everybody stops, hands still on shoulders, completely still)

Walk the whole room. Then turn round so the child at the back becomes the head and do it again — the tail leads, and it is a different animal entirely.

Nobody is left holding nobody. If a child does not want to be touched they walk beside the line as the zebra's foal and nothing is said about it.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing, both hands ready to stripe. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? How the Zebra Got Its Stripes — a traditional African tale, in any picture-book edition you can find. Let them do the zebra's kick and the baboon's shriek every time it comes round.

WEDNESDAY
Table Mountain, Flat on Top
Today's words: mountain (flag · zebra come back)
Grab: a big photograph of Table Mountain and a fistful of wooden blocks in the Magic Box; a square of white cloth or a handful of cotton wool for the cloud; a real small table if you have one; the mountain card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is heavy and it clonks — wood on wood. Chant, then shake it once, hard, and let everybody hear it.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Reach in and pull out one block. Then another. Then a whole handful, dropped in a heap on the mat. Last of all, the photograph — held up flat against your chest, face hidden, for one long beat before you turn it round.

Teacher(turn the photograph round) “A mountain… but look at the TOP.”

Flat hands held out level, like a tabletop: “Mountain!”

4 MINTEACH · MOUNTAIN

Everybody stands up for this one. Say it twice; the second time they do every action with you:

A mountain goes UP, up, up, (both hands climb, higher and higher)

But this one stops — FLAT! (slap both flat hands out level, hard)

The clouds come down like a tablecloth, (flutter your fingers down over the flat hands)

And that is that! (fold your arms, done)

Then build. Two mountains out of the blocks, side by side: one pointy, one flat on top. Point at each in turn and let the class vote with their bodies — pointy hands over the head, or flat hands out level — for which one is Table Mountain. Then lay the white cloth over the flat one, slowly, so it hangs down the sides: that is the real cloud that sits on it, and in South Africa people call it the tablecloth.

Littles hold flat hands and say “Mountain!” Bigs owe you the frame: “The mountain is flat on top.” “Table Mountain is in South Africa.”

Teacher fails: build a beautiful pointy mountain, announce it is Table Mountain, and try to lay the cloth on the point. It slides off. Try again. It slides off again. Keep going until somebody takes the cloth off you.

Chinese moment: the mountain card joins flag · horse — high mountain, and this one is Table Mountain: table-mountain, the same word in both languages. Draw mountain in the air with one finger, big, all together: it is a mountain with three peaks — and then flatten the middle one with your hand.

3 MINGAME · FLAT TOP / POINTY TOP

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” No blocks now — the bodies are the mountains.

Teacher“Pointy top!” (everybody makes a point over their head with both hands) “Flat top!” (flat hands out level) “Pointy… flat… FLAT!”

Faster and faster, and then a trick: call “Table Mountain!” and see who goes flat. Littles copy whoever is nearest, which is exactly how they learn it. Last round, a Big calls the shapes and you play in the circle with everybody else.

Two shapes only, over and over. The listening is the lesson: they are matching a whole body to one English word, which is what they must do to hear the difference between any two words.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody standing, flat hands ready. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Jamela's Dress (Niki Daly) — a South African girl, a length of beautiful cloth, and one very big mistake. It is set in the same country as the mountain, and the cloth ties straight back to yesterday's stripes.

THURSDAY
Sawubona!
Today's words: hello (Sawubona) (flag · zebra · mountain come back)
Grab: a Zulu beaded bracelet in the Magic Box; a bowl of big wooden beads and laces on the shelf tray with the red · yellow · blue pattern card; the good card; the class name list, so you can greet every single child by name.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

Today the box is nearly empty and it makes a tiny, hard, seed-like rattle — you have to hold it right up to your ear to hear it. Chant, then hold it to your ear and go completely still.

Everyone“What's in the box? What's in the box?”

Hold it to one child's ear. Then another's. Then lift out the bracelet on one finger and turn it slowly so the light runs round the beads.

Teacher“These beads were made by hand — in a place called KwaZulu-Natal, where my family lives.” (hold it out flat on your palm and let it go all the way round the circle)

A big two-handed wave: “Sawubona!”

4 MINTEACH · HELLO · SAWUBONA

Nothing in your hands for this one. Say it twice; the second time they wave it with you:

Sawubona! (a big two-handed wave, right over your head)

It means HELLO. (wave again, smaller, at one child)

Say it soft… (whisper it, one hand beside your mouth)

say it SLOW. (say it in four slow pieces — sa · wu · BO · na — clapping each one)

Then the round, and this is the work: you go to every child in turn, look at them, and say “Sawubona, !” They answer “Yebo, sawubona!” — yes, I see you too. Say the true thing once, plainly: this is Zulu, one of the twelve languages of South Africa, and it does not really mean “hello” at all — it means “I see you.” In this room we already speak two languages. Now we have three.

Littles wave and say “Sawubona!” Bigs owe you the frame: “Sawubona means hello.” “Sawubona, teacher!”

Teacher fails: greet the shelf with an enormous “SAWUBONA!”, then the door, then the whale on the wall. Shake the door's handle warmly. Wait for them to point you at an actual person.

Chinese moment: the good card joins flag · horse · mountain — hello. Then the three greetings in one breath, round the whole circle: hello · sawubona. Three languages, one circle, ten seconds.

3 MINGAME · PASS THE BRACELET

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody in a circle, knees touching. The bracelet goes round hand to hand, two hands to give and two hands to take.

Teacher“Look at the bead under your thumb — what colour is it? Say it… then say sawubona to the friend you give it to.” (they name the colour, turn, and greet the next child by name)

Round the whole circle. Littles just name the colour and wave; Bigs use the name: “Sawubona, Amy!” Nobody wears it and nobody keeps it — it is a thing that travels.

Go slowly enough that every single child is greeted by their own name, out loud, in front of everybody. That is the real lesson of the day and it takes exactly as long as it takes.

1 MINCLOSE

Everybody on their feet, both hands up ready to wave. Whisper → normal → shout:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave on the shout)

Before home time: tomorrow everybody greets everybody, three times over, in three languages. Walk the circle once today in silence and practise just the looking — eyes up, find the face, then wave. It makes Friday twice as good.

Got 3 spare minutes? Handa's Surprise (Eileen Browne) — a girl carries a basket of fruit to her friend and one animal after another helps themselves. Let them shout the animal's name before you turn the page.

FRIDAY
My Country + Big Review
Today's words: South Africa — and flag · zebra · mountain · hello, the whole week
Grab: every prop from the week packed back into the box, plus Teacher Tredoux's own photographs from home and an African drum; a raw egg for Spring Equinox; the awards (print pack page 17); the south card.
2 MINMAGIC BOX HOOK

The box comes out bulging and will not quite shut.

Teacher“Today the box is FULL — it's got our whole WEEK inside it!”

Pull each thing out and hold it up in complete silence. They will supply the word before you can.

The flag → “Flag!”  The zebra → “Zebra!”  The blocks and the photo → “Mountain!”  The bracelet → “Sawubona!”

Last of all, right at the bottom, a small bundle of photographs. Lift them out and hold up the first one. “SOUTH AFRICA!”

4 MINTEACH · SOUTH AFRICA, THEN THE WHOLE WEEK

Everybody stands. We tell the whole week with our bodies, twice — slow, then fast:

A flag with six bright colours, (wave it overhead)

A zebra, black and white, (stripes down your body)

A mountain flat on top, (flat hands out level, held high)

Sawubona — that's “hello”! (a big two-handed wave)

This is SOUTH AFRICA — my home! (both arms thrown wide, then both hands over your heart)

Then the photographs, one at a time, slowly, and told as plainly as you can: this is the town I come from — Newcastle, in KwaZulu-Natal. This is the house. This is the road I walked to school on. This is my family. Let them hold the pictures. A real photograph of a real person's real house does more in ten seconds than any poster on any wall.

Littles do the five gestures and shout the five words. Bigs owe you a whole sentence each: “This is South Africa.” “The zebra is black and white.” “Sawubona means hello.”

Teacher fails: on “this is my home”, point confidently at the classroom window and name this city. Look certain. Then look at the photograph, then out of the window, then at the photograph again. Let them sort you out.

Chinese moment: the south card completes the row — flag · horse · mountain · good · south, the whole week on the strip, and south is the one that matters: South Africa, south-Africa, the country at the bottom. Then Spring Equinox: it fell on Sunday, and on Spring Equinox the day and the night are exactly the same length. Stand the raw egg on its end on a flat table — the Spring Equinox egg-standing — and let them take turns until somebody does it.

3 MINGAME · SAWUBONA ROUND

“One, two, three — eyes on me!” Everybody stands in the circle facing a partner. This is the whole week, performed.

Teacher“Look at them… wave… and say it three times: sawubona… … hello.” (then everybody takes one step to the left and does it again with the next friend)

Round and round until every child has greeted every child. Bigs use the name every time: “Sawubona, Amy!” Littles wave and say one of the three — any one of them — and that is a full win.

Say the names out loud with them as they go. Twenty children hearing their own name twenty times, in three languages, in one circle, is the best ten minutes of language work you will do all term.

1 MINCLOSE + AWARDS

Every child gets a “You are a STAR!” award to take home. Then, standing in the circle with the drum, all five gestures:

Everyone“Flag… zebra… MOUNTAIN! SAWUBONA, SOUTH AFRICA!” (wave the flag · stripes down your body · flat hands overhead · a big two-handed wave, with the drum, on the shout)

Got 3 spare minutes? Africa, Amazing Africa (Atinuke) — one spread per country, big pictures, short lines. Find South Africa at the very bottom and read only that page. Next week we come back to where we are standing: spring, and everything waking up.

Can't manage it yet? Four slow downs per bar works fine — the children only hear the wave and the stripes.

Practice this loop for five minutes tonight: C (4 strums) → F (4 strums) → C → G7 → C. That is the whole verse, and the chorus adds one shape only — Am, one finger on the top string at the second fret, which is the same finger you already use for F, just moved. C is one finger, Am is one finger, F is two, G7 is three; learn them in that order. If your fingers get stuck, put the uke down, give one child the drum and do the finger-play instead: the class will not notice, and the wave and the stripes carry the meaning either way.
