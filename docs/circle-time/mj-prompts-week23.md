# Midjourney prompts — Circle Time, site Week 21 · “The Seven Continents” (Mar 1–5)

Sheet week 23 in the principal's plan. 37 images → `public/circle-time-images/week21/`.
Runbook: `docs/circle-time/mj-prompts-README.md`. Style is LOCKED — do not paraphrase the suffix.

- **8 posters** `--ar 3:4`, saved 928×1232 · **28 cards + 1 badge** `--ar 1:1`, saved 1000×1000
- Flags on every prompt: `--raw --stylize 50 --v 8.2`
- Submit one prompt per 30 s through the authenticated `midjourney.com/imagine` tab.
- Cards are hyper-literal and singular — one object, centred, nothing else in frame.
- **🚨 Every map, globe and land shape must be BLANK.** Continents are plain rounded green
  (or plain yellow, for Asia) shapes on plain blue water — no coastlines copied from a real
  atlas, no country outlines, no borders. Every such prompt carries *no writing, no place
  names, no flags* on purpose; do not soften it. MJ renders map lettering as nonsense, and
  the locked negative list already forbids text. The children read the names off the printed
  labels and the classroom puzzle map, never off the art.
- **Every prompt that shows a person** (all eight posters that have a child in them and all
  eight action cards) carries *clothed, flat cartoon illustration, children's book art, no
  photo-realism*: the AI Moderator flags people-heavy prompts and this is the wording that
  clears it. Never give an age in years, never describe a body, never mention skin.
- The panda is the only animal in the set, and only on the two `asia` images — it is how a
  three-year-old recognises “our continent” before they can read anything at all.
- Save each upscale as the exact `.png` filename below; `mj_convert.sh 21` turns them into the
  `.jpg` the page asks for.
