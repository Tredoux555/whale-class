Letter sounds — recording script

One continuous take. Read straight down the list. The spoken label before each sound is what stops them getting jumbled again — don't skip it.

The pattern

"A for apple."  — beat —  "aaaaa"  — three seconds of silence —
"B for ball."  — beat —  "b"  — three seconds of silence —

Before you start

  1. Leave a real beat between the label and the sound. Say "A for apple", stop for a full second — count one-one-thousand — then make the sound. This is the one that matters most: with no gap I cannot cut the label off, and the clip ships saying "A for apple, aaa".
  2. Record five seconds of silence first. Don't speak. This gives me clean room tone to denoise the whole take against.
  3. A hand's width from the mic, speaking past it — not straight into it. Otherwise p, t, b and k will pop and clip.
  4. Three full seconds between sounds. Count it. Plosives are quiet and short; tight gaps are what break the auto-split.
  5. Don't move between letters. Consistent distance keeps levels even.
  6. Fluffed one? Don't restart. Pause, say "A for apple, again," and redo it. I'll take the last good version.
  7. Order doesn't matter — the labels sort it out. Go alphabetically or in teaching order, whichever feels natural.
